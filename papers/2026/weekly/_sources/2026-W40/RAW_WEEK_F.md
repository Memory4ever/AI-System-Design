L15: cite10†View PDF cite11†HTML (experimental) L16: > Abstract:In-context imitation learning allows robots to acquire skills from demonstrations, yet one-shot trajectory generation remains fragile under environmental variation. We propose SAIL, a framework that reframes robot imitation as an iterative refinement problem capable of scaling with test-time compute. SAIL utilizes Monte Carlo Tree Search, where each node is a complete trajectory and edges correspond to trajectory refinements.
L17: The process is guided by three core components: an automated archive of successful trajectories for contextually relevant retrieval, a vision language model-based scoring mechanism for trajectory evaluation, and a step-level feedback that provides trajectory-aligned scores for iterative refinement.
L18: Experiments across six diverse manipulation tasks in simulation and real-world validation clearly demonstrate that increasing test-time compute consistently improves success rates, achieving up to 95% on complex tasks. Our results suggest that trajectory-level test-time scaling is a robust path toward more generalizable robotic agents.

L10@P0: Long-term memory is essential for conversational agents to maintain coherence, track persistent tasks, and provide personalized interactions across extended dialogues. However, existing approaches as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and graph-based memory mostly
L11@P0: rely on pairwise relations, which can hardly
L12@P0: capture high-order associations, i.e., joint dependencies among multiple elements, causing
L13@P0: fragmented retrieval. To this end, we propose HyperMem, a hypergraph-based hierarchical memory architecture that explicitly models such associations using hyperedges. Particularly, HyperMem structures memory into three
L14@P0: levels: topics, episodes, and facts, and groups
L15@P0: related episodes and their facts via hyperedges,
L16@P0: unifying scattered content into coherent units.

L18@P0: lexical-semantic index and a coarse-to-fine retrieval strategy, supporting accurate and efficient retrieval of high-order associations. Experiments on the LoCoMo benchmark show
L19@P0: that HyperMem achieves state-of-the-art performance with 92.73% LLM-as-a-judge accuracy,
L20@P0: demonstrating the effectiveness of HyperMem
L21@P0: for long-term conversations.
