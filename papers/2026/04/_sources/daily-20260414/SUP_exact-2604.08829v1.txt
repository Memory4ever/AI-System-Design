Abstract
The self-attention mechanism at the core of modern Transformer
models treats every pair of tokens in a sequence with equal
architectural capacity, regardless of how far apart they are.
This single-scale bias limits performance on tasks that
require reasoning simultaneously at short and long range,
while incurring quadratic computational cost in sequence length.
The Hierarchical Kernel Transformer (HKT) is proposed,
a multi-scale attention mechanism that processes the input
sequence at multiple resolution levels simultaneously.
At each level, a compressed version of the sequence is obtained
by trainable causal downsampling; attention scores are computed
independently at each level and combined through learned weights.
This design captures both local patterns and long-range structure
with a total computational cost of at most 4/34/3 times that of
standard attention, regardless of the number of levels
(1.3125×1.3125\times for three levels).
Four theoretical contributions are established:
(i) the hierarchical scoring function defines a positive
semidefinite kernel under a sufficient condition on the
symmetrised bilinear form (Proposition 3.1);
(ii) the asymmetric score matrix decomposes uniquely into
a symmetric component controlling reciprocal attention and
an antisymmetric component controlling directional attention,
with HKT providing LL independent such pairs across scales
(Propositions 3.5–3.6);
(iii) the approximation error decomposes into three interpretable
components with an explicit non-Gaussian correction and a
geometric decay bound in the number of levels
(Theorem 4.3, Proposition 4.4);
(iv) HKT strictly subsumes standard attention and causal
convolution in the single-head setting (Proposition 3.4).
Empirically, HKT achieves consistent gains over retrained
standard attention baselines across three tasks of different
modalities (mean ±\pm std, 3 seeds):
+4.77+4.77pp on synthetic ListOps (55.10±0.29%55.10\pm 0.29\% vs
50.33±0.12%50.33\pm 0.12\%, T=512T=512),
+1.44+1.44pp on sequential CIFAR-10 (35.45±0.09%35.45\pm 0.09\% vs
34.01±0.19%34.01\pm 0.19\%, T=1,024T=1{,}024),
and +7.47+7.47pp on IMDB character-level sentiment classification
(70.19±0.57%70.19\pm 0.57\% vs 62.72±0.40%62.72\pm 0.40\%, T=1,024T=1{,}024),
all at 1.31×1.31\times computational overhead.

1 Introduction
The self-attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) has become the
dominant computational primitive in sequence modelling.
For a sequence of TT tokens, attention computes a weighted average
of value vectors, where the weights are determined by pairwise
dot products between query and key projections.
This single operation, repeated across layers, has proven
remarkably expressive across a wide range of tasks.
Yet this simplicity conceals a structural limitation.
Standard self-attention is scale-blind: a token at position
tt attends to every other token with the same architectural
capacity, whether the target is adjacent or at distance T/2T/2.
The network must learn to ignore distant tokens when local context
suffices, and to attend globally when long-range reasoning is
required, with no structural prior to guide either regime.
This limitation becomes critical on long-sequence tasks.
The Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark (Tay et al., 2021) systematically
documents this failure: standard MHA achieves only 36.37%36.37\% on
ListOps at sequence length T=2,048T=2{,}048.
Efficient attention variants (Beltagy et al., 2020; Choromanski et al., 2021)
address computational cost but not the structural bias: they
restrict which pairs are computed, not how multi-scale structure
is captured.
A different approach is taken here.
Rather than sparsifying or approximating the attention matrix,
it is factored across scales.
The Hierarchical Kernel Transformer (HKT) computes attention at
LL resolution levels, with each level operating on a downsampled
representation of the sequence.
The level-ll score matrix captures interactions at scale sls^{l},
where s≥2s\geq 2 is the downsampling stride.
The final attention is a learned convex combination of level-specific
scores, upsampled to the original resolution.
This design has three properties that standard attention lacks.
First, it is structurally multi-scale: level ll captures
the frequency band [π/sl,π/sl−1][\pi/s^{l},\pi/s^{l-1}] in the sequence
spectrum (Proposition 3.3).
Second, it is computationally efficient: the total cost
is 43​(1−4−L)\frac{4}{3}(1-4^{-L}) times MHA, bounded above by
43\frac{4}{3} regardless of LL (Proposition 4.6).
Third, it admits information-theoretic guarantees: the
approximation error per hierarchical level is bounded in terms of
the mutual information I⁡(S(l),f⁡(X))I(S^{(l)};f(X)), related explicitly
to the squared multiple correlation ρl2\rho_{l}^{2} and the
non-Gaussianity index κl\kappa_{l} (Theorem 4.3).
Contributions.
The following contributions are made:
1.
Architecture (Section 3).
HKT is defined as a hierarchical attention mechanism combining
LL resolution levels with a hybrid conv/attention head and
input-dependent level fusion.
2.
Kernel theory (Section 3).
The hierarchical score matrix is shown to define a PSD kernel
under a sufficient condition on the symmetrised bilinear form
(Proposition 3.1), its Gram matrix factorises as
a sum of per-level PSD matrices with an explicit rank bound
(Proposition 3.2), and HKT is shown to strictly
subsume single-head attention and causal convolution in the
single-layer, H=1H=1 setting
(Proposition 3.4).
3.
Asymmetric score analysis (Section 3.6).
A direct analysis of the asymmetric score function used in
practice is provided, without symmetrisation.
The symmetric-antisymmetric decomposition
M(l)=Ms(l)+Ma(l)M^{(l)}=M_{s}^{(l)}+M_{a}^{(l)} is shown to separate
reciprocity (mutual attention strength, controlled by
Ms(l)M_{s}^{(l)}) from directionality (attention asymmetry,
controlled by Ma(l)M_{a}^{(l)})
(Proposition 3.5, Corollary 3.7).
HKT with LL levels disposes of LL independent such pairs,
one per scale (Proposition 3.6).
This analysis applies to the operational model and explains
why the PSD condition is not satisfied in trained models.
4.
Approximation theory (Section 4).
A three-term error decomposition is derived with an explicit
non-Gaussian bound on the information-theoretic reduction
per level, presented as a motivated approximation framework
extending the Gaussian process results of Roberts et al. (2022)
to finite-width networks.
5.
Experiments (Section 5).
Consistent improvements over retrained MHA baselines are
obtained on two tasks: +4.7+4.7pp on synthetic ListOps
(T=512T=512) and +1.98+1.98pp on sequential CIFAR-10 (T=1,024T=1{,}024),
both at 1.31×1.31\times computational overhead.
Sensitivity analysis across L∈{1,2,3,4}L\in\{1,2,3,4\} and
s∈{2,3}s\in\{2,3\} confirms that the gains are robust to
hyperparameter choice.
2 Related Work
Efficient attention.
A large body of work reduces the 𝒪⁡(T2)\mathcal{O}(T^{2}) cost of
attention through sparsity (Beltagy et al., 2020; Zaheer et al., 2020),
low-rank approximation (Wang et al., 2020; Choromanski et al., 2021),
or linear kernels (Katharopoulos et al., 2020).
These methods address computational cost without introducing
hierarchical structure. HKT is orthogonal: hierarchical structure
is added at bounded additional cost rather than reducing
existing capacity.
Hierarchical and multi-scale transformers.
Hierarchical processing is central to convolutional
networks (LeCun et al., 1998) and has been incorporated into
vision transformers through patch-merging windows (Liu et al., 2021a),
pooling-based token compression (Liu et al., 2021b),
and cluster-based token merging (Wang et al., 2021).
These methods operate on spatial data and downsample hidden states
rather than attention scores.
More recently, FasterViT (Hatamizadeh et al., 2024)
decomposes global self-attention into local window attention
and hierarchical carrier tokens, achieving competitive
accuracy-throughput trade-offs on image classification.
HAT-Net (Liu et al., 2024) proposes hierarchical MHSA that
computes local attention first and then models global
dependencies on merged tokens.
Both FasterViT and HAT-Net target vision tasks and reduce
computational cost by restricting attention scope;
HKT instead constructs independent score matrices at each
resolution and fuses them with learned weights, maintaining
full expressivity at each scale while bounding total cost.
Hierarchical transformers for sequences.
For text, hierarchical transformers exploit document structure
(words →\to sentences →\to sections) to handle long inputs
efficiently.
HDT (He et al., 2024) processes long documents by interleaving
local and global attention in a single layer using an efficient
hierarchical attention pattern.
HIBRIDS (Cao and Wang, 2022) introduces hierarchical biases
for structure-aware long document summarisation.
These methods rely on explicit structural segmentation of the input.
HKT instead learns a continuous multi-scale decomposition via
causal downsampling, making no assumptions about document structure
and extending naturally to non-textual sequences.
State space models.
Structured state space models (SSMs) such as Mamba
(Gu and Dao, 2023) offer an alternative to attention for
long-sequence modelling, achieving linear time complexity via
selective state propagation.
Mamba achieves strong results on the LRA benchmark and
matches or exceeds transformers of similar size on language tasks.
HKT and Mamba address different aspects of the long-sequence
problem: Mamba replaces attention with a recurrent mechanism,
while HKT retains the attention mechanism and augments it with
explicit multi-scale structure.
The two approaches are complementary and could in principle
be combined (multi-scale Mamba layers with hierarchical
state updates), which we leave as future work.
Kernel methods for attention.
The connection between attention and kernel methods has been
formalised in Tsai et al. (2019); Katharopoulos et al. (2020); Choromanski et al. (2021).
Performers (Choromanski et al., 2021) approximate the softmax kernel
with random feature maps.
HKT employs a sum of hierarchical kernels, each PSD under a
sufficient condition (Proposition 3.1), without
approximation.
The asymmetric score decomposition
M(l)=Ms(l)+Ma(l)M^{(l)}=M_{s}^{(l)}+M_{a}^{(l)} (Section 3.6)
provides a direct analysis of the operational model that
complements the kernel-theoretic view.
Long Range Arena.
Tay et al. (2021) introduced LRA as a systematic benchmark for
long-range sequence models, comprising five tasks with sequence
lengths up to 4,0964{,}096.
On ListOps, standard MHA achieves 36.37%36.37\%, Longformer 35.63%35.63\%,
and Performer 18.01%18.01\%.
The synthetic variant used here reproduces the algebraic structure
of ListOps at T=512T=512.
Gaussian process limits.
Roberts et al. (2022) establish that sufficiently wide networks
converge to Gaussian processes, with corrections of order
𝒪⁡(1/d)\mathcal{O}(1/d).
Lemma 4.2 extends this analysis to the
information content of hierarchical score matrices, providing
an explicit non-Gaussian correction for the finite-width regime.

Remark on the PSD condition.
M(l)M^{(l)} is symmetric by construction but not necessarily PSD,
since WQ(l)⊤​WK(l)W_{Q}^{(l)\top}W_{K}^{(l)} may have negative eigenvalues.
The condition M(l)⪰0M^{(l)}\succeq 0 can be enforced by

projecting M(l)M^{(l)} onto the PSD cone after each gradient step.
In practice, neither constraint is imposed.
We verified empirically that the PSD condition is not satisfied
by trained models: across all 12 configurations (4 encoder
layers ×\times 3 hierarchy levels) of a trained HKT-Small,
approximately 50%50\% of eigenvalues of M(l)M^{(l)} are negative
(min eigenvalue ranging from −0.51-0.51 to −0.08-0.08).
This is consistent with M(l)M^{(l)} being a generic symmetric
matrix: without an explicit PSD constraint, gradient descent
has no reason to confine M(l)M^{(l)} to the PSD cone.
The symmetrised kernel therefore serves as a theoretical
surrogate rather than a description of the actual trained model
(see Definition 1 below).

Definition 1 (Practical note on asymmetric scores).
In practice, WQ(l)≠WK(l)W_{Q}^{(l)}\neq W_{K}^{(l)} and the raw score

Closing this gap formally — proving a PSD result directly

non-symmetric positive definite framework, both of which
reduce expressivity.

3.3 Hybrid head and dynamic fusion

Remark 1 (Connection to the empirical PSD failure).
The empirical finding that Ms(l)M_{s}^{(l)} is not PSD for any
trained configuration (Section 6)
is not explained by Ma(l)≠0M_{a}^{(l)}\neq 0 alone —
the two facts are logically independent
(a matrix can have Ma≠0M_{a}\neq 0 and still have Ms⪰0M_{s}\succeq 0,
as the example M=(2102)M=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\
0&2\end{pmatrix}
shows).
The PSD failure is instead a consequence of the unconstrained
optimisation: gradient descent has no objective that would
confine Ms(l)M_{s}^{(l)} to the PSD cone.
The directional energy analysis and the PSD analysis are
therefore complementary, not redundant:
Corollary 3.7 characterises the asymmetric
component; Proposition 3.1 characterises
a constrained variant in which the symmetric component
would be PSD.
Implication for HKT.
Proposition 3.5 shows that the asymmetric
attention mechanism of HKT is not a defect of the PSD
surrogate analysis but a structural feature:
Ma(l)≠0M_{a}^{(l)}\neq 0 is what allows the model to represent
directed relationships (token ii queries token jj
differently from how jj queries ii).
Proposition 3.6 shows that HKT amplifies
this capacity: with LL levels, it can represent LL
independent directional patterns, each at a different
spatial scale.
This is a direct theoretical result about the
operational model, not about a symmetrised surrogate.

Remark 2 (Interpretation and empirical calibration).
Assumption H4 requires that each new level reduces the
residual error by at least a fraction δ\delta of the
current error — a “diminishing returns” condition that
is weaker than requiring a fixed absolute reduction.
The bound (17) shows that the hierarchical
error decays geometrically in LL, with rate 1−δ1-\delta.
Empirically, the sensitivity table
(Table 3, s=2s=2) shows accuracy
increasing from 49.9%49.9\% (L=1L=1) to 55.7%55.7\% (L=2L=2)
to 55.3%55.3\% (L=3L=3) to 57.7%57.7\% (L=4L=4), a pattern
consistent with diminishing but positive returns at each level.
Assuming a linear relationship between accuracy and
−εhier-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hier}}, the empirical gain

The assumption H4 is not verified theoretically; it is
presented as a sufficient condition that makes the decay

6 Discussion
On the multi-task results.
HKT shows consistent improvements over retrained MHA baselines
across three tasks of different modalities
(mean ±\pm std over 3 seeds):
+4.77+4.77pp on synthetic ListOps
(55.10±0.29%55.10\pm 0.29\% vs 50.33±0.12%50.33\pm 0.12\%, Experiment 1),
+1.44+1.44pp on sequential CIFAR-10
(35.45±0.09%35.45\pm 0.09\% vs 34.01±0.19%34.01\pm 0.19\%, Experiment 3),
and +7.47+7.47pp on IMDB character-level sentiment
(70.19±0.57%70.19\pm 0.57\% vs 62.72±0.40%62.72\pm 0.40\%, Experiment 4).
The gain varies by task in a theoretically interpretable way:
it is largest on IMDB-char, where both local nn-gram
patterns and long-range semantic dependencies are essential;
modest on sCIFAR-10, where local texture dominates;
and intermediate on ListOps, which has a purely
hierarchical algebraic structure.
The low standard deviation across seeds (all ≤0.57\leq 0.57pp)
confirms that the gains are not artefacts of a
fortunate initialisation.
The ablation (Experiment 2) confirms that the hierarchical
structure drives the gains: removing the hierarchy (L=1L=1)
is the largest single drop in the ablation table,
while parameter count plays a negligible role.
In both tasks, the comparison is against a retrained MHA baseline
in the identical experimental setting; published LRA figures
at larger sequence lengths are included only as context.
On the gap between PSD theory and trained models.
A natural question raised by Proposition 3.1 is
whether the sufficient condition M(l)⪰0M^{(l)}\succeq 0 holds
in practice.
We measured the eigenvalues of Ms(l)M_{s}^{(l)} for all 12
configurations of a trained HKT-Small (Experiment 6):
in every configuration, approximately 50%50\% of eigenvalues
are negative, confirming that the PSD condition is not
satisfied.
This is expected: without an explicit constraint, gradient
descent has no incentive to keep M(l)M^{(l)} in the PSD cone.
Proposition 3.1 therefore describes a constrained
variant; the asymmetric score drives the empirical gains.
Complementarily, the symmetric-antisymmetric decomposition
M(l)=Ms(l)+Ma(l)M^{(l)}=M_{s}^{(l)}+M_{a}^{(l)}
(Proposition 3.5) provides direct insight
into the operational model.
The ratio ‖Ms(l)‖F/‖Ma(l)‖F\|M_{s}^{(l)}\|_{F}/\|M_{a}^{(l)}\|_{F} decreases
monotonically with level: from 1.531.53 at l=0l=0 (local)
to 1.111.11 at l=2l=2 (global), a 27%27\% reduction.
This is consistent with the multi-scale hypothesis:
global attention allocates relatively more weight to the
directional component Ma(l)M_{a}^{(l)}, which encodes asymmetric
long-range dependencies.
On the non-Gaussian regime.
Mardia’s test statistic reaches the upper boundary of its
asymptotic calibration range for every (n,p,d)(n,p,d) combination
tested (n≤5,000n\leq 5{,}000, d≤512d\leq 512).
To avoid misreading: this does not mean κl\kappa_{l} is bounded by
the ceiling value — it means the test can only confirm
κl\kappa_{l} exceeds the calibrated range, not quantify by how much.
The actual values κl≈24\kappa_{l}\approx 24–3333 reported in
Appendix D are the true normalised kurtosis
(directly computable as b2,p/120b_{2,p}/120), not test statistics.
The HKT score distributions are therefore in the heavily
non-Gaussian regime, and the correction term
(κl−1)​ρl2/2(\kappa_{l}-1)\rho_{l}^{2}/2 in Lemma 4.2
is a dominant contribution rather than a refinement.
Whether κl→1\kappa_{l}\to 1 for d≫512d\gg 512, as predicted by
Roberts et al. (2022), remains open.
Limitations.
(i) The experiments use a synthetic ListOps variant (T=512T=512)
rather than the original LRA dataset (T=2,048T=2{,}048).
(ii) The ablation is conducted on the same synthetic task.
(iii) Verification of H2’ at d≥1,024d\geq 1{,}024 with
n≥104n\geq 10^{4} samples lies outside the current computational budget.
Future directions.
(i) Evaluation on the full LRA suite and on PathFinder-32
(Tay et al., 2021) (publicly available on HuggingFace).
(ii) Measurement of ρl2\rho_{l}^{2} per level to validate
Corollary 4.4 and enable data-driven
weight selection.
(iii) Scaling to d≥1,024d\geq 1{,}024 to test κl→1\kappa_{l}\to 1.
7 Conclusion
The central question motivating this work is whether the
single-scale architecture of standard self-attention is a
fundamental limitation or merely a design choice.
The results presented here suggest the latter.
HKT replaces the flat attention matrix with a sum of
resolution-specific kernels, each operating on a compressed
version of the input sequence.
The compression is trainable, causal, and computationally cheap:
the total overhead converges to 4/34/3 as the number of levels
grows, and is 1.31×1.31\times for three levels.
The theoretical analysis reveals a structure that flat attention
cannot express: the Gram matrix of HKT factorises as a sum of
per-level PSD matrices (Proposition 3.2), each
encoding co-occurrence patterns at a distinct spatial scale.
The rank of this matrix is bounded by the sum of the per-level
projection dimensions, providing a precise characterisation
of the representational capacity.
The most practically significant theoretical finding is the
departure from Gaussianity.
Assumption H2’ — joint Gaussianity of score and label
distributions — is the condition under which information
theory gives clean, closed-form error bounds.
Mardia’s test rejects H2’ at all tested model widths
(d≤512d\leq 512), and the test statistic saturates the ceiling
at every sample size and dimensionality tested.
This is not a failure of the theory: the non-Gaussian bound of
Lemma 4.2 is valid without H2’,
and the correction term (κl−1)​ρl2/2(\kappa_{l}-1)\rho_{l}^{2}/2
is empirically dominant.
The open question is whether the Gaussian limit is recovered
at larger widths (d≥1,024d\geq 1{,}024), as predicted by the
infinite-width theory of Roberts et al. (2022).
Empirically, the +4.7+4.7pp gain over a retrained standard
attention baseline on synthetic ListOps (T=512T=512) is modest
in absolute terms, but the ablation localises almost all of it
to the hierarchical structure rather than parameter count.
HKT-Small with 11M parameters matches HKT-Large with 4545M
parameters within 0.40.4pp.
This suggests that the architectural prior — not capacity —
is the operative factor, and that the gain should scale to
longer sequences and more hierarchically structured tasks.
Three directions follow directly.
First, evaluation on the full Long Range Arena suite
(Tay et al., 2021) would establish whether the advantage
is task-specific or general.
Second, measurement of ρl2\rho_{l}^{2} per level would validate
Corollary 4.4 and enable data-driven
weight selection, potentially improving on the learned weights.
Third, scaling to d≥1,024d\geq 1{,}024 with sufficient sample
sizes would resolve the Gaussianity question empirically.
