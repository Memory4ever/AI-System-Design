Abstract.
Large Language Models (LLM) have been widely used in reranking. Computational overhead and large context lengths remain a challenging issue for LLM rerankers. Efficient reranking usually involves selecting a subset of the ranked list from the first stage, known as ranked list truncation (RLT). The truncated list is processed further by a reranker. For LLM rerankers, the ranked list is often partitioned and processed sequentially in batches to reduce the context length. Both these steps involve hyperparameters and topic-agnostic heuristics. Recently, LLMs have been shown to be effective for relevance judgment. Equivalently, we propose that LLMs can be used to generate reference documents that can act as a pivot between relevant and non-relevant documents in a ranked list. We propose methods to use these generated reference documents for RLT as well as for efficient listwise reranking. While reranking, we process the ranked list in either parallel batches of non-overlapping windows or overlapping windows with adaptive strides, improving the existing fixed stride setup. The generated reference documents are also shown to improve existing efficient listwise reranking frameworks. Experiments on TREC Deep Learning benchmarks show that our approach outperforms existing RLT-based approaches. In-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that our proposed methods accelerate LLM-based listwise reranking by up to 66% compared to existing approaches. This work not only establishes a practical paradigm for efficient LLM-based reranking but also provides insight into the capability of LLMs to generate semantically controlled documents using relevance signals.

1. Introduction
Retrieval and reranking pipelines are increasingly being adopted in search systems. Managing the efficiency-effectiveness trade-off for multi-stage retrieval pipelines poses a significant challenge due to the use of effective but computationally expensive reranking models (Ma et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2021; Lien et al., 2019a). Reranking strategies vary significantly across models and can be broadly categorized into pointwise, pairwise, and listwise setups (Pradeep et al., 2021a; Qin et al., 2024; Sinhababu et al., 2024; Sinhababu et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2023).
Typically, in a pointwise or pairwise setup, the first-stage retrieve candidate list is pruned before reranking (Nogueira and Cho, 2019). This method of pruning before reranking in a typical “retrieve then rerank” setup is known as ranked list truncation (RLT) and has been widely adopted in reranking pipelines (Lien et al., 2019b; Meng et al., 2024). However, recent literature shows that a fixed cut-off or score-based heuristic method is often suboptimal because they ignore query-specific relevance distributions and reranking gains (Bahri et al., 2020). Most existing approaches view truncation as a selection over the retrieved list, without exploiting richer semantic structure (Gao et al., 2022). Additionally, there are instances where reranking a larger ranked list without truncation is beneficial to facilitate performance gains, particularly for listwise LLM rerankers (Sun et al., 2023; Pradeep et al., 2023b). However, the improvement in retrieval performance comes at the expense of added reranking time, which harms the overall responsiveness of retrieval pipelines, particularly in real-time applications (Pradeep et al., 2021b).
Recent studies indicate that LLMs demonstrate strong capabilities in understanding semantics and query-document relationships (Thomas et al., 2024; Faggioli et al., 2023b). In particular, LLMs have been successfully utilised for zero-shot, few-shot, and instruction-tuned relevance judgment, surpassing even human annotators in terms of consistency and completeness (Faggioli et al., 2023b). However, while research has extensively explored the capability of LLMs for relevance judgment, their potential to generate semantically controlled reference-documents within retrieval pipelines remains largely unexplored (Shen et al., 2025).
Our work investigates the complementary role of LLMs in multi-stage retrieval, utilizing them to generate a moderately relevant reference-document for a given query and then leveraging this document as a semantic pivot to distinguish between highly relevant and non-relevant documents in a ranked list. The idea of leveraging a semantic pivot enables designing a new RLT approach for efficient reranking in pointwise and pairwise setups that is both adaptive and model-agnostic, thereby improving efficiency without requiring explicit model training or parameter tuning. In listwise reranking, for which LLMs are now commonly used, the ranked list is often partitioned into overlapping windows and processed sequentially in batches to reduce the context length, known as the sliding window (Sinhababu et al., 2025; Parry et al., 2024). Using the generated pivot, we propose methods that can process the ranked list in either parallel batches of non-overlapping windows or overlapping windows with adaptive strides, improving the existing fixed stride setup. We further investigate the application of this semantic pivot in existing methods that require partitioning to facilitate efficient listwise reranking.
Our Contributions.
The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
(1)
LLM-generated semantic reference document: We leverage the generation capability of LLMs to generate a semantically controlled pivot-document that acts as a pivot distinguishing relevant from non-relevant documents within a ranked list.
(2)
Adaptive truncation via pivot placement: We propose PSI-Rank to utilize the generated pivot for RLT in pointwise and pairwise setups, wherein the retriever itself determines the pivot’s position in the ranked list. This approach requires only a single model inference call for scoring the pivot, thereby improving reranking efficiency without exhaustive scoring.
(3)
Parallel listwise reranking through document propagation: In listwise reranking, we incorporate the semantic pivot to enable document propagation across non-overlapping windows (SNOW), facilitating parallel batch processing and substantially reducing inference latency.
(4)
Adaptive stride mechanism for sliding windows: We extend the conventional sliding window strategy by introducing an adaptive stride mechanism (VS-Sliding). The pivot dynamically estimates relevance density across reranking windows to balance contextual completeness and computational efficiency.
(5)
Enhanced anchor for TD-Part framework: We adapt the TD-Part algorithm for efficient listwise reranking and demonstrate that substituting the conventional top-document anchor with our LLM-generated pivot yields improved relevance consistency and ranking stability (GPTD-Part).
Our experiments across in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that our truncation mechanism outperforms existing RLT-based approaches, while requiring no task-specific training or sensitive hyperparameter tuning. Furthermore, our method accelerates LLM-based list-wise reranking by up to 66% relative to strong baselines, while maintaining, and in many cases improving, retrieval effectiveness. We highlight not only the practical significance of using LLM-generated documents to improve RLT but also offer insight into their robustness, model dependence, and application in multi-stage retrieval pipelines. 2. Related Work
Ranked List Truncation
Traditionally, RLT has been formulated as a cascade ranking problem. These methods determine the optimal cut-off depth for a ranked list before reranking to balance effectiveness against efficiency. Lien et al. (Lien et al., 2019b) explored efficient top-kk processing by dynamically pruning candidates unlikely to affect the final evaluation metric.
For neural retrieval, RLT has evolved from heuristic score-based thresholding to learned models. Bahri et al. (Bahri et al., 2020) introduced Choppy, a transformer-based model that predicts query-specific cut-offs by analyzing the score distribution of the top retrieved documents. Similarly, Wu et al. (Wu et al., 2021) proposed AttnCut, which utilizes an attention mechanism to model the dependencies between document scores and the optimal truncation point. More recently, Meng et al. (Meng et al., 2024) investigated RLT specifically for reranking pipelines, demonstrating that fixed-depth reranking is often suboptimal and that dynamic truncation can significantly improve the efficiency of LLM-based rerankers.
LLM-based reranking
The recent developments in LLM capabilities and scale, especially large context windows, have led to the emergence of zero-shot listwise reranking with generative ranking models (Tang et al., 2024; Gangi Reddy et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2023). Listwise objectives better capture inter-document dependencies than pointwise or pairwise methods or traditional cross-encoders (Sun et al., 2023). Distilled LLMs, trained via permutation distillation on teacher rankings from zero-shot prompts, outperform much larger supervised cross-encoders like monoT5 (Sun et al., 2023; Pradeep et al., 2023a; Pradeep et al., 2023b)
Despite these gains, inference efficiency remains the main challenge while deploying listwise LLM rankers at scale. Recent approaches, such as FIRST and its variants, limit generation to a single identifier token, thereby improving retrieval efficiency (Gangi Reddy et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025). Li et al. (Li et al., 2025) use documents from the ranked list as references for efficient zeroshot LLM reranking. Parry et al. (Parry et al., 2024) proposed Top-Down Partitioning (TDPart), which replaces window traversal with a pivot-guided selection that can be reranked in parallel.
LLMs for Judgment and Generation
The capabilities of LLMs extend beyond ranking to relevance judgment and data generation. Thomas et al. (Thomas et al., 2024) and Faggioli et al. (Faggioli et al., 2023b) have benchmarked LLMs extensively as automated relevance judges. Upadhyay et al. propose UMBRELA for relevance judgments with zeroshot and fewshot prompting. Meng et al. (Meng et al., 2026) employ reranking frameworks for improved relevance assessment. These frameworks demonstrate the LLM’s intrinsic understanding of query-document relevance relationships (Upadhyay et al., 2024).
Consequently, LLMs have been successfully employed to generate synthetic training data to enable high performance dense retrieval with minimal examples (Shen et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2022). Approaches like HyDE (Gao et al., 2022) and Query2Doc (Wang et al., 2023) generate hypothetical documents to improve retrieval via zeroshot expansion. Ruiyang et al. (Ren et al., 2025) use finetuned LLMs to generate global scores for the documents in the ranked list, improving listwise reranking efficiency.
3. Reference-Document Generation and its Utility
In this section, we propose that an LLM can generate a moderately relevant answer (reference-document) to a query. This reference-document, based on its ranking position, can serve as a pivot in a ranked list to separate relevant from non-relevant documents.
Motivation
Prior work has shown that LLMs have been successfully utilized to generate relevance judgments for query document pairs (Rahmani et al., 2024; Upadhyay et al., 2024; Faggioli et al., 2023a). LLMs serve as effective judges for relevance in IR collections, often demonstrating high correlation with human annotators (Rahmani et al., 2024; Faggioli et al., 2023a). This implies that LLMs can understand graded relevance between query-document pairs when provided with a relevance scale similar to that of human assessors (Upadhyay et al., 2024). However, the focus of existing strategies remains solely on LLMs as judges, rather than generating documents with a specified relevance label.
Crucially, we experiment on the capability of LLMs to generate documents of a particular relevance label. We acknowledge that the capability of LLMs in document generation controlled with varying levels of relevance can be useful. In particular, we are focused on generating documents with moderate relevance, as these can serve as a controlling pivot between highly relevant and less relevant documents.

Since D∗D^{*} is generated independently of the first-stage retriever or downstream reranker, it can also serve as a universal reference point across retrieval frameworks. 3.1. Generative Pointwise Reranking
We propose Ranked List Partitioning using Single Inference (PSI-Rank), which uses the same retriever to score the pivot document, that retrieves the ranked list as shown in Figure 3. This requires only a single additional inference (scoring the pivot), resulting in negligible inference cost for positioning the pivot within the ranked list. Documents that are scored higher than the pivot are considered more promising candidates and, therefore, only those documents are reranked. We obtain the final reranked list by appending the remaining documents to the reranked list.
Formally, we consider θ​(Q)m={D1,…,Dm}\theta(Q)_{m}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}, where θ\theta is the initial ranker that retrieves top-mm documents in a multi-stage retrieval pipeline. Following which, a reranker ϕ\phi reranks the top-nn candidates from the initial ranked list θ​(Q)m\theta(Q)_{m}, producing a reordered sub-list ϕ​(Q,θ​(Q)m)n\phi(Q,\theta(Q)_{m})_{n}, where m≥nm\geq n. PSI-Rank employs two complementary truncation strategies, depending on the availability of calibration data (i.e., topics from a similar collection).
Dynamic Cutoff (Per-Query Hard Boundary)
Our method PSI-RankDyn controls the semantic anchoring of D∗D^{*} to establish precise, query-adaptive truncation without requiring any parametric supervision compulsory in existing dynamic RLT methods (Bahri et al., 2020). We score D∗D^{*} using θ⁡(Q,D∗)\theta(Q,D^{*}) such that the current ranked list now becomes {D1,D2,…,D∗,…,Dm−1,Dm}\{D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D^{*},\ldots,D_{m-1},D_{m}\}. We partition the ranked list into D+={Di∈θ​(Q)m∣θ⁡(Q,Di)>θ⁡(Q,D∗)}D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})>\theta(Q,D^{*})\} and D−={Di∈θ​(Q)m∣θ⁡(Q,Di)<θ⁡(Q,D∗)}D^{-}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})<\theta(Q,D^{*})\}. Then we rerank D+D^{+} using Z=ϕ​(Q,D+)nZ=\phi(Q,D^{+})_{n}, where n=|D+|n=|D^{+}| defines the dynamic reranking depth. The final ranked list is constructed by concatenating the reranked list with the remaining ranked list: ℛ=Z⊕D−\mathcal{R}=Z\oplus D^{-}.
Average Cutoff (Query Set Statistics)
We calculate cutoff from a calibration query set by calculating the mean pivot score θ¯=1|𝒞|​∑Q∈𝒞θ⁡(Q,D∗)\bar{\theta}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\theta(Q,D^{*}), where 𝒞\mathcal{C} denotes the calibration queries. In contrast to the dynamic mechanism, which can be influenced by query-specific bias, our method PSI-RankAvg relies on a global instance θ¯\bar{\theta} that remains fixed across all test queries Q∉𝒞Q\notin\mathcal{C}. This mitigates per-query outliers in pivot scoring, yielding consistent reranking depths across diverse queries. This provides statistical stability at the cost of query adaptivity. The ranked list is then partitioned using θ¯\bar{\theta} into D+={Di∈θ​(Q)m∣θ⁡(Q,Di)>θ¯}D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})>\bar{\theta}\} and D−={Di∈θ​(Q)m∣θ⁡(Q,Di)<θ¯}D^{-}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})<\bar{\theta}\}, followed by reranking Z=ϕ​(Q,D+)nZ=\phi(Q,D^{+})_{n}, where n=|D+|n=|D^{+}|. We yield the final list as ℛ=Z⊕D−\mathcal{R}=Z\oplus D^{-}.
This dual-mechanism design ensures PSI-Rank remains retriever-agnostic, requiring only a single-inference pivot generation per query, and eliminates the need for learned thresholds, embedding models, or query-specific hyperparameter tuning, unlike prior RLT approaches (Wu et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2024).

It takes long strides at the bottom and gradually takes smaller strides when ranking top positions, where the density of relevant documents is higher. RLT using Pivot
LLM-based reranking is typically performed using pointwise, pairwise, or listwise strategies, depending on the model and type of task (Sinhababu et al., 2025; Sinhababu et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024). Among these reranking strategies, the listwise approach is generally preferred due to the fewer inferences and contextual comparisons required (Sinhababu et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2023).
Background
Listwise ranking is implemented using either a single window or a sliding window approach. However, existing literature suggests that a single window approach can rerank approximately 20 documents from the first stage, limiting its applicability when more documents need to be reranked (Sinhababu et al., 2025; Parry et al., 2024). In contrast, the sliding window can rerank a larger number of documents by overlapping windows that slide across the entire document span. The use of overlaps ensures that potentially relevant documents are propagated across adjacent windows, allowing documents initially positioned at lower ranks to be promoted toward the top positions. Since this method lets the model focus on smaller windows iteratively, it enables reranking without exceeding model context limits. However, the drawback of this strategy is the need for ≈n/S\approx n/S serial inferences, where nn is the number of documents to be reranked and SS is the stride.
Reranking using Non-Overlapping Window
Our proposed solution is to consider multiple single windows that contain non-overlapping sets of documents, i.e., implementing a sliding window with a stride equal to the window size (S=w)(S=w). We name our reranking method as Shared Non-Overlapping Window (SNOW). Under this strategy, rankings are generated independently for each localized window. However, the absence of overlapping documents between windows restricts the possibility of repositioning relevant documents from one window to another, thereby limiting the overall effectiveness. Therefore, to make this approach functional, it is essential to avoid this restriction and allow for the propagation of documents across adjacent windows.
SNOW employs the same pivot document D∗D^{*} generated by PSI-Rank as a shared semantic reference point across all non-overlapping windows. Unlike traditional sliding window approaches that rely on overlapping documents for propagation, SNOW injects D∗D^{*} into each window independently. This design exploits the pivot’s query-specific relevance boundary, thereby enabling document propagation without sequential dependencies. Documents are promoted or demoted based on their ranking relative to D∗D^{*} within their respective windows as shown in Figure 3. Critically, this requires no additional pivot generation or retrieval scoring beyond the single inference already performed in PSI-Rank, making it computationally efficient while maintaining semantic consistency across all parallel reranking operations.
Formally, the ranked list from the first stage ranker is partitioned into K=⌊m/w⌋K=\lfloor m/w\rfloor non-overlapping windows. Considering j=[1,…,K]j=[1,\dots,K] windows, each batch is Wj={D(j−1)​w+1,…,Dj​w}{W}_{j}=\{D_{(j-1)w+1},\ldots,D_{jw}\}. For each window, D∗D^{*} is appended to form Wj′=Wj∪{D∗}W^{\prime}_{j}=W_{j}\cup\{D^{*}\}, followed by listwise reranking using ϕ⁡(Q,Wj′)→Dj+>{D∗}>Dj−\phi(Q,W^{\prime}_{j})\rightarrow D^{+}_{j}>\{D^{*}\}>D^{-}_{j}, where Dj+={Di∈Wj∣ϕ⁡(Q,Di)>ϕ⁡(Q,D∗)}D^{+}_{j}=\{D_{i}\in W_{j}\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})>\phi(Q,D^{*})\} and Dj−={Di∈Wj∣ϕ⁡(Q,Di)<ϕ⁡(Q,D∗)}D^{-}_{j}=\{D_{i}\in W_{j}\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})<\phi(Q,D^{*})\} denotes the subset of documents ranked higher than the pivot, leveraging pivot promotion to reveal query-adaptive local relevance boundaries. Global high-priority (D+=D1+⊕⋯⊕DK+D^{+}=D^{+}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D^{+}_{K}) and low-priority (D−=D1−⊕⋯⊕DK−D^{-}=D^{-}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D^{-}_{K}) lists are concatenated to preserve cross-window ordering via shared pivot semantics, before a final single-window pass ℛ=ϕ​(Q,(D+⊕D−))w\mathcal{R}=\phi(Q,(D^{+}\oplus D^{-}))_{w} captures global promotions within context limits.
Stride Adaptation in Sliding Window
Here, we present another application of the generated pivot in a sliding window setup. We observe that the standard sliding window technique uses a fixed stride value over the entire ranked list. However, in a reranking setup, the density of actual relevant documents at the end of the list is relatively lower compared to the initial portion. This is because reranking is performed on a list generated by a first-stage ranker. With this, we introduce Variable Stride-Sliding Window (VS-Sliding) reranking, a framework that enhances the existing sliding window mechanism by allowing for an adaptable stride as shown in Figure 3.

This density-adaptive mechanism accelerates through sparse tail regions (low |Dj+||D^{+}_{j}|) by skipping non-relevant documents, while decelerating in relevant-dense regions (high |Dj+||D^{+}_{j}|).
Generative Top-Down Partitioning
Top-Down Partitioning (TDPart) (Parry et al., 2024) improves reranking efficiency by recursively partitioning the search space against a pivot element selected from the top-ww window. However, TDPart relies on an internal pivot Dk∈θ​(Q)mD_{k}\in\theta(Q)_{m}, making it sensitive to the initial retrieval noise; a non-relevant pivot can compromise the integrity of all subsequent partitions. To address this dependency, we introduce Generated Pivot Top-Down Partitioning (GPTD-Part), which replaces the unstable internal pivot with our proposed semantically controlled anchor D∗D^{*}.
Unlike TDPart, GPTD-Part injects D∗D^{*} as an external reference independent of the initial ranking quality. We first determine the pivot’s effective rank kp=rankθ​(D∗∣θ​(Q)m)k_{p}=\text{rank}_{\theta}(D^{*}\mid\theta(Q)_{m}) using the first-stage retriever θ\theta. This position kpk_{p} initializes the cutoff boundary. Subsequently, the listwise reranker ϕ\phi processes the initial window Wtop={D1,…,Dw,D∗}W_{\text{top}}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{w},D^{*}\} and deeper partitions, anchoring all comparisons to D∗D^{*}. Formally, for any candidate within WW encountered during traversal, we derive the high-relevance partition D+D^{+} as D+={Di∈W∣ϕ⁡(Q,Di)>ϕ⁡(Q,D∗)}D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in W\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})>\phi(Q,D^{*})\}. This ensures that document promotion is governed by a query-specific, semantically grounded threshold rather than arbitrary retrieval artifacts. GPTD-Part is resilient against retrieval noise while retaining the parallel efficiency of top-down traversal. We have outlined the GPTD-Part method in Figure 3.
4. Evaluation
In this section, we provide empirical evidence and experimental details to demonstrate the utility of the generated pivot in RLT and LLM-based listwise rerankers. The effectiveness of our proposed approach depends on several critical assumptions, which we systematically investigate through three research questions.
4.1. Research Questions
The fundamental hypothesis of our approach is that LLM-generated pivots can serve as an adaptive threshold between relevant and non-relevant documents. Unlike fixed thresholds that remain constant across queries, our pivots should theoretically adjust based on query complexity, domain characteristics, and the distributional properties of retrieved candidates. This question examines whether our generated pivots genuinely exhibit threshold-like behavior, creating meaningful boundaries in the ranking space, or merely act as arbitrary reference points. Understanding this property is essential for our adaptive RLT, making our first research question:
RQ1: Does the generated pivot function as a dynamic threshold for RLT in pointwise and pairwise reranking setups?
Even if pivots function as dynamic thresholds, their utility depends on whether they translate into measurable ranking improvements. Listwise reranking with LLMs faces challenges due to significantly higher inference overhead compared to other reranking methods. We investigate whether pivot-guided comparisons help LLMs make more accurate relative judgments by providing stable reference points throughout the ranking process. This question directly addresses the practical value of our generated reference-document and therefore makes our second research question:
RQ2: Does the generated pivot effectively improve LLM listwise reranking performance and efficiency?
The quality of generated pivots likely depends on the generating LLM’s understanding of relevance, domain knowledge, and text generation capabilities. If ranking performance varies substantially based on which model generates the pivot, this suggests that pivot generation itself is a critical bottleneck that requires careful model selection. Conversely, if diverse LLMs produce functionally equivalent pivots, it indicates that RLT is robust to variations in pivot quality. This question addresses whether investing in stronger models specifically for pivot generation is necessary or whether lightweight models are sufficient, making our third research question:
RQ3: Does the ranking effectiveness significantly change with pivots generated by different LLMs?

4.3. Evaluation Measures
To assess the performance of the proposed approach against the baselines, three key metrics that capture both the effectiveness and efficiency of the ranking pipelines are employed.
•
Mean Average Precision (MAP): We report the mean average precision (MAP) at cut-off 100 (MAP@100) with the binary relevance threshold set to 2 for DL-19 and DL-2020 topics only.
•
Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG): We use nDCG as the standard measure because it accounts for a document’s relevance and its position in the ranked list. We report this metric for both in-domain and out-of-domain evaluation (Thakur et al., 2021).
•
Inferences Per-Query (IPQ): Since the initial ranked list is truncated before reranking in pointwise and pairwise setups, we report IPQ by dividing the total inferences by the number of queries in the test set 1. A method with a lower IPQ is considered better when overall performance is not significantly reduced.
•
Speed-Up (SU):
To quantify efficiency gains in listwise reranking setups, we report the proportional gains in run-time relative to the sliding window baseline. SU values greater than 1 indicate an improvement in efficiency. We include the time required to generate the pivot in the execution time for methods that use it.
4.4. Models
4.4.1. Phase-one
We retrieve the top-100 documents in a ‘retrieve then rerank’ pipeline. For testing our RLT method (PSI-Rank), we use only BM25 as the first stage, as it is the most commonly used first-stage ranker in RLT setups (Wu et al., 2021). For LLM-based list-wise reranking setups, we use both BM25 and SPLADE. We include SPLADE to study LLM rerankers’ dependence on first-stage retrieval. Although it is a very strong model, its performance is significantly improved when used in conjunction with LLM rerankers.

4.4.3. RLT Baselines
To establish the effectiveness of our approach, we compare against a set of RLT baseline methods:
•
Fixed-kk truncates all queries at a pre-determined depth (e.g., k=100k=100 for BM25, mono-T5 and k=50k=50 for duo-T5).
•
Greedy-kk (Lien et al., 2019a):
This is a data-driven variant that selects a single global cut-off depth kk that maximizes the efficiency-effectiveness trade-off. We use β=2\beta=2 as per the literature (Meng et al., 2024) to favor efficiency-focused retrieval scenarios.
•
BiCut (Lien et al., 2019a):
This method uses Bi-LSTM to process the ranked list. It makes a binary decision at each rank position. We set η=0.6\eta=0.6, for prioritizing higher efficiency (Meng et al., 2024).
•
Distribution-based Models:
We include three supervised models that predict the optimal cut-off position directly from a distribution of candidates. Choppy (Bahri et al., 2020) employs a Transformer encoder to capture global list context. AttnCut (Wu et al., 2021) utilizes a hybrid Bi-LSTM and Transformer architecture. MtCut (Wang et al., 2022) extends AttnCut via multi-task learning. Similar to Greedy-kk, these models are optimized using the EET metric with β=2\beta=2 for efficiency focused truncation policy (Meng et al., 2024).
4.4.4. Reranking Models
•
mono-T5:
A widely adopted point-wise neural reranker that independently scores each document with respect to the query using a sequence-to-sequence formulation (Nogueira et al., 2020).
•
duo-T5:
A pair-wise reranker that directly compares pairs of documents for a given query to determine relative relevance. By modeling document interactions explicitly, it often achieves higher ranking accuracy than point-wise approaches (Pradeep et al., 2021a).
•
Rank-Zephyr:
A list-wise LLM-based reranker designed to jointly assess and reorder a set of candidate documents in a single inference. It captures global ranking context and has demonstrated strong effectiveness in modern retrieval benchmarks (Pradeep et al., 2023b).
•
Rank-Vicuna:
A list-wise reranker built on the Vicuna instruction-tuned LLM, utilizing conversational reasoning to perform holistic ranking over document lists. It provides competitive performance while remaining adaptable across ranking setups (Pradeep et al., 2023a).
•
Rank-GPT:
A list-wise reranking framework that utilizes large instruction-following language models to generate relevance-aware rankings over candidate lists. In our experiments, we use GPT-4o-mini for its balance between efficiency and effectiveness (Sun et al., 2023; Sinhababu et al., 2025).
4.4.5. Reranking Methods
•
BM25 (✂) →\rightarrow mono-T5: This pipeline performs RLT on top-100 BM25 retrieved documents and then reranks with mono-T5.
•
BM25 (100) →\rightarrow mono-T5 (✂) →\rightarrow duo-T5: This three-stage pipeline performs RLT on top-100 mono-T5 reranked documents on top-100 BM25 retrieval and finally reranks with duo-T5.
•
BM25 (✂) →\rightarrow mono-T5 (✂) →\rightarrow duo-T5: This three-stage pipeline performs RLT twice, first on BM25 top-100 documents and then on mono-T5 truncated list before finally reranking with duo-T5.
•
Sliding: We use a sliding window of size ww and stride SS for listwise reranking over the candidate set, progressively refining the ranking to a target depth mm. For our experiments, we use standard hyperparameter values of w=20w=20 and S=10S=10 (Gangi Reddy et al., 2024; Sinhababu et al., 2025).
•
TD-Part: Employs a top-down partitioning strategy that selects a pivot within an initial top window and recursively partitions the ranking to depth kk for efficient listwise reranking (Parry et al., 2024). We set w=20w=20, consistent with the standard settings (Parry et al., 2024).

4.5.1. Generated pivot functions as a dynamic threshold for RLT in pointwise and pairwise reranking configurations
LLM-generated pivots can be used to dynamically adjust reranking depth without requiring model training or hyperparameter tuning, as shown in Figure 4. Results from Table 2 show that PSI-Rank in both pointwise (BM25→\rightarrowmono-T5) and pairwise (BM25→\rightarrowmono-T5→\rightarrowduo-T5) pipelines consistently achieves competitive or superior performance while requiring substantially reduced reranker inferences, answering our first research question (RQ1).
Under BM25→\rightarrowmono-T5, in-domain analysis from Table 2 demonstrates PSI-RankAvg as the overall best-performing model with an exceptional MAP and nDCG score (.337.337 and .685.685 in DL-19), with an impressive 55.855.8 IPQ. For DL-20, PSI-RankAvg outperforms the Fixed-kk mono-T5 (100) along with all other RLT methods, while requiring only ≈55%\approx 55\% of the total inferences. However, PSI-RankDyn with BM25 as the pivot scorer results in an early cutoff, truncating many relevant documents, achieving the lowest IPQ of 36.936.9, but significantly degrades performance. In this setup, out-of-domain analysis deviated only for Choppy, which showed an insignificant improvement over PSI-RankAvg on Touche and DBPedia.
In the full three-stage pipeline with mono-T5 as the pivot scorer model and duo-T5 as the reranker, PSI-RankDyn and PSI-RankAvg both demonstrate balanced effectiveness–efficiency trade-offs. The pairwise ranker duo-T5 did not show significant performance improvement across any test sets. However, our RLT methods promote performance by pruning irrelevant documents, improving pairwise reranking performance and stability across all test collections. Specifically, PSI-RankDyn effectively minimizes inference ≈(35−66%)\approx(35-66\%) in-domain and ≈(53−65%)\approx(53-65\%) in out-of-domain collections while still matching or surpassing majority baselines (except MAP in DL-20 and nDCG on COVID, where Choppy attains the best scores).
A major advantage of our PSI-Rank approach is its low inference cost and initial-ranker dependence, making it particularly useful for cascaded RLT across multiple stages. Here, the pivot is scored by both BM25 and mono-T5, resulting in an optimally truncated ranked list for duo-T5 reranking. We observe that baseline models in this setting did not improve over their single-level truncation, due to their ranker independent truncation process. In contrast, our approaches exhibit the highest efficiency, reaching the lowest IPQ of 77 with negligible performance drop (PSI-RankAvg,Dyn on DL-19). We observe a similar balance between performance and efficiency in out-of-domain settings, where with 7.67.6 IPQ, our method PSI-RankDyn,Dyn attains the highest nDCG of 66.966.9 in the pairwise pipeline.
Collectively, these observations support our hypothesis that generated reference-document functions as a semantic pivot rather than arbitrary anchors, enabling adaptive ranked list truncation in both pointwise and pairwise reranking setups. Empirically, we observe substantial variance in reranking depth across queries without degradation in performance metrics, which would be expected if the pivot behaved randomly.

Under BM25 as a first-stage retriever, SNOW performs best on COVID (nDCG of .690.690) and second best on SciFact (nDCG of .730.730). However, SNOW degrades performance on other collections compared to the VS-Sliding method. VS-Sliding accelerates through sparse tail regions while adaptively slowing in dense relevant regions, achieving ≈2×\approx 2\times (maximum S​U=1.98SU=1.98) stride efficiency over fixed-stride sliding windows by leveraging pivot-defined relevance density. VS-Sliding, while the second-most efficient (S​U≈1.8SU\approx 1.8), outperforms or maintains performance relative to all other methods (the highest MAP of .347.347 and .411.411 in DL-19 and DL-20, respectively).
With SPLADE first-stage, SNOW attains near-SOTA nDCG (.767.767 in DL-19, .810.810 in DL-20), highlighting pivot stability in the dense retrieval contexts. We observe that SNOW and VS-Sliding generally outperform baselines, except for DL-19, where Sliding shows only marginal improvement. VS-Sliding exhibits a balance between efficiency and performance gain, irrespective of the first stage. While GPTD-Part substantially improves over the TD-Part baseline by incorporating a generated pivot, showing the effectiveness of our pivot across different strategies. However, SNOW and VS-Sliding demonstrate better overall effectiveness than GPTD-Part, particularly in out-of-domain collections. Since, top-down approach generally relies heavily on the initial retriever, it shows degraded performance under BM25. Overall, our observation indicates that SNOW and VS-Sliding are efficient yet effective listwise reranking strategies, achieving state-of-the-art performance with the highest reported LLM-based listwise reranking efficiency.

Considering the smallest model in our experiment, our proposed methods, SNOW and VS-Sliding, consistently improve nDCG@10 across benchmarks. The larger model GPT-4o also shows similar improvements, indicating that our pivot-generation method is stable across both the small and the larger models. Overall, our results demonstrate that our pivot generation method is adaptable across different architectures, finetuning, and model sizes.
5. Conclusion
Our empirical analysis confirms that LLM-generated pivot documents function as effective semantic thresholds for adaptive ranked list truncation (RLT) in both pointwise and pairwise reranking pipelines, achieving competitive MAP and nDCG@10 scores with 35-66% inference reductions across in-domain and out-of-domain collections. Our proposed pivot-guided listwise reranking methods (SNOW, VS-Sliding, and GPTD-Part) consistently outperform baselines across test collections while showing stability across multiple LLMs (RankZephyr, RankVicuna, RankGPT). Our methods in listwise setups achieved the significant efficiency gain while conserving or improving performance. Pivot generation proves robust across different models, exhibiting very low variance. These findings establish LLM-generated pivots as stable anchors that enhance multi-stage reranking efficiency and stability without model training, solely by leveraging LLMs’ generative capabilities.
