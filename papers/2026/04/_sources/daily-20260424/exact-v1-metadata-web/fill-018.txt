L10: 
L11: Authors:cite6†Markus Schütz , cite7†Lukas Lipp , cite8†Elias Kristmann , cite9†Michael Wimmer L12:

L14: 
L15: cite10†View PDF cite11†HTML (experimental) L16: > Abstract:Previous work shows that small triangles can be rasterized efficiently with compute shaders. Building on this insight, we explore how far this can be pushed for massive triangle datasets without the need to construct acceleration structures in advance.
L17: > Method: A 3-stage rasterization pipeline first rasterizes small triangles directly in stage 1, using atomicMin to store the closest fragments. Larger triangles are forwarded to stages 2 and 3.
L18: > Results: CuRast can render models with hundreds of millions of triangles up to 2-5x (unique) or up to 12x (instanced) faster than Vulkan. Vulkan remains an order of magnitude faster for low-poly meshes.
L19: > Limitations: We currently focus on dense, opaque meshes that you would typically obtain from photogrammetry/3D reconstruction. Blending/Transparency is not yet supported, and scenes with thousands of low-poly meshes are not implemented efficiently.
L20: > Future Work: To make it suitable for games and a wider range of use cases, future work will need to (1) optimize handling of scenes with tens of thousands of nodes/meshes, (2) add support for hierarchical clustered LODs such as those produced by Meshoptimizer, (3) add support for transparency, likely in its own stage so as to keep opaque rasterization untouched and fast.

L9: # Title:Why are all LLMs Obsessed with Japanese Culture? On the Hidden Cultural and Regional Biases of LLMs
L10: 
L11: Authors:cite5†Joseba Fernandez de Landa , cite6†Carla Perez-Almendros , cite7†Jose Camacho-Collados L12:

L14: 
L15: cite8†View PDF cite9†HTML (experimental) L16: > Abstract:LLMs have been showing limitations when it comes to cultural coverage and competence, and in some cases show regional biases such as amplifying Western and Anglocentric viewpoints. While there have been works analysing the cultural capabilities of LLMs, there has not been specific work on highlighting LLM regional preferences when it comes to cultural-related questions. In this work, we propose a new dataset based on a comprehensive taxonomy of Culture-Related Open Questions (CROQ).
L17: The results show that, contrary to previous cultural bias work, LLMs show a clear tendency towards countries such as Japan. Moveover, our results show that when prompting in languages such as English or other high-resource ones, LLMs tend to provide more diverse outputs and show less inclinations towards answering questions highlighting countries for which the input language is an official language.
L18: Finally, we also investigate at which point of LLM training this cultural bias emerges, with our results suggesting that the first clear signs appear after supervised fine-tuning, and not during pre-training.

L9: # Title:A Robust Framework for Two-Sample Mendelian Randomization under Population Heterogeneity
L10: 
L11: Authors:cite6†Dingke Tang , cite7†Xuming He , cite8†Shu Yang L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled A Robust Framework for Two-Sample Mendelian Randomization under Population Heterogeneity, by Dingke Tang and 2 other authors
L14: 
L15: cite9†View PDF cite10†HTML (experimental) L16: > Abstract:Mendelian randomization is a powerful tool for causal inference in observational studies. The two-sample summary-data design, which estimates genetic associations with exposures and outcomes in separate cohorts, is the most widely used Mendelian randomization approach in large-scale genomic studies. However, this approach relies on a strong assumption of population homogeneity across the two samples.
L17: In practice, available samples often differ in ancestry, demographics, socioeconomic factors, covariate adjustment, and measurement protocols. Violations of the homogeneity assumption can bias causal effect estimates and undermine the credibility of Mendelian randomization findings. We introduce a robust, model-free Mendelian randomization framework that directly addresses population heterogeneity in the two-sample summary-data setting.
L18: Our method avoids parametric assumptions about population differences and is designed to address real-world challenges, including measurement error, weak instruments, and pleiotropy. We show that the proposed estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal under heterogeneous designs, and may offer efficiency gains over the classic estimator even in homogeneous settings.
L19: Through numerical simulations and a real data analysis for estimating the causal effect of body mass index on high-density lipoprotein cholesterol across ancestrally diverse populations, we demonstrate the practical utility, stability, and robustness of our approach.
