L38: # Quark and gluon production in the presence of the time-varying chiral magnetic current
L39: 
L40: Kirill Tuchin Affiliation:  Department of Physics and Astronomy, Iowa State University, Ames, Iowa, 50011, USA
L41: 
L42: August 11, 2026
L43: ###### Abstract
L44: The chiral magnetic effect (CME) consists in the induction of the electric current along the direction of the magnetic field. The corresponding transport coefficient $b_{0}$, known as the chiral magnetic conductivity, is proportional to the chiral imbalance in the medium. In many systems, such as quark-gluon plasma, $b_{0}$ is time-dependent.
L45: This paper studies the effect of the time variation of $b_{0}$ on the particle spectra and energy loss produced through the chiral Cherenkov and associated processes in Abelian and non-Abelian systems. The rates of all processes are derived in the ultra-relativistic approximation. The results are applied to the relativistic heavy-ion collisions utilizing a specific model describing the relaxation of the initial $P$-odd domain within the quark-gluon plasma. The corresponding energy loss is computed.
L46: The results suggest strong polarization of jets in quark-gluon plasma.
L47: ## I Introduction
L48: An ultra-relativistic particle losses most of its energy in a medium due to radiation. The amount of energy lost and the spectrum of the emitted radiation reflect the medium properties. This paper investigates novel mechanisms of energy loss in chiral medium, which contains chiral fermions. The peculiar properties of energy loss in a chiral medium are due to the chiral anomaly, which induces P and CP-odd transport phenomena cite26†Kharzeev 2014 .
L49: One such phenomenon is the chiral magnetic effect (CME), whereby electric current is induced in the direction of an external magnetic field: ${\bm{j}}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}$ cite27†Kharzeev 2006 ; cite28†Kharzeev et al. 2008 ; cite29†Kharzeev 2010 ; cite30†Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007 ; cite31†Fukushima et al. 2008 ; cite32†Barredo-Alamilla et al. 2023 ; cite33†von Dossow et al. 2026 .
L50: Another closely related phenomenon is the anomalous Hall effect, where a current is induced in the direction perpendicular to an external electric field: ${\bm{j}}={\bm{b}}\times{\bm{E}}$. The chiral magnetic conductivity $b_{0}$ is proportional to the chiral chemical potential $\mu_{5}$, which reflects the net chirality in the medium. The non-Abelian version of the chiral magnetic effect was investigated in cite34†Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013 ; cite35†Duari et al. 2026 .
L51: As a result of the chiral magnetic effect, new channels of energy loss are opened in empty space due to the momentum supplied by the chiral magnetic current. These processes include the chiral Cherenkov radiation and its cross channels, which have been studied in Ref. cite36†Tuchin 2018a ; cite37†Huang and Tuchin 2018 ; cite38†Tuchin 2018b ; cite39†Hansen and Tuchin 2021 ; cite40†Hansen and Tuchin 2022 ; cite41†Hansen and Tuchin 2023 ; cite42†Hansen et al. 2025 , and recently reviewed in cite43†Hansen and Tuchin 2024a .
L52: The cascade induced by these novel channels were studied in cite44†Hansen and Tuchin 2025 . Additionally, the non-Abelian version of the chiral Cherenkov radiation was explored in cite45†Hansen and Tuchin 2024b . While the anomalous Hall current also contributes to energy loss, as discussed in cite37†Huang and Tuchin 2018 ; cite38†Tuchin 2018b ; cite42†Hansen et al. 2025 ; cite46†Tuchin 2025 , the present paper assumes a spatially uniform medium, so we disregard the anomalous Hall currents in our analysis.
L53: The references mentioned above ignore the time-variation of the chiral magnetic conductivity $b_{0}$. However, in the quark-gluon plasma produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions the chirality is sourced by the parallel chromoelectric and chromomagnetic fields in the initial stage following a heavy-ion collision cite47†Lappi and Schlichting 2018 or by sphaleron transitions during the plasma’a evolution cite31†Fukushima et al. 2008 ; cite48†Arnold et al. 1997 ; cite49†Arnold et al. 1999 ; cite50†Bodeker 1998 .
L54: These processes introduce the time-dependence of $b_{0}$. The time-dependence of $b_{0}$ can also be driven by the chiral plasma instability cite51†Joyce and Shaposhnikov 1997 ; cite52†Boyarsky et al. 2012 ; cite53†Hirono et al. 2015 ; cite34†Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013 ; cite54†Tuchin 2015 ; cite55†Buividovich and Ulybyshev 2016 ; cite56†Manuel and Torres-Rincon 2015 ; cite57†Kirilin and Sadofyev 2017 .
L55: This motivated my recent study of photon production in the presence of time-dependent chiral imbalance cite46†Tuchin 2025 ; cite58†Tuchin 2026 . This paper builds upon that work and presents a comprehensive calculation of all processes contributing to energy loss in both Abelian and non-Abelian media.
L56: I derive general expressions for the rates of all parton splitting processes at any $b_{0}(t)$ and then apply these expressions to a specific model describing particle production in the presence of a decaying $P$-odd domain in heavy-ion collisions.
L57: The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. cite7†II , the photon and gluon wave functions in the presence of the chiral magnetic current are derived. Sec. cite11†III computes the rates of the various splitting channels. Fig. Fig. cite59†1 , Fig. cite60†2 , and Fig. cite61†3 exhibit the gluon and quark spectra produced in the processes $q\to q+g$, $g\to q+\bar{q}$ and $g\to g+g$ respectively. The energy loss induced by the chiral magnetic current is computed in Sec. cite21†IV and is shown in Fig. cite62†4 .
L58: The concluding remarks are presented in Sec. cite22†V .
L59: I employ the natural units $\hbar=c=1$, $\alpha=g^{2}/4\pi$, and the notation $p^{\mu}=(E,{\bm{p}})$, where $p\equiv|{\bm{p}}|$.
L60: ## II Photon and gluon wave functions in the presence of CME
L61: ### II.1 QED
L62: 
L63: Perturbative calculations in chiral medium in the presence of the chiral magnetic current are facilitated by an effective field theory obtained by adding the Chern-Simons term to the Maxwell Lagrangian cite63†Wilczek 1987 ; cite64†Carroll et al. 1990 ; cite65†Sikivie 1984 :
L64: 
L65:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{\mu\nu}^{2}+c_{1}\theta F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,$  |  | (1)
L66: where $F_{\mu\nu}$ is the electromagnetic field tensor, $\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}$ is its dual and $c_{1}$ is the chiral anomaly coefficient given by $c_{1}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}N_{c}\sum_{f}q_{f}^{2}$. The Chern-Simons term is coupled to an external pseudo-scalar field $\theta$ chosen to reproduce the chiral magnetic and anomalous Hall currents.
L67: Namely, $c_{1}\partial_{\mu}\theta=b_{\mu}=(b_{0},-{\bm{b}})$, where $b_{\mu}$ is a function of time but not spatial coordinates. The axial chemical potential $\mu_{5}$ is identified with $\dot{\theta}$, so that $b_{0}=c_{1}\mu_{5}$. The medium is assumed to be isotropic, implying that ${\bm{b}}=0$. Perturbation theory in the presence of finite ${\bm{b}}$ has been discussed in cite37†Huang and Tuchin 2018 ; cite38†Tuchin 2018b ; cite42†Hansen et al. 2025 ; cite46†Tuchin 2025 .
L68: The electromagnetic field, in the radiation gauge $A^{0}=0$, ${\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bm{A}}=0$, is described by the vector potential ${\bm{A}}$ that satisfies the equation:
L69: 
L70:  | $\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}(t){\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}=0\,.$  |  | (2)
L71: It is assumed that the chiral magnetic conductivity $b_{0}$ is a given function of time that is unaffected by the radiation. The wave function of a photon of energy $\omega$, momentum ${\bm{k}}$ and polarization $\lambda$ is a plane wave solution to (cite66†2 ):
L72: 
L73:  | $\displaystyle{\bm{A}}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}a(t){\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,.$  |  | (3)
L74: The photons must be circularly polarized with the polarization vector ${\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}$, $\lambda=\pm 1$, which obeys the equation $i\hat{{\bm{k}}}\times{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}=\lambda{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}$. The product of the photon polarization vectors satisfies the following identity:
L75:  | $\displaystyle{\bm{\epsilon}}^{i*}_{\lambda}{\bm{\epsilon}}^{j}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)+i\lambda\varepsilon^{ij\ell}\frac{k^{\ell}}{k}\right]\,,$  |  | (4)
L76: 
L77: where no summation over $\lambda$ is implied.
L78: 
L79: Substituting (cite67†3 ) into (cite68†1 ) one finds that the amplitude $a(t)$ is governed by the equation
L80: 
L81:  | $\displaystyle\ddot{a}(t)+\Omega^{2}(t)a(t)=0\,,$  |  | (5)
L82: 
L83: where
L84: 
L85:  | $\displaystyle\Omega^{2}(t)=k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}(t)k\,.$  |  | (6)
L86: Eq. (cite69†5 ) can be solved in a general form, assuming that $\Omega(t)$ is slowly varying function (see e.g. cite46†Tuchin 2025 ):
L87: 
L88:  | $\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\Omega}}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}\Omega(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\,.$  |  | (7)
L89: 
L90: The amplitude $a$ is normalized such that the wave function (cite67†3 ) represents one photon per unit volume. Expanding $\Omega$ in the ultra-relativistic limit $k\gg b_{0}$:
L91: 
L92:  | $\displaystyle\Omega(t)\approx k-\frac{\lambda b_{0}(t)}{2}$  |  | (8)
L93: leads to the final result for the amplitude:
L94: 
L95:  | $\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ikt+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\,.$  |  | (9)
L96: 
L97: The validity of (cite70†9 ) is restricted to photon momenta $k$ that satisfy the condition:
L98: 
L99:  | $\displaystyle\left|\dot{b}_{0}\right|\ll k^{2}\,,$  |  | (10)
L100: 
L101: where the dot indicates the time-derivative.
L102: 
L103: The scattering matrix elements are proportional to the Fourier image of the amplitude:
L104:  | $\displaystyle\tilde{a}(q)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{i(q+i\epsilon\sgn t)t}a(t)dt\,.$  |  | (11)
L105: 
L106: The positive infinitesimal parameter $\epsilon$ ensures convergence of the integral. Substituting (cite70†9 ) into (cite71†11 ) one obtains:
L107: 
L108:  | $\displaystyle\tilde{a}(q)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\cos\left[(k-q)t-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right]dt\,,$  |  | (12)
L109: where the functions
L110: 
L111:  | $\displaystyle b_{0}^{\prime}(t)$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}(t)+b_{0}(-t)}{2}\,,$  |  | (13)
L112:  | $\displaystyle b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}(t)-b_{0}(-t)}{2}\,,$  |  | (14)
L113: 
L114: are respectively the even and odd components of $b_{0}(t)$.
L115: 
L116: The wave functions of fermions are not modified and given by the standard expressions. Hence the wave function of an incident fermion with momentum ${\bm{p}}$ and spin $s$ reads:
L117:  | $\displaystyle\psi_{{\bm{p}}s}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2EV}}u_{s}({\bm{p}})e^{-iEt+i{\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,,$  |  | (15)
L118: 
L119: where $E=\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}$.
L120: 
L121: Another formulation of the effective theory is possible if one uses the expression for the divergence of the axial vector current $j^{\mu 5}=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi$ to obtain the effective Lagrangian
L122: 
L123:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\theta\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu 5}\,.$  |  | (16)
L124: Integrating the second term by parts yields a term proportional to $b_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi$. In this formulation, the chiral anomaly modifies only the fermion sector. The two formulations (cite68†1 ) and (cite72†16 ) appear to be equivalent in the chiral limit, as they transform into each other through the chiral rotation. However, this contention has not been rigorously verified.
L125: ### II.2 QCD
L126: 
L127: The color version of the chiral magnetic effect, which describes the induction of the color current in the direction of the color magnetic field, can be similarly described by adding the corresponding non-Abelian Chern-Simons term to the Yang-Mills Lagrangian. The effective Lagrangian is given by:
L128: 
L129:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right)-\frac{c_{2}}{2}\theta\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,$  |  | (17)
L130: The field tensor and the potentials are defined as usual:
L131: 
L132:  | $\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-ig[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\,,$  |  | (18)
L133: 
L134: $A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}$, $F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}t^{a}$, where $t^{a}$ are the SU(3) generators and the anomaly coefficient is $c_{2}=\frac{g^{2}N_{f}}{4\pi^{2}}$.
L135: The linearized equations of motion clearly have the same form as those discussed in the preceding section for the Abelian case. The corresponding plane wave solutions are given by
L136: 
L137:  | $\displaystyle{\bm{A}}^{b}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}(t,{\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}a(t)e^{b}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\,.$  |  | (19)
L138: Here, $e^{b}$ is a unit vector in the adjoint color representation, which we will omit from the equations henceforth, as per usual convention. The amplitude $a(t)$ is given by (cite70†9 ) in the ultra-relativistic approximation.
L139: 
L140: Unlike the Abelian Chern-Simons term, the non-Abelian one includes a cubic term that contributes to the triple-gluon interaction vertex. Substituting the identity
L141:  | $\displaystyle\Tr\left(F^{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)=2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\mu}\Tr\left(A_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}A_{\nu}A_{\rho}A_{\sigma}\right)$  |  | (20)
L142: 
L143: into the second term in (cite73†17 ) and integrating by parts yields
L144:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=c_{2}\partial_{\mu}\theta\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{2}A^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A^{a}_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}\frac{1}{4}if^{abc}A^{a}_{\nu}A^{b}_{\rho}A^{c}_{\sigma}\right)\,.$  |  | (21)
L145: 
L146: The first term in (cite74†20 ) contributes to the equation of motion, while the second one to the triple-gluon vertex. The corresponding Feynman rule for the anomalous contribution to the triple-gluon vertex in momentum space is:
L147:  | $\displaystyle\text{anomalous triple-gluon vertex}=gb_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}f^{abc}\,,$  |  | (22)
L148: 
L149: where we follow the conventions of cite75†Peskin and Schroeder 1995 . It was shown in cite45†Hansen and Tuchin 2024b that this contribution is suppressed in the ultra-relativistic limit by the inverse power of particles’ energy.
L150: 
L151: In summary, in the ultra-relativistic limit the effect of anomaly is to impart a time- and helicity-dependent phase
L152:  | $\displaystyle e^{\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}$  |  | (23)
L153: 
L154: to the photon and gluon fields, as compared to their expressions in empty space. Note also that all medium effects other than the anomalous chiral magnetic and Hall currents are omitted for simplicity.
L155: ### II.3 Plasma oscillations
L156: 
L157: Thus far, we ignored the collective medium effects on the wave functions of gauge bosons. In the ultra-relativistic limit discussed in this work, these effects can be described by the plasma frequency $\omega_{p}$. In the quark-gluon plasma at temperature $T$ it is given by $\omega_{p}^{2}=\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{18}(2N_{c}+N_{f})$ cite76†Rischke 2004 .
L158: 
L159: The plasma oscillations modify Eq. (cite66†2 ), which is obeyed by the electromagnetic vector potential, as follows:
L160:  | $\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}-\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}+b_{0}(t){\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}}-\omega_{p}^{2}{\bm{A}}=0\,.$  |  | (24)
L161: 
L162: As a result, the function $\Omega(t)$ reads:
L163: 
L164:  | $\displaystyle\Omega(t)=\sqrt{k^{2}-\lambda b_{0}(t)k+\omega_{p}^{2}}\approx k-\frac{\lambda b_{0}}{2}+\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{2k}\,,$  |  | (25)
L165: where we assumed that $k\gg\omega_{p}\gg|b_{0}|$ as favored by the phenomenological applications. A similar equation holds for the gluon field. In place of (cite70†9 ), we obtain for the amplitude:
L166: 
L167:  | $\displaystyle a(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ikt+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-\frac{i\omega_{p}^{2}t}{2k}}\,.$  |  | (26)
L168: 
L169: To keep equations less bulky, the plasma frequency will be omitted in all expressions unless otherwise indicated.
L170: ## III Parton spectra
L171: 
L172: Now, I move on to the main section of the paper, where the expressions for the photon and gluon fields derived in the previous section are utilized to calculate the differential probabilities of photon, gluon, and quark production.
L173: ### III.1 $q\to q+\gamma$ and $q\to q+g$
L174: 
L175: First, consider photon radiation by an ultra-relativistic quark. The initial and final quark momenta are $p_{z}\hat{{\bm{z}}}$ and ${\bm{p}}’$, respectively. The photon’s momentum is ${\bm{k}}=xp_{z}\hat{{\bm{z}}}+{\bm{k}}_{\bot}$, where $x$ is the longitudinal momentum fraction it carries and ${\bm{k}}_{\bot}$ its component in the $xy$-plane. The corresponding energies are
L176:  | $\displaystyle E$  | $\displaystyle=p_{z}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{2p_{z}^{2}}\right)\,,$  |  | (27)
L177:  | $\displaystyle E^{\prime}$  | $\displaystyle=(1-x)p_{z}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}+k_{\bot}^{2}}{2(1-x)^{2}p_{z}^{2}}\right)\,,$  |  | (28)
L178: 
L179: where the terms of the order $k_{\bot}^{2}/p_{z}^{2}$ and higher are neglected. The energy is not conserved unless $b_{0}$ is constant.
L180: 
L181: The element of the scattering matrix describing photon radiation reads:
L182:  | $\displaystyle S(q\to q\gamma)$  | $\displaystyle=e\int\bar{\psi}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{A}}^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}\psi_{{\bm{p}}s}d^{3}xdt=e\frac{\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}^{\prime}){\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}^{*}_{\lambda}u_{s}({\bm{p}})}{2V^{3/2}\sqrt{EE^{\prime}}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bm{p}}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\tilde{a}^{*}(E-E^{\prime})\,,$  |  | (29)
L183: where $\tilde{a}$ is given by (cite77†12 ) and $e$ is the quark’s electric charge. The photon radiation probability is then calculated as
L184: 
L185:  | $\displaystyle dw$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda,s,s^{\prime}}|S(q\to q\gamma)|^{2}\frac{Vd^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{Vd^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{e^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}E}\sum_{\lambda}|\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})|^{2}d^{2}k_{\bot}\frac{dx}{1-x}\Tr[(\not{p}+m)\not{\epsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}(\not{p}^{\prime}+m)\not{\epsilon}_{\lambda}]\,,$  |  | (30)
L186: which, upon using (cite78†27 ),(cite79†28 ) and (cite80†4 ), yields the differential probability cite46†Tuchin 2025 :
L187: 
L188:  | $\displaystyle\frac{dw(q\to q\gamma)}{d^{2}k_{\bot}dx}$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8(2\pi)^{3}E}\sum_{\lambda}|\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})|^{2}\frac{2e^{2}}{x(1-x)^{2}}\left\{k_{\bot}^{2}\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}+m^{2}x^{3}\right\}\,.$  |  | (31)
L189: 
L190: Using (cite77†12 ) and taking into account that
L191: 
L192:  | $\displaystyle k-E+E^{\prime}=\frac{k_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{2x(1-x)p_{z}}\,,$  |  | (32)
L193: 
L194: one obtains:
L195:  | $\displaystyle\tilde{a}(E-E^{\prime})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\cos\left[\frac{k_{\bot}^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{2x(1-x)p_{z}}t-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right]dt\,.$  |  | (33)
L196: 
L197: The gluon spectrum emitted by an ultra-relativistic quark differs from (cite81†31 ) only by a color factor and the replacement $e\to g$:
