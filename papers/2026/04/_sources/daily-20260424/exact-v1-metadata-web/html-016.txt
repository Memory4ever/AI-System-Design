L33: # Dean-Kawasaki fluctuating hydrodynamics for backscattering hard rods
L34: 
L35: Mrinal Jyoti Powdel Email: mrinal.jyoti@icts.res.in Affiliation: International Centre for Theoretical Sciences, Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Bengaluru 560089, India
L36: ###### Abstract
L37: We study a system of backscattering hard rods in one dimension. Contrary to the usual ballistic hard rods, these hard rods flip the sign of their velocities with a rate $\gamma$. This leads to the decay of the odd moments of velocity while preserving the even moments: the number of conserved quantities in the system becomes half. The introduction of the flipping rate, $\gamma$, is a kind of integrability-breaking perturbation.
L38: One expects a change in the transport properties in the system due to the integrability breaking. We show using a Dean-Kawasaki fluctuating hydrodynamic formulation that for $t\gg 1/\gamma$, the two-time density density correlation spreads in a diffusive manner, and for $t\ll 1/\gamma$, the correlation spreads ballistically.
L39: ## I Introduction
L40: Over the past decade, the framework of generalised hydrodynamics (GHD) has been notably successful in providing a reliable coarse-grained theory for out-of-equilibrium dynamics in integrable systems [cite21†8 , cite22†4 , cite23†11 , cite24†21 , cite25†2 , cite26†12 ]. Interest in such systems stemmed in large part from the seminal experimental realization of trapped 1D cold atoms that failed to equilibrate over long times due to integrable dynamics [cite27†17 , cite28†9 ].
L41: Such realizations of integrable systems is, however, not an easy feat, since integrability is a fine-tuned property and an integrable system is highly sensitive to inhomogeneities and external perturbations. This explains why most physical systems are either non-integrable or are only approximately integrable [cite29†3 ]. In the presence of integrability breaking perturbations, it is hence naturally expected that in the long-time limit, the framework of GHD reduces to the conventional hydrodynamics.
L42: The understanding of the precise mechanism that leads to this transition is a very interesting question, one that is also experimentally tractable, owing to the tunability of integrability breaking perturbations [cite30†27 ]. Various implementations [cite31†15 , cite32†29 , cite33†16 , cite34†5 , cite35†25 , cite36†26 , cite37†1 , cite38†6 ] of integrability breaking mechanisms have confirmed that a breakdown of integrability leads to an onset of chaoticity through a change in the transport properties of the system.
L43: A proper theoretical understanding of the underlying mechanism, hence, requires an in-depth analysis of various integrable models under different integrability breaking mechanisms.
L44: Among classical integrable systems, a very good staging ground to understand the consequence of integrability breaking is a one-dimensional system of hard rods [cite39†28 ]. A hard-rod system is simply a collection of finitely-sized segments (or rods) that interact with one another through a hard core potential.
L45: It has been shown [cite40†7 ] that when integrability is broken in this system by means of confinement in a harmonic trap, it displays a transient chaotic regime in its dynamics before ultimately failing to thermalize at long times. Other novel mechanisms of integrability breaking can also be explored in this system owing to its analytical tractability and easy numerical implementation.
L46: One such mechanism is to modify the dynamics of the system by adding a noise that flips the sign of individual hard rod velocities at random times. Previously studied in [cite41†19 ], this modification gives rise to backscattering events of hard rods in addition to the usual inter-particle scattering due to collisions. As an immediate effect, the number of conserved charges gets reduced by half: only the even moments of velocity survive while the odd moments decay.
L47: In this work, we revisit the case of ‘backscattering’ hard rods. We start by introducing the system and its conserved densities in Section cite7†II following the procedure outlined in [cite42†24 ]. From this, we move on to deriving a Dean-Kawasaki fluctuating hydrodynamic description [cite43†10 , cite44†14 ] of the system in Section cite8†III , using which we compute in Section cite9†IV , unequal space-time correlations of densities, providing an insight into the transport properties in the system.
L48: Our approach reproduces results from [cite41†19 ], while also demonstrating the wider applicability of a Dean-Kawasaki fluctuating hydrodynamic description in the analysis of an integrable system where individual quasiparticle dynamics is affected by a stochastic noise.
L49: ## II The system and its conserved densities
L50: Our system consists of $N$ backscattering hard rods (BHRs), each having length $a$ and unit mass moving in one dimension. Each rod interacts with other rods through two-body elastic collisions and can randomly flip the sign of its velocity with a rate, $\gamma$.
L51: The system at any time, $t$ is fully described by the set $\{\mathtt{x}_{i}(t),w_{i}(t),\sigma_{i}(t)\}$ where $\mathtt{x}_{i}(t)$ is the position, $w_{i}(t)$ is the speed and $\sigma_{i}(t)$ is the direction of propagation of the $i^{\text{th}}$ hard rod at time, $t$.
L52: An equivalent description of the system is given in terms of velocity tracers or quasiparticles. These are particles which follow the bare velocities of individual hard rods. The exchange of velocities in the event of a collision is depicted in terms of ‘jump’ events in quasiparticle trajectories: a collision from the right makes it jump by a distance $a$ to the right and so on. In this depiction, a quasiparticle may only change its direction of motion due to a flipping event.
L53: The flipping dynamics in this system can also be conveniently visualized by considering each quasiparticle carrying a stopwatch set to a waiting time, $\mathtt{t}$ drawn from an exponential distribution, $\gamma e^{-\gamma\mathtt{t}}$, where $\gamma$ is the flipping rate. In the absence of any external potential, each quasiparticle moves ballistically in between collisions until the timer in the stopwatch reaches zero.
L54: When that happens, a left moving quasiparticle ($\sigma=-1$) turns into a right moving one ($\sigma=+1$) and vice-versa, and the stopwatch of the particular quasiparticle is restarted from a new randomly drawn time, $\mathtt{t}^{\prime}$. In the event of a collision, while hard rods exchange their velocities as well as their clocks, a quasiparticle retains both while undergoing jumps by rod length $a$.
L55: GHD is written in terms of the mean phase space densities (PSDs), $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ of the quasiparticles [cite45†13 ] which are defined as the following
L56: 
L57:  | $$\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)=\sum_{i}\langle\delta_{\sigma,\sigma_{i}(t)}\penalty\ \delta(\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{x}_{i}(t))\penalty\ \delta(w-w_{i})\rangle\ ,$$  |  | (1)
L58: where the angular brackets denote averaging over the initial conditions. The computation of mean PSDs of a system of hard rods becomes very convenient via the following mapping [cite46†18 , cite47†23 ]
L59: 
L60:  | $$\begin{split}x_{i}(t)=x(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t))&=\mathtt{x}_{i}(t)-a\sum_{j\neq i}\Theta(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t)-\mathtt{x}_{j}(t)),\\
L61: w_{i}(t)&=w_{i}(t)\ ,\ \sigma_{i}(t)=\sigma_{i}(t)\end{split}$$  |  | (2)
L62: where the position, speed and direction of propagation of the $i^{th}$ BHR is mapped to the same for the $i^{th}$ point particle (PP) denoted by $\{x_{i}(t),w_{i},\sigma_{i}(t)\}$. Here, $\Theta(x)$ is the Heaviside Theta function. The mapping for $i^{th}$ HR to PP effectively removes inaccessible space between consecutive hard rods below $\mathtt{x}_{i}$. Due to the absence of jump events in quasiparticle trajectories, a system of hard PPs is effectively non-interacting.
L63: The PSDs in the HR picture are then conveniently expressed in terms of PSDs, $\bar{f}(x,v,t)$ in the PP picture by the following
L64:  | $$\begin{split}\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\binom{N-1}{i-1}\bigg(\frac{\bar{F}(x(\mathtt{x}_{i}))}{N}\bigg)^{i-1}\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x(\mathtt{x}_{i}),w,t)\\
L65: \bigg(1-\frac{\bar{F}(x(\mathtt{x}_{i}))}{N}\bigg)^{N-i}\end{split}$$  |  | (3)
L66: 
L67: where, $\bar{F}(x,t)=\int^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\penalty\ (\bar{f}_{+}(y,w,t)+\bar{f}_{-}(y,w,t))$ is the mean cumulative density upto position $x$ in the PP picture.
L68: Figure 1: Mass density, $q_{0}(\mathtt{x},t)$, momentum density, $q_{1}(\mathtt{x},t)$ and energy density, $q_{2}(\mathtt{x},t)/2$ of a system of backscattering hard rods of length $a=1$ at different times. The solid lines represents theory and the circles represent the results from molecular dynamics. Here, the initial positions and velocities are drawn from a gaussian distribution (cite48†6 ) with $\sigma_{x}=5$ and $T=1$.
L69: The numerical results have been obtained after averaging over $5\times 10^{6}$ realizations.
L70: The mapping defined in Eq. (cite49†2 ) maps the system of BHRs to a system of non-interacting run and tumble particles (RTPs). The mean PSD for RTPs, $\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,|v|,t)$ satisfies the following Fokker-Planck equation [cite50†20 ]:
L71: 
L72:  | $$\begin{split}\frac{\partial\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,|v|,t)}{\partial t}=&-\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigg(|v|\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,|v|,t)\bigg)\\[2.0pt]
L73: +&\gamma(\bar{f}_{-\sigma}(x,|v|,t)-\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,|v|,t)).\end{split}$$  |  | (4)
L74: Eq. (cite51†4 ) can be solved for various boundary conditions. For PSDs conditioned on fixed initial conditions, i.e., $f_{\sigma}(x,t|x_{0},|v|)$, we outline the method to solve Eq. (cite51†4 ) in Appendix cite13†A for hard wall boundary conditions and in Appendix cite17†B for an infinite line. By integrating $\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,t|x_{0},|v|)$ over initial conditions, one can compute the full joint PSD, $\bar{f}_{\sigma}(x,|v|,t)$ of RTPs for a particular $\sigma$. When this is feeded into Eq.
L75: (cite52†3 ), it gives us the mean PSD, $\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},v,t)$ in the HR picture.
L76: The conserved densities of a system are computed from the PSDs in the following way:
L77: 
L78:  | $$q_{\alpha}(\mathtt{x},t)=\int\text{d}w\penalty\ w^{\alpha}\penalty\ (\mathfrak{f}_{+}(\mathtt{x},w,t)+(-1)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{f}_{-}(\mathtt{x},w,t))\ \ .$$  |  | (5)
L79: Here, $\alpha=0,1,2$ corresponds to mass, momentum and energy density respectively. In Fig. cite53†1 , we show that the expression for the conserved densities defined in Eq. (cite54†5 ) and obtained using Eq. (cite52†3 ) has an excellent agreement with results from molecular dynamics simulations. For showing this agreement, the following initial distribution of positions and speeds is considered in the PP picture:
L80:  | $$\mu(x_{0},|v|)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{x}^{2}}}\exp\bigg[-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2\sigma_{x}^{2}}\bigg]\times\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi T}}\exp\bigg[-\frac{v^{2}}{2T}\bigg]\ ,$$  |  | (6)
L81: 
L82: where, $T$ is the temperature.
L83: ## III Hydrodynamics of fluctuating Phase space densities
L84: Our analysis of the system of BHRs through a computation of mean PSDs, though quite helpful in appreciating the global effects of backscattering-induced integrability breaking, is far from complete. It is necessary to understand how a system responds to fluctuations about a mean state, and this is possible through an analysis of unequal space-time correlations of the conserved densities. Using the mapping to hard point particles defined in Eq.
L85: (cite49†2 ), one can also compute unequal space-time correlations for this system, and procedure to compute such correlations in ballistic hard rods is outlined in [cite55†22 ]. However, for systems which are affected by a stochastic noise (brownian noise or tumbling noise, for example), another elegant way to describe the dynamics is provided by the Dean-Kawasaki formulation [cite43†10 , cite44†14 ] of fluctuating hydrodynamics.
L86: We define the fluctuating PSD, $\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ for a system of BHRs as the following:
L87: 
L88:  | $$\begin{split}\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)&=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{f}^{(i)}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\ \ \\
L89: \text{where}\ \ \ \mathfrak{f}^{(i)}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)&=\delta_{\sigma_{i}(t),\sigma}\penalty\ \delta(\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{x}_{i}(t))\penalty\ \delta(w-w_{i})\ \ .\end{split}$$  |  | (7)
L90: It is easy to see that the mean PSD, $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ defined in Eq. (cite56†1 ) is obtained from the fluctuating PSD upon averaging over initial conditions. Now, the trajectory of the $i^{\text{th}}$ backscattering hard rod (BHR) at time, $t$ is given by the inverse mapping of Eq. (cite49†2 ), i.e.,
L91: 
L92:  | $$\mathtt{x}_{i}(t)=x(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t))+a\penalty\ \sum_{j\neq i}\Theta(x(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t))-x(\mathtt{x}_{j}(t)))\ \ ,$$  |  | (8)
L93: where, $\Theta(x)$ is the heaviside theta function such that $\Theta(x)=1\ \text{for}\ x\geq 0$ and 0 otherwise. It is very convenient to track the position of the BHR from the position of the corresponding PP, which as already stated, is an RTP.
L94: 
L95: Any function $\mathtt{q}$ along the trajectory of the $i^{th}$ BHR can be defined from the fluctuating PSD, $\mathfrak{f}^{(i)}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ corresponding to the $i^{\text{th}}$ BHR i.e.
L96:  | $$\begin{split}\mathtt{q}_{\sigma_{i}(t)}(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t),w_{i}(t))=\sum_{\sigma}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{x}\penalty\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\penalty\ \mathtt{q}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w)\penalty\ \mathfrak{f}^{(i)}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\ \ ,\end{split}$$  |  | (9)
L97: 
L98: where $\mathfrak{f}^{(i)}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ is as defined in Eq. (cite57†7 ). Taking derivative on both sides with respect to time, $t$ and summing over $i$ on both sides gives us
L99:  | $$\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{Q}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}&=\sum_{\sigma}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{x}\penalty\ \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\penalty\ \mathtt{q}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w)\penalty\ \partial_{t}\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\end{split}$$  |  | (10)
L100: 
L101: where we have used Eq. (cite57†7 ) and the following definition of $\mathtt{Q}(t)$,
L102: 
L103:  | $$\mathtt{Q}(t)=\sum_{i}\mathtt{q}_{\sigma_{i}(t)}(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t),w_{i}(t))\ .$$  |  | (11)
L104: As can be seen, $\mathtt{Q}(t)$ is a functional of the trajectories of all the BHRs. Now, any change in $\mathtt{Q}$ in a time interval $\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t$ is given by (to order $\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{x}_{i}^{2}$),
L105:  | $$\begin{split}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{Q}(t)=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\mathtt{q}_{\sigma_{i}(t)}(\mathtt{x}_{i}(t),w_{i}(t))}{\partial\mathtt{x}_{i}(t)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{i}(t)\\[3.0pt]
L106: +\sum_{i}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{N}^{(i)}_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}+\mathcal{O}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{x}_{i}^{2})\ .\end{split}$$  |  | (12)
L107: Here, the first term corresponds to a change in $\mathtt{Q}$ brought about by the displacement of each of the BHRs while the second term, $\sum_{i}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{N}^{(i)}_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}$ is a noise term due to a flipping event of the BHRs in a time interval $\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t$. From this, the process of deriving the equations of hydrodynamics is straightforward and is outlined in Appendix cite18†C . The most important step is the determination of the properties of the noise term.
L108: In the case of BHRs, the flipping event is Poisson process and that dictates the properties of the noise. Likewise, for Brownian hard rods, the contribution to $\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{Q}$ from terms of $\mathcal{O}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathtt{x}_{i}^{2})$ is also $\mathcal{O}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)$ and hence should be taken into account.
L109: When all the dust has settled, we obtain the following equation of motion for the fluctuating PSDs of backscattering hard rods
L110: 
L111:  | $$\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)+\partial_{\mathtt{x}}{v_{\sigma}}_{\rm eff}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\\[3.0pt]
L112: =&\gamma(\mathfrak{f}_{-\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)-\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t))+\sigma\sqrt{\gamma\penalty\ \bar{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathtt{x},w,t)}\penalty\ \xi(\mathtt{x},w,t)\end{split}$$  |  | (13)
L113: where
L114: 
L115:  | $${v_{\sigma}}_{\rm eff}(\mathtt{x},w,t)=\frac{w\sigma-a\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{w}\penalty\ \mathrm{w}\sigma^{\prime}\penalty\ \bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathtt{x},\mathrm{w},t)}{1-a\bar{\rho}(\mathtt{x},t)}$$  |  | (14)
L116: 
L117: is the effective velocity of the quasiparticle at position $\mathtt{x}$ with bare velocity $w\sigma$ at time, $t$, and the noise $\xi(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ has a zero mean with the following correlation:
L118:  | $$\langle\xi(\mathtt{x},w,t)\penalty\ \xi(\mathtt{x}^{\prime},w^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle_{c}=\delta(\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{x}^{\prime})\delta(w-w^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})\ .$$  |  | (15)
L119: 
L120: Note that in our derivation of Eq. (cite58†13 ), we have not done a scale by scale coarse graining either in space or time.
L121: Figure 2: Unequal space-time correlation of mass densities for a hard rod system consisting of $N=400$ backscattering hard rods each of length $a=0.01$ for a) $\gamma=0.0005$ and b) $\gamma=2$. Here, we have set $\mathtt{x^{\prime}}=0,t^{\prime}=0$. The black dashed lines represent theory (Eq. (cite59†23 )) and the colored circles represent results from molecular dynamics for different values of $\tau=t-t^{\prime}$, where we have also subtracted off fluctuations due to finite size effects.
L122: Initially, all the hard rods are distributed uniformly in the region $[-10,10]$ and the velocities were drawn from a gaussian distribution with temperature or variance, $T=1$. The boundary conditions imposed are periodic. On the left, we see that for $\tau<<\gamma^{-1}$, the correlation has a ballistic space-time scaling. While, on the right, for $\tau>>\gamma^{-1}$, the correlation has a diffusive space-time scaling. The numerical results have been obtained after averaging over $10^{6}$ realizations.
L123: While Eq. (cite58†13 ) superficially resembles the Fokker-Planck equation in Eq. (cite51†4 ), it is distinguished from the latter because it incorporates the effect of hard core interactions in the effective velocity, ${v_{\sigma}}_{\rm eff}$ of the quasiparticle and with the presence of a stochastic noise term on the right hand side signifying the fluctuating nature of the PSDs. If one takes an average over initial conditions on both sides of Eq.
L124: (cite58†13 ), the noise term vanishes and the resultant equation gives the evolution equation for the mean PSD. The presence of the noise term is significant as it facilitates computation of unequal space-time correlations as we show below.
L125: ## IV Unequal space-time correlations
L126: 
L127: We are interested to compute unequal space-time correlations of mass densities, i.e., $\langle\rho(\mathtt{x},t)\rho(\mathtt{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle_{c}$. The first step is the linearization of Eq. (cite58†13 ). To do that, we consider our system in a state that is slightly perturbed from a homogeneous stationary state. The state for both $\sigma=\pm 1$ is given by the following fluctuating PSD, $\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}$:
L128:  | $$\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)=\frac{\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{0}(w)}{2}+\delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\ \ ,$$  |  | (16)
L129: where $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}^{0}(w)$ is the full homogeneous mean PSD and $\delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ denotes a fluctuation in space-time. We define $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}^{0}(w)$ as being a constant function of $\mathtt{x}$ and an even function of velocity such that the odd moments of the velocity, $v=w\sigma$ are 0. We would compute $\langle\rho(\mathtt{x},t)\rho(\mathtt{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle_{c}$ for the following choice of the homogeneous stationary state:
L130:  | $$\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{0}(w)=\varrho_{0}\penalty\ \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi\penalty\ T}}\exp\bigg[-\frac{w^{2}}{2T}\bigg]\ $$  |  | (17)
L131: 
L132: When we plug the form of $\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}$ given in Eq. (cite60†16 ) into Eq. (cite58†13 ), we get, to linear order in fluctuations,
L133:  | $$\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)+\frac{w\sigma}{1-a\varrho_{0}}\partial_{\mathtt{x}}\delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\\
L134: +&\frac{\mathfrak{f}^{0}(w)}{2}\bigg[\frac{aw\sigma}{(1-a\varrho_{0})^{2}}\partial_{\mathtt{x}}\delta\varrho(\mathtt{x},t)+\frac{\partial_{t}\delta\varrho(\mathtt{x},t)}{1-a\varrho_{0}}\bigg]\\
L135: =&\gamma(\delta\mathfrak{f}_{-\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)-\delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t))+\sigma\sqrt{\gamma\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_{0}}\penalty\ \xi(\mathtt{x},w,t)\ .\end{split}$$  |  | (18)
L136: where, $\delta\varrho(\mathtt{x},t)=\sum_{\sigma}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\penalty\ \delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)$ is the resultant fluctuation in the mass density and obeys the following equation of motion:
L137:  | $$\begin{split}\partial_{t}\delta\varrho(\mathtt{x},t)+\partial_{\mathtt{x}}\delta(\varrho u(\mathtt{x},t))=0\ \ ,\ \ \text{where}\\
L138: \delta(\varrho u(\mathtt{x},t))=\sum_{\sigma}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\penalty\ w\sigma\penalty\ \delta\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)\ \ .\end{split}$$  |  | (19)
L139: 
L140: Eq. (cite61†18 ) can be simplified by rewriting it in terms of the normalized fluctuating PSD, $\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{\sigma}$ defined as
L141:  | $$\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)=\frac{\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{x},w,t)}{1-a\varrho(\mathtt{x},t)}\ .$$  |  | (20)
L142: 
L143: In some literature, $\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{\sigma}$ is also referred to as occupation number density. Whenever required, we would sometimes refer to $\mathfrak{f}_{\sigma}$ as the ‘unnormalised’ fluctuating PSD for the remainder of the paper. In terms of $\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{\sigma}$, Eq. (cite61†18 ) can be rewritten as
