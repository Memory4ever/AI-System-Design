L22: ###### Abstract
L23: Recently, the observation of superconductivity (SC) with $T_{c}$ $\approx$ 3.6 K in the pressurized 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} has attracted considerable interest. Here, we systematically investigate the electronic properties and superconducting mechanism of 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} using density functional theory plus dynamical mean-field theory (DFT+DMFT) and random phase approximation (RPA).
L24: Our DFT+DMFT calculations reveal that the single-layer (SL) subsystem exhibits nearly insulating behavior, with the $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital showing Mott physics, while the trilayer (TL) subsystem remains metallic. This indicates that SC primarily resides in the TL subsystem, whose Ni-$e_{g}$ orbitals are found to be hole-doped relative to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}. Based on DFT+DMFT-derived low-energy Hamiltonian, RPA-based analysis yields an $s^{\pm}$-wave pairing symmetry within the TL subsystem.
L25: Importantly, we identify two key factors that contribute to the significant suppression of $T_{c}$ in 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} compared to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}. First, the hole doping in the TL subsystem, as established by DMFT, leads to a decreased pairing strength, as confirmed by RPA calculations—a trend resembling that in bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}. Second, the SL subsystem acts as a bridge connecting adjacent superconducting TL subsystems, thereby forming an S-N-S Josephson junction.
L26: The resulting interlayer Josephson coupling governs the phase coherence between TL subsystems and further suppresses the global $T_{c}$. Combinedly, our findings suggest that the high-$T_{c}$ phase in the RP La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} family should be attributed to the 2222 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} rather than the 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}.
L27: Introduction. Since the discovery of superconductivity (SC) above the liquid-nitrogen temperature in pressurized La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} [cite6†37 ], nickelates have emerged as a hot research focus in condensed matter physics, triggering an intense surge of both theoretical and experimental investigations [cite7†25 , cite8†22 , cite9†12 , cite10†43 , cite11†42 , cite12†26 , cite13†40 , cite14†39 , cite15†36 , cite16†44 , cite17†6 , cite18†14 , cite19†10 , cite20†46 , cite21†23 , cite22†32 , cite23†41 , cite24†13 , cite25†5 , cite26†34 , cite27†2 , cite28†3 ].
L28: Subsequently, the observation of SC in pressurized La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}, ambient-pressure thin-film La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}, and doped La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} has further catalyzed research in this field, establishing nickelates as an important platform for realizing high-$T_{c}$ SC [cite29†54 , cite30†49 , cite31†15 , cite32†53 , cite33†20 , cite34†31 ]. In the Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) phases La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} and La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}, each Ni atom is octahedrally coordinated by O atoms.
L29: Under ambient pressure, these octahedra exhibit tilting or rotation. Upon increasing external pressure, the system undergoes a structural phase transition that straightens the interlayer Ni–O–Ni bond angles to 180^{∘}, accompanied by the metallization of the $\sigma$-bonding states.
L30: Furthermore, whether SC can also be realized in hybrid RP nickelates such as the 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11}, 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}, and 2323 La_{7}Ni_{5}O_{17} phases has become an important question of interest [cite35†51 , cite36†4 , cite37†30 , cite38†19 , cite39†28 ]. To date, both 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11}, 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} and 2323 La_{7}Ni_{5}O_{17} have been successfully synthesized experimentally [cite36†4 , cite37†30 , cite38†19 , cite40†27 ].
L31: In particular, the 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} exhibits SC up to 64 K under high pressure [cite41†35 ], and SC up to 50 K has also been achieved in ambient-pressure thin-film 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} samples [cite40†27 ]. Moreover, angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) experiments reveal that, near the Fermi level, only bands originating from the bilayer subsystem are observed in thin-film 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} [cite40†27 ].
L32: However, there is ongoing theoretical debate regarding the pairing mechanism in 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11}. A random phase approximation (RPA)-based study attributed the SC to the single-layer (SL) subsystem, but the resulting pairing strength is too weak to account for the experimentally observed high $T_{c}$ [cite42†52 ].
L33: Other works suggest that SC in 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} arises primarily from the bilayer subsystem [cite43†47 , cite39†28 , cite44†18 ], with the SL subsystem forming a Josephson coupling structure with the bilayer subsystem, which explains the dome-shaped dependence of $T_{c}$ observed in experiments [cite43†47 ].
L34: Recently, the SC of 1313 phase of La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} has attracted significant attention. A previous experimental study has reported the observation of filamentary SC up to 80 K in 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} via high-pressure transport measurements [cite37†30 ]. However, it was noted that the possibility of high-$T_{c}$ arising from other phases, such as 2222 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}, could not be ruled out.
L35: More recently, new experimental work has demonstrated that 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} exhibits SC with a maximum $T_{c}$ of 3.6 K above 19 GPa, accompanied by a pressure induced structural phase transition at 13 GPa [cite45†11 ]. Recently, thin-film experiments have revealed that while the 2222 phase of La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} can manifest SC, the 1313 phase does not [cite40†27 , cite46†7 ], casting further doubt on the intrinsic SC of 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}.
L36: Theoretically, studies on the nature of SC in 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} have yielded conflicting conclusions. An earlier RPA-based study proposed that the SL subsystem hosts a leading $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ pairing state, with the nonsuperconducting trilayer (TL) subsystem in-between [cite47†50 ].
L37: In contrast, density functional theory plus dynamical mean-field theory (DFT+DMFT) investigations have revealed that the SL subsystem exhibits Mott insulating behavior, similar to that of the 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11}, rendering it an unlikely host for superconductivity [cite48†29 , cite49†17 ]. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of the electronic and superconducting properties of 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} becomes an urgent and crucial issue in the field.
L38: In this work, we systematically study the electronic properties using DFT+DMFT calculations, followed by an RPA based analysis to investigate the superconducting mechanism in hybrid 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}. Our DMFT results reveal a layer-dependent correlation effect, where the SL subsystem manifests as a nearly insulating bad metal, while the TL subsystem remains metallic and features hole-doped Ni-$e_{g}$ orbitals relative to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}.
L39: Using DFT+DMFT derived effective model, our RPA analysis identifies an $s^{\pm}$-wave pairing symmetry in the TL subsystem of 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}. The pairing strength within the TL subsystem shows a similar doping trend to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}, which decreases upon hole doping, implying a lower $T_{c}$ compared to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}.
L40: Additionally, the SL and TL subsystems form an S-N-S Josephson junction, where the SL subsystem acts as a weak link, suppressing phase coherence and leading to a significantly reduced global $T_{c}$. Our findings clarify the genuine high-$T_{c}$ phase in RP La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} series and highlight the important difference between hybrid-phase and pure-phase nickelate superconductors.

L42: Electronic correlation. Hybrid 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} features an alternating stacking structure composed of SL La_{2}NiO_{4} and TL La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10} subsystems, with a translation of $(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})$ between SL and TL subsystems, as shown in Fig.cite51†1 . Each Ni is surrounded by six oxygen atoms, forming an octahedron. At ambient pressure, 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} crystallizes in the orthorhombic $Cmmm$ space group [cite45†11 ].
L43: By applying external pressure, a structural phase transition occurs at approximately 13 GPa, leading to a tetragonal phase with $P4/mmm$ space group. Distinct from the 2222 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} and TL La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}, hybrid 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} exhibits no octahedral tilt or rotation of the NiO_{6} octahedron at ambient pressure.
L44: Throughout the pressure-induced structural phase transition, the out-of-plane Ni-O-Ni bond angle within the TL subsystem remains 180^{∘}, analogous to the hybrid 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} [cite41†35 ].

The inner and outer layers are indicated in Fig.cite51†1 L46: Figure cite53†2 (a) presents the fully self-consistent DFT+DMFT calculated correlated electronic structure of the 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} at 20 GPa. The momentum-resolved spectral function $A(\mathbf{k},\omega)$ reveals pronounced band renormalization effects, with the Ni-$e_{g}$ bandwidth reduced from 4 eV in DFT calculation to approximately 2.5 eV in DMFT calculation, indicating strong electronic correlations in the 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7}.
L47: Around the M point, the bands originating from the TL subsystem become flattened due to renormalization effects, and the bonding band slightly crosses the Fermi level, leading to the $\gamma$ pocket on the FS.
L48: To further elucidate the characteristics of the bands near the FS, Figure cite53†2 (b) and (c) show the orbital-resolved imaginary part of Matsubara self-energy function $\mathrm{Im}\sum(i\omega_{n})$ and orbital-resolved spectral functions $A(\mathbf{k},\omega)$. The self-energy function $\mathrm{Im}\sum(i\omega_{n})$ of the SL subsystem exhibits distinct behaviors between $d_{z^{2}}$ and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbitals at low frequencies.
L49: Specifically, the SL-$d_{z^{2}}$ orbital manifests Mott physics, whereas the SL-$d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital displays a large intercept at zero-frequency, indicating the intrinsic strong correlations of the SL subsystem. This is consistent with the well-known Mott-insulating nature of La_{2}NiO_{4} [cite54†33 , cite55†45 , cite56†8 ]. The local spectral functions $A(\mathbf{k},\omega)$ of the SL subsystem exhibit a pronounced difference from those of TL subsystem near the Fermi level.
L50: Note that the $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbital exhibits a tiny spectral function intensity in the vicinity of the Fermi energy. However, the spectrum is dispersed throughout the entire momentum space, without manifesting well-defined quasiparticle excitations, as illustrated in Fig.cite53†2 (a). Therefore, the SL subsystem behaves as a nearly insulating bad metal, characterized by a gap near the Fermi level and the large imaginary part of self-energy, consistent with previous studies [cite49†17 , cite48†29 ].
L51: Regarding the TL subsystem, the self-energy functions reflect that it belongs to a correlated metal, while the inner and outer layers exhibit quantitative differences at low frequencies. In particular, the outer layer Ni-$e_{g}$ orbitals display stronger correlations with a slightly larger intercept at zero frequency, similar to the bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10} [cite57†38 ].
L52: The spectral function of the $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital in the TL subsystem exhibits a peak at the Fermi level, corresponding to the two flat bands, bonding and nonbonding bands, at the M point in Fig.cite53†2 (a). Collectively, these findings suggest that SC in the 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} should originate primarily from the TL subsystem, while the SL subsystem is unlikely to host the superconducting carriers.

L61: Note that this reduction reflects the actual decrease after fully accounting for $d$-$p$ hybridization, and thus should be distinguished from the electron filling used in the tight-binding (TB) model for SC calculations discussed below. Previous studies have shown that hole doping suppresses SC of bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10} [cite59†48 , cite60†24 ], which may account for the significantly lower $T_{c}$ observed in 1313 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} compared to bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10}.
L62: Renormalized trilayer two-orbital model. Given that SC is likely to primarily originate from the TL subsystem, we now focus on the electronic properties of TL subsystem. To further investigate the electronic properties and renormalization effects of the TL subsystem, we construct a TL two-orbital model based on the DFT+DMFT results as described in Supplementary Materials. The renormalized TL two-orbital model encompasses five Fermi pockets in the TL subsystem, comprising four electron pockets ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\beta^{\prime}$, and $\varepsilon$) and one hole pocket ($\gamma$).
L67: Among these, the $\varepsilon$ and $\gamma$ pockets are primarily contributed by the $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital, whereas the $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\beta^{\prime}$ pockets result from the mix of $d_{z^{2}}$ and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$ orbitals.

Our theoretical results are in agreement with ARPES experiments, including the presence of the $\varepsilon$ pocket around the $\Gamma$ point.
L69: In addition, the $\gamma$ band located below the Fermi level crosses the Fermi energy under high pressure, undergoing a Lifshitz transition, which is a common phenomenon observed in RP nickelate superconductors such as 2222 La_{3}Ni_{2}O_{7} and 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} [cite6†37 , cite43†47 ]. L70: The interlayer $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital hopping $t_{[0,0]}^{z,IO}$ is reduced from its non-interacting value of -0.650 to -0.223, which results in the flatter bonding and non-bonding bands being closer to the Fermi level. Additionally, our renormalized model reveals an intertrilayer $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital hopping of 0.112 meV, which is two orders of magnitude smaller than that of bulk La_{4}Ni_{3}O_{10} and comparable to the interbilayer $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital hopping in hybrid 1212 La_{5}Ni_{3}O_{11} [cite43†47 ].

L81: RPA study of the SC. Since the low-energy degrees of freedom of the system are dominated by the TL subsystem, whose electronic correlations are relatively moderate, we employ the multi-orbital RPA approach (see Supplementary Material) to study its SC. Based on the renormalized TB model in Eq. cite67†1 , we incorporate the following Hubbard interactions:
L82:  | $\displaystyle H_{int}$  | $\displaystyle=U^{eff}\sum_{i\tilde{\mu}}n_{i\tilde{\mu}\uparrow}n_{i\tilde{\mu}\downarrow}+V^{eff}\sum_{i,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}n_{i1\sigma}n_{i2\sigma^{\prime}}$  |
L83:  |  | $\displaystyle+J_{H}^{eff}\sum_{i\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Big[c^{\dagger}_{i1\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{i2\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i1\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i2\sigma}+(c^{\dagger}_{i1\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{i1\downarrow}c_{i2\downarrow}c_{i2\uparrow}+h.c.)\Big].$  |  | (2)
L84: Here, $U^{eff}$, $V^{eff}$, and $J_{H}^{eff}$ denote the intra-orbital, inter-orbital Hubbard repulsion, and Hund’s coupling (including pair hopping) respectively, which satisfy the relation $U^{eff}=V^{eff}+2J_{H}^{eff}$. $U^{eff}$ can be regarded as the strength of certain residual interactions renormalized by a factor $Z^{2}$, where $Z$ is the quasiparticle weight.
L85: Since the quasiparticle weight $Z$ of the outer-layer $d_{z^{2}}$ orbital is quite small (about 0.23), $U^{eff}$ is significantly reduced compared to the unrenormalized TB value. $\tilde{\mu}\in(1,2)$ labels the two orbitals ($d_{z^{2}}$ and $d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}$) associated with each Ni atom. $i$ denotes the lattice sites belonging to the TL subsystem. In the following calculations, we set $J_{H}^{eff}=U^{eff}/4$. The renormalized spin (s) or charge (c) susceptibilities are obtained in the RPA level as

L87: Here $\bm{\chi}^{(0)}$ is bare susceptibility for the non-interacting case, $U^{(s,c)}$ is critical interaction strength. $\bm{\chi}^{(s,c)}(\bm{q},i\omega),\bm{\chi}^{(0)}(\bm{q},i\omega)$, and $U^{(s,c)}$ are 36$\times$36 matrices in this six-orbital model, with $\bm{\chi}^{(0)}(\bm{q},i\omega)$ and $U^{(s,c)}$ provided in the Supplementary Material. Generally, repulsive Hubbard interactions suppress the charge susceptibility, but enhance the spin susceptibility.
