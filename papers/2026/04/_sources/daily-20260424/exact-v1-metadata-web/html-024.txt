L34: # High-energy photon hologram of a photon gas
L35: 
L36: P.O. Kazinski^{1)}  and A.A. Sokolov^{1),2)}
L37: ^{1)} Physics Faculty, Tomsk State University, Tomsk 634050, Russia
L38: ^{2)} Tomsk Polytechnic University, Tomsk 634050, Russia E-mail: kpo@phys.tsu.ru E-mail: asokolov@tpu.ru
L39: ###### Abstract
L40: The photon hologram of a one-particle density matrix of a photon gas is derived including the case where the energy of a probe photon is above the electron-positron pair creation threshold. The explicit expressions for the holograms of a photon gas with one-particle density matrix in the form of a single Gaussian and of coherent and incoherent lattices of Gaussians are obtained. The conditions for resonant cones of coherent scattering by coherent and incoherent lattices are found.
L41: These conditions turn out to be different. The explicit expression for the dielectric susceptibility tensor of a photon gas and of a single photon prepared in arbitrary quantum states are derived on the probe photon mass-shell. It is established that a photon gas and a single photon behave in coherent photon scattering as a medium with linear and circular birefringences that is transparent below the electron-positron creation threshold and is absorbing otherwise.
L42: It is shown that, for the probe photon energies of order $1$ GeV and higher, the energies of target photons of order $1$ eV and higher, and the photon gas density such that the classical intensity parameter is of order unity, the hologram of the photon gas can be measured with existing experimental facilities.
L43: ## 1 Introduction
L44: Recently, it has been shown that even elementary particles can be endowed by some properties that usually are ascribed to macroscopic objects [cite22†1 , cite23†2 , cite24†3 , cite25†4 , cite26†5 , cite27†6 , cite28†7 ]. One of such properties is a dielectric susceptibility that appears in describing coherent scattering of photons by elementary particles interacting with electromagnetic field.
L45: Due to light-by-light scattering [cite29†8 , cite30†9 , cite31†10 , cite32†11 , cite33†12 , cite34†13 ], the dielectric susceptibility tensor can be introduced even for a single photon prepared in a certain quantum state. This tensor was constructed in [cite24†3 ] for the energies of a probe photon lower than the electron-positron pair creation threshold.
L46: Such a susceptibility tensor determines the outcomes of coherent scattering [cite23†2 , cite35†15 , cite36†14 ] of probe photons on target ones that form the hologram of the quantum state of a target photon on the detector [cite26†5 , cite28†7 ]. This hologram is a result of the interference between the free passed part of the probe photon wave function and its scattered part. In this sense, it is a realization of a simple Gabor scheme for construction of a hologram [cite37†16 , cite38†17 ].
L47: In the present paper, we generalize the results of [cite24†3 ] and construct the dielectric susceptibility tensor and the hologram of a photon gas and of a single photon prepared in an arbitrary quantum state in the framework of perturbative quantum electrodynamics. The probe photon energies are assumed to be arbitrary but much lower than the muon pair creation threshold. The quantum state of target photons is supposed to be such that the usual perturbation theory is applicable.
L48: The holograms and the susceptibility tensor of target electromagnetic fields arise naturally in strong field quantum electrodynamics in studying the so-called vacuum diffraction and birefringence in the presence of strong laser fields [cite39†18 , cite40†19 , cite41†20 , cite42†21 , cite43†22 , cite44†23 , cite45†24 , cite46†25 , cite47†26 , cite48†27 , cite49†28 , cite50†29 , cite51†30 , cite52†31 , cite53†32 , cite54†33 , cite55†34 , cite56†35 , cite57†36 ].
L49: These studies are commonly conducted either in the formalism of the Heisenberg-Euler effective action [cite58†37 , cite59†38 , cite40†19 , cite46†25 , cite60†39 , cite49†28 , cite51†30 , cite50†29 , cite39†18 , cite57†36 , cite56†35 , cite55†34 , cite34†13 , cite54†33 , cite53†32 , cite52†31 , cite48†27 , cite44†23 , cite45†24 , cite43†22 , cite42†21 , cite41†20 , cite61†40 , cite62†41 ] or by using the photon polarization operator found in the presence of a traveling plane electromagnetic wave [cite63†42 , cite64†43 , cite47†26 ].
L50: In both cases, the state of a target electromagnetic field is assumed to be coherent and so can be described by classical fields. Moreover, in the latter case this classical field has a very specific profile. We get rid of these limitations at the cost of treating light-by-light scattering perturbatively and show that the hologram and the dielectric susceptibility tensor of a photon gas are determined by its one-particle density matrix.
L51: Light-by-light scattering is a tiny effect and has not been verified experimentally yet with all photons participating in the process on-shell [cite47†26 , cite49†28 , cite51†30 , cite53†32 , cite54†33 , cite55†34 , cite56†35 , cite57†36 , cite59†38 , cite65†44 , cite66†45 , cite67†46 , cite68†47 , cite69†48 ]. Therefore, one of our aims is to find such parameters of the target photon gas and of the probe photon that the hologram of the target can be observed with existing experimental facilities.
L52: It is known [cite70†49 ] that the probability of this process grows with the center-of-mass energy $\sqrt{s}$ up to the electron-positron pair creation threshold. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that the holograms of a sufficiently dense photon gas constructed with the aid of high energy probe photons can be recorded by the existing detectors [cite47†26 , cite65†44 , cite66†45 , cite67†46 , cite68†47 , cite69†48 ].
L53: We show that this is indeed the case and derive a simple analytic expression for the hologram of a photon gas with one-particle density matrix given by a narrow in momentum space Gaussian with the dispersion matrix of a general form.
L54: Furthermore, we investigate the influence of coherence of the target quantum state on its hologram. To this end, we consider coherent scattering of a probe photon on the two types of lattices made of photons. The one-particle density matrix of the first lattice is pure and is described by a coherent superposition of identical Gaussians with centers located at the lattice sites in momentum space at the instant of time $t=0$ in the interaction picture.
L55: The one-particle density matrix of the second lattice is mixed and is obtained in the same way as in the case of a coherent lattice but with Gaussians multiplied by stochastic phases. It turns out that the holograms of these two lattices differ qualitatively even in the case when the corresponding photon number densities coincides in the coordinate space at the instant of time $t=0$. This demonstrates a strong dependence of the hologram on the properties of a target quantum state.
L56: The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. cite7†2 , we derive the general expression for the inclusive probability to record a probe photon in scattering by a gas of photons in the leading nontrivial order of perturbation theory. This leading nontrivial order in the coupling constant describes coherent scattering and is nothing but a hologram of a target photon gas. In Sec. cite8†3 , we particularize the general formulas of Sec.
L57: cite7†2 and obtain the expression for the density matrix of a scattered probe photon that determines the hologram. Section cite9†4 is devoted to evolution of the Stokes parameters of a probe photon in coherent scattering in the small momentum transfer limit. In this limit, we obtain the equations specifying a change of the probe photon density matrix in coherent scattering. In Sec. cite10†5 , we consider several examples of holograms of a photon gas prepared in different quantum states.
L58: Here we deduce the explicit expressions for the holograms and investigate an impact of the quantum state coherence of a photon gas on its hologram. We also provide the estimates for the relative magnitude of the effect of coherent light-by-light scattering. The tensor of dielectric susceptibility of a photon gas is derived in Sec. cite15†6 . We also give simple physical explanation of a large magnitude of coherent scattering in an almost head-on configuration. In Conclusion, we summarize the results.
L59: Some tedious derivations and bulky formulas are removed to Appendices cite17†A , cite18†B , and cite19†C . We use the system of units such that $c=\hbar=1$ and $e^{2}=4\pi\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the fine structure constant. The summation over repeated indices is implied unless otherwise stated.
L60: ## 2 Hologram
L61: Consider scattering of a probe photon on a target consisting of photons in the framework of perturbative quantum electrodynamics. The system is prepared at the instant of time $t_{in}\rightarrow-\infty$ and a single probe photon is measured in the state picked out by the projector $D_{\bar{\gamma}\gamma}$ at the final instant of time $t_{out}\rightarrow+\infty$. Hereinafter, we will always imply the interaction picture (see the details of the general formalism in the papers [cite23†2 , cite25†4 ]).
L62: The initial state of the system is taken in the form
L63:  | $$\hat{R}=\hat{R}_{ph}\otimes|0\rangle_{e^{-}}\langle 0|_{e^{-}}\otimes|0\rangle_{e^{+}}\langle 0|_{e^{+}},$$  |  | (1)
L64: 
L65: where $\hat{R}_{ph}$ is the photon density matrix, $\ket{0}_{e^{-}}$ and $\ket{0}_{e^{+}}$ are the vacuum states of electrons and positrons. The photon density matrix is given by
L66: 
L67:  | $$\hat{R}_{ph}=\rho^{h}_{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\gamma}\hat{R}^{s}\hat{c}_{\bar{\gamma}},$$  |  | (2)
L68: where $\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\gamma}$ and $\hat{c}_{\bar{\gamma}}$ are the creation and annihilation operators of photons, $\rho^{h}_{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}$ is the density matrix of a hard probe photon, $\hat{R}^{s}$ is the density matrix of soft target photons, and

L74:

L76:  | $$\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\sum$}}}\slimits@_{\gamma}\rho^{h}_{\gamma\gamma}=1,\qquad\operatorname{Sp}\hat{R}^{s}=1.$$  |  | (5)
L77: 
L78: Also we need the $M$-particle density matrix (see for details Appendix C of the paper [cite25†4 ])
L79: 
L80:  | $$\rho^{(M)}_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{M}|\bar{\gamma}_{M}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{1}}=\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{R}_{ph}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\bar{\gamma}_{M}}\cdots\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\bar{\gamma}_{1}}\hat{c}_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\hat{c}_{\gamma_{M}}).$$  |  | (6)
L81: Taking into account relations (cite72†4 ), the $M$-particle density matrix is written as
L82:  | $$\rho^{(M)}_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{M}|\bar{\gamma}_{M}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{1}}=\rho^{s(M)}_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{M}|\bar{\gamma}_{M}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{1}}+\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\sum$}}}\slimits@_{k,l=1}^{M}\rho^{h}_{\gamma_{l}\bar{\gamma}_{k}}\rho^{s(M-1)}_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\hat{\gamma}_{l}\cdots\gamma_{M}|\bar{\gamma}_{M}\cdots\hat{\bar{\gamma}}_{k}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{1}},$$  |  | (7)
L83: 
L84: where the hat over the index means that this index is absent.
L85: The measurement at the final instant of time is specified by the projector
L86: 
L87:  | $$\hat{\Pi}_{D}=(1-\hat{\tilde{\Pi}}_{D})\otimes\hat{1}_{e^{-}}\otimes\hat{1}_{e^{+}},\qquad\hat{\tilde{\Pi}}_{D}=:\exp(-\hat{c}^{\dagger}D\hat{c}):,$$  |  | (8)
L88: where the colons denote the normal ordering. L89: 
L90:  | $$P_{D}=\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{\Pi}_{D}\hat{S}\hat{R}\hat{S}^{\dagger}).$$  |  | (9)
L91: 
L92: We suppose that with good accuracy
L93:  | $$\rho^{s}_{\gamma\gamma_{M}\cdots\gamma_{1}|\bar{\gamma}_{1}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{M}\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}}D_{\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}\bar{\gamma}}=0=D_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\rho^{s}_{\gamma^{\prime}\gamma_{M}\cdots\gamma_{1}|\bar{\gamma}_{1}\cdots\bar{\gamma}_{M}\bar{\gamma}},$$  |  | (10)
L94: 
L95: i.e., the target photons are prepared in the state that does not overlap with the state of a recorded photon.
L96: Let us write out the contributions to the $S$-matrix up to the fourth power in the creation-annihilation operators taking into account the conservation laws. In that case, keeping in mind the form of the initial state (cite73†1 ), we have
L97: 
L98:  | $$\hat{S}=\hat{1}+\hat{F}+\hat{C}+\ldots,$$  |  | (11)

The operator $\hat{C}$ is of fourth order in the coupling constant and describes light-by-light scattering.

L104:  | $$\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}=\hat{1},$$  |  | (13)
L105:

L109: This relation can also be derived with the aid of the Cutkosky rules applied to the diagrams of light-by-light scattering.
L110: 
L111: On substituting expression (cite74†11 ) for the $S$-matrix to the inclusive probability (cite75†9 ), we arrive at
L112: 
L113:  | $$P_{D}=\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{\Pi}_{D}\hat{R})+\big[\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{\Pi}_{D}\hat{C}\hat{R})+c.c.\big]+\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{\Pi}_{D}\hat{F}\hat{R}\hat{F}^{\dagger}),$$  |  | (15)
L114: in the leading nontrivial order in the coupling constat. The arising traces of operators are reduced to
L115:  | $$\begin{split}\operatorname{Sp}(\hat{\Pi}_{D}\hat{R}_{ph})=&\,1-\rho^{(0)}_{\tilde{D}},\\
