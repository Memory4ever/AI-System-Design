L43: ###### Abstract.
L44: Let $-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}+V$ be the Schrödinger operator on $\mathbb{H}$ where $V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})$ for some $p>0$. If $(X_{n})$ is a uniformly discrete sequence of compact hyperbolic surfaces with a uniform spectral gap that Benjamini-Schramm converges to $\mathbb{H}$, we prove quantum mixing for the eigenfunctions of $-\Delta_{X_{n}}+V_{n}$ in any sufficiently large spectral window $I$, where $V_{n}$ is the potential on $X_{n}$ induced by $V$.
L45: These apply to large degree lifts of a potential on a base surface such as congruence covers of arithmetic surfaces, with high probability to random hyperbolic surfaces in the Weil-Petersson model of large genus, and to Hartree one-particle operators arising in thermodynamic limit of many-body Bose gas on hyperbolic surfaces. The proof uses the Duhamel formula for the hyperbolic wave equation together with exponential mixing of the geodesic flow on $T^{1}X_{n}$.
L46: ## 1. Introduction
L47: ### 1.1. Background
L48: Understanding eigenfunctions of quantum Hamiltonians is central in both physics and mathematics, as they determine the time evolution and statistical behaviour of quantum systems. A guiding principle in quantum chaos is that the spectral properties of a quantum system reflect the dynamics of the associated classical flow. In particular, when the classical dynamics is ergodic, one expects delocalisation of eigenfunctions.
L49: This is formalised by the quantum ergodicity theorem of Shnirelman, Zelditch, and Colin de Verdière [cite28†92 , cite29†100 , cite30†34 ], which asserts that for manifolds with ergodic geodesic flow, eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator equidistribute in the high energy limit outside a density zero subsequence.
L50: Further developments include entropy bounds of Anantharaman [cite31†3 , cite32†10 ], results towards quantum unique ergodicity [cite33†70 , cite34†93 ], and connections to the fractal uncertainty principle and spectral gaps [cite35†38 , cite36†37 ]. We refer to [cite37†4 , cite38†36 ] for comprehensive surveys.
L51: Over the past decade, there has been substantial progress in discrete analogues of quantum chaos. In the setting of large graphs, the high energy limit is replaced by a large size limit, and quantum ergodicity has been established for sequences of regular graphs converging locally to trees [cite39†5 , cite40†26 ], building on earlier delocalisation results [cite41†27 ].
L52: These results have been extended to non-regular graphs, Cayley graphs and to Schrödinger operators (including Anderson-type models) under assumptions on the limiting spectral measure [cite42†12 , cite43†11 , cite44†13 , cite45†73 , cite46†23 ]. Parallel developments in random matrix theory and random graphs, such as local semicircle laws and eigenvector delocalisation [cite47†41 , cite48†40 , cite49†20 , cite50†19 ].
L53: In the continuous setting, the natural analogue of large graphs is given by sequences of hyperbolic surfaces whose genus tends to infinity and which converge to the hyperbolic plane in the Benjamini-Schramm sense. Quantum unique ergodicity for sequences of arithmetic surfaces in this setting was considered by Nelson [cite51†81 ], and Nelson, Pitale, and Saha [cite52†82 ] (QUE in the level aspect) and various generalisations.
L54: Quantum ergodicity in this regime was established by Le Masson and the fourth author [cite53†67 ] showing that eigenfunctions in a fixed spectral window equidistribute as the genus grows under natural geometric assumptions such as uniform injectivity radius and spectral gap.
L55: Related developments include results on Eisenstein series and cusp forms [cite54†68 ], Lie groups [cite55†1 ], higher rank [cite56†84 , cite57†29 , cite58†28 ], and probabilistic models of random hyperbolic surfaces and their spectral properties [cite59†79 , cite60†80 , cite61†48 , cite62†62 ]. Stronger dynamical notions such as quantum mixing have recently been established both for large graphs [cite46†23 ] and for hyperbolic surfaces [cite63†56 ].
L56: Moreover, there has been substantial activity recently to transfer spectral theory from tree-like graphs to surfaces, in particular works on finding optimal spectral gap in various random models of surfaces [cite64†6 , cite65†8 , cite66†7 , cite67†9 , cite68†53 , cite69†54 , cite70†72 ] based on new techniques such as strong convergence [cite71†55 , cite72†22 ], Friedman-Ramanujan functions [cite64†6 , cite65†8 , cite66†7 , cite67†9 ], the polynomial method [cite70†72 , cite73†30 ] of which the latter has also just recently led to a major consequence on polynomial sup-norm bounds for eigenfunctions [cite62†62 ].
L57: Despite these advances, most results in the continuous setting concern the Laplacian, corresponding to free motion. Introducing a potential leads to Schrödinger operators $H=-\Delta+V$, which break the symmetries underlying Selberg theory and spherical analysis. In contrast, in the discrete setting, methods based on Green’s functions and equidistribution properties of non-backtracking operators have led to quantum ergodicity and even quantum unique ergodicity for large graphs [cite42†12 , cite45†73 ].
L58: On trees giving universal cover of the sequences of graphs, the progress is enabled by a detailed understanding of spectral theory, notably Klein’s work on absolutely continuous spectrum on trees [cite74†63 ]. However, these techniques rely crucially on the tree structure leading to recursive properties of Green’s functions that do not extend to continuous spaces like hyperbolic surfaces.
L59: Understanding Schrödinger operators $H=-\Delta+V$ can also give insight into many-body quantum chaos, where fundamental problems such as the eigenstate thermalisation hypothesis [cite75†35 ] are mathematically poorly understood, with only limited rigorous results available [cite76†60 ]. For example, in Lemm and Siebert [cite77†69 ] studied the thermodynamic limit of Bose gases on sequences of hyperbolic surfaces, which provide a natural model of coupled chaotic dynamical systems.
L60: Assuming a uniform spectral gap for the sequence of surfaces, they proved Bose-Einstein condensation onto the constant mode in the dilute regime. At the mean-field level, this leads to an effective one-particle dynamics governed by a Schrödinger operator $H=-\Delta+V$ with a Hartree potential $V$, see Examples cite78†1.3 below.
L61: Another related problem is that of Anderson localisation and delocalisation. The study of Anderson localisation was initiated by the pioneering work of Anderson that established exponential localisation of eigenfunctions in lattice models with strong disorder [cite79†14 ]. The phenomenon of Anderson localisation is well understood in the literature; see [cite80†24 , cite81†45 , cite82†47 , cite83†50 ]. However, a full proof of Anderson delocalisation seems out of reach.
L62: Progress has been made on this problem by Klein [cite74†63 ] who proved spectral and dynamical delocalisation for the Bethe lattice, by Aizenman and Warzel [cite84†2 ] on trees, by Erdős, Salmhofer and Yau who established quantum diffusion in a scaling limit for the Anderson model [cite85†43 ]. The delocalisation for eigenfunctions of Schrödinger operators in different models has been studied in the Euclidean case; see e.g., [cite86†95 , cite87†90 , cite88†52 , cite89†65 , cite90†64 ].
L63: Formulations of these questions in hyperbolic spaces seem to be more approachable due to chaotic free motion, making re-collisions less likely.
L64: Our work contributes to this program by establishing quantum ergodicity and quantum mixing for Schrödinger operators on sequences of hyperbolic surfaces in the large genus limit. Quantum mixing we study here originates in the work of Zelditch [cite91†101 , cite92†103 ], where the semiclassical quantum ergodicity theorem [cite28†92 , cite29†100 , cite93†33 ] is extended to off-diagonal averages.
L65: More precisely, for a manifold with weakly mixing geodesic flow, the sum of squared transition amplitudes $|\langle\psi_{j_{l}},A\psi_{k}\rangle|^{2}$ of a pseudo-differential operator $A$ for $\sqrt{-\Delta}$ from energy $\sqrt{\lambda_{j_{l}}}$ to energies $\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}$ within a window of width $\delta$ centered at spectral separation $\tau\in\mathbb{R}$ from $\sqrt{\lambda_{j_{l}}}$ can be made arbitrarily small, for sufficiently small $\delta$, along almost all subsequences $(j_{l})$ corresponding to growing energies.
L66: The relationship between classical ergodicity and quantum weak mixing of the geodesic flow, and their semiclassical counterparts, is captured by a theorem synthesising the works [cite28†92 , cite93†33 , cite29†100 , cite91†101 , cite94†46 , cite95†66 , cite92†103 , cite96†102 , cite97†104 , cite98†94 ]: the geodesic flow on a compact Riemannian manifold (possibly with boundary) is quantum ergodic if and only if it is ergodic, and it is quantum mixing if and only if it is weakly mixing.
L67: Large-scale quantum mixing builds on the ideas of large-scale quantum ergodicity. An analogue of this for the Wigner ensemble was proved by Cipolloni, Erdős, and Yau [cite99†32 ] as the eigenstate thermalisation for Wigner matrices.
L68: The first author proved quantum mixing for hyperbolic surfaces complementing the quantum ergodicity results of Le Masson and the fourth author [cite53†67 , cite54†68 ] and recently Bordenave, Letrouit, and Sabri on sequences of Schreier graphs [cite46†23 ] using strong convergence [cite72†22 ].
L69: In our work we prove quantum ergodicity and quantum mixing for the Laplacian to Schrödinger operators with potential adapting Duhamel identity for the hyperbolic wave equation and exponential mixing of the geodesic flow [cite100†85 , cite101†75 ]. We apply this in particular to potentials arising from functions in $L^{p}(\mathbb{H})$, sufficiently separated point processes, as well as Hartree-type potentials associated with thermodynamic limit of Bose gas.
L70: Let us now describe the main results and examples in detail and comparisons of our methods to other works.
L71: ### 1.2. The main results and examples
L72: 
L73: Let $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of compact connected hyperbolic surfaces. We will be interested in sequences of surfaces that satisfy the following conditions.
L74: 
L75:   1. (BSC):
L76: 
L77: Benjamini-Schramm convergence: $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges to $\mathbb{H}$ in the Benjamini-Schramm topology. That is for any $R>0$, we have
L78: 
L79:  | $$\frac{|\{x\in X_{n}\ :\ \InjRad_{X_{n}}(x)\leq R\}|}{|X_{n}|}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.$$  |
L80: 
L81:   2. (UND):
L82: Uniform discreteness: $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ the injectivity radius $\InjRad_{X_{n}}$ has uniform lower bound.
L83: 
L84:   3. (EXP):
L85: 
L86: Expander property: $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ has a uniform lower bound for the spectral gap $\lambda_{1}(X_{n})$.
L87: These conditions on the sequence of surfaces are the same in works [cite53†67 , cite63†56 ]. In the first work, quantum ergodicity is established, and in the second quantum mixing. For both works, they consider any orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Our aim here is to generalise this to sequences of Schrödinger operators defined on each $X_{n}$. That is, operators of the form
L88: 
L89:  | $$H_{V_{n}}=-\Delta_{X_{n}}+V_{n},$$  |
L90: where $-\Delta_{X_{n}}$ is the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator and $V_{n}$ is a potential. We will make the following assumption on the sequences of potentials.
L91: 
L92:   1. (POT):
L93: 
L94: $\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a sequence of potentials on $(X_{n})$ satisfying for some $C_{\min},C_{\max}\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
L95: 
L96:  | $$C_{\min}\leq V_{n}\leq C_{\max}\qquad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}$$  |
L97: 
L98: and $\left\lVert V_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{n})}^{2}=o(g_{n})$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.
L99: The property (POT) can be achieved by fixing $V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})$ for some $p>0$ and setting $V_{n}:=V\bm{1}_{D_{n}}$ extended as a $\Gamma_{n}$ periodic function on $\mathbb{H}$, where $D_{n}$ is a fundamental domain of $X_{n}=\Gamma_{n}\setminus\mathbb{H}$, see Examples cite78†1.3 below. Conditions such as $V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H})$ or other strong decay conditions like Kato-type conditions are typically assumed in e.g.
L100: Strichartz estimates and dispersivity properties of the wave- and Schödinger equations in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\mathbb{H}$ when the Laplacian is perturbed by a potential, see e.g. [cite102†58 , cite103†42 , cite104†49 , cite105†21 , cite106†31 , cite107†15 , cite108†16 , cite109†17 , cite110†18 , cite111†89 , cite112†57 , cite113†51 ].
L101: With these we can state the main theorem.
L102: ###### Theorem 1.1.
L103: Let $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of compact connected hyperbolic surfaces that satisfies (BSC), (UND), and (EXP), and let $\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of potentials that satisfies (POT) with the two numbers $C_{\min}$ and $C_{\max}$.
L104: Consider for each $n\in\mathbb{N}$ the Schrödinger operator $H_{V_{n}}^{(n)}=-\Delta_{X_{n}}+V_{n}$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_{1}^{(n)}\leq\lambda_{2}^{(n)}\leq\lambda_{3}^{(n)}\leq\lambda_{4}^{(n)}\cdots$ and eigenvectors $\{\psi_{j}^{(n)}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$. For all $n,j\in\mathbb{N}$ set $\varrho_{j}^{(n)}=\sqrt{\lambda_{j}^{(n)}-\frac{1}{4}}$.
L105: Fix $\frac{1}{4}<a^{\prime}<a<b<b^{\prime}$ such that $b-C_{\max}>a-\min(0,C_{\min})$ and a sequence of spectral windows $I_{n}$ such that $[a,b]\subset I_{n}\subset[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]$. Then for any uniformly bounded sequence of measurable functions $\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ we have the following three properties:
L106:   1. (1)
L107: 
L108:  | $$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}\big(H_{V}^{(n)},I_{n}\big)}\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}^{(n)}\in I_{n}}\Big|\langle a_{n}\psi_{j}^{(n)},\psi_{j}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}-\frac{1}{\Vol(X_{n})}\int_{X_{n}}a_{n}(x)\,dx\Big|^{2}=0,$$  |
L109: 
L110: where $dx$ is the volume form on $X_{n}$.
L111: 
L112:   2. (2)
L113: 
L114: For every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta(\varepsilon)>0$ such that
L115:  | $$\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}\big(H_{V}^{(n)},I_{n}\big)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k:\lambda_{j}^{(n)},\lambda_{k}^{(n)}\in I_{n}\\
L116: |\varrho_{j}^{(n)}-\varrho_{k}^{(n)}|<\delta(\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}\Big|\langle a_{n}\psi_{j}^{(n)},\psi_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(X_{n})}\Big|^{2}<\varepsilon.$$  |
L117: 
L118:   3. (3)
L119: 
L120: For every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta(\varepsilon)>0$ such that for all $\tau\in\mathbb{R}$
L121:  | $$\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}\big(H_{V}^{(n)},I_{n}\big)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k:\lambda_{j}^{(n)},\lambda_{k}^{(n)}\in I_{n}\\
L122: |\varrho_{j}^{(n)}-\varrho_{k}^{(n)}-\tau|<\delta(\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}\Big|\langle a_{n}\psi_{j}^{(n)},\psi_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(X_{n})}\Big|^{2}<\varepsilon.$$  |
L123: 
L124: In all three cases $\mathcal{N}(H_{V}^{(n)},I_{n})$ is the number of eigenvalues of $H_{V}^{(n)}$ in $I_{n}$ counted with multiplicity.
L125: We note that, as we can consider induced potentials $V_{n}$ by a fixed bounded $V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H})$, Theorem cite114†1.1 allows us to consider sequences of potentials that do not vanish in the limit. Thus, Theorem cite114†1.1 is not just in the weak coupling regime; see Examples cite78†1.3 below for more discussion and examples. Moreover, the theorem applies in a fixed window $I_{n}=I$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ as more commonly stated in e.g.
L126: [cite53†67 , cite54†68 , cite55†1 , cite56†84 , cite57†29 , cite58†28 , cite63†56 , cite37†4 , cite46†23 , cite44†13 , cite42†12 , cite40†26 ]. We write the result in this more general form to be able to apply to the thermodynamic limits of hyperbolic Bose gas example.
L127: Conditions (BSC), (UND), and (EXP) hold for arithmetic surfaces by the works of Katz, Schnaps, and Vishne [cite115†59 ] and Selberg [cite116†91 ]. In the first mentioned work, it is shown that the injectivity radius grows, which ensures the properties (BSC) and (UND). In the second work, a uniform lower bound of $3/16$ is established on the spectral gap that ensures the assumption (EXP).
L128: Moreover, they are generically valid for random surfaces of a large genus, whose spectral geometry has seen much progress in recent years. We can here rephrase the result in probabilistic language using the Weil-Peterson random model, but this could also be done in other models of random surfaces such as the random covering model [cite117†71 ]. For background on the Weil-Petersson model random model and its use in spectral geometry, see, e.g.
L129: [cite118†78 , cite119†98 , cite64†6 , cite61†48 , cite60†80 ] and more recent references. Our main result rephrased in this probabilistic language takes the following form.
L130: ###### Theorem 1.2.
L131: Let $\{X_{g}\}_{g\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of random compact hyperbolic surfaces, where the distribution of $X_{g}$ is given by the Weil-Petersson distribution on the moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{g}$ for all $g\in\mathbb{N}$, where $g$ is the genus. Suppose that $\{V_{g}\}_{g\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a sequence of potentials that satisfies the condition (POT) with the two numbers $C_{\min}$ and $C_{\max}$.
L132: Consider for each $g\in\mathbb{N}$ the Schrödinger operator $H_{V_{g}}^{(g)}=-\Delta_{X_{g}}+V_{g}$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_{1}^{(g)}\leq\lambda_{2}^{(g)}\leq\lambda_{3}^{(g)}\leq\lambda_{4}^{(g)}\cdots$ and eigenvectors $\{\psi_{j}^{(g)}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$. For all $g,j\in\mathbb{N}$ set $\varrho_{j}^{(g)}=\sqrt{\lambda_{j}^{(g)}-\frac{1}{4}}$.
L133: Fix $\frac{1}{4}<a^{\prime}<a<b<b^{\prime}$ such that $b-C_{\max}>a-\min(0,C_{\min})$ and a sequence of spectral windows $I_{g}$ such that $[a,b]\subset I_{g}\subset[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]$. Then for any uniformly bounded sequence of measurable functions $\{a_{g}\}_{g\in\mathbb{N}}$ the properties (1)–(3) of Theorem cite114†1.1 hold with high probability as the genus goes to infinity.
L134: A major difference between the deterministic setting and this random setting is that the properties (BSC), (UND), and (EXP) are not vaild almost surely. Instead, the properties (BSC) and (UND) are replaced with quantitive bounds in terms of the genus, which are valid with high probability. The property (EXP) will be valid with high probability.
L135: What we mean by the phrase with high probability is that for all large enough $g\in\mathbb{N}$ there exists a set in $\mathcal{A}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}$ such that $\mathcal{\mathbb{P}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\rightarrow 1$ as $g\rightarrow\infty$, where $\mathcal{\mathbb{P}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}$ is the Weil-Petersson probability measure.
L136: If we then sample $X_{g}$ from $\mathcal{A}_{g}$ for each $g$ and a sequence of potentials defined on each $X_{g}$ that satisfies our assumptions, then properties (1)–(3) of Theorem cite114†1.1 will be satisfied. We will give a short overview on how to prove Theorem cite120†1.2 in Section cite25†6 .
L137: We now give the main examples of potentials $V_{n}$ where Theorems cite114†1.1 and cite120†1.2 apply:
L138: ###### Examples 1.3.
L139: 
L140: In the following, we let $\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of compact connected hyperbolic surfaces that satisfies (BSC), (UND), and (EXP). We let $\{D_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be the associated sequence of fundamental domains.
L141: (a) Potentials induced by a limit potential. If $V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})$ we set $V_{n}=V\bm{1}_{D_{n}}$ extended as a $\Gamma_{n}$ periodic function on $\mathbb{H}$, where $D_{n}$ is a fundamental domain of $X_{n}=\Gamma_{n}\setminus\mathbb{H}$, then (POT) is satisfied for the sequence of potentials $(V_{n})$. In the case $0<p\leq 2$ we have
L142:  | $$\left\lVert V_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{n})}^{2}\leq\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})}^{2-p}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H})}^{p}$$  |
L143: 
L144: and if $p>2$ we have by Hölder’s inequality that
L145: 
L146:  | $$\left\lVert V_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{n})}^{2}\leq\Vol(X_{n})^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H})}^{2}.$$  |
L147: In particular, the potentials $V_{n}$ do not vanish in the limit as they converge to $V\neq 0$. Such condition on $V$ or strong forms such as Kato condition guarantee the essential spectrum remains in the same place and in the literature commonly leads to Strichartz estimates and dispersivity properties of the wave and Schödinger equations when the Laplacian is perturbed by a potential [cite102†58 , cite103†42 , cite104†49 , cite105†21 , cite106†31 , cite107†15 , cite108†16 , cite109†17 , cite110†18 , cite111†89 , cite112†57 , cite113†51 ].
L148: (b) Point cloud potentials. Alternatively, we can also apply the result if the potential is a superposition over a point set that is sufficiently sparse. Suppose that $\mathcal{X}$ is a locally finite set in $\mathbb{H}$ such that for some $\delta>0$ it holds $\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},x_{j})>2\delta$, for all $x_{i},x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}$ such that $x_{i}\neq x_{j}$. Then, depending on the density of $\mathcal{X}$ in $D_{n}$ we have the following two cases.
L149: Suppose first that $\#\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}=o(g_{n})$ and that $W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})$ are such that $\mathrm{supp}(W)\subset B(i,\delta)$. Define the sequence $\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ of potentials given by $V_{n}(x)=\sum_{x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}}W(T_{x_{j}}(x)),$ where $T_{x_{j}}$ is the unique translation that maps $x_{j}$ to $i$. Then this sequence of potentials will satisfy (POT). Due to the assumptions, we have that the sequence is uniformly bounded.
L150: For the $L^{2}$-norm bound, we have
L151: (1.1)  |  | $$\left\lVert V_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{n})}^{2}\leq C\sum_{x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}}\int_{D_{n}}|W(T_{x_{j}}(x))|^{2}\,dx\leq C\sum_{x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}}\left\lVert W\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H})}=o(g_{n}).$$  |
L152: This verifies that $\left\lVert V_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{n})}^{2}=o(g_{n})$. Now, if we have instead that $\#\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}=\mathcal{O}(g_{n})$ and that $\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a uniformly bounded sequence in $L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})$ with $\mathrm{supp}(W_{n})\subset B(i,\delta_{n})$ with $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{n}=0$.
L153: Define the sequence $\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ of potentials given by $V_{n}(x)=\sum_{x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}\cap D_{n}}W_{n}(T_{x_{j}}(x)),$ Then this sequence of potentials will satisfy (POT). This is seen by an analogous argument to the one given above. These examples complement the low density limit or Boltzmann-Grad limit in the Euclidean case, where the linear Boltzmann equation can also be derived as the associated macroscopic transport equation; see, e.g. [cite121†39 , cite122†77 ].
