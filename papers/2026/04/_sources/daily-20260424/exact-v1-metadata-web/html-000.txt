L59: # Downlink Channel Matrix Estimation from PMI-Only Feedback in FDD Systems: Maximum Likelihood and Sharp Excess Risk Bound
L60: Jinchi Chen Affiliation: School of Mathematics, East China University of Science and Technology, Shanghai, China.    Mingxi Hu Affiliation: School of Data Science, Fudan University, Shanghai, China    Peigang Jiang Affiliation: Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China    Xin Meng Affiliation: Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China    Ke Wei ^{†}^{†}thanks: Corresponding author (kewei@fudan.edu.cn).
L61: Affiliation: School of Data Science, Fudan University, Shanghai, China    Xianyin Zhang Affiliation: School of Data Science, Fudan University, Shanghai, China
L62: August 24, 2026
L63: ###### Abstract
L64: We study downlink channel estimation in a frequency-division duplex (FDD) massive MIMO system from PMI-only feedback under a 5G NR-type limited-feedback architecture. In this architecture, the user selects a preferred codeword from a shared codebook based on the reduced-dimensional channel and only reports its index (known as the precoding matrix indicator, PMI) back to the base station. Therefore, the channel must be estimated from these highly quantized, nonlinear PMI observations.
L65: Based on a probabilistic perturbation model, a constrained maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is proposed for this estimation problem, whose objective can also be interpreted as a relaxation of the hard empirical decision error. The Cramér–Rao bound is derived for the complex-valued model, with the global phase ambiguity handled via gauge-fixing.
L66: For the real-valued setting, a global excess-risk bound of order $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ is established, which is then refined to a sharp local rate of order $O(1/T)$ under suitable identifiability conditions. Numerical results show that the MLE asymptotically attains the Cramér–Rao bound and outperforms several baseline methods on both synthetic data and realistic FDD channels.
L67: ## 1 Introduction
L68: 
L69: This paper studies downlink channel estimation in a frequency-division duplex (FDD) massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) system. We focus on the single-antenna user setting, where the base station has $d$ antennas and the user has a single antenna. Thus the downlink channel can be represented by $\bm{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}$, and the goal is to estimate $\bm{h}$ at the base station from quantized feedback observations generated under a 5G NR-type limited-feedback mechanism.
L70: In this mechanism, the base station and the user interact over multiple communication rounds, each consisting of a measurement phase and a feedback phase. More precisely, at $t$-th communication round, the base station selects a dimensionality-reduction matrix $\bm{Q}_{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times p}$ and transmits pilots, from which the user computes the effective channel. Assume that the base station and the user share a codebook
L71:  | $$\bm{V}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{v}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{v}_{N}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times N},$$  |
L72: 
L73: whose columns are candidate precoding vectors in the reduced-dimensional domain. The user then feeds back a precoding matrix indicator (PMI), which is the index of the codeword that maximizes the effective channel gain:
L74: 
L75:  | $$i_{t}=\argmax_{i=1,\dots,N}\big|\bm{v}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{Q}_{t}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{h}\big|^{2},\quad t=1,\cdots,T.$$  |  | (1.1)
L76: The corresponding channel quality indicator (CQI) is given by
L77: 
L78:  | $$\eta_{t}=\big|\bm{v}_{i_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{Q}_{t}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{h}\big|^{2},\quad t=1,\cdots,T.$$  |
L79: Since the base station does not directly observe $\bm{h}$, it must recover the channel solely from the feedback indices $\{i_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$, and possibly the CQI values $\{\eta_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$. In this paper, however, we focus on the more challenging setting where the base station only observes the PMI sequence $\{i_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$ and does not use the CQI values $\{\eta_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$.
L80: Therefore, each measurement round provides only a discrete comparison outcome, rather than a direct linear measurement or a scalar gain value.
L81: The estimation problem can therefore be stated as follows: given the measurement matrices $\{\bm{Q}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$ and the corresponding PMI feedback indices $\{i_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$, recover the channel vector $\bm{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}$. This model captures the main difficulty of FDD downlink estimation under limited feedback: recovering a high-dimensional complex channel from highly nonlinear and compressed observations.
L82: While we focus on the single-antenna user case for simplicity, the proposed method can also be extended to the multi-antenna setting.
L83: ### 1.1 Related Work
L84: Accurate downlink channel state information is essential for beamforming in FDD massive MIMO systems. This task is difficult because the BS cannot rely on channel reciprocity, while the channel dimension and the feedback cost both grow with the array size. A natural way to reduce this burden is to exploit long-term structure shared by uplink and downlink channels.
L85: Representative works estimate downlink covariance or angular information from uplink observations and then use it for downlink acquisition and beamforming [cite47†43 , cite48†19 , cite49†22 , cite50†3 ]. These methods can reduce the feedback overhead and are effective when the channel geometry is sufficiently structured. However, the limitation is that they rely on a strong relation between uplink and downlink statistics or angular support.
L86: This relation can become weak when the frequency separation is non-negligible or when the propagation environment is rich [cite51†2 ].
L87: To avoid this dependence on UL/DL structural extrapolation, many works aim at more direct downlink CSI acquisition. A common approach is to exploit channel sparsity and use compressive sensing for channel estimation and feedback reduction [cite52†23 , cite53†6 , cite54†32 , cite55†35 , cite56†15 , cite57†10 ].
L88: Another approach is to learn a compact representation of CSI from data and use neural networks for CSI compression and recovery, as in CsiNet and its variants [cite58†42 , cite59†9 , cite60†18 , cite61†29 , cite62†30 , cite63†31 , cite64†7 ]. These methods can recover higher-resolution CSI and often achieve strong empirical performance. However, they usually assume feedback mechanisms based on compressed CSI values or learned latent variables, rather than the standard codebook reports used in practical FDD systems.
L89: Consequently, a gap remains between these techniques and the codebook-based limited-feedback interface of current cellular standards. To bridge this gap, another line of work adheres to the standard codebook-based limited-feedback architecture, and seeks to improve the feedback interface itself, for example through higher-resolution codebooks within the standard framework or through environment-aware and learned codebook design [cite65†34 , cite66†12 , cite67†48 , cite68†33 ].
L90: The works most closely related to ours are those that retain the Type-I limited-feedback architecture and seek to recover a hidden channel object directly from PMI/CQI feedback [cite69†25 , cite70†24 , cite71†27 , cite72†26 , cite73†45 ]. In particular, Li et al. [cite69†25 ] study downlink channel covariance matrix reconstruction from Type-I feedback and use the recovered second-order statistics to support beamforming and related tasks.
L91: The same authors [cite70†24 ] later show that CQI carries information beyond a mere weighting role and can be used to estimate an enhanced beamforming vector directly from PMI/CQI observations. In subsequent work, Li et al. [cite71†27 ] aim at higher-resolution CSI recovery itself, and formulate CSI sensing as a constrained phase retrieval problem under PMI/CQI feedback, further exploiting auxiliary structure such as spatial consistency and heterogeneous feedback capabilities. More recently, Li et al.
L92: [cite72†26 ] couple feedback-driven estimation with adaptive pilot design and develop an online beam refinement framework that combines channel exploration and beamforming updates over multiple communication rounds.
L93: While these works rely on both PMI and CQI and primarily target covariance recovery, beamforming enhancement, CSI sensing, or feedback refinement, our focus is on direct channel estimation from PMI-only feedback, emphasizing the statistical inference problem induced by such highly quantized ordinal observations.
L94: The patent disclosure in [cite73†45 ] also considers channel-related estimation under PMI-only feedback and proposes a heuristic spectral method that constructs an empirical data matrix from the feedback observations and uses its leading eigenvector as the estimator. In contrast, we develop a principled statistical inference framework for direct channel estimation from PMI-only feedback.
L95: ### 1.2 Main Contributions
L96: 
L97: The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.
L98: 
L99:   * •
L100: Maximum likelihood formulation. We formulate direct downlink channel estimation from PMI-only feedback as a statistical inference problem. In contrast to existing works that rely on the joint use of PMI and CQI or target covariance recovery, beam refinement, or heuristic reconstruction, we focus on recovering the channel itself from ordinal codebook-comparison observations.
L101: To this end, we introduce a probabilistic perturbation model for PMI generation and derive a constrained maximum likelihood estimator (MLE). Moreover, we also show that the likelihood objective can be understood as a two-stage relaxation of the hard empirical decision error. Furthermore, we establish the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) for the probabilistic model, properly accounting for the global phase ambiguity.
L102:   * •
L103: Sharp excess risk bound of MLE. We establish a sharp local excess-risk bound of order $O(1/T)$ for the MLE in the real-valued single-antenna user setting. To this end, we first prove a global excess-risk bound of order $O(1/\sqrt{T})$. Combined with basic properties of the likelihood function, this bound guarantees that the estimator falls into a small neighborhood of the ground truth as $T$ grows.
L104: Within this local region, the population risk is strongly convex, allowing us to sharpen the global bound and obtain the fast $O(1/T)$ rate under mild conditions. It is worth noting that, although the analysis is carried out for the real-valued single-antenna user case, the MLE formulation itself is equally applicable to complex-valued channels and to the multi-antenna user setting. We leave the extension of the sharp risk theory to these more general cases for future work.
L105:   * •
L106: Numerical validation. We validate the proposed MLE method through numerical experiments on both synthetic data and FDD channels generated by QuaDRiGa. The experiments show that the MLE asymptotically attains the derived CRB as the number of feedback rounds grows.
L107: Furthermore, on the QuaDRiGa-generated FDD channels, the proposed method outperforms baselines such as the spectral method, alternating minimization, and subspace phase retrieval, demonstrating its effectiveness in both single-stream and two-stream scenarios.
L108: ### 1.3 Notation and Organization
L109: Throughout this paper, vectors and matrices are denoted by bold lowercase and bold uppercase letters, respectively. For any matrix $\bm{X}$, $\tr(\bm{X})$, $\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}$, and $\bm{X}^{\mathsf{H}}$ denote its trace, transpose, and conjugate transpose, respectively. We use $\|\bm{X}\|$ and $\|\bm{X}\|_{F}$ to denote the spectral norm and the Frobenius norm of $\bm{X}$, respectively. L110: For two vectors $\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}$, define the phase-invariant distance as $\dist(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\min_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi)}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}e^{j\varphi}\|_{2}$. In real case, it reduces to $\dist(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\min_{s\in\{\pm 1\}}\|\bm{x}-s\bm{y}\|_{2}$. For two symmetric matrices $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$, we write $\bm{A}\preceq\bm{B}$ or $\bm{B}\succeq\bm{A}$ if $\bm{B}-\bm{A}$ is positive semidefinite.
L111: We define the matrix inner product between $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$ as $\langle\bm{A},\bm{B}\rangle=\tr(\bm{A}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{B})$. The symbols $c,C,c_{1},C_{1},\ldots$ denote absolute positive constants whose values may vary from line to line. The notation $T\gtrsim f(\gamma)$ means that there exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that $T\geq cf(\gamma)$.
L112: The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section cite10†2 introduces the probabilistic PMI model, derives the maximum likelihood estimator, and presents the Cramér–Rao bound. Section cite14†3 establishes the sharp excess-risk bound for the MLE. Section cite15†4 reports numerical experiments on both synthetic data and realistic FDD channels. Sections cite29†5 and cite33†6 contain the proofs of the main results and technical lemmas, respectively.
L113: We conclude the paper in Section cite44†7 with a discussion of future directions.
L114: ## 2 MLE and Cramér-Rao Bound
L115: ### 2.1 MLE
L116: 
L117: In order to derive the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) for the PMI-only feedback, we approximate the feedback via a stochastic perturbation model. For ease of notation, let
L118: 
L119:  | $$\bm{a}_{t,i}=\bm{Q}_{t}\bm{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times 1}.$$  |
L120: 
L121: Consider the following random model for the observed index at the $t$-th round:
L122: 
L123:  | $\displaystyle I_{t}=\argmax_{i\in[N]}\left\{\frac{|\bm{a}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}}{\tau}+g_{t,i}\right\},$  |
L124: where $\tau>0$ is a fixed temperature parameter, and $g_{t,i}$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1)$ random variables whose probability density is given by $f(x)=\exp\bigl(-(x+e^{-x})\bigr)$. By the Gumbel-max trick, conditional on $\bm{h}$ and $\bm{Q}_{t}$, it can be directly verified that the random variable $I_{t}$ follows a multinomial distribution [cite74†17 ]. That is, for any $i\in[N]$,
L125: 
L126:  | $\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=i\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}=p_{t}(i;\bm{h})$  |
L127: 
L128: where
L129:  | $\displaystyle p_{t}(i;\bm{x}):=\frac{\exp\big({|\bm{a}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}}/{\tau}\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\!\big({|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}}/{\tau}\big)}.$  |
L130: 
L131: In other words, we replace the hard $\arg\max$ in (cite75†1.1 ) with a softmax distribution over the codewords, where $\tau$ controls the sharpness of the approximation: as $\tau\to 0^{+}$, the softmax distribution tends to a one‑hot vector associated with the PMI feedback.
L132: 
L133: Define
L134:  | $\displaystyle\ell_{t}(i;\bm{x})=-\log p_{t}(i;\bm{x})=\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\left(\frac{|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}-|\bm{a}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}}{\tau}\right).$  |  | (2.1)
L135: 
L136: Then, given $T$ independent observations $I_{1},\cdots,I_{T}$, the negative log-likelihood function is given by
L137:  | $\displaystyle L_{T}(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t}(I_{t};\bm{x})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\left(\frac{|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}-|\bm{a}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}}{\tau}\right).$  |  | (2.2)
L138: 
L139: We consider the following constrained maximum likelihood approach for the estimation of $\bm{h}$,
L140: 
L141:  | $\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{h}}_{T}\in\argmin_{x:\|\bm{x}\|_{2}\leq R}L_{T}(\bm{x}),$  |  | (2.3)
L142: where $R>0$ is a fixed radius parameter. The constraint $\|\bm{x}\|_{2}\leq R$ is introduced to restrict the parameter space to a compact set. This is without loss of generality, since the channel vector $\bm{h}$ has finite energy in practical systems, and $R$ can be chosen sufficiently large such that the true channel lies in this set.
L143: From a statistical perspective, this constraint ensures well-posedness of the estimation problem and enables uniform control of the likelihood function and its derivatives, which is essential for establishing finite-sample guarantees.
L144: Let $\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}(\bm{x}):=\mathbb{E}\left\{L_{T}(\bm{x})\rule{0.0pt}{8.53581pt}\right\}$ and define the excess risk as
L145: 
L146:  | $\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}(\bm{x})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}(\bm{h}).$  |  | (2.4)
L147: 
L148: A direct calculation yields that
L149:  | $$\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}(\bm{x})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}(\bm{h})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(p_{t}(\cdot;\bm{h})\,\|\,p_{t}(\cdot;\bm{x})\bigr),$$  |  | (2.5)
L150: 
L151: where $\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\cdot\,\|\,\cdot\bigr)$ denotes the KL divergence of two probability distributions. In Section cite14†3 , we will establish the global and local excess risks for the MLE.
L152: ### 2.2 Alternative Interpretation of MLE
L153: 
L154: A straightforward way to estimate $\bm{h}$ from the hard PMI observations is to minimize the empirical fraction of decision errors. That is, given the observations $\{I_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}$, consider
L155: 
L156:  | $\displaystyle L_{T}^{0-1}(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}1\Big\{I_{t}\notin\arg\max_{j\in[N]}|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}\Big\},$  |
L157: 
L158: Noting that
L159:  | $\displaystyle I_{t}\notin\arg\max_{j\in[N]}|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}\Longleftrightarrow\max_{j\in[N]}|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}-|\bm{a}_{t,I_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}>0.$  |
L160: 
L161: Therefore, $L_{T}^{0-1}(\bm{x})$ can be equivalently rewritten as
L162: 
L163:  | $\displaystyle L_{T}^{0-1}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}1\Big\{\max_{j\in[N]}|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}-|\bm{a}_{t,I_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}>0.\Big\}.$  |
L164: Though it is the most direct formulation of empirical error minimization under the hard PMI rule, the objective is discontinuous and difficult to optimize.
L165: 
L166: We now smooth this objective in two steps. First, replacing the discontinuous indicator loss by the margin yields
L167: 
L168:  | $\displaystyle L^{\mathrm{margin}}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\max_{j\in[N]}|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}-|\bm{a}_{t,I_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}\right),\quad\bm{x}\neq 0.$  |
L169: This loss function is continuous, but still contains the nonsmooth max operator. Thus, we further relax the $\max$ function with its Moreau envelop (i.e., the log-sum-exp function),
L170: 
L171:  | $$\max\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\rightarrow\tau\log\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\exp(x_{i}/\tau)\right),$$  |
L172: 
L173: leading to
L174: 
L175:  | $$L_{T}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\tau\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(|\bm{a}_{t,j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x}|^{2}/\tau)-(\bm{a}_{t,I_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{x})^{2}\right)-\tau\log N,$$  |
L176: which is exactly equivalent to the MLE loss. The parameter $\tau$ controls the sharpness of this relaxation. A smaller $\tau$ gives a sharper model and a sharper surrogate.
L177: ### 2.3 Cramér-Rao Bound
L178: 
L179: We use the Cramér–Rao bound as a benchmark for evaluating the performance of an estimator. Let ${\cal M}$ be the realification operator that maps a Hermitian matrix in $\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}$ to a real symmetric matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}$, i.e., for any Hermitian matrix $\bm{A}$,
L180: 
L181:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\bm{A})=\begin{bmatrix}\Re(\bm{A})&-\Im(\bm{A})\\
L182: \Im(\bm{A})&\Re(\bm{A})\\
L183: \end{bmatrix}.$  |
L184: Note that since $\bm{A}$ is a Hermitian matrix, one has $\Re(\bm{A})=\Re(\bm{A})^{\mathsf{T}},\Im(\bm{A})=-\Im(\bm{A})^{\mathsf{T}}$, hence ${\cal M}(\bm{A})$ is real symmetric.
L185: ###### Lemma 2.0 (Fisher information matrix).
L186: 
L187: Suppose that $\{I_{t}\}_{t=1,\cdots,T}$ are independent with $I_{t}\sim p_{t}(\cdot;\bm{h})$. Let $\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}=[\Re(\bm{h})^{\mathsf{T}}~\Im(\bm{h})^{\mathsf{T}}]^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{A}_{t,i}=\bm{a}_{t,i}\bm{a}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{H}}$ and $\bm{g}_{t,i}=\mathcal{M}(\bm{A}_{t,i})\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}$. Then the Fisher information matrix with respect to $\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}$ is given by
L188:  | $\displaystyle\bm{F}(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}})=\frac{4}{\tau^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{t}(i;\bm{h})\bm{g}_{t,i}\bm{g}_{t,i}^{\mathsf{T}}-\frac{4}{\tau^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{t}(i;\bm{h})\bm{g}_{t,i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{t}(i;\bm{h})\bm{g}_{t,i}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}.$  |
L189: 
L190: The proof of this lemma is presented Section cite34†6.1 . The following lemma establishes the equivalence of the Fisher information matrix over the set $\{\bm{h}e^{-i\varphi}\}$.
L191: ###### Lemma 2.0.
L192: 
L193: Let $\bm{h}_{\varphi}:=\bm{h}e^{i\varphi}$ and define
L194:
