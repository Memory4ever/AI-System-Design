L48: Abstract
L49: Theory was formulated for scattering by a coated chiral sphere of a plane wave of arbitrary polarization state with amplitude modulated by a Gaussian pulse. The spherical core and the concentric shell of the sphere were composed of two different homogeneous materials, both isotropic chiral. Calculations of energy efficiencies for extinction, total scattering, and absorption were carried out for the shell material with experimentally determined constitutive parameters, the core being vacuous.
L50: All three energy efficiencies depend on the relative thickness of the shell and the circular polarization state of the carrier plane wave.
L51: ## 1 Introduction
L52: Frequency-domain scattering by a sphere is a classic problem in electromagnetic field theory. The scattering of a linearly polarized plane wave by a homogeneous sphere composed of an isotropic dielectric material was solved by Lorenz [cite15†1 ] and Mie [cite16†2 ] well over a century ago.
L53: Subsequently, the theory has been extended to spheres made of an isotropic dielectric-magnetic material [cite17†3 , cite18†4 ] or an isotropic chiral material [cite19†5 , cite20†6 , cite21†7 ], as well as to concentrically multilayered spheres [cite22†8 , cite23†9 , cite24†10 , cite25†11 ]. However, the focus of theoretical work has almost entirely been on linearly polarized incidence.
L54: Circularly polarized incidence, which is a more natural choice for spheres made of isotropic chiral materials [cite26†12 ], and the more general elliptically polarized incidence have received scant attention [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ].
L55: Whereas a few analyses of time-domain scattering by an isotropic dielectric sphere [cite29†15 , cite30†16 , cite31†17 ] (or isotropic dielectric cylinder [cite32†18 ]) have been reported, including for a sphere undergoing uniform translational motion [cite33†19 ], time-domain scattering by an isotropic chiral sphere has not been addressed hitherto, to the best of our knowledge.
L56: In this letter we develop the theory for scattering by a coated chiral sphere of a signal carried by a plane wave of arbitrary polarization state. A Gaussian pulse is chosen for amplitude modulation of the carrier plane wave.
L57: Numerical results, based on the measured frequency-dependent constitutive parameters of a real isotropic chiral material [cite34†20 ], are presented to highlight the dependencies of the energy efficiencies for extinction, total scattering, and absorption [cite33†19 ] on the polarization state of the incident carrier plane wave.
L58: In the notation is adopted, vector quantities are denoted by boldface and 2$\times$2 matrix quantities are enclosed in square brackets and double underlined. The unit vectors $\hat{{\bf u}}_{x}$, $\hat{{\bf u}}_{y}$, and $\hat{{\bf u}}_{z}$ relate to the Cartesian coordinate system $(x,y,z)$. The unit vectors $\hat{{\bf u}}_{r}$, $\hat{{\bf u}}_{\theta}$, and $\hat{{\bf u}}_{\phi}$ are associated with the spherical coordinate system $(r,\theta,\phi)$.
L59: The free-space permittivity, permeability, wave number, and intrinsic impedance are denoted by $\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}$, $\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}$, $k_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}=\omega\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}$, and $\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}=\sqrt{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}/\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}$, respectively, with $\omega$ being the angular frequency.
L60: The speed of light in free space is $c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}=1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}$. Also, $i=\sqrt{-1}$ and the complex conjugate is denoted by a superscripted asterisk.
L61: ## 2 Theory
L62: ### 2.1 Coated chiral sphere
L63: 
L64: The coated chiral sphere is centered at the coordinate origin ${\bf r}={\bf 0}$. The core $r<a$ is made of a homogeneous material labelled 1, whereas the shell $a<r<b$ is made of a homogeneous material labelled 2. The two materials are specified by the frequency-domain constitutive relations [cite26†12 ]
L65:  | $$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf D}({\bf r},\omega)=\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\,\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)+i\,\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\,\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf H}({\bf r},\omega)\\
L66: {\bf B}({\bf r},\omega)=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\,\mu_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf H}({\bf r},\omega)-i\,\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\,\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)\end{array}\right\},$$  |  | (1)
L67: where ${\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}$, etc., are phasors. Furthermore, the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}$, relative permeability $\mu_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}$, and relative chirality parameter $\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}$ for $\ell\in\left\{1,2\right\}$.
L68: ### 2.2 Incident signal
L69: 
L70: Without loss of generality, the incident signal propagates along the $+z$ axis. Thus, the electric field of the incident signal may be expressed as
L71: 
L72:  | $$\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)=\mathrm{Re}\left\{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}f(\tau)\right\},$$  |  | (2)
L73: 
L74: wherein the scalar $\tau=t-(z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})$. Following Garner et al. [cite33†19 ], we set
L75: 
L76:  | $$f(\tau)=\exp\left(i2\pi\nu_{\mathrm{c}}\tau\right)\,\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),$$  |  | (3)
L77: with $\nu_{\mathrm{c}}$ being the frequency of the carrier plane wave and $\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}/\nu_{\mathrm{c}}$ being the carrier wavelength. The Gaussian pulse is centered at $t=z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$ effectively determines the pulse duration.
L78: The polarization-state vector ${\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}=a_{s}\hat{{\bf u}}_{x}+a_{p}\hat{{\bf u}}_{y}$ with $a_{s}\in\mathbb{C}$ and $a_{p}\in\mathbb{C}$ specifies an elliptically polarized plane wave propagating along the $+z$ axis. The carrier plane wave is linearly polarized if either $a_{s}=0$ or $a_{p}=0$, left-circularly polarized (LCP) if $a_{p}=ia_{s}$, and right-circularly polarized (RCP) if $a_{p}=-ia_{s}$.
L79: For an adequate discrete-time representation of $\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)$ with sampling period $t_{\mathrm{s}}$, the rate of sampling $1/t_{\mathrm{s}}$ must be at least twice as great as the highest frequency in the Fourier transform of the incident signal. The total number $N$ of samples has to be selected by trial and error to ensure that $Nt_{\mathrm{s}}$ exceeds the larger of the durations of the incident signal and the scattered signal.
L80: The discrete Fourier transform of $f(\tau)$ is given by
L81: 
L82:  | $$\bar{f}(\nu_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}f(\tau_{q})\exp\left(i\omega_{p}\tau_{q}\right),$$  |  | (4)
L83: 
L84: with $\tau_{q}=qt_{\mathrm{s}}-(z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})$, $\omega_{p}=2\pi\nu_{p}$, and $\nu_{p}=p/Nt_{\mathrm{s}}$. Hence, inversion of the discrete Fourier transform delivers
L85: 
L86:  | $${f}(\tau_{q})=\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\bar{f}(\nu_{p})\exp\left(-i\omega_{p}\tau_{q}\right).$$  |  | (5)
L87: By substituting Eq. (cite35†5 ) into Eq. (cite36†2 ), the electric field for the incident signal can be approximated by an ensemble of $N$ plane waves as
L88: 
L89:  | $$\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)\approx\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Re}\left\{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}\,\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\,\exp\left[i\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}z-\omega_{p}t\right)\right]\right\},$$  |  | (6)
L90: where $\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})$ denotes the Fourier transform of ${f}(\tau)$ at $z=0$. Each plane-wave component has frequency $\nu_{p}$, wavenumber $k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}=2\pi\nu_{p}/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}$, and electric field phasor
L91: 
L92:  | $${\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},2\pi\nu_{p})=\frac{\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\,\exp(ik_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}z)}{N}\,{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}.$$  |  | (7)
L93: Following Lorenz–Mie theory [cite17†3 , cite18†4 ], ${\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})$ is expressed in terms of the vector spherical harmonics ${\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ and ${\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ as
L94:  | $${\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)\right]\Big\},$$  |  | (8)
L95: 
L96: where the coefficients
L97:  | $$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{A}_{emnp}^{(1)}=-2n(n+1)i^{n}a_{p}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\delta_{m1}\\
L98: \mathcal{A}_{omnp}^{(1)}=2n(n+1)i^{n}a_{s}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\delta_{m1}\end{array}\right\}$$  |  | (9)
L99: 
L100: with $\mathcal{B}_{emnp}^{(1)}=-i\mathcal{A}_{omnp}^{(1)}$ and $\mathcal{B}_{omnp}^{(1)}=i\mathcal{A}_{emnp}^{(1)}$, $\delta_{mm^{\prime}}$ is the Kronecker delta, and
L101: 
L102:  | $$D_{mn}=(2-\delta_{m0})\frac{2n+1}{4n(n+1)}\frac{(n-m)!}{(n+m)!}$$  |  | (10)
L103: is a normalization factor. Explicit expressions for ${\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ and ${\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ are available elsewhere [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ].
L104: ### 2.3 Scattered signal
L105: 
L106: Each plane-wave component with electric field phasor ${\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})$ gives rise to a scattered field with electric field phasor
L107:  | $${\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}{\bf M}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}{\bf N}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)\right]\Big\}$$  |  | (11)
L108: for $r>b$. Explicit expressions for ${\bf M}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ and ${\bf N}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)$ are also available elsewhere [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ].
L109: Our task now is to derive expressions for the coefficients $\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}$ and $\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}$. We do so by enforcing boundary conditions on the surfaces $r=a$ and $r=b$. Since the coated sphere is made of isotropic chiral materials, we use the left- and right-handed vector spherical Beltrami functions [cite22†8 , cite26†12 ]
L110:  | $$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf L}^{(\ell,g)}_{smnp}({\bf r})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[{\bf M}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{L}_{\ell p}{\bf r})+{\bf N}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{L}_{\ell p}{\bf r})\right]\\
L111: {\bf R}^{(\ell,g)}_{smnp}({\bf r})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[{\bf M}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{R}_{\ell p}{\bf r})-{\bf N}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{R}_{\ell p}{\bf r})\right]\end{array}\right\},$$  |  | (12)
L112: 
L113: with $\ell\in\left\{1,2\right\}$, $g\in\left\{1,3\right\}$, and the wavenumbers
L114:  | $$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}k^{L}_{\ell p}=k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}\left[\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}\sqrt{\mu_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}+\kappa_{\ell}(\omega_{p})\right]\\
L115: k^{R}_{\ell p}=k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}\left[\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}\sqrt{\mu_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}-\kappa_{\ell}(\omega_{p})\right]\end{array}\right\}\,.$$  |  | (13)
L116: 
L117: Consequently the internal electric field phasor for the spectral component of frequency $\nu_{p}$ may expressed as
L118:  | $${\bf E}_{{2}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\{1,3\}}\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{C}_{smnp}^{(g)}{\bf L}_{smnp}^{(2,g)}({\bf r})+\mathcal{D}_{smnp}^{(g)}{\bf R}_{smnp}^{(2,g)}({\bf r})\right]\Big\}$$  |  | (14)
L119: 
L120: in the shell $b>r>a$ and
L121:  | $${\bf E}_{{1}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{E}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf L}_{smnp}^{(1,1)}({\bf r})+\mathcal{F}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf R}_{smnp}^{(1,1)}({\bf r})\right]\Big\}$$  |  | (15)
L122: 
L123: in the core $r<a$.
L124: The coefficients $\mathcal{C}_{smnp}^{{(g)}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{smnp}^{{(g)}}$ in Eq. (cite37†14 ) as well as the coefficients $\mathcal{E}_{smnp}^{{(1)}}$ and $\mathcal{F}_{smnp}^{{(1)}}$ in Eq. (cite38†15 ) are determined by imposing the standard electromagnetic boundary conditions at $r=a$ and $r=b$, and exploiting the orthogonalities of the trigonometric functions and associated Legendre functions [cite22†8 ]. Thereby, the scattered-field coefficients are related to the incident-field coefficients as
L125:  | $$\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_{smnp}\\
L126: \mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}{c}_{np}&{d}_{np}\\
L127: {d}_{np}&{e}_{np}\end{matrix}\right)\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(1)}\\
L128: \mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(1)}\end{matrix}\right).$$  |  | (16)
L129: Explicit expressions for the matrix components ${c}_{np}$, ${d}_{np}$, and ${e}_{np}$ are provided in Appendix 1. Parenthetically,in the case of a homogeneous isotropic chiral sphere, compact expressions for ${c}_{np}$, ${d}_{np}$, and ${e}_{np}$ are available elsewhere [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ].
L130: 
L131: At sufficiently great distances from the coated chiral sphere, the electric phasor for each spectral component of the scattered field may be approximated as
L132:  | $${\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})\approx{\bf F}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\dfrac{\exp(i\,{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}}r)}{r},$$  |  | (17)
L133: 
L134: where the $r$-independent vector
L135: 
L136:  | $${{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}}\Big[{L}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\frac{\hat{{\bf u}}_{\theta}+i\hat{{\bf u}}_{\phi}}{\sqrt{2}}+{R}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\frac{\hat{{\bf u}}_{\theta}-i\hat{{\bf u}}_{\phi}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big]\,.$$  |  | (18)
L137: The complex amplitudes of the left- and right-handed components of $k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)$ are
L138: 
L139:  | $$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{L}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\dfrac{\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)-i\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)}{\sqrt{2}}\\
L140: {R}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\dfrac{\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)+i\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}\right\}\,,$$  |  | (19)
L141: 
L142: with
L143:  | $$\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\bigg\{(-i)^{n}D_{mn}\left[-i\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)\right]\bigg\}$$  |  | (20)
L144: 
L145: and
L146:  | $$\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\bigg\{(-i)^{n}D_{mn}\left[i\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)\right]\bigg\}\,.$$  |  | (21)
L147: 
L148: Explicit expressions for the orientation-dependent coefficients $f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)$ and $g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)$ are available elsewhere [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ].
L149: Finally, the electric field $\tilde{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},t)$ of the scattered signal in the far zone emerges as
L150: 
L151:  | $$\tilde{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},t)\approx\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}{{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\dfrac{\exp\left[i\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}r-\omega_{p}t\right)\right]}{r}\right\}\,.$$  |  | (22)
L152: ### 2.4 Energy efficiencies
L153: 
L154: The presence of the coated chiral sphere results in extinction of the incident signal. This extinction is attributable to both scattering by the sphere and absorption by the chiral materials within the sphere.
L155: 
L156: In terms of each spectral component of frequency $\nu_{p}$ belonging to the incident signal, the extinction power efficiency is given by [cite27†13 , cite28†14 ]
L157:  | $${Q}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\nu_{p})=-\frac{2}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}^{2}b^{2}}{\mathrm{Re}}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(2n+1)(c_{np}+e_{np})\right]-4\frac{{\mathrm{Im}}\left(a_{s}a_{p}^{*}\right)}{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(2n+1)d_{np}\right]\right\}$$  |  | (23)
L158: 
L159: and the total power scattering efficiency by
L160:  | $\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt{Q}_{\mathrm{sca}}(\nu_{p})=\frac{2}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}^{2}b^{2}}\Bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(2n+1)\left[{|c_{np}|^{2}+|e_{np}|^{2}+2|d_{np}|^{2}}\right]$  |
L161:  | $\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt-4\frac{\mathrm{Im}(a_{s}a_{p}^{*})}{|{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}}|^{2}}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\left[(2n+1)(c_{np}+e_{np})d_{np}^{*}\right]\right\}\Bigg)\,.$  |  | (24)
L162: The power absorption efficiency can then be obtained as ${Q}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\nu_{p})={Q}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\nu_{p})-{Q}_{\mathrm{sca}}(\nu_{p})$.
L163: Whereas ${Q}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}(\nu_{p})$ formulated in terms of the time-averaged powers of the incident and the scattered waves are appropriate for the scattering of time-harmonic fields [cite18†4 , cite39†21 ], analogous quantities for time-dependent fields require temporal integration. Thus, the energy extinction efficiency, total energy scattering efficiency, and the energy absorption efficiency are given as [cite40†22 ]
L164:  | $${\tilde{Q}}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}=\frac{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}t_{\mathrm{s}}}{2U_{\mathrm{inc}}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}\left[{Q}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}(\nu_{p})|\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})|^{2}\right]\,,$$  |  | (25)
L165: 
L166: where
L167:  | $$U_{\mathrm{inc}}=\frac{1}{{2}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t={\frac{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}{2\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}}$$  |  | (26)
L168: 
L169: is the energy density of the incident signal.
L170: ## 3 Numerical results
L171: In order to illustrate the theory developed in §cite6†2 with some numerical results, we set the constitutive parameters $\varepsilon_{1}=\mu_{1}\equiv 1$ and $\kappa_{1}\equiv 0$, thereby making the core vacuous [cite22†8 ]. For the shell, we chose a composite material for which the constitutive parameters $\varepsilon_{2}$, $\mu_{2}$, and $\kappa_{2}$ had been experimentally determined for $\nu=\omega/2\pi\in[7.0,12.2]$ GHz [cite34†20 ].
L172: The real and imaginary parts of $\varepsilon_{2}$, $\mu_{2}$, and $\kappa_{2}$ are plotted against $\nu$ in Fig. cite41†1 . Clearly, the chosen material 2 is quite dissipative.
L173: cite42†Image: Refer to caption Figure 1: Real and imaginary parts of (a) $\varepsilon_{2}$, (b) $\mu_{2}$, and (c) $\kappa_{2}$ plotted as functions of $\nu$.
L174: With $\nu_{\mathrm{c}}=9.6$ GHz chosen as the carrier frequency, the standard deviation $\sigma$ of the Gaussian pulse was fixed equal to $3/\nu_{\mathrm{c}}$ and the outer radius $b$ of the coated chiral sphere to $5\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}$. The series in Eqs. (cite43†8 ), (cite44†11 ), (cite37†14 ), and (cite38†15 ) were truncated so that $n\in[1,\bar{N}]$ rather than $n\in[1,\infty)$.
L175: Calculations were then carried out for increasing values of $\bar{N}$ such that the constraints $\Delta_{\rm ext}\leq 1\times 10^{-10}$ and $\Delta_{\rm sca}\leq 1\times 10^{-10}$ were satisfied, where
L176:  | $$\Delta_{\rm ext,sca}=\left|1-\frac{{\tilde{Q}}_{\rm ext,sca}^{[\bar{N}]}}{{\tilde{Q}}_{\rm ext,sca}^{[\bar{N}+1]}}\right|$$  |  | (27)
L177: 
L178: and ${\tilde{Q}}_{\rm ext,sca}^{[\bar{N}]}$ denotes the value of ${\tilde{Q}}_{\rm ext,sca}$ calculated with the truncated series. A sample period of $t_{\mathrm{s}}=0.1/\nu_{\mathrm{c}}$ was found to be adequate. Also, convergence was achieved with $\bar{N}\leq 50$, with smaller values of $\bar{N}$ adequate for smaller $b$.
L179: In the interests of generality, let us present results for an elliptically polarized carrier wave specified by $a_{s}=1/\sqrt{5}$ V m^{-1} and $a_{p}=2ia_{s}$. With the core radius $a=4.5\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}$, computations were performed for $t\in(0,300\sigma]$.
L180: Figure cite45†2 presents the time traces of the left- and right-handed components of $\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}(\pm 30b\hat{{\bf u}}_{z},t)$ vs. $t$. Figure cite45†2 (a1) isolates the incident signal at ${\bf r}=-30b\hat{{\bf u}}_{z}$, Fig. cite45†2 (b1) the backscattered signal at the same location, and Fig. cite45†2 (c1) the forward-scattered signal at ${\bf r}=30b\hat{{\bf u}}_{z}$.
