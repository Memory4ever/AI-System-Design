L27: # Ground-state properties of superheavy $Z=122$ isotopes within the deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum
L28: Jin-Hong Zhuang Affiliation: Mathematics and Physics Department, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China    Zhen-Hua Zhang Email: zhzhang@ncepu.edu.cn Affiliation: Mathematics and Physics Department, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China Affiliation: Hebei Key Laboratory of Physics and Energy Technology, North China Electric Power University, Baoding 071000, China    Yuan-Yuan Wang Affiliation: Mathematics and Physics Department, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China Affiliation: Hebei Key Laboratory of Physics and Energy Technology, North China Electric Power University, Baoding 071000, China    Cong Pan Affiliation: Department of Physics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241000, China    Kai-Yuan Zhang Affiliation: Institute of Nuclear Physics and Chemistry, China Academy of Engineering Physics, Mianyang, Sichuan 621900, China    Huan-Yu Zhang Affiliation: Mathematics and Physics Department, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China    Yu Sun Affiliation: Mathematics and Physics Department, North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, China

L30: ###### Abstract
L31: The ground-state properties of superheavy $Z=122$ isotopes are investigated using the deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DRHBc). Bulk properties, including binding energies, Fermi energies, nucleon separation energies, quadrupole deformations, and root-mean-square radii, are calculated. The results are compared with those obtained from the relativistic continuum Hartree-Bogoliubov (RCHB) theory.
L32: By examining the dependence on the angular-momentum cutoff and the effects of triaxial and octupole deformations, a strategy for determining the ground states is suggested. Furthermore, based on an analysis of the Fermi and nucleon separation energies, the proton and neutron drip lines for $Z=122$ isotopes are determined within both the DRHBc and RCHB frameworks. The possible magic numbers $N=184$, 258, and 350 are also suggested.
L33: Finally, the evolution of single-particle levels, deformation, charge and neutron radii as well as average pairing gaps with increasing neutron number, is discussed.
L34: ## I Introduction
L35: The synthesis of new superheavy elements is one of the most important research frontiers in nuclear physics, as it helps us to address fundamental questions regarding the boundaries of the nuclear chart and the limits of charge within atomic nuclei. Experimentally, significant progress has been made in the synthesis of superheavy elements. To date, those with atomic numbers $Z\leq 118$ have been synthesized through cold and hot fusion reactions cite14†Hofmann and Münzenberg 2000 ; cite15†Morita et al.
L36: 2004 ; cite16†Oganessian 2007 ; cite17†Oganessian et al. 2010 ; cite18†Zhou 2014 ; cite19†Zhou 2017 ; cite20†Jiang et al. 2025 ; cite21†Huang et al. 2025a . Currently, the synthesis of even heavier new superheavy elements, specifically in the range of $Z=119$-122, represents a highly competitive frontier in nuclear research. Most of these experiments are concentrating on $Z=119$ and $120$. Despite extensive attempts, no experiment has yet succeeded in synthesizing these new elements cite22†Oganessian et al.
L37: 2009 ; cite23†Hofmann et al. 2016a ; cite24†Hofmann et al. 2016b ; cite25†Khuyagbaatar et al. 2020 ; cite26†Sakai et al. 2022 ; cite27†Tanaka et al. 2022 ; cite28†Gan et al. 2022 ; cite29†Gates et al. 2024 ; cite30†Zhang et al. 2025a .
L38: Understanding the structure of the superheavy elements is quite helpful for synthesizing them. Theoretically, extensive investigations have been conducted using various models, such as macroscopic-microscopic models cite31†Sobiczewski et al. 1966 ; cite32†Meldner 1966 ; cite33†Nilsson et al. 1968 ; cite34†Nilsson et al. 1969 ; cite35†Mosel and Greiner 1969 ; cite36†Möller and Nix 1994 ; cite37†Mo et al. 2014 ; cite38†Wang et al. 2014 and self-consistent mean-field models cite39†Bender et al.
L39: 2003 including non-relativistic cite40†Ćwiok et al. 1994 ; cite41†Ćwiok et al. 1996 ; cite42†Ćwiok et al. 1999 ; cite43†Ćwiok et al. 2005 ; cite44†Robledo et al. 2018 , and relativistic cite45†Lalazissis et al. 1996 ; cite46†Zhang et al. 2005 ; cite47†Afanasjev et al. 2003 ; cite48†Li et al. 2014 ; cite49†Agbemava et al. 2015 ; cite50†Afanasjev et al. 2018 ; cite51†Agbemava et al. 2019 ; cite52†Agbemava and Afanasjev 2021 ; cite53†Taninah et al. 2020 density functional theories.
L40: As for the superheavy region, there is little experimental information. Therefore, the extrapolation power of these models is very important.
L41: The deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DRHBc) cite54†Zhou et al. 2010 ; cite55†Li et al. 2012a ; cite56†Li et al. 2012b ; cite57†Zhang et al. 2020 ; cite58†Pan et al. 2022 , which can concurrently take into account pairing correlations, continuum effects, and degrees of freedom in deformation, is one of the most potent models.
L42: It can be viewed as the deformed counterpart of the relativistic continuum Hartree-Bogoliubov (RCHB) theory cite59†Meng and Ring 1998 ; cite60†Meng and Ring 1996 ; cite61†Meng 1998 ; cite62†Meng et al. 2006 , and it has demonstrated remarkable ability in the satisfactory description of ground-state properties through powerful explorations. Based on the DRHBc, numerous interesting nuclear phenomena have been investigated, such as the halo phenomena cite54†Zhou et al. 2010 ; cite63†Meng and Zhou 2015 ; cite64†Sun et al.
L43: 2018 ; cite65†Sun et al. 2020 ; cite66†Sun 2021 ; cite67†Sun and Zhou 2021 ; cite68†Zhong et al. 2022 ; cite69†Zhang et al. 2023a ; cite70†Wang et al. 2024a ; cite71†Zhang et al. 2024a ; cite72†Pan et al. 2024 ; cite73†An et al. 2024 ; cite74†Zhang and Lu 2025 ; cite75†Papakonstantinou et al. 2025 , the shape evolution and shape coexistence cite76†Kim et al. 2022 ; cite77†Choi et al. 2022 ; cite78†Guo et al. 2023 ; cite79†Zhang et al. 2023b ; cite80†Mun et al. 2024 ; cite81†Mun et al. 2025a , the shell evolution cite82†Zhang et al. 2023c ; cite83†Zheng et al.
L44: 2024 ; cite84†Zhang et al. 2024b ; cite85†Huang et al. 2025b , fission barriers cite86†Zhang et al. 2024c , $\alpha$-decay half-lives cite87†Choi et al. 2024 ; cite88†Mun et al. 2025b , the one-proton emission cite89†Xiao et al. 2023 ; cite90†Lu et al. 2024 , etc. In addition, the ground-state properties of those nuclei in the whole nuclear chart have been calculated and the corresponding nuclear mass tables based on the RCHB and DRHBc theories have been established cite91†Xia et al. 2018 ; cite92†Zhang et al. 2022a ; cite93†Guo et al.
L45: 2024 .
L46: In Ref. cite93†Guo et al. 2024 , the ground-state properties of even-$Z$ nuclei have been calculated systematically up to $Z=120$, which is predicted to be a proton magic number. As discussed in Ref. cite94†Indelicato and Karpov 2013 , the upper limit of the nuclear charge number may extend to $Z=173$ according to quantum electrodynamics calculations. Therefore, it is essential to extend theoretical investigations beyond $Z=120$ within the DRHBc framework.
L47: The DRHBc Mass Table Collaboration is now working on the whole nuclear chart including the superheavy nuclei with $121\leq Z\leq 136$. In this work, we present the calculations for the ground-state properties across the entire $Z=122$ isotopic chain using the DRHBc theory, including binding energies, Fermi energies, nucleon separation energies, quadrupole deformations, and root-mean-square (rms) radii, etc.
L48: Note that IUPAC has already provided a temporary name “Unbibium” and symbol “Ubb” for the element with $Z=122$ cite95†Chatt 1979 .
L49: This paper is organized as follows. Section cite7†II provides a brief introduction to the framework of DRHBc. The numerical details of the calculations are described in Sec. cite8†III . In Sec. cite9†IV , we present the ground-state properties of nuclei in the $Z=122$ isotopic chain obtained from the DRHBc calculations, with corresponding RCHB results included for comparison. Finally, a brief summary is given in Sec. cite10†V .
L50: ## II Theoretical framework
L51: 
L52: The details of the DRHBc can be found in Refs. cite56†Li et al. 2012b ; cite96†Chen et al. 2012 ; cite57†Zhang et al. 2020 ; cite58†Pan et al. 2022 . Here we briefly present its formalism. The relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov (RHB) equation reads cite97†Kucharek and Ring 1991 L53:  | $\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{D}-\lambda_{\tau}&\Delta\\
L54: -\Delta^{*}&-h_{D}^{*}+\lambda_{\tau}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}U_{k}\\
L55: V_{k}\end{array}\right)=E_{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{k}\\
L56: V_{k}\end{array}\right),$  |
L57: 
L58: where $\lambda_{\tau}$ is the Fermi energy for neutrons or protons ($\tau=n$ or $p$), and $E_{k}$ and ($U_{k}$, $V_{k}$)^{T} are the quasiparticle energy and wave function, respectively. $h_{D}$ is the Dirac Hamiltonian,
L59:  | $\displaystyle h_{D}(\bm{r})=\bm{\alpha}\cdot\bm{p}+V(\bm{r})+\beta[M+S(\bm{r})],$  |  | (8)
L60: 
L61: where $M$ is the nucleon mass, $S(\bm{r})$ and $V(\bm{r})$ are the scalar and vector potentials, respectively. $\Delta$ is the pairing potential,
L62: 
L63:  | $\displaystyle\Delta\left(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\right)=V^{\rm pp}\left(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\right)\kappa\left(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\right),$  |  | (9)
L64: where $\kappa$ is the pairing tensor cite98†Ring and Schuck 1980 and $V^{\rm pp}$ is the pairing interaction in the particle-particle channel. Here a density-dependent zero-range pairing force is adopted,
L65: 
L66:  | $\displaystyle V^{\rm pp}\left(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\right)=V_{0}\frac{1}{2}\left(1-P^{\sigma}\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{\rho\left(\bm{r}_{1}\right)}{\rho_{\rm sat}}\right),$  |  | (10)
L67: in which $V_{0}$ is the pairing strength, $\rho_{\rm sat}=0.152$ fm^{-3} denotes the saturation density of nuclear matter, and $\frac{1}{2}\left(1-P^{\sigma}\right)$ represents the projector for the spin $S=0$ component in the pairing channel.
L68: 
L69: In the DRHBc, since axial and reflection symmetries are assumed, the pairing tensor, various densities and potentials are expanded in terms of the Legendre polynomials,
L70:  | $\displaystyle f(\bm{r})=\sum\limits_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(r)P_{\lambda}(\cos\theta),\quad\lambda=0,2,4,\ldots$  |  | (11)
L71: 
L72: The RHB equations are solved in the Dirac Woods-Saxon (DWS) basis cite99†Zhou et al. 2003 ; cite100†Zhang et al. 2022b , which can provide equivalent description to coordinate-space solutions and appropriately describe the large spatial extension of weakly bound nuclei.
L73: For an odd-$A$ nucleus, one needs to further take into consideration the blocking effect for the unpaired single proton or neutron cite55†Li et al. 2012a . The equal filling approximation is adopted to deal with the blocking effects in the DRHBc cite58†Pan et al. 2022 .
L74: ## III Numerical details
L75: In this work, the relativistic density functional PC-PK1 cite101†Zhao et al. 2010 is adopted. The DWS basis is constructed in a box of $R_{\rm{box}}=20$ fm, with a mesh of $\Delta r=0.1$ fm. For the basis space, the angular momentum cutoff is chosen as $J_{\rm max}=31/2~\hbar$, and the energy cutoff in the Fermi sea is $E_{\mathrm{cut}}=300$ MeV. The maximum expansion order in Eq. (cite102†11 ) is $\lambda_{\rm max}=12$, which is sufficient for our study.
L76: For the particle-particle channel, the pairing strength $V_{0}=-300$ MeV$\cdot$fm^{3} in Eq. (cite103†10 ), and the pairing window is chosen as 100 MeV. The examinations for the above numerical cutoffs and the pairing parameters have been carried out for $Z=134$ and 135 isotopes cite104†Wang et al. 2024b . For comparison, the RCHB calculations are also performed for all the $Z=122$ isotopes considered. The corresponding numerical details can be found in Ref. cite91†Xia et al. 2018 .
L77: To examine the effects of octupole and triaxial degrees of freedom on the potential energy curves (PECs), the calculations with multi-dimensionally constrained covariant density functional theory (MDC-CDFT) are also carried out for ^{384}Ubb as an example. For comprehensive details on the MDC-CDFT framework, see Refs. cite105†Lu et al. 2012 ; cite106†Lu et al. 2014 ; cite107†Zhou 2016 ; cite108†Zhao et al. 2017 . In these calculations, the density functional PC-PK1 is also adopted.
L78: The Dirac equation is solved by an expansion in the axially deformed harmonic oscillator basis with $N_{f}=20$ major shells, which is enough for the superheavy nuclei. Pairing correlations are taken into account in the BCS approximation with a finite range separable pairing force cite109†Tian et al. 2009 . The pairing strength and effective range are taken as $G/G_{0}=1.1$ and $a=0.644$ fm, with $G_{0}=728$ MeV$\cdot$fm^{3}.
L79: The pairing strength is obtained by reproducing the odd-even differences in binding energies of the actinides and superheavy nuclei cite110†Zhao et al. 2015 ; cite111†Meng et al. 2020 ; cite112†Wang et al. 2021 ; cite113†Deng and Zhou 2023 . Both in the DRHBc and MDC-CDFT, the microscopic center-of-mass correction is considered cite114†Bender et al. 2000 ; cite115†Long et al. 2004 ; cite116†Zhao et al. 2009 .
L80: ## IV Results and discussion
L81: 
L82: The nuclei in the $Z=122$ isotopic chain with neutron number from $N=171$ to 352 are calculated by the DRHBc. Bulk properties for the ground states, including binding energies, Fermi energies, separation energies, quadrupole deformations, and rms radii, are obtained. The results are also compared with those obtained by the spherical RCHB calculations.
L83: cite117†Image: Refer to caption Figure 1: The evolution of PECs for the $Z=122$ isotopic chain as a function of neutron number, from $N=172$ to $N=352$, obtained from constrained DRHBc calculations using the density functional PC-PK1. The calculations are performed at intervals of $\Delta N=10$. In each panel, the PECs for ^{294}122 ($N=172$) and ^{394}122 ($N=272$) are renormalized to the energy of their respective ground states (indicated by red solid triangles).
L84: The remaining PECs are successively shifted upward by 1 MeV for each additional neutron. The ground-state deformations determined from unconstrained DRHBc calculations are marked by solid red triangles on the corresponding curves.
L85: Figure cite118†1 presents the evolution of the PECs for the $Z=122$ isotopic chain, calculated from $N=172$ to $N=352$ using constrained DRHBc calculations at intervals of $\Delta N=10$. The ground-state deformations from unconstrained calculations are indicated by solid red triangles. These ground states correspond to the global minima on their respective PECs, validating the self-consistence of the DRHBc calculations. It can be seen that, each PEC typically exhibits several local minima.
L86: According to Ref. cite104†Wang et al. 2024b , an angular momentum cutoff of $J_{\rm max}=31/2~\hbar$ in the DRHBc has been verified as sufficient for nuclei with proton numbers $Z=122$-136.
L87: That study also recommended that, with $J_{\rm max}=31/2~\hbar$, only the lowest minimum in the small-deformation region $|\beta_{2}|<0.3$ can be reliably identified as the ground state, whereas the lowest minimum at larger deformations $|\beta_{2}|>0.3$ should be checked carefully, because the PECs in the large-deformation region vary significantly as $J_{\text{max}}$ increases.
L88: In the present work, numerous minima appear at large oblate ($\beta_{2}\sim-0.4$ to $-0.5$) and prolate ($\beta_{2}>0.5$) deformations. It is therefore essential to determine which minimum in each PEC represents the actual ground state.
L89: cite119†Image: Refer to caption Figure 2: The PECs of ^{384}Ubb obtained by the DRHBc calculations with the angular momentum cutoff $J_{\rm max}=23/2~\hbar$ (black solid squares), 31/2 $\hbar$ (blue open circles) and 33/2 $\hbar$ (red solid triangles).
L90: The nucleus ^{384}Ubb, which exhibits several minima in its PEC, is taken as an example to show the strategy for determining the ground state. Figure cite120†2 shows the PECs of ^{384}Ubb calculated using the DRHBc with different angular momentum cutoffs: $J_{\text{max}}=23/2~\hbar$ (black solid squares), $31/2~\hbar$ (blue open circles), and $33/2~\hbar$ (red solid triangles). Note that for $J_{\text{max}}=23/2~\hbar$, the pairing interaction strength is set to $V_{0}=-325$ MeV$\cdot$fm^{3} cite57†Zhang et al.
L91: 2020 . It can be seen that at $J_{\text{max}}=23/2~\hbar$, only two minima are present: one spherical minimum and a very shallow prolate minimum around $\beta_{2}\sim 0.25$. When $J_{\text{max}}$ increases to $31/2~\hbar$, the PEC drops significantly in both the prolate and oblate large-deformation regions. An obvious oblate minimum appears near $\beta_{2}\sim-0.45$, while the prolate minimum shifts to $\beta_{2}\sim 0.65$. This behavior is consistent with the findings in Ref. cite104†Wang et al.
L92: 2024b regarding the convergence with respect to $J_{\text{max}}$. In addition, a very flat oblate minimum also appears near $\beta_{2}\sim-0.25$. Further increasing $J_{\text{max}}$ to $33/2~\hbar$ leads to nearly converged PECs across most deformation regions, except for the very large prolate deformation ($\beta_{2}>0.6$) shifted a little (about 0.05). This indicates that an angular momentum cutoff of $J_{\text{max}}=31/2~\hbar$ is sufficient for the present calculations.
L93: Therefore, from the perspective of the axially symmetric and reflection symmetric DRHBc model, the minimum with large oblate deformation $\beta_{2}\sim-0.45$ should be regarded as the ground state, given its lower energy and stability upon further increasing $J_{\text{max}}$. This finding supersedes the previous identification of the minimum within $|\beta_{2}|<0.3$ as the ground state cite104†Wang et al. 2024b .
L94: In contrast, whether the lowest minimum with large prolate deformation ($\beta_{2}>0.5$) corresponds to the ground state remains uncertain since it changes slightly with further increasing $J_{\text{max}}$.
L95: cite121†Image: Refer to caption Figure 3: The PECs of ^{384}Ubb obtained by the DRHBc (blue open circles) and the MDC-CDFT with various self-consistent symmetries imposed: axially symmetric and reflection symmetric (AS-RS) deformation (black solid curve), axially symmetric and reflection asymmetric (AS-RA) deformation (red dashed curve) triaxial and reflection symmetric (TA-RS) deformation (olive dash dotted curve).
L96: The energy at $\beta_{2}=0$ is subtracted in the DRHBc and MDC-CDFT calculations, respectively.
L97: It is well known that for actinides and superheavy nuclei, the inner fission barrier is generally lowered when triaxial deformation is allowed, while the outer barrier is further reduced when octupole deformation is considered cite122†Damgaard et al. 1969 ; cite123†Möller and Nilsson 1970 ; cite124†Girod and Grammaticos 1983 ; cite125†Rutz et al. 1995 ; cite126†Abusara et al. 2010 ; cite86†Zhang et al. 2024c . In some cases, the outer barrier may even disappear after including octupole degrees of freedom.
L98: Therefore, it is important to examine whether the minima identified in Fig. cite120†2 remain stable when triaxial and octupole deformations are taken into account. However, the present DRHBc calculations keep both axial and reflection symmetry. Although a triaxial version of the DRHBc has recently been developed cite127†Zhang et al. 2023d ; cite128†Zhang et al. 2025b , systematic calculations for superheavy nuclei within this framework remain computationally prohibitive.
L99: The MDC-CDFT developed by Lu et al., can break both axial and reflection symmetries cite105†Lu et al. 2012 ; cite106†Lu et al. 2014 . To examine the effects of octupole and triaxial deformations on the energy minima of $Z=122$ isotopes, we still take ^{384}Ubb as an example and calculate its PECs using MDC-CDFT under different self-consistent symmetry constraints, as shown in Fig. cite129†3 .
L100: The black solid curve represents the result with axially symmetric and reflection-symmetric (AS-RS) deformation, the red dashed curve corresponds to the axially symmetric but reflection-asymmetric (AS-RA) case, and the olive dash-dotted curve shows the result with triaxial and reflection-symmetric (TA-RS) deformation. Results obtained from the DRHBc calculations are also plotted as blue open circles for comparison.
L101: For clarity, the energy at $\beta_{2}=0$ has been subtracted in both the DRHBc and MDC-CDFT calculations. It can be seen that the minima corresponding to spherical, oblate, and prolate deformations are essentially the same between the DRHBc and MDC-CDFT (AS-RS) calculations. The differences arise from the different pairing interactions and basis choices employed in these two methods. When reflection symmetry is broken (AS-RA), the prolate minimum near $\beta_{2}\sim 0.7$ disappears.
L102: This indicates that an energy minimum exhibiting very large quadrupole deformation should not be identified as the ground state, despite possessing the lowest energy. When axial symmetry is broken (TA-RS), the barrier around $\beta_{2}\sim 0.2$ is lowered. Nevertheless, these minima still persist, suggesting that the oblate minimum with $\beta_{2}\sim-0.4$ to $-0.5$ should indeed be considered as the ground state if it possesses the lowest energy.
