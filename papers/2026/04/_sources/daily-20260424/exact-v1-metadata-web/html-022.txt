L48: # Magnetar Engines in Broad-lined Type Ic Supernovae and a Unified Picture for Magnetar-powered Stripped-envelope Supernovae

L52: ###### Abstract
L53: We model the multi-band lightcurves of 80 broad-lined Type Ic supernovae (SNe Ic-BL), including 11 associated with long-duration gamma-ray bursts (lGRBs), using a magnetar engine model with ^{56}Ni decay. We find that the data are all consistent with a magnetar central engine, and such a model yields high-quality fits across the sample.
L54: The medians with $1\sigma$ regions of the key parameters are the initial spin period $P_{\rm{i}}\sim 2.04^{+1.84}_{-0.96}\,{\rm{ms}}$, magnetic field $B_{\rm{p}}\sim 3.96^{+3.28}_{-1.40}\times 10^{15}\,{\rm{G}}$, ejecta mass $M_{\rm{ej}}\sim 2.30^{+1.48}_{-1.02}\,M_{\odot}$, and ^{56}Ni mass $M_{\rm{Ni}}\sim 0.18^{+0.14}_{-0.09}\,M_{\odot}$, with strong and statistically significant correlations observed for both $M_{\rm{ej}}-P_{\rm{i}}$ (anti-correlation) and $M_{\rm{Ni}}-M_{\rm{ej}}$ (correlation).
L55: Comparing the SN Ic-BL samples with and without lGRB association using fitting parameters, we find no significant difference between them, although the GRB-associated sample is slightly brighter, possibly due to an observational bias (dimmer ones overshone by the afterglow).
L56: Relative to ordinary SNe Ic, SNe Ic-BL have similar ^{56}Ni and ejecta masses, suggesting comparable pre-SN progenitor properties, with differences possibly arising from the presence of a magnetar engine that significantly affects the SN dynamics.
L57: In comparison with other possible magnetar-powered stripped-envelope SNe (SESNe), including Type Ic superluminous SNe (SLSNe Ic) and fast blue optical transients (FBOTs), we confirm a strong universal $M_{\rm{ej}}-P_{\rm{i}}$ correlation, indicating a common origin. SNe Ic-BL and SLSNe Ic have similar ejecta mass distributions, typically $M_{\rm ej}\gtrsim 0.5\,M_{\odot}$, while FBOTs mostly lie below this value.
L58: Differences between SNe Ic-BL and SLSNe Ic may arise from magnetar properties, with SN Ic-BL magnetars rotating faster and having stronger fields. Moreover, the $P_{\rm{i}}-B_{\rm{p}}$ distribution of lGRB magnetars largely overlaps with that of SN Ic-BL magnetars. In connection with binary simulation results, we propose a unified physical classification and progenitor framework for magnetar-powered and ordinary SESNe.

L66: ## I Introduction
L67: Stripped-envelope supernovae (SESNe), including Types IIb, Ib, and Ic SNe (SNe IIb/Ib/Ic), characterized by varying degrees of absence of hydrogen and/or helium features in their spectra [cite43†48 , cite44†119 , cite45†103 , cite46†107 , e.g.,], are believed to originate from massive stars that have lost part or all of their hydrogen-rich and/or helium envelopes before the explosions [cite47†206 , cite48†164 , cite49†43 , cite50†38 , cite51†3 , e.g.,].
L68: Within the SESN population, the diversity in envelope stripping extends to extreme cases such as ultra-stripped SNe (USSNe), typically classified as SNe Ib/Ic, which represent an extreme subclass distinguished by unusually small ejecta masses and are likely produced by progenitors that have undergone extreme mass loss and extensive stripping before explosions [cite52†184 , cite53†185 , cite54†178 ].
L69: Moreover, there exists a distinct class of energetic SNe Ic, with significantly broader spectral lines and higher ejecta velocities than those observed in ordinary SESNe Ic, hence the name “broad-lined Type Ic SNe” [cite55†58 , cite56†77 , cite57†121 , SNe Ic-BL;].
L70: Most SNe Ic-BL are discovered as isolated SN events, while only a small fraction are found in association with high-energy transient counterparts, likely powered by relativistic jets from progenitors [cite58†65 , cite59†60 , $\sim 3-30\%$ of SNe Ic-BL may possibly generate relativistic jets;].
L71: Almost all SNe associated with long gamma-ray bursts (lGRBs) were identified as SNe Ic-BL [see cite60†205 , cite61†221 , for reviews], with famous examples such as GRB 980425/SN 1998bw [cite55†58 , cite56†77 ], GRB 030329/SN 2003dh [cite62†70 ], and GRB 060218/SN 2006aj [cite63†15 , cite64†140 ], except for the ultra-long GRB 111209A, which is associated with the hydrogen-poor superluminous supernova (SLSN Ic) SN 2011kl [cite65†64 ].
L72: In addition to lGRBs, a few fast X-ray transients (FXTs) recently detected by the Einstein Probe [cite66†217 , cite67†216 ] have also been confirmed to be associated with SNe Ic-BL, such as EP240414a/SN 2024gsa [cite68†175 , cite69†13 , cite70†172 , cite71†192 ] and EP250108a/SN 2025kg [cite72†97 , cite73†46 , cite74†150 , cite75†169 ].
L73: The origin of these FXTs remains under active debate, with proposed scenarios including cooling emission of the inner cocoon associated with GRB jets [cite76†224 , cite77†226 , cite78†223 ], weak jet emission [cite68†175 , cite72†97 , cite79†67 , cite80†68 , cite81†159 ], or shock breakout emission [cite73†46 , cite82†170 ].
L74: The explosion energy of SESNe is generally believed to be supplied by the neutrino-driven mechanism, where $\sim 1\%$ of the gravitational binding energy ($\sim 10^{53}\,{\rm erg}$) released during core collapse is deposited into the shock via neutrino heating, finally leading to a typical SN explosion energy of $\sim 10^{51}\,{\rm erg}$ [cite83†9 , cite84†81 , cite85†173 ].
L75: The explosion energies of SNe Ic-BL inferred from the broad spectral lines can reach up to a few $10^{52}\,{\rm erg}$ [cite86†180 , cite87†171 , e.g.,], far exceeding those expected from standard neutrino-driven mechanisms and thus suggesting the presence of additional energy sources. However, the energy sources of SNe Ic-BL remain uncertain.
L76: One of the most popular origin models for lGRBs and SNe Ic-BL is the black hole (BH) collapsar scenario, in which a massive, rapidly rotating Wolf-Rayet star undergoes core collapse to form a spinning BH surrounded by an accretion disk with sufficient angular momentum [cite88†208 , cite89†139 , cite90†109 ].
L77: Such a BH-disk system can potentially drive powerful relativistic jets via the Blandford-Znajek mechanism [cite91†11 ] and simultaneously produce energetic SN Ic-BL explosions [cite92†108 , cite93†222 , cite94†7 , cite95†63 , cite96†162 , cite97†163 , e.g.,].
L78: In addition to the collapsar scenario, the magnetar model has also been extensively proposed, where a newborn, rapidly rotating, strongly magnetized neutron star (a “millisecond magnetar”) powers both lGRBs and SNe Ic-BL by releasing its rotational energy through spin-down processes [cite98†190 , cite99†33 , cite100†87 , cite101†220 , cite102†186 , cite103†219 , cite104†118 , cite105†143 , cite106†197 , cite107†98 , cite108†135 ].
L79: Following the core collapse of progenitors, the SN Ic-BL emission is typically thought to be attributed to heating from the radioactive decay of ^{56}Ni and its daughter ^{56}Co synthesized in the explosion [cite109†31 ]. However, it has been found that the classical “Arnett-like” one-dimensional SN model [cite110†6 ], which considers only the radioactive heating of centrally located ^{56}Ni, fails to reproduce both the peak and the linear decline phase of the SN Ic-BL lightcurves simultaneously.
L80: Specifically, the ^{56}Ni inferred from the linear decline phase (i.e., ^{56}Ni tail) always predicts a relatively lower peak luminosity than that observed in SNe Ic-BL [cite111†161 , cite112†45 , cite113†1 , cite114†207 , cite115†166 , cite116†152 , cite117†153 , e.g.,].
L81: To address this issue, [cite118†110 ] proposed that the SN Ic-BL ejecta may consist of two components with different velocities and ^{56}Ni masses, with the outer component shaping the lightcurve near the peak and the dense inner component governing the late-time linear decline. Similarly, [cite86†180 ] and [cite74†150 ] suggested that reproducing the observed lightcurves of SNe Ic-BL requires strong ^{56}Ni mixing within the ejecta.
L82: Beyond adjustments to the geometry and distribution of the ejecta, models with an additional long-lived central energy source have also been actively considered, most commonly a magnetar, to explain the excess peak luminosity relative to that predicted by the ^{56}Ni tail [cite112†45 , cite113†1 , cite117†153 , e.g.,].
L83: Using hybrid models that incorporate both a magnetar and ^{56}Ni, the bolometric lightcurves of SNe Ic-BL, both associated and unassociated with GRBs, have been reproduced with better fits than the ^{56}Ni-only scenario [cite119†19 , cite120†198 , cite106†197 , cite113†1 , e.g.,].
L84: The magnetars powering SNe Ic-BL are typically inferred to have initial spin periods of $\gtrsim 10\,{\rm ms}$, corresponding to initial rotational energies of $\lesssim 10^{50}\,{\rm erg}$, with magnetic field strengths of $\sim 10^{14}-10^{16}\,{\rm G}$ [see also cite121†90 , cite122†91 , for similar magnetar parameters derived by modeling SNe Ic-BL with pure magnetar central engines].
L85: However, this leads to an “energy puzzle” for SNe Ic-BL: the measured explosion energies, which can even reach up to a few $10^{52}\,{\rm erg}$, always far exceed the energies provided by both magnetar central engines and neutrino-driven mechanisms. If the measured explosion energies of SNe Ic-BL are primarily supplied by magnetar injection, the initial spin periods must instead be on the order of a few milliseconds.
L86: This inconsistency indicates that current lightcurve fittings for SNe Ic-BL in the literature may not accurately reflect the underlying magnetar parameters and ejecta properties, with the resulting “energy puzzle” potentially arising from at least three factors: (1) fitting the bolometric lightcurves provides limited constraints on SN parameters, lacking information from ejecta velocities and temperature evolution; (2) the initial ejecta kinetic energy is often treated as a free parameter, although it may, in fact, be set by neutrino-driven mechanisms; (3) treating the initial kinetic energy as a free parameter can lead to models that attribute most of the magnetar rotational energy to the peak emission, whereas in reality most of it is converted into ejecta kinetic energy, thereby underestimating the true initial rotational energy.
L87: These considerations highlight the need to investigate how lightcurve fitting of SNe Ic-BL can more accurately constrain their magnetar parameters and ejecta properties.
L88: Beyond SNe Ic-BL, there also exists a diverse population of extensively studied transients potentially originating from the core-collapse of massive stripped-envelope stars that may be driven by magnetar central engines. A prominent example is the class of SLSNe Ic, a population of intrinsically bright SNe with lightcurve luminosities and total radiated energies that always exceed those of ordinary SNe by a factor of $\sim 10-100$ [cite123†149 , cite124†56 , cite125†57 ].
L89: While the extremely high luminosities and radiated energies of SLSNe Ic require a ^{56}Ni mass far exceeding what is typically produced in ordinary core-collapse SNe, these events can be readily explained by the heating of ejecta from a magnetar central engine [cite126†84 ], making it the most widely accepted scenario to date.
L90: Moreover, fast blue optical transients (FBOTs) are a class of fast-evolving, very blue, and luminous transients [cite127†41 , cite128†148 , cite129†76 ] whose progenitors and power sources remain actively debated in the literature. The fact that most FBOTs are observed exclusively in star-forming host galaxies suggests a likely core-collapse origin connected to massive stars [cite127†41 , cite128†148 , cite130†204 ].
L91: However, similar to SLSNe Ic, the peak luminosities of most FBOTs significantly exceed the maximum achievable luminosity of a standard ^{56}Ni-powered core-collapse SNe, indicating that an additional power source is required [cite129†76 ].
L92: Given that FBOT host galaxies share similar star-formation rates and metallicities with those of SLSNe Ic, SNe Ic-BL and lGRB hosts [cite130†204 ], FBOTs may be physically related to SLSNe Ic and lGRBs in terms of their progenitors and power sources (i.e., magnetar central engine).
L93: Indeed, the magnetar engine model provides a more viable explanation for the observational properties of at least a subset of FBOTs than other alternative scenarios [cite131†113 , cite132†200 , cite133†100 , cite134†95 , cite135†94 , cite136†42 , e.g.,].
L94: While statistical studies using the magnetar engine model have been extensively explored for SLSNe Ic [cite137†215 , cite138†102 , cite139†131 , cite140†26 , e.g.,] and FBOTs [cite133†100 , e.g.,], a similar comprehensive statistical analysis for SNe Ic-BL has not yet been systematically explored. Addressing this gap is crucial for understanding the potential connections and distinctions among these diverse transients, particularly in terms of their magnetar properties and progenitor origins.
L95: Thanks to the recent wide-field and high-sensitivity transient surveys such as iPTF, ZTF, and LSST, an increasing number of SNe Ic-BL have been discovered [cite86†180 , cite87†171 , e.g.,].
L96: In this study, we perform a statistical analysis of SNe Ic-BL using the largest sample available from the literature under a magnetar central engine, aiming to investigate the statistical properties of SNe Ic-BL and their magnetars, and to compare them with ordinary SESNe and other magnetar-powered explosions (lGRBs, SLSNe Ic, and FBOTs). The structure of this work is as follows. Section cite7†II introduces the magnetar-powered model with ^{56}Ni contribution.
L97: In Section cite10†III , we present the observational data and lightcurve fitting. The statistics of the model parameters for SNe Ic-BL, as well as the comparison between SNe Ic-BL associated with lGRBs and those unassociated with lGRBs (abbreviated as GRB-SNe and Non-GRB-SNe, hereafter), are presented in Section cite11†IV . In Section cite17†V , we compare the magnetar and explosion parameters of SNe Ic-BL with those of ordinary SESNe and other types of magnetar-powered SESNe.
L98: The discussion is presented in Section cite22†VI , where we, in particular, provide a possible unified picture of the phenomenological classification and progenitors of magnetar-powered SESNe and ordinary SESNe. The summary and conclusion are given in Section cite29†VII . Here, a standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology of $H_{0}=67.4\,{\rm km}\,{\rm s}^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, $\Omega_{\rm m}=0.315$, and $\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.685$ [cite141†144 ] is adopted.
L99: ## II Modeling
L100: ### II.1 Lightcurves of Magnetar-powered SESNe with ^{56}Ni Contribution
L101: 
L102: Here, we adopt a semi-analytical model [cite126†84 , cite142†75 , cite137†215 , e.g.,] to calculate the emission of magnetar-powered SESNe with ^{56}Ni contribution. The SN bolometric luminosity can be roughly expressed as [cite126†84 ]
L103: 
L104:  | $$L_{\rm SN}\approx\frac{cE_{\rm int}}{R\tau}(1-e^{-\tau}),$$  |  | (1)
L105: where $E_{\rm int}$ is the internal energy of the ejecta, $c$ is the speed of light, $R$ is the radius, and $\tau\approx 3\kappa M_{\rm ej}/4\pi R^{2}$ is the optical depth, with $\kappa\approx 0.07\,{\rm cm}^{2}\,{\rm g}^{-1}$ being the opacity commonly used in modeling SNe Ic-BL [cite118†110 , cite46†107 , cite143†104 , cite86†180 , cite144†4 , cite87†171 , e.g.,] and $M_{\rm ej}$ the ejecta mass.
L106: The radius $R$ can be given by $R=\int_{0}^{t}v_{\rm ej}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}+R_{\star}$, where $v_{\rm ej}$ is the ejecta velocity, $t$ is the time, and $R_{\star}\simeq R_{\odot}$ denotes a typical radius of a Wolf-Rayet star. For $\tau\gg 1$, the above equation reads $L_{\rm SN}=cE_{\rm int}/R\tau$; otherwise, when $\tau\ll 1$, it simplifies to $L_{\rm SN}=cE_{\rm int}/R$.
L107: By considering the energy conservation of the ejecta, the evolution of its internal energy $E_{\rm int}$ over time $t$ can be calculated as
L108: 
L109:  | $$\frac{dE_{\rm int}}{dt}=L_{\rm sd}(t)+L_{\rm rad}(t)-p\frac{dV}{dt}-L_{\rm SN}(t),$$  |  | (2)
L110: where $L_{\rm sd}$ is the energy injection rate from the magnetar’s spin-down, and $L_{\rm rad}$ is the energy injection rate due to the radioactive decay of ^{56}Ni and ^{56}Co. Both the spin-down energy injection and ^{56}Ni are assumed to be centrally located. The term $pdV/dt$ represents the adiabatic cooling of the ejecta, in which the internal energy is used to accelerate the ejecta, where $p=E_{\rm int}/4\pi R^{3}$ is the pressure and $dV/dt=4\pi R^{2}v_{\rm ej}$ is the change rate of volume.
L111: Thus, one can express the dynamical equation as
L112:  | $$\frac{dv_{\rm ej}}{dt}=\frac{4\pi R^{2}p}{M_{\rm ej}}.$$  |  | (3)
L113: 
L114: The initial kinetic energy of the ejecta $E_{\rm kin,i}$ is set by the neutrino-driven mechanism, while the final kinetic energy of the ejecta can be written as $E_{\rm kin,f}=M_{\rm ej}v_{\rm ej,f}^{2}/2$, where $v_{\rm ej,f}$ is the final characteristic ejecta velocity.
L115: We assume that the newborn magnetar formed after the SN explosion can lose its rotational energy via the magnetic dipole spin-down process, with a luminosity of [cite145†136 ]
L116: 
L117:  | $$L_{\rm sd}=\frac{L_{\rm sd,i}}{(1+t/t_{\rm sd})^{2}},$$  |  | (4)
L118: with an initial luminosity of $L_{\rm sd,i}\simeq E_{\rm rot,i}/t_{\rm sd}=B_{\rm p}^{2}R_{\rm mag}^{6}\Omega_{\rm i}^{4}/6c^{3}\simeq 10^{49}(B_{\rm p}/10^{15}\,{\rm G})^{2}(P_{\rm i}/1\,{\rm ms})^{-4}\,{\rm erg}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and a spin-down timescale of $t_{\rm sd}=3c^{3}I_{\rm mag}/B_{\rm p}^{2}R_{\rm mag}^{6}\Omega_{\rm i}^{2}\simeq 2\times 10^{3}(B_{\rm p}/10^{15}\,{\rm G})^{-2}(P_{\rm i}/1\,{\rm ms})^{2}\,{\rm s}$, where $B_{\rm p}$ is the magnetar’s polar magnetic field strength, $E_{\rm rot,i}=I_{\rm mag}\Omega_{\rm i}^{2}/2$ is the initial rotational energy, $\Omega_{\rm i}$ and $P_{\rm i}=2\pi/\Omega_{\rm i}$ are the initial angular frequency and spin period, $I_{\rm mag}\simeq 10^{45}\,{\rm g}\,{\rm cm}^{2}$ is the moment of inertia, and $R_{\rm mag}\simeq 10^{\rm 6}\,{\rm cm}$ is the magnetar’s radius.
L119: The radioactivity luminosity is given by [cite146†127 , cite147†5 ]
