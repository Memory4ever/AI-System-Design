L36: # Three-dimensional time-periodic problem on the Boltzmann equation with external force
L37: 
L38: Renjun Duan Address: Department of Mathematics, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shatin, Hong Kong, P.R. China Email address: rjduan@math.cuhk.edu.hk and Jinkai Ni Address: School of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210093, P.R. China Email address: jinkaini123@gmail.com
L39: 
L40: Date: August 24, 2026
L41: ###### Abstract.
L42: The time-periodic problem on the Boltzmann equation with a given time-periodic external force in the three-dimensional whole space has remained open since it was first studied in [cite24†13 ] for only spatial dimensions not less than five. The goal of this paper is to give an affirmative answer to this problem provided that the external force is sufficiently small in the function space $\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R};\dot{B}^{-3/2}_{2,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{N})$ with $N\geq 4$.
L43: The proof is based on Serrin’s method through studying the global-in-time stability of the Cauchy problem with time-periodic external forces. As a direct consequence, the result also yields the existence and stability of stationary solutions to the physically realistic three-dimensional Boltzmann equation when the external force is time-independent.
L44: ###### Key words and phrases:
L45: 
L46: Boltzmann equations; Time-periodic solution; Global well-posedness; Asymptotic stability; Decay rates.
L47: 
L48: ###### 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
L49: 
L50: 35A01, 35B10, 35B35, 76P05.
L51: ## 1. Introduction
L52: 
L53: The Boltzmann equation for the hard-sphere gas under the influence of an external force in three-dimensional space is expressed as (cf.[cite24†13 ])
L54: 
L55: (1.1)  |  | $\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F+E\cdot\nabla_{v}F=Q(F,F),$  |
L56: where the unknown non-negative function $F=F(t,x,v)$ represents the velocity distribution function of gas particles at time $t\in\mathbb{R}$, position $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$, and velocity $v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$. The given external force field is denoted as $E=E(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ independent of velocity $v$. The bilinear collision operator $Q(\cdot,\cdot)$ for the hard-sphere model takes the form (cf. [cite25†5 ]):
L57:  | $\displaystyle Q(F,G)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{*})\cdot\omega|(F^{\prime}G^{\prime}_{*}-FG_{*}){\rm d}\omega{\rm d}v_{*},$  |
L58: 
L59: where
L60: 
L61:  | $\displaystyle F=F(t,x,v),\quad F^{\prime}=F(t,x,v^{\prime}),\quad G_{*}=G(t,x,v_{*}),\quad G_{*}^{\prime}=G(t,x,v_{*}^{\prime}),$  |
L62: 
L63: with
L64: 
L65:  | $\displaystyle v^{\prime}=v-[(v-v_{*})\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v_{*}^{\prime}=v_{*}+[(v-v_{*})\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.$  |
L66: In this paper, we focus on the existence and stability of time-periodic solutions to the Boltzmann equation (cite26†1.1 ) when the external force is time-periodic of period $T>0$, i.e., $E(t,x)=E(t+T,x)$ for any $t\in\mathbb{R}$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$, which is also applicable to the stationary case if $E$ is further independent of time. This three-dimensional time-periodic problem was left open in [cite24†13 ] where the only case spatial dimensions are not less than five was treated.
L67: For the purpose, we consider the following time-periodic problem:
L68: (1.2)  |  | $$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F_{T}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{T}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{T}=Q(F_{T},F_{T})\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3},\\
L69: &F_{T}(t,x,v)=F_{T}(t+T,x,v)\quad\text{for}\quad(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.$$  |
L70: 
L71: We look for solutions to the time-periodic problem (cite27†1.2 ) around the normalized global Maxwellian $M$, which is defined as
L72:  | $\displaystyle M=M(v)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}.$  |
L73: 
L74: Subsequently, we define the standard perturbation $f(t,x,v)$ with respect to $M$ as
L75: 
L76:  | $\displaystyle F=M+\sqrt{M}f,$  |
L77: 
L78: from which the Boltzmann equation (cite26†1.1 ) for the perturbation $f$ can be reformulated as follows:
L79: 
L80: (1.3)  |  | $\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\mathcal{L}f=\Gamma(f,f)-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{1}{2}E\cdot vf+E\cdot v\sqrt{M},$  |
L81: for $(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}$. In (cite28†1.3 ), the linearized collision operator $\mathcal{L}$ and the nonlinear collision operator $\Gamma$ are given by
L82: 
L83:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}g=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\big[Q(M,\sqrt{M}g)+Q(\sqrt{M}g,M)\big],$  |
L84: 
L85: and
L86: 
L87:  | $\displaystyle\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}Q(\sqrt{M}g_{1},\sqrt{M}g_{2}).$  |
L88: Recall (cf. [cite25†5 ]) that the linearized collision operator $\mathcal{L}$ is non-negative, and its null space is five-dimensional, defined by
L89: 
L90:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\ker\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{span}\big\{1,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},|v|^{2}\big\}\sqrt{M}.$  |
L91: 
L92: For any fixed $(t,x)$, we define $\mathbf{P}$ as the orthogonal projection from $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3})$ onto $\mathcal{N}$. For later use, for any function $f(t,x,v)$, we introduce the macro-micro decomposition as
L93: (1.4)  |  | $\displaystyle f=\mathbf{P}f+\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f,$  |
L94: 
L95: where $\mathbf{P}f$ is called the macro (fluid) part of $f$, and $\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f$ is the micro (non-fluid) part.
L96: There is an extensive literature on the global existence and asymptotic behavior theory of Boltzmann’s Cauchy problem. For the Boltzmann equation without external forces, DiPerna and Lions [cite29†9 ] first established the global existence of renormalized weak solutions to the Cauchy problem with general large initial data. However, the uniqueness of such solutions remains open.
L97: For the initial-boundary value problem under different boundary conditions, Hamdache [cite30†23 ] and Arkeryd-Maslova [cite31†1 ] obtained the global existence of renormalized weak solutions. Desvillettes and Villani [cite32†8 ] obtained the almost exponential convergence result for large-data solutions satisfying some additional regularity assumptions. Subsequently, Gualdani, Mischler, and Mouhot [cite33†17 ] extent the result for attaining optimal exponential convergence.
L98: For classical solutions near the global Maxwellian equilibrium, significant progress has been made on both global well-posedness and time-asymptotic behavior. Ellis and Pinsky [cite34†14 ] developed the spectral analysis of the linearized Boltzmann equation and its fluid-dynamical limits. Based on the spectral analysis theory of the Boltzmann equation and the bootstrap argument, Ukai [cite35†32 ] first established the global existence and exponential decay of strong solutions on the torus.
L99: The corresponding algebraic decay of strong solutions on the whole spaces can be found in [cite36†28 , cite37†33 ]. Moreover, regarding the long time behavior, by combining the compensation function and the energy method, the time decay rates of the Boltzmann equation were investigated in [cite38†25 , cite39†16 ]. Furthermore, there are also extensive results concerning the global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to the Boltzmann equation near vacuum.
L100: Interested readers can refer to [cite40†4 , cite39†16 , cite41†24 ]. On the other hand, to study the global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the Boltzmann equation, Guo [cite42†20 ] proposed a nonlinear energy method based on macro-micro decomposition around the global Maxwellian. Liu-Yang-Yu [cite43†26 ] developed the energy method around local Maxwellians.
L101: The study of time-periodic solutions is a classical and significant issue in both kinetic theory and gas dynamics. Regarding the Navier–Stokes equations, these solutions and their stability characteristics have been comprehensively analyzed in various settings; for instance, refer to Beirão da Veiga [cite44†3 ], Feireisl et al. [cite45†15 ], Tsuda [cite46†31 ], and Valli–Zajaczkowski [cite47†37 ].
L102: On the kinetic aspect, the periodic solutions to the Boltzmann equation driven by time-periodic source terms were investigated in [cite48†35 , cite49†34 ], provided that an extra spatially zero-average condition is imposed on the inhomogeneous source term $S_{0}$ in case of the physically relevant three dimensions. Recently, the authors of this paper with Lei [cite50†11 ] removed this restriction on $S_{0}$ and developed a global dynamic theory in three dimensions.
L103: Nevertheless, for the Boltzmann equation with a time-periodic external force in the whole space, the theory remains far from being complete. As far as we know, the existing result in [cite24†13 ] is applicable in only dimensions $d\geq 5$, and the physically crucial $d=3$ case remains unresolved. The aim of this paper is to address this problem for $d=3$.
L104: Before studying the time-periodic solution of (cite28†1.3 ), we first consider the following Cauchy problem:
L105: 
L106: (1.5)  |  | $$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\mathcal{L}f\\
L107: &\quad=\Gamma(f,f)-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{1}{2}E\cdot vf+E\cdot v\sqrt{M},\quad(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v},\\
L108: &f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)=\frac{F_{0}(x,v)-M}{\sqrt{M}},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}.\end{aligned}\right.$$  |
L109: Similarly to [cite50†11 ], for the low-frequency part, we do not select the general $L^{2}_{x,v}$-norm; instead, we choose the $L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,\infty})$. Our selection of homogeneous Besov spaces $L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,\infty})$ is motivated by [cite51†7 ], which originally noted that $\frac{1}{|x|}\in\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{1/2}$.
L110: To begin with, we show the global existence of strong solutions to the Cauchy problem (cite52†1.5 ) in order to apply Serrin’s approach in [cite53†29 ] for further studying the time-periodic problem (cite27†1.2 ). For later use, we introduce the energy norm $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}^{s,N}}$ as
L111:  | $\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}^{s,N}}:=$  | $\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{N})}+\|\langle v\rangle f\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1})}+\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f\|_{L_{v}^{2}(L^{2})}$  |
L112: (1.6)  |  |  | $\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|\beta|\leq N\\
L113: |\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\end{subarray}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{v}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f\|_{L_{v}^{2}(L^{2})}.$  |
L114: 
L115: The first main result is stated as follows.
L116: ###### Theorem 1.1.
L117: 
L118: Let $F_{0}(x,v)=M+\sqrt{M}f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0$ with
L119: 
L120:  | $$f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N}),\quad\langle v\rangle f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1}),\quad\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f_{0}\in H^{N}_{x,v},$$  |
L121: 
L122: for an integer $N\geq 3$, then there exists a constant $\delta_{0}>0$ such that if the external force $E$ and the initial data $f_{0}$ satisfy
L123: (1.7)  |  | $$\|f_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2},N}}+\|E(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R};\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N})}\leq\delta_{0},$$  |
L124: 
L125: then the Cauchy problem (cite52†1.5 ) admits a unique global solution $f(t,x,v)$ that satisfies $F(t,x,v)=M+\sqrt{M}f(t,x,v)\geq 0$ with
L126:  | $\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,\infty);L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N})\big),\ \langle v\rangle f\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,\infty);L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1})\big),\ \{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,\infty);H^{N}_{x,v}\big),$  |
L127: 
L128: and
L129: (1.8)  |  | $\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2},N}}\leq\,C_{0}\left(\|f_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2},N}}+\|E(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R};\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N})}\right),$  |
L130: 
L131: for any $t\geq 0$. Here $C_{0}$ is a positive constant independent of $t$.
L132: ###### Remark 1.1.
L133: Unlike [cite50†11 , Theorem 1.1], the nonlinear terms involve the $v$-weighted term and $v$-derivative term corresponding to $E\cdot vf$ and $E\cdot\nabla_{v}f$. Therefore, relying only on the initial data condition $f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{N})$ remains insufficient.
L134: To address this trouble, we require additional initial-data conditions for velocity weight and velocity derivative, namely, $\langle v\rangle f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1})$, $\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(L^{2})$, and $\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{v}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(L^{2})$ with $|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N$ and $1\leq|\beta|\leq N$.
L135: Next, we demonstrate the asymptotic stability of the Cauchy problem (cite52†1.5 ) that also needs to be used in the proof of the time-periodic problem (cite27†1.2 ).
L136: ###### Theorem 1.2.
L137: Let $0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}$ and let $N\geq 4$ be an integer. Let $F^{(1)}_{0}(x,v)=M+\sqrt{M}f^{(1)}_{0}(x,v)\geq 0$ and $F^{(2)}_{0}(x,v)=M+\sqrt{M}f^{{(2)}}_{0}(x,v)\geq 0$, and let $f^{(1)}$ and $f^{(2)}$ be the pair of solutions of the Cauchy problem (cite52†1.5 ) with initial data $f^{(1)}_{0}(x,v)$ and $f^{(2)}_{0}(x,v)$, respectively. Suppose that the external force $E$ and the initial data $f^{(1)}_{0}$ and $f^{(2)}_{0}$ satisfy (cite54†1.7 ) in Theorem cite55†1.1 with $\delta_{0}>0$.
L138: Then, if the initial data further satisfy
L139: (1.9)  |  | $\displaystyle f^{(1)}_{0}-f^{(2)}_{0}\in L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{s_{0}}),$  |
L140: 
L141: for some $s_{0}\in\big(-\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big]$, then we obtain the following time-weighted estimate
L142:  |  | $\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{s-s_{0}}{2}}\big\|(f^{(1)}-f^{(2)})(t)\big\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1})}$  |
L143:  |  | $\displaystyle+\sup_{t\geq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon-s_{0}}{2}}\big\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}(f^{(1)}-f^{(2)})(t)\big\|_{L_{x,v}^{2}}$  |
L144:  |  | $\displaystyle+\sup_{t\geq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon-s_{0}}{2}}\big\|\langle v\rangle(f^{(1)}-f^{(2)})(t)\big\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-2})}$  |
L145:  |  | $\displaystyle+\sup_{t\geq 0}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon-s_{0}}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|\beta|\leq N-1\\
L146: |\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\big\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{v}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}(f^{(1)}-f^{(2)})(t)\big\|_{L_{x,v}^{2}}$  |
L147: (1.10)  |  | $\displaystyle\leq\,$  | $\displaystyle C_{1}\|f^{(1)}_{0}-f^{(2)}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{s_{0},N-1}},$  |
L148: for any $s\in\big[-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon\big]$ with $s\geq s_{0}$. Here $C_{1}$ is a positive constant independent of $t$.
L149: ###### Remark 1.2.
L150: Our method here differs from that in [cite51†7 , cite50†11 ]. We need to overcome the difficulty caused by the additional velocity weight and velocity derivative. This is resolved by obtaining the higher time-rates of $\langle v\rangle\widetilde{f}$ and $\nabla_{v}\widetilde{f}$ at higher frequencies and then iteratively returning to lower frequencies.
L151: However, despite of such difficulty we overcame, in contrast to the method in [cite51†7 , cite50†11 ], we have not obtained the optimal rate of $\widetilde{f}$ in the $L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{s})$-norm for $s\in\big(1-\varepsilon,\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon\big]$.
L152: ###### Remark 1.3.
L153: 
L154: Unlike the case of $N\geq 3$ in Theorem cite55†1.1 , we here require $N\geq 4$ in Theorem cite56†1.2 . This is because when estimating $\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}(f^{(1)}-f^{(2)})$, due to technical reasons, we need to ensure the embedding $\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}$ in case of three spatial dimensions.
L155: With the aid of Theorems cite55†1.1 and cite56†1.2 , we further consider the time-periodic problem (cite27†1.2 ). For the sake of notational simplicity, we set $F_{T}=M+\sqrt{M}f_{T}$. Then, we rewrite the problem (cite27†1.2 ) as
L156: (1.11)  |  | $$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f_{T}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{T}-\mathcal{L}f_{T}\\
L157: &\quad=\Gamma(f_{T},f_{T})-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{T}+\frac{1}{2}E\cdot vf_{T}+E\cdot v\sqrt{M},\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3},\\
L158: &f_{T}(t,x,v)=f_{T}(t+T,x,v)\quad\text{for}\quad(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.$$  |
L159: where $E(t,x)=E(t+T,x)$ for $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}$. Finally, we state the theorem about the existence and stability of the time-periodic problem (cite57†1.11 ).
L160: ###### Theorem 1.3.
L161: 
L162: Let $T>0$ and let $N\geq 4$ be an integer. There exists a small constant $\delta>0$ such that if the time-periodic force term $E$ with the time period $T>0$ satisfies
L163: 
L164: (1.12)  |  | $$\|E(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R};\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N})}\leq\delta,$$  |
L165: 
L166: then we have the following time-periodic results:
L167: 
L168:   * •
L169: (Existence of time-periodic solutions) There exists a unique time-periodic solution $f_{T}$ of the same time period $T$ satisfying $F_{T}=M+\sqrt{M}f_{T}\geq 0$ and
L170: 
L171: (1.13)  |  | $$\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f_{T}(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2},N}}\leq C_{2}\delta.$$  |
L172: 
L173:   * •
L174: 
L175: (Stability of time-periodic solutions) If initial data $f_{0}(x,v)$ satisfy that $F_{0}=M+\sqrt{M}f_{0}\geq 0$,
L176: 
L177:  | $\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2},N}}\leq\delta,$  |
L178: 
L179: with $\delta>0$ sufficiently small, and
L180:  | $\displaystyle f_{0}-f_{T}(0)\in L^{2}_{v}L^{p},$  |
L181: 
L182: with $1<p\leq 2$, then the Cauchy problem (cite52†1.5 ) admits a unique global solution $f$ satisfying $F=M+\sqrt{M}f\geq 0$,
L183: 
L184:  | $\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,\infty);L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{N}))}+\|(\langle v\rangle f(t),\nabla_{v}f(t))\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,\infty);L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-1}))}\leq C_{2}\delta,$  |
L185: 
L186: and the time decay estimate:
L187:  |  | $\displaystyle\,\|(f-f_{T})(t)\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{s})}$  |
L188:  | $\displaystyle\leq$  | $\displaystyle\,C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\Big(\|(f-f_{T})(0)\|_{L_{v}^{2}(L^{p}\cap\dot{H}^{N})}+\|\langle v\rangle(f-f_{T})(0)\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{N-2})}$  |
L189: (1.14)  |  |  | $\displaystyle+\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}(f-f_{T})(0)\|_{L_{x,v}^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|\beta|\leq N-1\\
