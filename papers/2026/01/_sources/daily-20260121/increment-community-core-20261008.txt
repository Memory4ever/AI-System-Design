L9: From Local Services to Agentic AI: Practical Lessons Building Action-Oriented Assistants for Real Businesses
L10: I’m experimenting with AI agents not in a lab environment, but inside real service businesses — local transport and home renovation services.
L11: 
L12: My focus is not conversation quality, but action, conversion, and ROI.
L13: 
L14: Most assistants today are good at answering questions, but struggle with what actually matters in practice:
L15: 
L16:   * closing the loop
L17: 
L18:   * triggering real-world actions
L19: 
L20:   * taking responsibility for outcomes
L21: I want to share a few early observations from building action-oriented agents, and ask the community how others approach similar problems.
L22: 
L23: The core problem I’m seeing
L24: 
L25: In real businesses, users don’t want a “smart chat”.
L26: 
L27: They want a result.
L28: 
L29: What repeatedly fails in practice:
L30: 
L31:   * assistants that ask too many clarifying questions
L32: 
L33:   * assistants that optimize for politeness instead of decisions
L34: 
L35:   * assistants that stop at recommendations instead of execution
L36: In many cases, the difference between a successful interaction and a lost lead is one extra question.
L37: 
L38: What I built (conceptually)
L39: 
L40: Without going deep into implementation details, the agent flow looks like this:
L41: 
L42:   * natural language request
L43: 
L44:   * intent detection and service classification
L45: 
L46:   * minimal clarifying questions (2–4 max)
L47: 
L48:   * rough price estimation logic
L49: 
L50:   * explicit action trigger (callback request, booking, or quote handoff)
L51: The key shift was treating the agent as a decision system, not a chat interface.
L52: 
L53: What didn’t work as expected
L54: 
L55: A few things surprised me:
L56: 
L57:   * More intelligence ≠ better conversion
L58: Simpler decision paths often perform better.
L59: 
L60:   * Users don’t want transparency, they want confidence
L61: Explaining internal reasoning reduced trust in some cases.
L62: 
L63:   * Agents need a clear “exit decision”
L64: Endless clarification loops kill momentum.
L65: 
L66: These lessons only became obvious once the system interacted with real customers.
L67: Where I’m stuck / open questions
L68: 
L69: I’m curious how others here approach these challenges:
L70: 
L71:   * How do you define agent accountability when an action affects real customers?
L72: 
L73:   * Where do you draw the boundary between recommendation and execution?
L74: 
L75:   * Do you expect agentic commerce to be opt-in by businesses, or centrally curated by platforms?
L76: 
L77:   * How are people measuring success beyond raw conversation metrics?
L78: 
L79: Closing thought
L80: I strongly believe the next step for AI assistants is not better language, but better decisions and real actions.
L81: 
L82: Would love to hear how others in this community are thinking about agentic systems in real-world environments, not just demos.
