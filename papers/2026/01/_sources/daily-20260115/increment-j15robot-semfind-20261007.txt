L196@P2: vision–language modeling and multimodal reasoning,

L206@P2: 3. Language-Level Misalignment Metrics
L207@P2: 3.1. Motivation
L208@P2: Conventional semantic segmentation metrics such as
L209@P2: mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) quantify pixel-level
L210@P2: agreement between predicted and ground-truth labels.
L211@P2: While effective for evaluating visual perception in
L212@P2: isolation, these metrics do not capture whether perception
L213@P2: errors meaningfully affect downstream multimodal
L214@P2: reasoning. In safety-critical autonomous systems, small
L215@P2: segmentation errors may lead to qualitatively different
L216@P2: failures in semantic interpretation, including hallucinated
L217@P2: objects, omission of critical scene elements, or unsafe
L218@P2: action recommendations.
L219@P2: To bridge this gap, we introduce language-level
L220@P2: misalignment metrics that explicitly measure semantic
L221@P2: failures in VLM outputs conditioned on degraded visual
L222@P2: inputs. These metrics are designed to be interpretable, taskagnostic, and directly relevant to embodied autonomy.
