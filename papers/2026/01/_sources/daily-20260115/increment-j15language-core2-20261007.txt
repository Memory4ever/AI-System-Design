L168: 
L169: In this work, we presented a mechanistic analysis of TabPFN, moving beyond post-hoc feature importance to investigate the internal algorithmic structure of Tabular Foundational Models. Our experiments shine light on the TabPFN black-box and reveal that TabPFN constructs its answers systematically across several layers with varying functions and importance, with intermediate results linearly represented in the residual stream.
L170: Our results regarding intermediate value probing offer strong evidence that TabPFN implements systematic reasoning. By successfully recovering the intermediate term $a\cdot b$ in the calculation of $z=a\cdot b+c$, particularly in the middle layers, we show that the model systematically constructs the answer, respecting the mathematical order of operations within its residual stream.

L139: #### 4.1.2 Scoring Protocol: Directional Relevance over Magnitude
L140: 
L141: Original CD-T scores components using an unsigned magnitude ratio (Eq. cite101†2 ) cite84†Hsu et al. (2025) , but high magnitude does not necessarily mean task support: a head can be non-discriminative or even anti-aligned with the gold label. This can cause magnitude-based selection to include components that amplify spurious features or push probability mass away from the correct label.
L142: To address this limitation, we propose a directional relevance scoring protocol that explicitly measures whether a head’s contribution supports the correct prediction direction (i.e., increases the logit of the gold label relative to alternatives), rather than merely having large activation magnitude. We use this directional score for circuit selection throughout our experiments.
L143: ##### Directional relevance at the logit readout (classification margin).
L144: 
L145: Let $y_{c}$ be the correct label and $\mathcal{Y}_{\text{other}}=\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{y_{c}\}$. When the target $t$ is a logit readout, we score head $s$ using the relevant contribution to the correct logit relative to the average contribution to incorrect logits:
L146: 
L147:  | $$R(s,t)=\beta_{s\to t}(y_{c})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}_{\text{other}}|}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\text{other}}}\beta_{s\to t}(y).$$  |  | (4)
L148: This measures discriminative contribution at the decision boundary: positive scores indicate that the head increases the correct class logit more than the competing classes.
L149: ##### Directional relevance beyond the logit layer (task-direction projection).
L150: Intermediate layers do not write directly to logits; instead, they update the residual stream, and the final logits are obtained by a linear readout via the unembedding matrix $W_{U}$ (cite102†Belrose et al., 2023 ; cite103†Nanda, 2024 ). This makes $W_{U}$ a natural label-specific reference direction for judging whether an intermediate residual update increases the gold-label logit relative to competing labels.
L151: To evaluate whether an intermediate contribution supports the correct class, we define a task direction using the unembedding matrix $W_{U}$:
L152:  | $$\mathbf{v}_{\text{task}}=W_{U}(y_{c})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}_{\text{other}}|}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\text{other}}}W_{U}(y).$$  |  | (5)
L153: 
L154: This vector approximates the readout direction that separates the correct label from alternatives at the output layer. For an intermediate contribution vector $\beta_{s\to t}(x)$ we define:
L155: 
L156:  | $$R(s,t)=\frac{\beta_{s\to t}(x)\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\text{task}}}{\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\text{task}}\rVert_{2}}.$$  |  | (6)
L157: This score measures alignment with the task direction: positive values indicate alignment with the correct label, and negative values indicate opposition. We empirically validate this scoring choice via circuit faithfulness in Section cite32†6.4 and use this scoring mechanism to conduct our circuit selection.
L158: ### 4.2 Phase 2: Mechanism-Guided Tuning
L159: 
L160: Given the selected attention-head circuit $\mathcal{C}$, we conduct Circuit-Targeted Supervised Fine-Tuning (CT-SFT) by restricting optimization to the heads in $\mathcal{C}$. This focuses adaptation on task-relevant components identified in Phase 1, rather than on globally distributed weight updates.
L161: In our surgical setting, we freeze all model parameters except LayerNorm and apply head-level gradient masking. This ensures that only the parameters of heads in $\mathcal{C}$ are updated while keeping MLPs, token embeddings, the unembedding matrix, and all unselected heads fixed. This approach localizes learning to the discovered circuit, clarifying the connection between behavioral changes and the mechanism.
