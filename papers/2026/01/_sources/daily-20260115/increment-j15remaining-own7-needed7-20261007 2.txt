citeturn30817view0 [wordlim: 200] Crawled: today; Content type: text/html; Source: open({"ref_id":"https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08403v1","lineno":424}); Total lines: 851
L415: Our approach leverages Owen-Shapley values to redistribute the sequence-level GRPO advantage to individual tokens while explicitly eliminating length bias. Given a sequence $y$ with GRPO advantage $\hat{A}$ and Owen values $\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t=1}^{|y|}$ (obtained via the efficient approximation in cite11†Section 3.1 ), we face a redistribution challenge. A naive proportional approach would compute token advantages as $\tilde{A}_{t}=\hat{A}\cdot\frac{\Phi_{t}}{\sum_{t^{\prime}}\Phi_{t^{\prime}}}$.
L416: However, since Owen values satisfy the efficiency axiom ($\sum_{t}\Phi_{t}=R$, where $R$ is the terminal reward), this denominator equals the reward $R$, causing per-token advantages to scale as $\hat{A}/R$. For sequences with correlated advantages and rewards, this creates the same length bias as vanilla GRPO, where the average token advantage $\frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t}\tilde{A}_{t}\propto\frac{1}{|y|}$.
L417: To eliminate this dependency, we propose two redistribution strategies that multiply the proportional allocation by sequence length $|y|$, ensuring the average token advantage equals the original GRPO advantage regardless of sequence length. This length normalization is critical: it decouples gradient magnitude from verbosity while preserving Owen-based prioritization of important tokens.
L418: ##### Proportional Owen Redistribution.
L419: 
L420: Our first approach uses Owen values directly as importance weights:
L421: 
L422:  | $$\tilde{A}_{t}^{\text{prop}}=\hat{A}\cdot\frac{\Phi_{t}}{\sum_{t^{\prime}}\Phi_{t^{\prime}}}\cdot|y|$$  |  | (22)
L423: This strategy assigns credit proportional to each token’s marginal contribution, as quantified by its Owen value. Tokens in high-value^{6}^{6} 6 We assume that we have access to a reasonably efficient and realistic retrieval system that is more likely pull up relevant articles for product-specific attributes, while lowering those of filler words. phrases (e.g., “noise cancelling” in a headphone query) receive proportionally more advantage than tokens in low-value phrases (e.g., filler words).
L424: The length multiplication ensures that $\frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{t}\tilde{A}_{t}^{\text{prop}}=\hat{A}$, making the average token advantage independent of sequence length. Our experiments in product search tasks suggests that this redistribution is particularly effective when Owen values accurately reflect token importance—typically in short sequences with clear, attributable contributions.
L425: ##### Rank-Based Owen Redistribution.
L426: 
L427: Our second approach converts Owen values to ordinal ranks before redistribution:
L428: 
L429:  | $$\text{rank}_{t}=\text{argsort}(\{\Phi_{t^{\prime}}\}),\quad\tilde{A}_{t}^{\text{rank}}=\hat{A}\cdot\frac{\text{rank}_{t}}{\sum_{t^{\prime}}\text{rank}_{t^{\prime}}}\cdot|y|$$  |  | (23)
L430: This rank transformation acts as a form of regularization on the Owen-based credit assignment. While proportional redistribution can concentrate credit heavily on a few high-value tokens (e.g., one token receiving 80% of the advantage), rank-based redistribution spreads credit more uniformly according to relative importance order. For a sequence of length $N$, the top-ranked token receives weight $\frac{N-1}{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}k}=\frac{2}{N}$ instead of a potentially much larger proportion.
L431: This uniform spreading is advantageous in two scenarios: (1) when Owen value estimates are noisy due to complex token interactions in long sequences, the ordinal transformation filters magnitude errors while preserving relative importance; (2) when task success requires multiple complementary components (e.g., multiple attributes in a product query), rank-based redistribution prevents over-concentration on a single dominant feature and encourages the model to improve all relevant tokens.
L432: Both strategies maintain the theoretical guarantees of potential-based reward shaping (cite91†Ng et al., 1999 ). The choice between proportional and rank-based redistribution depends on the task structure: proportional excels when token contributions are clearly separable and accurately measurable, while rank-based provides robustness when contributions are compositional and interdependent.
