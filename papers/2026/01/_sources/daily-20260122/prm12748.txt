S3.SS3  3.3 Policy-Dependent Label Noise A fundamental limitation of MCE is that the estimated label y ^ t ​ ( π ) \hat{y}_{t}(\pi) depends on the future behavior of the sampling policy π \pi , rather than the intrinsic correctness of step s t s_{t} . This policy dependence introduces label noise, including: (i) False Positives (FP) , where incorrect steps receive high rewards due to downstream self-correction (As shown in Figure 2 Left), and (ii) False Negatives (FN) , where correct steps are penalized because later reasoning fails (As shown in Figure 2 Right). Mitigating such policy-induced noise is the central motivation of this work. 
S4  4 Method In this section, we propose a two-stage framework for training robust discriminative PRMs under noisy process supervision. Our method addresses policy-dependent noise introduced by MCE through (i) reflection-aware label correction during data annotation and (ii) noise-aware iterative training with selective label refinement. The overall workflow is illustrated in Figure 3 . 4.1 Reflection-Aware Label Correction As discussed in Section 3 , MCE assigns step-level labels by sampling K K trajectories from a generative policy conditioned on a given reasoning prefix. However, this procedure may produce false positives when an initially incorrect step is later corrected within a trajectory that still reaches the correct answer. To mitigate this issue, we introduce a reflection-aware correction mechanism that operates at the trajectory level prior to MCE aggregation. For each sampled trajectory τ ( i ) = ( s 1 , … , s T ) \tau^{(i)}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{T}) , we apply a large language model to identify whether the correctness of the final answer is achieved by explicitly revising or negating an earlier step s t s_{t} . The prompt template is shown in Appendix B . Formally, for a trajectory τ ( i ) \tau^{(i)} that reaches the correct final answer, we define a binary reflection indicator: Reflect ​ ( τ ( i ) , t ) ∈ { 0 , 1 } , \textsc{Reflect}(\tau^{(i)},t)\in\{0,1\}, (5) which evaluates whether any downstream step s k s_{k} ( k > t k>t ) explicitly corrects the content of s t s_{t} . If Reflect ​ ( τ ( i ) , t ) = 1 \textsc{Reflect}(\tau^{(i)},t)=1 , the trajectory is considered invalid evidence for the correctness of step s t s_{t} , and is re-labeled as unsuccessful with respect to s t s_{t} , even though it reaches the correct final answer. After applying reflection detection to all sampled trajectories, MCE aggregation is performed on the corrected trajectory outcomes. This mechanism is designed as a high-precision filter that selectively removes spurious success trajectories caused by self-correction. As a result, it substantially reduces false positives introduced by MCE. 4.2 Noise-Aware Iterative Training While reflection-aware correction substantially reduces false positives, residual label noise persists, particularly false negatives arising from limited policy capability. Directly correcting such cases via judgment-based supervision is inherently ambiguous, as downstream failures do not provide observable evidence that a preceding step is incorrect. To robustly train PRMs under this residual noise, we propose a noise-aware iterative training framework. S5.SS2  5.2 Label Quality Evaluation (RQ1) This section evaluates whether the proposed reflection-aware label correction can effectively reduce label noise introduced by MCE. Main Results. We evaluate label quality on the PRM800K test set using human-annotated step-level correctness labels, reporting Accuracy and F1. As shown in Table 2 , reflection-aware correction consistently outperforms standard MC-HE across all generators. Compared to MC-HE, F1 improves by 3.3–16.4 points (e.g., 0.521 → \rightarrow 0.685 for 7B-Inst. ), with concurrent gains in Accuracy (e.g., 0.610 → \rightarrow 0.710 for 7B-Inst. ). Across most settings, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct matches or slightly surpasses Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct in F1. Analysis. These results support our hypothesis that downstream self-corrections are a reliable signal for identifying policy-induced false positives in MCE. By filtering such trajectories prior to aggregation, reflection-aware correction substantially improves precision, leading to large F1 gains while maintaining comparable recall. This high-precision behavior reduces label noise without sacrificing coverage of correct steps, yielding cleaner hard supervision. Overall, the improved label quality provides a stronger foundation for training robust PRMs in the subsequent noise-aware iterative training stage.
