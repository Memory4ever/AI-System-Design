A1  Appendix A Training Details Software and hardware. We conduct our experiments on a server with NVIDIA 4xL20 GPUs (48GB VRAM) and NVIDIA 4xA800 GPUs (80GB VRAM). We use Ubuntu 22.04.2 LTS as the operating system, with NVIDIA CUDA Toolkit version 12.4. All experiments are implemented in Python 3.10.15 using the PyTorch 3 3 3 PyTorch v2.6.0, OpenRLHF 4 4 4 OpenRLHF/OpenRLHF v0.9.1 and vLLM 5 5 5 vllm-project/vllm v0.8.4 packages. Dataset Construction. We sample problems and ground-truth answers from the training splits of GSM8K, MATH-500, and PRM800K, and generate 128K reasoning trajectories (about 1M steps) using models from the Qwen2.5 and Qwen2.5-Math Series. Step-level labels are produced via Monte Carlo Estimation with hard estimation and K = 8 K=8 , using Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct as the sampling policy. The resulting dataset is referred to as MC-7B . Starting from MC-7B , we apply the proposed reflection-aware label correction to filter trajectories involving explicit self-correction before MCE aggregation, yielding the MCRD dataset. We use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the judge, producing the final dataset MCRD-7B_7B . Implementation Details. During the training of PRM, we continued the language modeling approach. Specifically, we introduced three additional special tokens into the model’s vocabulary: [PRM], [POS], and [NEG]. During training, we added [PRM] as a placeholder token after each reasoning step. When calculating the loss, the model masked the original text tokens and only output the logits corresponding to the last token of each step, namely [POS] and [NEG]. Then, we used Equation 4 to calculate the cross-entropy loss. The advantage of this method is that it can better adapt to the data distribution learned by the pre-trained model from a large-scale corpus, avoiding severe distribution shifts during training that could lead to a loss of basic semantic understanding capabilities.
