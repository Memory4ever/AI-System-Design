S3.SS3  3.3 Long-Context Reinforcement Learning with Process Advantage Shaping Analysis in Section 3.1 unveils the common “almost-there” phenomenon in existing RLVR methods, highlighting the inherent limitations of relying on sparse, outcome-based rewards tied exclusively to final correctness. Therefore, we introduce LongPAS , which incorporates process advantage shaping guided by on-policy reference trajectories to facilitate fine-grained credit assignment. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 3 . On-policy Supervision from GT-guided Trajectory Given a question Q Q and long context C C , in addition to regular group sampling rollouts T T , we perform auxiliary sampling by incorporating the ground-truth reasoning chain into the prompt. This process transforms condensed entity-relation phrases into a natural language trajectory τ p \tau_{p} , ensuring it exhibits reasoning patterns and thought processes consistent with the policy rollouts. Consequently, τ p \tau_{p} can serve as an on-policy reference supervision signal. To extract intermediate steps from reasoning trajectories, we craft a simple prompt template that instructs the LLM to structure its response into distinct steps. This template explicitly mandates a step-by-step format to facilitate parsing. The detailed prompt templates can be found in Appendix B . Hybrid Reward Design Unlike tasks such as Math and Coding in short-context scenarios, which allow for verification via rule-based functions, the long-context reasoning tasks (e.g., open-ended QA) present unique challenges due to inherent answer diversity. Therefore, we adopt a hybrid reward mechanism ( Wan et al., 2025 ) to balance precision and recall. Process Advantage Shaping We posit that the reference GT-guided trajectory τ p \tau_{p} encapsulates the grounded information scope and essential reasoning steps. This allows us to mitigate the limitation of “erroneous penalization of correct sub-steps” via stepwise advantage shaping. Specifically, for group sampling rollouts T = { τ 1 , τ 2 , … , τ n } T=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},...,\tau_{n}\} , we evaluate each sub-step s j s_{j} of rollout τ i \tau_{i} in two dimensions: validity and relevance . Validity is evaluated with an LLM-as-a-Judge, denoted as 𝕀 valid ​ ( s i , j ) = LLM J ​ u ​ d ​ g ​ e ​ ( s j , τ p ) \mathbb{I}_{\text{valid}}(s_{i,j})=\text{LLM}_{Judge}(s_{j},\tau_{p}) . The judge assesses whether τ i \tau_{i} aligns with the necessary entities and reasoning logic of the reference τ p \tau_{p} . Relevance is quantified by semantic similarity sim ​ ( s i , j , τ p ) \text{sim}(s_{i,j},\tau_{p}) and reflects the extent to which the sub-step s j s_{j} is semantically aligned with its rollout τ i \tau_{i} . To balance exploration and exploitation, we utilize these signals to perform granular stepwise advantage reweighting specifically on rollouts with negative outcomes, whereas positive rollouts remain intact to foster exploration of diverse reasoning paths. Consequently, when a sub-step in a negative rollout is deemed correct, the penalty vanishes ( A ^ i , j → 0 \hat{A}_{i,j}\to 0 ), whereas erroneous steps retain the full negative signal. For Thinking models, since their outputs contain “thinking trajectories” enclosed by “ <think> ” and “ </think> ” tokens, we assign the average advantage of the “response” part uniformly to each token within this thinking segment. We opt to mitigate penalties rather than assign positive rewards to avoid optimization ambiguity. Assigning positive advantages within negative rollouts risks incentivizing ”plausible but ineffective” reasoning paths that do not lead to correct outcomes. Instead, our approach acts as a conservative credit assignment mechanism: it signals that correct sub-steps were not the cause of failure—preventing them from being “unlearned”. S4  4 Experimental Setup Data Construction During the multi-hop QA synthesis pipeline in Section 3.2 , we employ KGGen ( Mo et al., 2025 ) to extract entities and triplets from plain texts. We sample reasoning paths from 2 to 30 hops to control the difficulties of corresponding questions. As a result, we construct the raw DeepReasonQA dataset containing 14,577 QA samples with documents up to 288K tokens in length. Prior to training LLMs, we filter the training questions to exclude trivial and insurmountable instances. Specifically, for each question, we perform 8 full-context rollouts with Qwen3-4B-Thinking and retain only those whose empirical success rate lies in the interval [0.25, 0.75]. This yields a filtered RL training dataset of 2,012 QA samples with documents up to 60K tokens. Detailed dataset statistics and data filtering pipeline are provided in Appendix A.1 and A.2 . Training Settings We conduct experiments on different LLM backbones: (1) Instruct Models: LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct; (2) Thinking Models: Qwen3-4B-Thinking and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking. We include RLVR as the baseline method implemented by naive GRPO ( Shao et al., 2024 ) , and trained on the same dataset. Our RL framework is implemented upon VeRL ( Sheng et al., 2024b ) . For the RL training, we use the AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of 2e-6 and a 5-step linear warmup. The maximum input length is set to 60K tokens, the maximum output length is set to 30K tokens for Thinking models and 10K for Instruct models. We conduct purely on-policy training, use a prompt batch size 128 and set group size N N to 8, sampling temperature to 0.7 and top- p p to 0.95. As for GT path guided sampling, temperature is set to 0 and top- p p is set to 1. We adopt GPT-OSS-120B as the LLM-as-Judge for answer evaluator and provide validity signals in Process Advantage Estimation. We use Qwen3-8B-Embedding to calculate semantic similarity between trajectory steps. Evaluation Configurations We evaluate all LLMs on three widely-used and challenging long-context QA benchmarks: (1) FRAMES ( Krishna et al., 2025 ) , which comprises questions requiring 2-15 Wikipedia articles to answer. (2) LongBench V2 ( Bai et al., 2025 ) , a realistic multi-choice QA benchmark that assess the ability of LLMs to handle long-context problems requiring deep understanding and reasoning, with contexts ranging from 8K to 2M words. (3) Multi-Hop QA: We adopt three subsets including 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA and MusiQue from Longbench ( Bai et al., 2024b ) , which cover 3-5 hop questions with corresponding documents. To contextualize performance, we also evaluate frontier LLMs in different sizes including GPT5-Nano ( OpenAI, 2025 ) , Gemini2.5-Flash-Thinking ( Deepmind, 2025 ) and GPT-OSS (20B and 120B) ( Agarwal et al., 2025 ) . More detailed evaluation configurations can be found in Appendix B . 
S5.SS2  5.2 Ablation Study In this section, we conduct ablation studies to investigate the key components in LongPAS that achieve long-context in-depth reasoning, including (1) the training data length; (2) process signals in advantage estimation; and (3) the role of on-policy trajectory supervision. Figure 5: Performance of LongPAS trained on different context windows (20K, 40K & 60K). Training Data Length We investigate the impact of training data length on the performance of LongPAS . Specifically, we train Qwen3-4B with varying maximum input lengths—20K, 40K, and 60K tokens—aligned with different context window sizes. As shown in Figure 5 , the performance of the LongPAS model, across both Qwen3-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-4B-Thinking variants, exhibits a monotonic increase with the expansion of the maximum input length during training, utilizing 20K, 40K, and 60K token context windows. This observation underscores the critical role of long-sequence training in improving the model’s long-context grounding and reasoning. Specifically, the FRAMES and Multi-Hop QA tasks register the most substantial performance gains. This indicates that training with extended context windows significantly enhances the model’s capability for complex information grounding and long-range dependency modeling, which are prerequisites for multi-step reasoning. While performance gains on LongBench V2 benchmark are less pronounced, the consistent positive correlation confirms that greater exposure to longer contexts during training systematically elevates the model’s overall proficiency across diverse long-context tasks. Process Signal of Advantage Shaping In Section 3.3 , we estimate the stepwise advantage shaping signals along two dimensions: Validity and Relevance . We perform an ablation by removing the Validity signal 𝕀 valid ​ ( s i , j ) = LLM J ​ u ​ d ​ g ​ e ​ ( s j , τ p ) \mathbb{I}_{\text{valid}}(s_{i,j})=\text{LLM}_{Judge}(s_{j},\tau_{p}) , and retaining only Relevance as the indicator of the necessity of sub-steps. As reported in Table 3 , removing the Validity signal causes a clear performance decline across all benchmarks, especially on long-context multi-hop reasoning tasks. On FRAMES, performance decreases from 64.90 to 62.68 (-2.28%), and on LongBench V2 it drops from 42.94 to 41.15 (-1.79%). We also evaluate an ablation that removes Relevance signal while keeping only Validity signal, which binarizes the continuous reweighting coefficient. A similar performance degradation is observed: -1.73% on FRAMES and -2.04% on Multi-Hop QA. These reductions indicate that both the Validity and Relevance signal play an essential role in filtering out incorrect or noisy intermediate steps, thereby ensuring that advantage shaping emphasizes only logically sound reasoning transitions. On-policy Trajectory Supervision During Process Advantage Shaping, we sample GT-guided reference trajectory via an on-policy strategy. To explore the role of on-policy supervision in Process Advantage Shaping, we conduct an ablation comparison by replacing it with off-policy supervision from teacher LLMs. Specifically, we sample GT-guided reference trajectories τ d \tau_{d} from Gemini2.5-Pro by incorporating the ground-truth reasoning chain into the prompt. As shown in Table 3 , we can also observe a performance drop across all benchmarks, especially on multi-hop reasoning benchmarks (-2.01% on FRAMES). We conclude this phenomenon for the following reasons: (1) Strategy-Data Distribution Mismatch: While τ p \tau_{p} provides a stable, efficient process advantage signal (expecially Relevance signal) by aligning with the current policy’s capability range P π ​ ( τ ) P_{\pi}(\tau) , the off-policy τ d \tau_{d} comes from a powerful Teacher LLM ( P Teacher ​ ( τ ) P_{\text{Teacher}}(\tau) ) that is out-of-distribution for the student model. This causes the training model to struggle with accurately estimating the advantage of paths it rarely explores, leading to unstable optimization; (2) Teacher LLM’s reasoning steps may be too complex or rely on knowledge organization the training model π \pi has not yet learned, and because τ d \tau_{d} is static, it cannot dynamically adapt to the policy’s learning progress like τ p \tau_{p} . Sx1  Limitations This paper aims to incentivize in-depth reasoning of long-context LLMs on long-range multi-hop QA tasks, and offers a recipe combining a KG-guided multi-hop QA synthesis framework with a process advantage shaping strategy. While it makes substantial progress in long-context reasoning, several limitations remain: Data Domain and Source The synthesis framework currently relies primarily on Wikipedia as the underlying knowledge source. While DeepReasonQA already yields substantial improvements and effectively incentivizes deep long-context reasoning patterns, incorporating long documents from domains such as law, finance, and medicine could introduce richer stylistic and structural diversity, potentially improving robustness and transferability to real-world long-context reasoning tasks. Coupling of Synthesis Framework and Process Supervision A potential limitation is the perceived coupling between LONGPAS and the KG-driven synthesis framework, as the algorithm currently utilizes the specific reasoning chains generated during synthesis as the primary source of process signals. While this might appear to restrict the scalability of LONGPAS to datasets where explicit ground-truth reasoning paths are available , we argue that the core contribution of LONGPAS lies in its generalizable mechanism for Process Advantage Shaping. Specifically, the method provides a robust framework for effectively leveraging any form of auxiliary supervision—whether they are KG-derived reasoning chains or standard reasoning trajectories distilled from teacher LLMs—to stabilize long-context RL training. By transforming these signals into fine-grained credit assignment, LONGPAS addresses the fundamental ”almost-there” bottleneck in a way that aligns with contemporary efforts ( Deng et al., 2025 ; Zhu et al., 2025b ) to integrate SFT-level supervision into the RL stage. Thus, rather than being a restricted implementation, our approach offers a novel and versatile perspective on mitigating reward sparsity in complex, long-context scenarios. Sophistication of the Reward Model Our current implementation uses a hybrid reward function that combines simple rule-based checks with an LLM-as-a-judge to balance precision and recall. This works well for tasks with clear correctness criteria, such as factoid multi-hop questions, but may be less effective for open-ended or subjective tasks where correctness is multifaceted. A promising direction is to develop more advanced, rubric-based LLM reward models that score responses along dimensions such as logical rigor, citation accuracy, and coherence, enabling our framework to better handle complex, agentic scenarios.
