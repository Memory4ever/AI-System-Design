The long-tail distribution of multi-head attention is critical for semantic understanding in LLMs, where a small fraction of positions carry disproportionately large, critical weights. Unconstrained linear updates to the remaining q/k/v weights shift these relative magnitudes, distorting the original long-tail pattern and undermining attention fidelity. As illustrated in Figure 2 , the non-weight-update method can better maintain the long-tail distribution while achieving worse model performance. However, by entirely refraining from updating pruned q/k/v weights, local errors propagate and accumulate across layers. Experiments Experimental Settings We compare D 2 ​ P ​ r ​ u ​ n ​ e D^{2}Prune with three main baselines, including weight-update pruning methods such as SparseGPT ( 14 ) and non-weight-update pruning methods such as Wanda ( 36 ) and Pruner-Zero ( 11 ) , and all experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA A40 GPU. Moreover, given the potential of our dynamic attention update mechanism for global modeling, we also compare our method with a global pruning approach, SparseLLM ( 1 ) , which iteratively updates activations and weights in the MLP modules to minimize global error detailed results in the Appendix B.5). In addition, we further evaluate our method on the latest large language models Qwen3-8B and 14B ( 2 ) , as well as the vision transformer model DeiT ( 38 ) built on the ViT ( 12 ) architecture detailed in Appendix B.3). Following prior studies, we assess and compare various pruning methods based on language modeling with the perplexity metric (ppl) and the zero-shot accuracy for downstream tasks. For all pruning algorithms, we use the C4 ( 32 ) training set as the calibration dataset, and we use 128 samples and segment the pre-trained LLMs according to their maximum embedding dimensions. For the language modeling comparison, we assess the perplexity on the WikiText2 ( 28 ) test set. For the zero-shot comparison, we use seven benchmark tasks including BoolQ ( 8 ) , HellaSwag ( 43 ) , WinoGrande, RTE ( 40 ) , ARC-c and ARC-e (ARC Easy and Challeng ( 9 ) ), and OBQA ( 29 ) from the EleutherAI LM Harness ( 15 ) .
