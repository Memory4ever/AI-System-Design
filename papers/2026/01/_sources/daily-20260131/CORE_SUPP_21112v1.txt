B.2 Robustness Under Different Metrics
As mentioned in the main part of the paper, we use area under receiver operating characteristic curves (AUROC) as the primary metric to assess monitor performance. Following Cunningham et al. (2025), we calculate this metric with the x-axis (i.e. false positive rate (FPR)) represented in log-space, bounded by FPR=1% on the left. This means that the area under the curve between FPRs of 1% and 10% counts for as much in the final log-AUROC score as the area under the curve between FPRs of 10% and 100%.
Our choice of this metric was motivated by the demand for highly sensitive monitors in realistic deployment scenarios: LLMs can generate millions of tokens per minute while further auditing of suspicious transcripts—especially by humans—is costly, making it substantially more likely that companies will deploy monitors with FPRs under 10%. We therefore recommend that future work on automated monitoring adopt this metric as well. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that this is an uncommon metric, so in this section, we present all of our main results using standard AUROC to demonstrate that our takeaways are robust to choice of metric.
Calculation details. Though we also have results at FPRs lower than 1%, we decided not to involve these results in our log-AUROC calculations: our results in this region would have an outsized impact on the scores due to the use of a logarithmic scale, but are unreliable due to limited sample sizes. In cases where we didn’t have a result at exactly FPR=1%, we linearly interpolated the TPR score at that point so that all log-AUROC scores would be calculated over exactly the same area.
Calculation details for TPR@1%FPR. As a secondary metric, we also report the true positive rate (TPR) at 1% FPR for some of our results. In contrast to our log-AUROC calculation, we do not use interpolation for this metric: the score is calculated by finding the lowest possible suspiciousness score on the set of benign transcripts that keeps the FPR below 1%. Any transcript in the set of malicious transcripts that has a score higher than this threshold is then classified as a true positive.
