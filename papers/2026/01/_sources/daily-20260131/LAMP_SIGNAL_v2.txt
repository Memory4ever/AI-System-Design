3.3 Attention and softmax
The self-attention mechanism is a distinctive feature of transformers [28].
In the simplest case, attention is computed as 𝐕⋅softmax⁡(𝐊⊺​𝐐)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\cdot\mathrm{softmax}(\bm{\mathrm{K}}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}). This is a composition of three functions: the product of the key and query matrices, the softmax function (6) applied columnwise, and the product of the value matrix and the output of softmax.
When the value-softmax product is followed by layer normalization, its LAMP evaluation can be performed as in Subsection 3.2.
Here, we study the LAMP problem (5) for
f:ℝn→ℝn,f⁡(𝐲)=softmax⁡(𝐲).f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad f(\bm{\mathrm{y}})=\mathrm{softmax}(\bm{\mathrm{y}}).
There is a remarkable distinction between the componentwise LAMP problems for RMS layer normalization and softmax.
Whereas the former admits a simple solution at the cost of one additional index, Appendix B shows that enlarging an optimal-sized greedy index set by any fixed or percentage-based margin is insufficient to ensure the componentwise LAMP objective (3) for softmax.
Because highly negative KQ inner products create pathological failure cases for the componentwise LAMP, we pivot to the normwise ℓ1\ell_{1} objective (4), which naturally bounds the total probability mass shift.
