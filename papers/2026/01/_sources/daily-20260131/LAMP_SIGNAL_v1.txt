2.3 Look-ahead mixed-precision evaluation
The two refinement approaches of Subsection 2.2 lead to very similar rounding error bounds for the composition.
The accuracy of the inner computation manifests itself in the second term, which we aim to reduce as follows: select a binary vector 𝐪∈{0,1}n\bm{\mathrm{q}}\in\{0,1\}^{n} so that
‖|diag​(f⁡(𝐲^))−1​𝐉f​(𝐲^)|​(𝟏−𝐪)‖∞≤τ\Big\|\big|\mathrm{diag}(f(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}))^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{J}}_{f}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\big|(\bm{\mathrm{1}}-\bm{\mathrm{q}})\Big\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\leq\tau
(3)
or
‖|diag​(f⁡(𝐲^))−1​𝐉f​(𝐲^)​diag​(𝐲^)|​(𝟏−𝐪)‖∞≤τ\Big\|\big|\mathrm{diag}(f(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}))^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{J}}_{f}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\big|(\bm{\mathrm{1}}-\bm{\mathrm{q}})\Big\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\leq\tau
(4)
for a given threshold τ≥0\tau\geq 0.
Let us discuss these objectives:
•
Objectives (3) and (4) improve the bound (2) for suitable τ\tau. Specifically, the unweighted (3) replaces the second term in (2) with 𝟏​τ​‖𝐲^‖∞​‖𝐜g‖∞​ug\bm{\mathrm{1}}\tau\|\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\|\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{g}\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\mathrm{u}_{g}, and the weighted (4) with 𝟏​τ​‖𝐜g‖∞​ug\bm{\mathrm{1}}\tau\|\bm{\mathrm{c}}_{g}\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\mathrm{u}_{g}.
Which of the two objectives to choose is a problem-specific question.
•
Both (3) and (4) can always be attained with 𝐪=𝟏\bm{\mathrm{q}}=\bm{\mathrm{1}}, i.e., when every component of the inner function gg is computed more accurately.
•
When 𝐪=𝟎\bm{\mathrm{q}}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}, the norms in (3) and (4) are proportional to the mixed and componentwise condition numbers of ff at 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}, respectively (Gohberg & Koltracht, 1993).
•
The exact f⁡(𝐲^)f(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}) and 𝐉f​(𝐲^)\bm{\mathrm{J}}_{f}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}) are not available in practice, so their FP computed values will be used.
If the baseline 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}} with 𝐪=𝟎\bm{\mathrm{q}}=\bm{\mathrm{0}} satisfies (3) or (4), we deem the computation complete.
Otherwise, we try a different 𝐪\bm{\mathrm{q}}, which entails the recomputation of 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}.
Such recomputations can be costly, so we assume that the Jacobian is stable with respect to small variations in 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}} and fix the matrix 𝐊∈{𝐊u,𝐊w}\bm{\mathrm{K}}\in\{\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{u},\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{w}\} corresponding to the baseline 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}, where44
4
If the Jacobian changes rapidly, the Hessian would need to be included in the analysis.
𝐊u=diag​(f⁡(𝐲^))−1​𝐉f​(𝐲^),\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{u}=\mathrm{diag}(f(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}))^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{J}}_{f}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}),
𝐊w=diag​(f⁡(𝐲^))−1​𝐉f​(𝐲^)​diag​(𝐲^).\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{K}}_{w}=\mathrm{diag}(f(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}))^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{J}}_{f}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}})\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}}).
Having found a suitable 𝐪\bm{\mathrm{q}} that satisfies the bound
‖|𝐊|​(𝟏−𝐪)‖∞=‖𝐊⁡(𝐈−diag⁡(𝐪))‖∞,∞≤τ,\big\||\bm{\mathrm{K}}|(\bm{\mathrm{1}}-\bm{\mathrm{q}})\big\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}=\big\|\bm{\mathrm{K}}\big(\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\mathrm{q}})\big)\big\|_{\mathrm{\infty,\infty}}\leq\tau,
we will recompute more accurately those components of 𝐲^\hat{\bm{\mathrm{y}}} that are indexed by the nonzeros of the binary vector 𝐪\bm{\mathrm{q}}.
To minimize the number of recomputations, we require 𝐪\bm{\mathrm{q}} to be sparse.
This leads to our look-ahead mixed-precision (LAMP) problem: seek 𝐪∈{0,1}n\bm{\mathrm{q}}\in\{0,1\}^{n} such that
‖𝐪‖0→mins.t.‖|𝐊|​(𝟏−𝐪)‖∞≤τ.\|\bm{\mathrm{q}}\|_{\mathrm{0}}\to\min\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\big\||\bm{\mathrm{K}}|(\bm{\mathrm{1}}-\bm{\mathrm{q}})\big\|_{\mathrm{\infty}}\leq\tau.

3.3 Attention and softmax
The self-attention mechanism is a distinctive feature of transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017).
In the simplest case, attention is computed as 𝐕⋅softmax⁡(𝐊⊺​𝐐)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\cdot\mathrm{softmax}(\bm{\mathrm{K}}^{\intercal}\bm{\mathrm{Q}}). This is a composition of three functions: the product of the key and query matrices, the softmax function (6) applied columnwise, and the product of the value matrix and the output of softmax.
When the value-softmax product is followed by layer normalization, its evaluation can be adapted as discussed in Subsection 3.2.
We shall study the LAMP evaluation of the key-query product by analyzing the LAMP problem (5) for softmax in the unweighted case (3).

To see this, it suffices to replace ziz_{i} with yi2/‖𝐲‖22y_{i}^{2}/\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}\|_{\mathrm{2}}^{2} and recall that ‖𝐳‖1=1\|\bm{\mathrm{z}}\|_{\mathrm{1}}=1 by the definition of softmax (6).
