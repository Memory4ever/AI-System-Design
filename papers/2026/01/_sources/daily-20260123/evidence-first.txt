Affiliation: College of Artificial Intelligence, Nankai University Email: lihuanss@nankai.edu.cn\nL71: ###### Abstract\nL72: Large Language Models (LLMs) achieve competitive performance across diverse natural language processing (NLP) tasks, yet pretraining is computationally demanding, making optimizer efficiency an important practical consideration. Muon accelerates LLM pretraining via orthogonal momentum updates that serve as a matrix analogue of the element-wise sign operator.\nL73: Motivated by the recent perspective that Adam is a variance-adaptive sign update algorithm, we propose two variants of Muon, Muon-NSR and Muon-VS, which apply variance-adaptive normalization to momentum before orthogonalization. Muon-NSR applies noise-to-signal ratio (NSR) modulation, while Muon-VS performs variance-based scaling without introducing additional hyperparameters.\nL74: Experiments on GPT-2 and LLaMA pretraining demonstrate that our proposed methods accelerate convergence and consistently achieve lower validation loss than both competitive, well-tuned AdamW and Muon baselines. For example, on the LLaMA-1.2B model, Muon-NSR and Muon-VS reduce the iterations required to reach the target validation loss by $1.36\\times$ relative to the well-tuned Muon following the recent benchmark (cite56†Wen et al., 2025 ).\nL75: Figure 1: Validation loss trajectories for LLaMA-1.2B on C4-en (Suite B). The plot illustrates the convergence of Muon-NSR, Muon-VS, and well-tuned Muon following the recent benchmark (cite56†Wen et al., 2025 ) over training iterations, with the model pretrained on the DCLM dataset under $1\\times$ Chinchilla compute budget.\nL76: ## 1 Introduction\nL77: Deep learning, particularly Transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs), has become a dominant paradigm in modern natural language processing (NLP), enabling substantial gains across a wide range of tasks (cite57†Yang et al., 2025 ; cite58†Comanici et al., 2025 ; cite59†Guo et al., 2025 ).\nL78: Central to this success is the regime of extensive self-supervised pretraining on massive corpora, which serves as the fundamental catalyst for the emergence of sophisticated reasoning and few-shot generalization capabilities  (cite60†Raffel et al., 2020 ; cite61†Brown et al., 2020 ; cite62†Kaplan et al., 2020 ).\nL79: However, given that such scaling incurs immense computational costs often spanning thousands of GPU-hours, enhancing the convergence speed and sample efficiency of the underlying optimizer has emerged as a paramount research priority  (cite63†Schneider et al., 2019 ; cite62†Kaplan et al., 2020 ; cite64†Hoffmann et al., 2022 ).\nL80: Adam and its variants constitute the de facto standard for LLM pretraining and have dominated deep learning training for the past decade  (cite65†Zhang et al., 2020 ; cite66†Kunstner et al., 2023 ). Recent theoretical insights suggest that, under equal EMA decay rates, Adam operates effectively as a variance-adaptive sign update, dynamically attenuating steps in high-variance regimes (cite67†Orvieto and Gower, 2025 ).\nL81: On the other hand, challenging Adam’s dominance, the recently introduced Muon optimizer (cite68†Jordan et al., 2024 ) employs Newton–Schulz (NS) iterations to approximate the orthogonal polar factor. By flattening the singular value spectrum to unity, Muon serves as a matrix-valued analogue of the sign update, preserving directional structure while strictly normalizing magnitude.\nL82: Motivated by the correspondence between Adam’s scalar variance adaptation and Muon’s matrix-level updates, the following question emerges:\nL83: Can variance-adaptive components be integrated into Muon to achieve acceleration similar to the improvements that Adam provides for scalar sign updates?\nL84: #### Contributions.\nL85: \nL86: To address the above question, this work proposes Muon-NSR and Muon-VS, two variance-adaptive extensions of the Muon. Our main contributions are:\nL87: \nL88:   * •\nL89: Variance-adaptive variants. We introduce Muon-NSR and Muon-VS, which integrate adaptive variance statistics into the orthogonal update structure. Muon-NSR employs explicit noise-to-signal ratio (NSR) modulation, while Muon-VS implements parameter-free variance scaling. Both approaches apply variance statistics before the Newton–Schulz (NS) step to preserve the matrix-signed update form.\nL90: \nL91:   * •\nL92: Superior empirical efficiency. Experimental results on GPT-2 and LLaMA pretraining demonstrate that the proposed variants achieve faster convergence and lower validation loss compared to well-tuned baselines, while requiring only a single additional buffer. For example, Muon-NSR and Muon-VS reduce the number of iterations required to reach the target validation loss on LLaMA-1.2B by 1.36$\\times$ relative to the well-tuned Muon following the recent benchmark (cite56†Wen et al., 2025 ).\nL93: ## 2 Related Works\nL94: Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) (cite69†Robbins and Monro, 1951 ) and its momentum-augmented variant, Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (SGDM) (cite70†Polyak, 1964 ), serve as the cornerstones of deep learning optimization. While these methods rely on dense gradient information, a parallel line of research explores sign-based optimization to enhance communication and computational efficiency.\nL95: By decoupling the update direction from its magnitude, these methods align with steepest descent in $L_{\\infty}$ geometry (cite71†Leplat et al., 2025 ). For instance, SignSGD (cite72†Bernstein et al., 2018 ) employs element-wise signs to compress gradients to 1-bit, significantly reducing communication overhead while maintaining convergence rates comparable to SGD.\nL96: Extending this paradigm, Signum (cite72†Bernstein et al., 2018 ) and Lion (cite73†Chen et al., 2023 ) integrate momentum into this framework by applying the sign operator to accumulated gradient statistics, thereby combining the efficiency of 1-bit compression with the stability of momentum.\nL97: However, relying on a global learning rate, SGD and sign-based methods struggle to adapt to the significant blockwise sharpness disparity inherent in Transformer architectures (cite74†Wang et al., 2025 ). To address this, adaptive gradient methods rescale updates using historical gradient statistics. AdaGrad (cite75†Duchi et al., 2011 ) pioneered this approach by normalizing updates via accumulated squared gradients, though it suffers from a premature decay of the effective learning rate.\nL98: This limitation is mitigated by RMSProp (cite76†Tieleman and Hinton, 2012 ) and Adam (cite77†Kingma, 2014 ), which utilize exponential moving averages (EMA) of second moments. AdamW (cite78†Loshchilov and Hutter, 2017 ) further refines this by decoupling weight decay from the gradient-based update. Due to its robustness, AdamW has been widely adopted in modern Large Language Model (LLM) training pipelines (cite65†Zhang et al., 2020 ; cite66†Kunstner et al., 2023 ) and serves as a primary baseline for this work.\nL99: Despite their success, standard adaptive methods operate in a coordinate-wise manner, effectively approximating the Hessian as a diagonal matrix and ignoring correlations between parameters (cite79†Martens and Grosse, 2020 ; cite80†Yao et al., 2021 ). To capture clearer structural information, geometry-aware optimizers exploit the matrix structure of parameters. Shampoo (cite81†Gupta et al., 2018 ) introduces Kronecker-factored preconditioners to efficiently approximate full-matrix second-moment scaling.\nL100: Advancing this paradigm, SOAP (cite82†Vyas et al., 2024 ) employs eigen-decomposition to refine the approximation of the local loss geometry. For matrix-valued parameters specifically, Muon (cite68†Jordan et al., 2024 ) orthogonalizes update directions via efficient Newton–Schulz (NS) iterations, achieving orthogonal updates with constrained singular values.\nL101: Recent studies demonstrate that Muon scales effectively to LLM training (cite83†Liu et al., 2025 ), with further efficiency gains achievable through adaptive magnitude control mechanisms, as proposed in AdaMuon (cite84†Si et al., 2025 ) and NorMuon (cite85†Li et al., 2025 ).\nL102: ## 3 Motivation from the Variance-Adaptive Perspective of Adam\nL103: #### An equivalent form of Adam with equal betas.\nL104: \nL105: The classical Adam consists of the following steps\nL106: \nL107:  |  | $\\displaystyle m_{t}=\\beta_{1}m_{t-1}+(1-\\beta_{1})g_{t},$  |  | (1)\nL108:  |  | $\\displaystyle v_{t}=\\beta_{2}v_{t-1}+(1-\\beta_{2})g_{t}^{2},$  |  | (2)\nL109:  |  | $\\displaystyle d_{t}=\\frac{m_{t}}{\\sqrt{v_{t}}}=\\frac{m_{t}}{\\sqrt{m_{t}^{2}+v_{t}-m_{t}^{2}}},$  |  | (3)\nL110:  |  | $\\displaystyle x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\\eta d_{t}.$  |  | (4)\nL111: Since Adam applies adaptive preconditioning coordinate-wise, the above steps focus on a single scalar component with stochastic gradient $g_{t}\\in\\mathbf{R}$. We also omit bias correction and the stability term $\\varepsilon$ for analytical tractability. Recently, cite67†Orvieto and Gower (2025) empirically observed that employing a shared EMA decay rate for both first- and second-moment estimators, denoted as $\\beta_{1}=\\beta_{2}=\\beta$, maintains competitive performance across various settings.\nL112: Under this shared parameterization, cite67†Orvieto and Gower (2025, Proposition 1) proved that $v_{t}-m_{t}^{2}$ satisfies the following recursion\nL113:  | $$\\begin{split}v_{t}-m_{t}^{2}=\\beta(v_{t-1}-m_{t-1}^{2})+\\beta(1-\\beta)(m_{t-1}-g_{t})^{2}.\\end{split}$$  |  | (5)\nL114: \nL115: Replacing (cite86†2 ) by (cite87†5 ) yielding an equivalent form.\nL116: #### Interpretation of Adam as variance-adaptive sign update.\nL117: Let the stochastic gradient sample $g_{t}$ at iteration $t$ be modeled as a realization drawn from a Gaussian distribution with unknown, slowly varying parameters: $g_{t}\\sim\\mathcal{N}(\\mu,\\sigma^{2}).$ Let $(\\mu_{t-1},\\sigma_{t-1}^{2})$ denote the estimate of the gradient’s mean and variance from the previous iteration.\nL118: Upon observing $g_{t}$, we define the updated estimate $(\\mu_{t},\\sigma_{t}^{2})$ as the solution to the following regularized maximum likelihood estimation problem (cite67†Orvieto and Gower, 2025 )\nL119:  | $$\\begin{split}&(\\mu_{t},\\sigma_{t}^{2})=\\mathop{\\mathbf{arg\\,min}}\\limits_{\\mu\\in\\mathbf{R},\\,\\sigma^{2}>0}\\biggl\\{-\\log p(g_{t}\\mid\\mu,\\sigma^{2})\\\\\nL120: &\\qquad\\qquad+\\frac{1}{\\lambda}\\,\\mathbf{KL}\\bigl(\\mathcal{N}(\\mu_{t-1},\\sigma_{t-1}^{2})\\parallel\\mathcal{N}(\\mu,\\sigma^{2})\\bigr)\\biggr\\},\\end{split}$$  |  | (6)\nL121: where $\\lambda>0$ is a regularization coefficient governing the trade-off between maximizing the likelihood of the current observation and minimizing the divergence from the previous estimate. The Gaussian negative log-likelihood is given by\nL122: \nL123:  | $$-\\log p(g_{t}\\mid\\mu,\\sigma^{2})=\\frac{1}{2}\\log\\sigma^{2}+\\frac{(g_{t}-\\mu)^{2}}{2\\sigma^{2}},$$  |\nL124: \nL125: and the Kullback–Leibler divergence from the prior estimate $(\\mu_{t-1},\\sigma_{t-1}^{2})$ to the candidate distribution is\nL126:  | $$\\mathbf{KL}\\bigl(\\mathcal{N}(\\mu_{t-1},\\sigma_{t-1}^{2})\\parallel\\mathcal{N}(\\mu,\\sigma^{2})\\bigr)\\\\\nL127: =\\frac{1}{2}\\biggl[\\frac{\\sigma_{t-1}^{2}}{\\sigma^{2}}+\\frac{(\\mu_{t-1}-\\mu)^{2}}{\\sigma^{2}}-1-\\log\\biggl(\\frac{\\sigma_{t-1}^{2}}{\\sigma^{2}}\\biggr)\\biggr].$$  |  | (7)\nL128: \nL129: Letting $\\beta=\\frac{1}{1+\\lambda}\\in(0,1)$,  cite67†Orvieto and Gower (2025, Theorem 4.1) proved that the minimizer of (cite88†6 ) admits a closed-form solution recursively defined as follows\nL130:  | $\\displaystyle\\mu_{t}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\beta\\mu_{t-1}+(1-\\beta)g_{t},$  |  | (8)\nL131:  | $\\displaystyle\\sigma_{t}^{2}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\beta\\sigma_{t-1}^{2}+\\beta(1-\\beta)(m_{t-1}-g_{t})^{2}.$  |  | (9)\nL132: Comparing (cite87†5 ) with (cite89†9 ) and (cite90†1 ) with (cite91†8 ) reveals that $\\sigma_{t}^{2}=v_{t}-m_{t}^{2}$ and $\\mu_{t}=m_{t}$, implying that $m_{t}$ and $v_{t}-m_{t}^{2}$ in Adam’s updates serve as online estimators of the local gradient mean and variance, respectively. Substituting into Adam’s update rule (cite92†3 ) yields a variance-adaptive formulation defined by\nL133:  | $$d_{t}=\\frac{m_{t}}{\\sqrt{m_{t}^{2}+\\sigma_{t}^{2}}}=\\frac{\\operatorname{sign}(m_{t})}{\\sqrt{1+\\sigma_{t}^{2}/m_{t}^{2}}},\\quad(m_{t}\\neq 0),$$  |  | (10)\nL134: \nL135: where the term $\\sigma_{t}/|m_{t}|$ represents the local noise-to-signal ratio (NSR). This derivation characterizes Adam as a Signum update (cite72†Bernstein et al., 2018 ) modulated by an NSR-dependent damping factor, thereby suppressing step sizes in noise-dominated regimes (cite67†Orvieto and Gower, 2025 ).\nL136: #### Muon: A Matrix Analogue of Signum.\nL137: The design of Muon (cite68†Jordan et al., 2024 ) generalizes Signum by transitioning from element-wise scalar normalization to spectral normalization, thereby preserving the intrinsic correlations within the parameter matrix. Formally, let $W_{t}\\in\\mathbf{R}^{m\\times n}$ and $G_{t}\\in\\mathbf{R}^{m\\times n}$ denote the parameter matrix and the stochastic gradient at iteration $t$.\nL138: The Muon optimizer maintains a momentum estimate $M_{t}$ and computes updates using a matrix-valued sign operator $\\operatorname{msign}(\\cdot)$ with decoupled weight decay according to the recursion\nL139:  | $\\displaystyle M_{t}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\beta M_{t-1}+G_{t},$  |  | (11)\nL140:  | $\\displaystyle O_{t}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\operatorname{msign}(\\beta M_{t}+G_{t})\\in\\mathbf{R}^{m\\times n},$  |\nL141:  | $\\displaystyle W_{t}$  | $\\displaystyle=W_{t-1}-\\eta_{t}\\Bigl(O_{t}+\\lambda W_{t-1}\\Bigr),$  |\nL142: where $\\beta\\in[0,1)$ is the EMA decay rate and $\\eta_{t}$ denotes the learning rate. The $\\operatorname{msign}(\\cdot)$ operator distinguishes this method by computing the orthogonal polar factor of the matrix. Specifically, for a singular value decomposition (SVD) $M=U\\Sigma V^{\\top}$ containing the singular value matrix $\\Sigma$, the operator is defined by\nL143: \nL144:  | $$\\operatorname{msign}(M)=UV^{\\top}.$$  |\nL145: Mathematically, this corresponds to replacing $\\Sigma$ with the identity matrix, which flattens the spectrum of the momentum matrix. This spectral normalization serves as a matrix-valued analogue to the scalar sign operation employed in Signum (cite68†Jordan et al., 2024 ; cite93†Chen et al., 2025 ).\nL146: #### Motivation: Muon with variance-adaptive scaling.\nL147: \nL148: The reformulation in (cite94†10 ) identifies the core mechanism of Adam as a sign update algorithm modulated by variance-adaptive scaling. In contrast, Muon generalizes this sign operation to the matrix domain via spectral normalization. Hence, this theoretical disconnect motivates integrating Adam’s variance adaptation into the Muon framework.\nL149: \nL150: Algorithm 1 Muon-NSR and Muon-VS\nL151: 0:  Parameters $W_{0}\\in\\mathbf{R}^{m\\times n}$; learning rate $\\eta$; weight decay $\\lambda$; EMA rate $\\beta$ (shared for first and second moments, i.e., $\\beta_{1}=\\beta_{2}=\\beta$); variance-sensitivity $\\gamma$; stabilizer $\\varepsilon$; Newton–Schulz iterations $K$; optimizer type (Muon-NSR or Muon-VS).\nL152: \nL153: 1:  $M_{0}\\leftarrow 0,\\;\\;\\Gamma_{0}\\leftarrow 0$\nL154: \nL155: 2:  for $t=1,2,\\ldots$ do\nL156: \nL157: 3:   $G_{t}=\\mbox{Stochastic Gradient Oracle}(W_{t-1})$\nL158: 4:   $\\Gamma_{t}\\leftarrow\\beta\\Gamma_{t-1}+\\beta(1-\\beta)\\,(M_{t-1}-G_{t})^{\\odot 2}$\nL159: \nL160: 5:   $M_{t}\\leftarrow\\beta M_{t-1}+(1-\\beta)G_{t}$\nL161: \nL162: 6:   $\\widehat{M}_{t}\\leftarrow\\frac{M_{t}}{1-\\beta^{t}}$, $\\widehat{\\Gamma}_{t}\\leftarrow\\frac{\\Gamma_{t}}{1-\\beta^{t}}$\nL163: \nL164: 7:   $\\tilde{M}_{t}\\leftarrow G_{t}+\\frac{\\beta}{1-\\beta}\\,\\widehat{M}_{t}$\nL165: \nL166: 8:   if type is Muon-NSR then\nL167: 9:    $\\overline{M}_{NSR,t}\\leftarrow\\frac{\\tilde{M}_{t}}{\\sqrt{~\\tilde{M}_{t}^{\\odot 2}+\\gamma\\,\\widehat{\\Gamma}_{t}}+\\varepsilon}$ {Muon-NSR}\nL168: \nL169: 10:   else\nL170: \nL171: 11:    $\\overline{M}_{VS,t}\\leftarrow\\frac{\\tilde{M}_{t}}{\\sqrt{\\widehat{\\Gamma}_{t}}+\\varepsilon}$ {Muon-VS}\nL172: \nL173: 12:   end if\nL174: \nL175: 13:   $O_{t}\\leftarrow\\mathbf{NS}_{K}(\\overline{M}_{NSR,t}\\;\\text{or}\\;\\overline{M}_{VS,t})$\nL176: \nL177: 14:   $W_{t}\\leftarrow W_{t-1}(1-\\eta\\lambda)-\\eta\\,s_{\\text{scale}}\\,O_{t}$\nL178: \nL179: 15:  end for\nL180: ## 4 Muon-NSR and Muon-VS\nL181: \nL182: Building upon the aforementioned motivation, this paper presents two variance-adaptive extensions of the Muon optimizer, namely Muon-NSR and Muon-VS. Both variants incorporate variance adaptivity into the matrix update while preserving the spectral normalization structure of Muon. The complete procedure is given in Algorithm cite95†1 .\nL183: #### Momentum and Variance.\nL184: At iteration $t$, we maintain two coupled exponential moving statistics that follow the derivation in Section cite9†3 . The momentum estimator $M_{t}$ is updated by  (cite91†8 ), which yields a smoothed estimate of the local gradient mean under a shared EMA decay rate and provides the directional signal for the subsequent polar-factor computation. In parallel, the variance surrogate $\\Gamma_{t}$ is updated by  (cite89†9 ).\nL185: This recursion depends on the elementwise squared $(G_{t}-M_{t-1})^{\\odot 2}$, which measures the deviation of the current stochastic gradient from the previous mean estimate. As a result, $\\Gamma_{t}$ captures dispersion around the evolving mean rather than accumulating second moments in Adam. The pair $(M_{t},\\Gamma_{t})$ then supplies the mean and variance information required by the normalization step used before applying the matrix sign operator.\nL186: #### Nesterov-style acceleration.\nL187: To incorporate Nesterov momentum consistent with the Muon optimizer, we derive an extrapolated update direction. While Muon updates its momentum by directly adding the current gradient as shown in the first line of  (cite96†11 ), our formulation instead maintains momentum as an EMA, as defined on line 5 of Algorithm cite95†1 . Consequently, relative to Muon, the contribution of each past gradient to $M_{t}$ is uniformly scaled by $(1-\\beta)$.\nL188: To recover the Muon-consistent weighting in the Nesterov extrapolation, we therefore normalize the momentum term by $(1-\\beta)^{-1}$, yielding the lookahead direction\nL189:  | $$\\tilde{M}_{t}\\;=\\;G_{t}\\;+\\;\\frac{\\beta}{1-\\beta}\\,M_{t}\\,.$$  |\nL190: \nL191: This normalization removes the attenuation introduced by the EMA parameterization, so that the resulting Nesterov-style direction is directly comparable to the Muon formulation.\nL192: #### Muon-NSR\nL193: \nL194: The Muon-NSR variant normalizes Muon’s update by integrating both $\\tilde{M}_{t}$ and $\\widehat{\\Gamma}_{t}$, which is motivated by (cite94†10 ). The update rule is formally expressed as\nL195: \nL196:  | $$\\overline{M}_{NSR,t}=\\frac{\\tilde{M}_{t}}{\\sqrt{~\\tilde{M}_{t}^{\\odot 2}+\\gamma\\,\\widehat{\\Gamma}_{t}}+\\varepsilon},$$  |  | (12)\nL197: where $\\gamma\\geq 0$ modulates the sensitivity to variance and $\\varepsilon>0$ ensures numerical stability.
