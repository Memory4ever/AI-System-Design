The researchers used a hyperparameter called $L_{\\text{classifier}}$L classifier​ with a weight ($cls\\_weight$c l s_w e i g h t) to control how much the model was forced to categorize rules during training.\n\nWhen this weight was high (1.0), the model showed clear \"disentanglement.\" Visualization of the latent space showed that examples belonging to the same rule clustered together into tight, distinct groups. As the weight decreased toward zero, these clusters merged and became messy. This suggests that without an explicit signal to learn rules, neural networks naturally default to memorizing lexical combinations rather than internalizing abstract reasoning logic.\n\nImage: Heatmaps showing the gradient cosine similarity for different rules. This counter-intuitive finding suggests that too much data can lead to \"memorization noise,\" where the model gets confused by slight variations. However, by using the VAE's latent injection, this bottleneck was mitigated, allowing the model to maintain higher accuracy.\n\n## Transformer Dynamics: Where are the Rules Stored?\n\nThe systematic study also investigated which parts of the Transformer architecture are most responsible for holding onto these reasoning rules.
