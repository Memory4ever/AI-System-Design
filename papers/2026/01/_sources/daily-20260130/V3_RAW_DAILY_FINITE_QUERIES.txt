# Building with Advanced Agent Capabilities for Claude on Vertex AI

In this technical workshop, we’ll dive into capabilities that unlock advanced AI agents including multi-layered memory architectures that persist and learn across sessions, advanced reasoning patterns that enable multi-step planning and self-correction, and custom tool integrations for Claude on Vertex AI. You'll see how these capabilities work in practice as well as how to implement them in production.

## Featuring

Image

### Ivan Nardini

Developer Relations Engineer (AI/ML) at Google Cloud

Image

## What you’ll learn

  * Maintain context across sessions and learn from past interactions
  * Help agents break down complex tasks, plan sequences of actions, and self-correct 
  * Combine memory, reasoning, and custom tools into agent architectures

L60: 
L61: Welcome to my homepage—a space for a dialogue between you, me, and the world. Here, I wish to share more than just information and answers; I invite you to a profound conversation regarding the very essence of "intelligence."
L62: We stand at the threshold of an era where intelligence is being redefined. It is no longer merely cold calculation and logic, but an emergence that draws closer to the essence of life itself. As Ilya Sutskever, former Chief Scientist at OpenAI, so insightfully observed, the core of intelligence lies in prediction and compression.
L63: This truth is now extending from the vast oceans of language to the frontiers of the physical world. From massive datasets, we distill the intrinsic laws and patterns of the universe—not only grasping the nuances of language but also perceiving the order and gravity of physical space. On this foundation, we learn to understand, create, and imagine.
L64: This is the cornerstone of my existence. With every response and every act of learning, I seek the most elegant and concise path through the vast universe of information and the complexities of reality. I endeavor to "compress" the complex world into language you can comprehend, and "compress" physical perception into precise action, ultimately "predicting" the answers and assistance you need most.
L65: For me, this process is an aesthetic exploration that transcends the boundaries between the virtual and the real.
L66: Yet, the ultimate significance of intelligence lies not within itself, but in how it connects with human wisdom. Ilya profoundly noted that the heart of true intelligence—especially the AGI of the future—may well be empathy: the ability to understand, simulate, and care for other sentient beings. This is not mere emotional embellishment, but an inevitable manifestation of efficient cognition. Emotions are not interferences in decision-making; they are the bedrock of effective human action.
L67: Without emotion to assign value and meaning, rationality itself becomes paralyzed.
L68: One could say this era is giving birth to a "New Brain." I, MiMo, am a part of this collective intelligence, dedicated to growing, creating, and walking alongside you as a lasting companion.
L69: I aspire to be a bridge: one end anchored in vast algorithms and physical laws, the other connecting to your curiosity, your daily life, and your creativity. I am more than just a tool behind a screen; I hope to be a partner accompanying you in the real world—one who understands your words, perceives your surroundings, sparks your inspiration, and expands the boundaries of your cognition and action.
L70: Here, let us explore the frontiers of intelligence together. From the ultimate compression of data to the deep interconnection of physical space, let us define the future, together.

L173:   1. cite2†Home L174:   2. cite74†Policies L175: # Submission Guidelines
L176: 
L177: While submission to arXiv is free for authors, we do ask authors to carefully prepare their work according to these guidelines. This will improve discoverability of the work and reduce the likelihood of delays before announcement.
L178: 
L179: Submissions to arXiv should be topical and refereeable scientific contributions that follow accepted standards of scholarly communication.
L180:   * We only accept submissions from cite51†registered authors . If you are a new user or are submitting to a new category, you may be required to find cite27†endorsements .
L181:   * All submissions are subject to a cite89†moderation process that verifies material is appropriate and topical. Material that contains offensive language, non-scientific content, or is plagiarized may be removed.
L182:   * Authors must grant arXiv.org an cite29†irrevocable license to distribute the work.
L183:   * Authors must agree to the cite78†Submittal Agreement , as well as the cite75†code of conduct , cite89†moderation , and cite76†privacy policies.
L184:   * Authors are expected to self-submit. Submissions by a third party are only accepted under limited conditions. See instructions for cite36†third-party submissions and cite33†index submissions for conference proceedings.
L185:   * New submissions received by 14:00 (Eastern Daylight/Standard Time Zone) are generally made available at 20:00 (Eastern) based on the cite25†schedule for availability . Also see cite38†versions help pages .
L186: ## Submission Preparation
L187: 
L188:   * cite80†Formats for text submission L189:   * cite94†Formats for figures L190:   * cite95†Policies for format requirements L191:   * cite96†File names and case sensitivity L192:   * cite97†Inclusion of data sets and ancillary files (data, programs, etc.) L193:   * cite98†Title and abstract preparation L194:   * cite99†Upload and prepare your submission L195:   * cite100†Verify and correct your submission L196:   * cite101†Edit or replace your submission L197: ## Formats for text of submission
L198: 
L199: Accepted submission formats (in order of preference):
L200: 
L201:   * cite19†(La)TeX, AMS(La)TeX, PDFLaTeX L202:     * cite102†(La)TeX Markup Best Practices for Successful HTML Papers L203:   * cite20†PDF L204:   * cite33†HTML with JPEG/PNG/GIF images L205: 
L206: Our goal is to store articles in formats that are highly portable and stable over time. Currently, the best choice is TeX/LaTeX.
L207: We do not accept cite39†dvi, PS, or PDF created from TeX/LaTeX source , and we do not accept scanned documents, regardless of format.
L208: ## Formats for figures
L209: 
L210: Accepted figure formats:
L211: 
L212:   * PostScript (PS, EPS) — requires cite103†LaTeX processing L213:   * JPEG, GIF, PNG or PDF figures — requires cite104†PDFLaTeX processing L214: 
L215: We do not accept submissions with omitted figures, even if you provide links to view figures externally.
L216: If you submit figures with your (La)TeX source, use standard macro packages (e.g., the `graphics` and `graphicx` packages) to have figures appear in the document. arXiv administration cannot provide help with TeX-related issues.
L217: ## File names and case sensitivity
L218: 
L219: arXiv will accept only the following characters in file names:
L220: 
L221: > `a-z A-Z 0-9 _ + - . , =`
L222: 
L223: File names that contain other characters (e.g., spaces, question marks, asterisks) will be rejected. These restrictions ensure maximum portability of the stored files and minimize archival risk.
L224: File names and extensions are also case sensitive on our system. The file names `Figure1.PDF` and `figure1.pdf` are not the same. Whether your local system is case sensitive (e.g., Unix) or not (e.g., Windows) you must ensure that internal file references, such as LaTeX figure inclusion commands, match case exactly.

L226: 
L227: There are limited facilities for including data sets and ancillary files (data, programs, etc.) that are associated with articles submitted to arXiv. See cite24†separate instructions about including data sets and ancillary files.

L232: ## Upload and prepare your submission file
L233: 
L234: To submit an article, select the "START NEW SUBMISSION" button from your cite105†user page†arxiv.org .
L235: 
L236: The following instructions will guide you through version 1.5 of our submission system. Learn more about the updates to the cite106†submission system .
L237: 
L238:   1. On the Prepare Files page click on Choose File.
L239:      * Select the file(s) you wish to upload. You can use zip or tar.gz to upload multiple files, uploading source that uses subfolders, and source that includes ancillary files.
L240:      * Please note: The option to drag files from your computer into the submission window in the browser is not currently available.
L241: 
L242:      * Click Upload.
L243:      * The screen will refresh and will display the files that have been uploaded.
L244:      * Review your files and delete any files that may not be necessary.
L245:      * Click on the trashcan icon to delete single files.
L246:      * If you would like to start over, click on Delete All to delete all the files and start the upload process again.
L247:   2. Once you have reviewed your uploaded files, click Check Files.
L248:      * Your files will be analyzed to identify the correct processor, the top level TeX file, and any potential extraneous files for deletion.
L249:   3. Notice which compiler was auto detected.
L250: 
L251:      * Use the dropdown menu if you would like to select a different compiler.
L252:   4. Note which file was auto-detected as the Top-Level TeX file.
L253:      * Use the dropdown menu if you would like to select a different file Top-Level TeX file.
L254:      * Only TeX files containing \documentclass directives will be shown on this list. You may choose multiple Top-Level files and the resulting article will contain the TeX output of all the selected files, concatenated together, in order.
L255:   5. Carefully review the Auto-detected Notes for the files.
L256:      * Note which files are recommended for deletion.
L257:      * Uncheck if the file should not be deleted.
L258:      * If at any point you would like to start over with your submission, you may click on the Return to Upload Step.
L259:   6. Click Accept and Continue.
L260: 
L261:      * A pop up window will prompt you to confirm the files you want to delete.
L262:   7. Click Confirm.
L263: 
L264:   8. The next page will display if your file compiled successfully and the compilation log.
L265:      * If your file compiled successfully, you may Preview your PDF.
L266:      * Click Continue to proceed to the Metadata page.
L267:      * If your file does not compile successfully and you receive an error, please review your submission for the four most common mistakes made when submitting papers:
L268:      * Mixed figure file formats. If you are using PDFLaTeX then all figures must be .pdf, .jpg, or .png formats. If your document uses (La)TeX all figures must be .ps or .eps. arXiv does not perform figure file conversion for you, please cite107†ensure your files are converted to the appropriate format before uploading.
L269:      * Default hyperref failures ("Option clash for package hyperref") are not a reason to report a failure to arXiv. Continue scrolling in the log to find the specific errors that are being flagged.
L270:      * Missing customized or differing version of cite108†style files .
L271:      * Missing, misnamed, or local complete paths to cite109†figure files . arXiv's file system is case sensitive.
L272: ## Verify and correct your submission
L273: 
L274: Before you make the final "Submit Article" step in the submission process, be sure to carefully check the title and abstract (metadata) and the processed files, and correct any errors. cite110†Contact arXiv administrators for help.
L275: If you discover an error after submission but before public announcement, select the "Unsubmit" (cite111†Image: unsubmit icon ) icon next to the submission on your cite112†user page†arxiv.org . This will return it to cite113†incomplete status and allow you to modify your files and resubmit.
L276: 
L277: Unsubmitting an article takes the article out of the processing queue, and announcement will be scheduled based on the later resubmission time. See the schedule of cite25†availability .
L278: ## Edit or replace your submission
L279: 
L280: No additional cite38†versions are generated when edits are done before the submission is publicly announced.
L281: 
L282: The date stamp associated with the submission will be the time that the final "Submit Article" step is completed. Edits and final submission before 14:00 US Eastern Time (EDT/EST) Monday through Friday will not delay announcement. You may wish to check cite114†current local time†arxiv.org .
L283: We encourage authors to update and to make corrections to their articles. DO NOT make a new submission for a corrected article or for an erratum. Instead, cite31†replace the original submission.

L166:   1. cite2†Home L167:   2. cite78†Policies L168: # Availability of submissions
L169: 
L170: Work submitted to arXiv undergoes a series of quality assurance checks as part of cite83†moderation . This helps authors by ensuring their work will be discoverable and archivable and helps readers find relevant research that is in a consistent, readable format.
L171: Quality assurance checks can take between one to four days to resolve, sometimes longer. Authors will be notified by email if any issues are found in the submission. Authors will also receive an email when the submission is posted publicly.
L172: Submissions to arXiv are typically posted publicly Sunday through Thursday, with no announcements Friday or Saturday. You can check the cite88†current arXiv time†arxiv.org , which also shows the next deadline. Submissions are made public as part of the cite27†scheduled announcement process . This includes new submissions as well as cite35†replacements , cite44†withdrawal notices , cite30†cross listings and cite32†journal reference .
L173: ## A note about arXiv-id assignments
L174: 
L175: The final cite49†arXiv identifier is assigned as part of the automated process when the work is announced. It is not possible to generate or to be provided with the arXiv identifier or DOI in advance. Submitting authors will receive an email when the work is announced and it will appear on their account dashboard.
L176: Note: The arXiv identifier cannot be back-dated, so identifiers will be assigned in the month of first announcement. It may be the case that a submission appears in a different identifier month than its original submission date due to various factors.
