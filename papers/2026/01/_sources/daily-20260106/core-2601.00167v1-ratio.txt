L126: ### 3.1 Removing Hindsight Return Relabeling
L127: When deploying DTs, the learner must specify a desired initial RTG, since the ground-truth future RTG is unknown in advance. In Online Decision Transformer (ODT), the learner typically sets a relatively high target RTG $g_{\mathsf{online}}$ during rollout to encourage optimistic exploration. During training, a key component of ODT—known as hindsight return relabeling—replaces the RTG tokens based on the actual achieved RTG $g_{\mathsf{actual}}$ (cite56†Zheng et al., 2022 ).
L128: When augmenting the supervised ODT objective with RL gradients from TD3, ODT+TD3 (cite58†Yan et al., 2024 ) also adopt the hindsight return relabeling step. cite58†Yan et al. (2024) further attempt to perform online finetuning of DTs using PPO (cite65†Schulman et al., 2017 )—an importance sampling-based RL algorithms—but find that PPO gradients lead to poor performance, ultimately reverting to TD3 gradients instead.
L129: While hindsight return relabeling works well under supervised learning objectives (see Fig. 5.4 in cite56†Zheng et al. (2022) ), we find it incompatible with importance sampling-based RL gradients that rely on the ratio $\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a\mid s,g)}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathsf{old}}}(a\mid s,g)}$, as in PPO and GRPO.
L130: The issue arises from a mismatch in the conditioning variable $g$: the learner conditions rollouts on a high RTG $g_{\mathsf{online}}$ for optimistic exploration, yet the achieved RTG $g_{\mathsf{actual}}$ is often much lower.
L131: If hindsight return relabeling is applied, actions sampled from $\pi_{\theta_{\mathsf{old}}}(a\mid s,g_{\mathsf{online}})$ are later trained as if they were drawn from $\pi_{\theta_{\mathsf{old}}}(a\mid s,g_{\mathsf{actual}})$, producing unreliable importance weights and unstable updates. This inconsistency explains why naive applications of PPO to ODT tend to fail (cite58†Yan et al., 2024 ).
L132: As shown in cite62†Fig. 1 , removing hindsight return relabeling significantly improves stability and overall performance. In simpler environments such as MuJoCo Hopper, relabeling may yield transient gains but eventually leads to collapse, whereas in more complex environments such as Adroit Door, the model fails to learn altogether when relabeling is enabled.
L133: Additionally, hindsight return relabeling requires access to the returns of the entire trajectory, which introduces additional challenges when optimizing over sub-trajectory rollouts (introduced in cite16†Section 3.2 ). In the experiments shown in cite62†Fig. 1 , we apply hindsight return relabeling to sub-trajectories by rolling out the full trajectory.
L134: Figure 1: Comparison of GRPO-DT with and without hindsight return relabeling when applied to sampled sub-trajectories. Hindsight return relabeling causes the importance-sampling ratio $\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a\mid s,g)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a\mid s,g)}$ to become highly unstable, which in turn degrades performance.
