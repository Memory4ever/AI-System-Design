L315: ## Limitations
L316: AstraFlow focuses on system abstractions for agentic RL workloads rather than proposing a new RL optimization algorithm. Our evaluation covers representative math, coding, multi-policy, elastic rollout, heterogeneous deployment, and data-algorithm workloads, but does not exhaust all agentic RL settings, such as long-horizon web agents, robotics environments, or safety-critical interactive systems.
L317: The observed benefits may depend on workload characteristics including rollout latency, trainer throughput, network bandwidth, weight-transfer frequency, and asynchronous data availability. In addition, some experiments use controlled or simulated deployment settings, and AstraFlow assumes that rollout tasks, trajectory metadata, and reward signals can be represented through the dataflow layer.
L318: While AstraFlow makes multi-policy and data-centric RL workloads easier to compose, model quality, robustness, and safety still depend on the underlying data, rewards, policies, and evaluation protocol.
L319: ## Social Impact
L320: AstraFlow is an infrastructure system for reinforcement learning of agentic language models. Its positive impact is to make advanced RL experimentation more modular and resource-efficient by allowing researchers to reuse rollout services and trainers, compose data algorithms, and run flexible multi-policy or heterogeneous deployments without rewriting the full training pipeline.
L321: At the same time, more efficient RL infrastructure may accelerate stronger agentic models for coding, tool use, and autonomous decision-making, which could be misused without appropriate evaluation, monitoring, access control, or domain-specific safeguards. AstraFlow does not release a new foundation model or dataset, but users should still evaluate trained agents for reliability, security, privacy, and misuse risks before deployment.

L322: ## Appendix A Experimental Settings
L323: This appendix provides the full experimental setup deferred from Section cite15†4 . We organize the appendix per-experiment so that each subsection is self-contained and reproducible. Section cite26†A.1 fixes the shared bits that recur across experiments (model cards, evaluation suites, decoding, reward functions); Sections cite27†A.2 –cite33†A.8 then specify, one per main-text experiment, the training data, algorithm hyperparameters, hardware and topology, baselines, and any experiment-specific knobs.
L324: ### A.1 Common Setup
L325: Evaluation protocol. Across all experiments in this appendix, we evaluate each benchmark by sampling 4 independent generations per question (temperature $0.6$, $n_{\mathrm{samples}}=1$) and reporting the mean per-question accuracy averaged over the benchmark. For every run we evaluate at a fixed cadence during training and report numbers from the checkpoint with the best benchmark-average accuracy. Per-experiment subsections below therefore omit this protocol and only note any deviations.
L326: ### A.2 Multi-policy Math (Section cite16†4.1 , Table cite102†2 )
L327: Models & Datasets. Both Solver and Verifier are initialized from Qwen3-8B [cite101†Yang et al., 2025 ] and trained as separate policies under the actor-and-verify workflow described in Section cite16†4.1 . We train on the DAPO RL math set [cite56†Yu et al., 2025 ] (Hugging Face: aaabiao/dapo_filter), filtering prompts to at most $2{,}000$ tokens; following Dr.
L328: MAS [cite66†Feng et al., 2026 ], the trailing “Let’s think step by step …\boxed{}” suffix is stripped at load time so the multi-agent workflow owns response formatting. Verbatim prompts are listed in Appendix cite34†A.9 .
L329: Training. Both policies are trained with M2PO ($m^{2}$-threshold $0.01$), with group-level reward normalization, batch-level advantage normalization, and a fixed KL penalty coefficient of $10^{-3}$ (no KL controller). We use AdamW with a constant learning rate of $5\mathrm{e}{-6}$ ($\beta_{1}{=}0.9$, $\beta_{2}{=}0.999$, weight decay $0.01$), no warmup, and gradient clipping at $1.0$.
L330: The training batch size is $256$ with $8$ rollouts per prompt and $4$ PPO mini-batches per iteration; rollout generation uses temperature $1.0$ and $\max_{\mathrm{new\_tokens}}=4{,}096$. We deliberately disable dynamic sampling in this run to align with the Dr. MAS [cite66†Feng et al., 2026 ] no-DS configuration that the Solver and Solver+Verifier (verl) baselines in Table cite102†2 report against; this keeps the rollout regime matched across the three rows.
L331: Training runs for $1{,}200$ iterations on a single 8$\times$NVIDIA H100 (80 GB) node, with $4$ GPUs serving the two SGLang RaaS instances (Solver and Verifier each at data-parallel size $2$) and $4$ GPUs split into two FSDP trainer groups of size $2$ (one per policy). Trainer$\leftrightarrow$RaaS weight transfer runs in TCP delta mode with a full-sync interval of $10$ iterations.
L332: Baseline and time measurement. The Solver and Solver+Verifier (verl) accuracy rows in Table cite102†2 are the corresponding numbers reported in Dr. MAS [cite66†Feng et al., 2026 ] (Qwen3-8B, GRPO over the same DAPO math set, no DS), which we adopt unchanged. The per-iteration training-time entries ($212.64$ s for verl, $77.65$ s for AstraFlow), in contrast, are both measured on the same 8$\times$H100 node used for the AstraFlow run: we re-run the verl-based Dr.
L333: MAS pipeline end-to-end on this hardware and time each iteration with eval steps excluded. For verl the iteration is rollout$+$train run sequentially under colocated synchronous execution; for AstraFlow the iteration is the trainer step time, with rollout overlap absorbed by the dataflow layer.
L334: Evaluation. We evaluate on AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, and Minerva Math every $25$ training iterations. Following the shared protocol in Section cite26†A.1 , we sample $4$ generations per question at temperature $0.6$ and report $\mathrm{pass}@1\,(\mathrm{avg}@4)$.
L335: ### A.3 Multi-policy Code (Section cite16†4.1 , Table cite103†3 )
L336: Models & Datasets. The three rows of Table cite103†3 (single-policy Solver, Solver + Selector, Solver + Test-Case Generator) all initialize from Qwen3-8B [cite101†Yang et al., 2025 ] and use the workflows described in Section cite16†4.1 ; multi-agent runs train each role as a separate policy. We train on the DeepCoder-Preview-Dataset [cite146†Luo et al., 2025a ] (Hugging Face: agentica-org/DeepCoder-Preview-Dataset, primeintellect subset, train split) and filter prompts to at most $6{,}000$ tokens.
L337: Reward in all three runs is the binary outcome of executing the final attempt against the problem’s hidden test cases. Verbatim Solver, Selector, and Test-Case Generator prompts are listed in Appendix cite34†A.9 .
L338: Training. All three runs use the same M2PO settings as Section cite27†A.2 , except: learning rate $3\mathrm{e}{-6}$, $1{,}000$ training iterations, dynamic sampling on (zero-advantage groups are filtered before training), and TCP full-sync weight transfer instead of delta. The training batch size is $256$ with $8$ rollouts per prompt; in the Solver + Selector run the Selector trainer uses a smaller batch size of $128$, matching its lower per-iteration production rate. All runs use NVIDIA H200 (141 GB) GPUs.
L339: The Solver baseline runs on a single 8$\times$H200 node, with $4$ GPUs serving the policy (data-parallel size $4$) and $4$ GPUs as one FSDP trainer group of size $4$. The two multi-agent runs each use two 8$\times$H200 nodes ($16$ GPUs total): one node fully dedicated to RaaS hosting both policies, and the other node split into $4$ RaaS GPUs and $4$ FSDP trainer GPUs (two groups of size $2$, one per policy).
L340: Rollout data-parallel sizes are $6$ + $6$ (Solver + Selector) and $9$ + $3$ (Solver + Test-Case Generator); the asymmetric v3 split reflects the Test-Case Generator’s lower per-rollout invocation rate.
L341: Evaluation. We evaluate on LiveCodeBench v5 [cite147†Jain et al., 2024 ], LiveCodeBench v6, and the Codeforces split of DeepCoder-Preview-Dataset every $25$ training iterations; multi-agent runs are scored end-to-end through the same workflow used in training. Following the shared protocol in Section cite26†A.1 , we sample $4$ generations per question at temperature $0.6$ and report $\mathrm{pass}@1\,(\mathrm{avg}@4)$.
L342: ### A.4 Rollout Auto-scaling (Section cite19†4.2.1 , Table cite105†4 )
L343: 
L344: Models & Datasets. A single Qwen3-14B [cite101†Yang et al., 2025 ] policy is trained on the DeepScaler RL math set [cite148†Luo et al., 2025b ], filtering prompts to at most $2{,}000$ tokens.
L345: Training. We use M2PO with $m^{2}$-threshold $0.002$, group-level reward and batch-level advantage normalization, and a fixed KL penalty coefficient of $10^{-3}$ (no KL controller). We optimize with AdamW at a constant learning rate of $3\mathrm{e}{-6}$ ($\beta_{1}{=}0.9$, $\beta_{2}{=}0.999$, weight decay $0.01$), no warmup, and gradient clipping at $1.0$.
L346: The training batch size is $256$ with $8$ rollouts per prompt and $4$ PPO mini-batches per iteration; rollout generation uses temperature $1.0$ with $\max_{\mathrm{new\_tokens}}=18{,}000$ and SGLang context length $20{,}480$; dynamic sampling is on (zero-advantage groups are filtered before training). Training runs for $1{,}200$ iterations on two 8$\times$NVIDIA H200 (141 GB) nodes ($16$ GPUs total).
L351: The report has three blocks: (i) a window block summarizing wall-clock time and the trainer waiting fraction $w=\sum_{k}\text{wait}_{k}/\sum_{k}\text{step}_{k}$ over the window; (ii) a production block aggregating produced/accepted/consumed tokens across all RaaS instances; (iii) a scaling decision block reporting the three-zone branch and the target GPU count $G_{\mathrm{target}}$.
L352: A per-RaaS layout table follows so the maintainer can preferentially retire suspect or low-throughput instances on scale-down. stale_skipped is advisory and does not enter the scaling math.
L353: The three-zone decision rule the agent reads off the branch field is: scale up via $G_{\mathrm{target}}=\lceil G/(1-w)\rceil$ when $w>\tau_{\mathrm{high}}{=}0.10$; scale down via $G_{\mathrm{target}}=\min(G,\lceil G\cdot(\text{consumed}/\text{accepted})\cdot\rho\rceil)$ with $\rho{=}1.10$ when $w<\tau_{\mathrm{low}}{=}0.05$ and the window saw both production and consumption; otherwise hold.
L354: Evaluation. We evaluate on AIME24, AIME25, AMC, MATH500, and Minerva Math every $50$ training iterations. Following the shared protocol in Section cite26†A.1 , we sample $4$ generations per question at temperature $0.6$ and report $\mathrm{pass}@1\,(\mathrm{avg}@4)$.
L355: ### A.5 Heterogeneous and Cross-region Training (Section cite20†4.2.2 )
L356: 
L357: Models & Datasets. A single Qwen3-14B [cite101†Yang et al., 2025 ] policy is trained on the DeepScaler RL math set [cite148†Luo et al., 2025b ], filtering prompts to at most $2{,}000$ tokens.
L358: Training. We use M2PO with $m^{2}$-threshold $0.002$, group-level reward and batch-level advantage normalization, and a fixed KL penalty coefficient of $10^{-3}$ (no KL controller). We optimize with AdamW at a constant learning rate of $3\mathrm{e}{-6}$ ($\beta_{1}{=}0.9$, $\beta_{2}{=}0.999$, weight decay $0.01$), no warmup, and gradient clipping at $1.0$.
L359: The training batch size is $256$ with $8$ rollouts per prompt and $4$ PPO mini-batches per iteration; rollout generation uses temperature $1.0$ with $\max_{\mathrm{new\_tokens}}=18{,}000$; dynamic sampling is on (zero-advantage groups are filtered before training). Training runs for $1{,}200$ iterations.
L360: All training settings (model, dataset, M2PO hyperparameters, batch, iterations, decoding, dynamic sampling) are identical to the auto-scaling experiment in Section cite29†A.4 ; only the deployment topology (cross-region heterogeneous vs. homogeneous local) and the weight-transfer mode (delta vs. full sync) differ. The Fixed-11-GPU row in Table cite105†4 (avg. accuracy $68.0$) therefore serves as the homogeneous local baseline against which we compare the cross-region run’s $67.6$ in Section cite20†4.2.2 .
L361: Cross-region topology and heterogeneity. We simulate a cross-region heterogeneous deployment over three NVIDIA H200 (141 GB) nodes: a 4-GPU FSDP trainer (data-parallel size $4$) on the local node, and three 4-GPU SGLang RaaS pools (each at tensor-parallel size $1$, data-parallel size $4$) — one co-located with the trainer and two on remote nodes.
L362: GPU heterogeneity is induced by per-GPU power caps: $700$ W on the local pool, and $400$ W and $250$ W on the two remote pools, yielding rollout-throughput shares of roughly $100\%$, $60\%$, and $30\%$ respectively (Figure cite106†16 ). The two remote links are shaped with tc/netem to $4$ Gbit/s effective bandwidth and $300$ ms round-trip latency; the local link is unshaped.
L363: Trainer$\leftrightarrow$RaaS weight transfer uses TCP delta mode with a full-sync interval of $20$ iterations; the periodic full syncs surface as the spikes in Figure cite106†16 .
L364: Evaluation. We evaluate on AIME24, AIME25, AMC, MATH500, and Minerva Math every $50$ training iterations. Following the shared protocol in Section cite26†A.1 , we sample $4$ generations per question at temperature $0.6$ and report $\mathrm{pass}@1\,(\mathrm{avg}@4)$.
L365: ### A.6 Delta-sparsity Measurement (Section cite20†4.2.2 , Figure cite107†17 )
L366: Models & Datasets. We instrument six independent RL training runs that span two axes: (i) model scale, training Qwen3-1.7B / 8B / 14B [cite101†Yang et al., 2025 ] on the DeepScaler math set [cite148†Luo et al., 2025b ]; (ii) task, training Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [cite149†Yang et al., 2024 ] on AlfWorld, WebShop, and search-based QA via the ASearcher workflow. All runs filter prompts to at most $2{,}000$ tokens (math) or $4{,}096$ tokens (agentic).
L367: Training.
