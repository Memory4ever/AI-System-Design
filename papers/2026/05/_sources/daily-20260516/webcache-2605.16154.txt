L71: # Learn Where Outcomes Diverge: Efficient VLA RL via Probabilistic Chunk Masking
L72: 
L73: Vaidehi Bagaria^{*}    Nikshep Grampurohit^{*}    Pulkit Verma^{†} Affiliation: Indian Institute of Technology Madras
L74: ###### Abstract
L75: Reinforcement learning (RL) allows vision-language-action (VLA) policies to generalize beyond their training distribution by optimizing directly for task success, but this post-training stage is computationally expensive. A natural response has been to speed up rollout data collection through faster simulators and world models.
L76: In GRPO-based VLA RL, however, we find that the dominant cost lies elsewhere: gradient computation accounts for $\sim$78% of wall-clock time per step in our runs, while rollout collection accounts for only $\sim$21%. Gradient cost dominates because much of this computation is spent on phases that contribute little to learning. GRPO’s learning signal is driven by advantage variance: only phases where successful and failed rollouts diverge produce useful learning signal.

L305: •   Exploration: Probabilistic $C_{c}$-weighted sampling (Eq. cite113†10 ) ensures that every phase has nonzero selection probability whenever its weight is above zero, providing the primary exploration mechanism.
L306: The $p_{\min}=0.1$ floor (Eq. cite112†9 ) acts as a safety net for low-scoring phases (e.g., tail): it prevents transient zero estimates from collapsing a phase’s weight to zero between buffer recomputes, which would otherwise cause online phase scores to fail to recover when the true $V_{c}$ ordering shifts during training.
L307: •   Adaptation: Approach and release-ramp allocations decline from $\sim$2 to $\sim$1 chunks and from $\sim$2.5 to $\sim$1.5 chunks, respectively, as the policy masters those phases, with budget shifting toward phases where learning signal concentrates. This online dynamic that adapts with evolving success-failure gap of the policy cannot be reproduced by static weighting.
L308: The ablations occupy degenerate corners of this design space. Random masking explores but lacks concentration, matching the suboptimal uniform-allocation regime in Theorem cite105†1 , where $b_{c}=B/K$ (equal budget across phases) is inefficient when $V_{c}$ is non-uniform across phases. Full masking concentrates updates on the highest-$C_{c}$ phase but discards weaker adjacent-phase signals and prevents exploration and adaptation.
L309: Only PCM has all three properties: $C_{c}$-weighted keep probabilities with a $p_{\min}$ floor to concentrate updates on contact-rich phases while retaining lower-scoring phases. The ablations show that variance-aware probabilistic selection substantially outperforms uninformed masking strategies in exploiting the concentrated structure of $C_{c}$.
L310: ## 6 Limitations and Future Work
L311: Our evaluation spans the three LIBERO suites (Object, Spatial, Goal), which test transfer of object, spatial, and task knowledge respectively (Table cite120†1 ). We do not test longer horizons or bimanual coordination; however, the underlying allocation principle applies whenever $V_{c}$ is non-uniform, a structural feature of any GRPO setting where the policy has not converged uniformly. Experiments use a gripper-based phase partition rule.
L312: Extension to other settings is straightforward in principle (any consistent decomposition suffices), though we do not validate alternative phase abstractions empirically. We do not directly benchmark against methods targeting sample efficiency through different objectives (e.g., entropy-based token selection or prompt filtering). These methods differ from PCM in both granularity (token vs. phase) and signal (policy-internal uncertainty vs.
L313: outcome-grounded divergence); adapting them to the VLA chunk-level setting is beyond the scope of this work.
L314: Future work includes extending PCM to longer-horizon and bimanual tasks, validating learned temporal phase segmenters or LLM-derived phase labelers, and applying the variance-allocation principle to LLM reasoning where $C_{c}$ can be computed against verified outcomes. Combining PCM with orthogonal sample-efficiency methods remains an open direction.

L241: ## 5 Empirical Evaluation
L242: 
L243: ### 5.1 Experimental Setup
L244: 
L245: #### Models and Benchmarks.
L246: 
L247: We run our experiments on OpenVLA-OFT (cite114†Kim et al., 2025 ), a 7B vision-language-action model that predicts 7-DoF action chunks with chunk length $L{=}8$. We evaluate on three benchmarks: LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Goal (cite70†Liu et al., 2023 ).
L248: #### Phase Labeling.
L249: We assign each chunk to one of $K{=}5$ semantic phases using a deterministic, multi-grasp-aware rule over the per-chunk gripper-close fraction $g_{f}[j]\in[0,1]$.
L250: Active-grip chunks ($g_{f}[j]\geq 0.5$) capture sustained grasp and transport; pre-grasp labels the up-to-three chunks immediately before sustained closure ($0.1\leq g_{f}[j]<0.5$); release-ramp labels the up-to-three chunks after; approach covers the remaining pre-contact chunks; and tail covers post-release open-gripper chunks; Overlapping windows are resolved by the priority ordering $\texttt{active-grip}>\texttt{pre-grasp}>\texttt{release-ramp}>\texttt{approach}>\texttt{tail}$.
L251: This rule is applied identically to successful and failed trajectories, enabling $C_{c}$ to compare outcomes under a shared phase partition. Full details are in Appendix cite56†E.2 .
L252: #### Training and Evaluation.
L253: We implement PCM on the SimpleVLA-RL (cite62†Li et al., 2025a ) verl (cite115†Sheng et al., 2025 ) pipeline with GRPO groups of 10 rollouts per prompt, comparing against the full-trajectory chunk-level GRPO baseline. We fine-tune OpenVLA-OFT with LoRA using 2 NVIDIA H100 GPUs. We evaluate every training step using 50 held-out validation rollouts. Results are averaged across three seeds, and we report per-step update time and peak GPU memory. Detailed experimental settings are in Appendix cite52†E .
L254: #### Research Questions.
L255: 
L256: Our experiments are designed to answer the following:
L257: 
L258:   * RQ1
L259: 
L260: Does variance-aware chunk selection preserve the learning dynamics of full-trajectory GRPO, measured by step-wise accuracy curves?
L261: 
L262:   * RQ2
L263: 
L264: Does selective gradient allocation reduce wall-clock time to reach a target success rate without affecting sample efficiency?
L265: 
L266:   * RQ3
L267: How sensitive is performance to the chunk budget $B$, and is there a stable operating point that balances gradient signal with per-step speedup?
L268: 
L269:   * RQ4
L270: 
L271: Is variance-aware probabilistic selection necessary, or do simpler masking strategies achieve comparable performance under the same chunk budget?

L273: #### RQ1: Learning Dynamics.
L274:

L276: We compare PCM with chunk budget $B{=}12$ against full-trajectory GRPO on LIBERO-Object using the same SFT-initialized OpenVLA-OFT checkpoint, identical rollout groups, rewards, advantages, and optimizer settings. L277: As a function of training step (Fig. cite117†3 b), PCM and GRPO exhibit nearly identical learning curves and reach matched final accuracy. This shows that PCM preserves per-step learning dynamics while improving efficiency, confirming that the selective gradient allocation does not degrade step-wise convergence behavior.

GRPO’s learning signal is driven by advantage variance: only phases where successful and failed rollouts diverge produce useful learning signal.
L77: However, GRPO assigns the same advantage to every chunk in a rollout. As a result, actor-update compute is spent uniformly across the trajectory, including phases the policy already handles after pre-training and supervised fine-tuning. This paper presents Probabilistic Chunk Masking (PCM), a drop-in modification to GRPO that allocates gradient computation to a small, probabilistically selected subset of chunks per trajectory.
L78: PCM scores semantic phases using success-failure action variance, a rollout-derived proxy for per-phase gradient variance, and samples a fixed chunk budget with online-updated phase-level keep probabilities. We formalize per-phase gradient variance as the quantity that determines where gradient computation is useful and show that success-failure action variance provides a measurable proxy for it. PCM requires no reward model or learned critic.

L241: ## 5 Empirical Evaluation
L242: 
L243: ### 5.1 Experimental Setup
L244: 
L245: #### Models and Benchmarks.
L246: 
L247: We run our experiments on OpenVLA-OFT (cite114†Kim et al., 2025 ), a 7B vision-language-action model that predicts 7-DoF action chunks with chunk length $L{=}8$. We evaluate on three benchmarks: LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Goal (cite70†Liu et al., 2023 ).
L248: #### Phase Labeling.
L249: We assign each chunk to one of $K{=}5$ semantic phases using a deterministic, multi-grasp-aware rule over the per-chunk gripper-close fraction $g_{f}[j]\in[0,1]$.
L250: Active-grip chunks ($g_{f}[j]\geq 0.5$) capture sustained grasp and transport; pre-grasp labels the up-to-three chunks immediately before sustained closure ($0.1\leq g_{f}[j]<0.5$); release-ramp labels the up-to-three chunks after; approach covers the remaining pre-contact chunks; and tail covers post-release open-gripper chunks; Overlapping windows are resolved by the priority ordering $\texttt{active-grip}>\texttt{pre-grasp}>\texttt{release-ramp}>\texttt{approach}>\texttt{tail}$.
L251: This rule is applied identically to successful and failed trajectories, enabling $C_{c}$ to compare outcomes under a shared phase partition. Full details are in Appendix cite56†E.2 .
L252: #### Training and Evaluation.
L253: We implement PCM on the SimpleVLA-RL (cite62†Li et al., 2025a ) verl (cite115†Sheng et al., 2025 ) pipeline with GRPO groups of 10 rollouts per prompt, comparing against the full-trajectory chunk-level GRPO baseline. We fine-tune OpenVLA-OFT with LoRA using 2 NVIDIA H100 GPUs. We evaluate every training step using 50 held-out validation rollouts. Results are averaged across three seeds, and we report per-step update time and peak GPU memory. Detailed experimental settings are in Appendix cite52†E .
L254: #### Research Questions.
L255: 
L256: Our experiments are designed to answer the following:
L257: 
L258:   * RQ1
L259: 
L260: Does variance-aware chunk selection preserve the learning dynamics of full-trajectory GRPO, measured by step-wise accuracy curves?
L261: 
L262:   * RQ2
L263: 
L264: Does selective gradient allocation reduce wall-clock time to reach a target success rate without affecting sample efficiency?
L265: 
L266:   * RQ3
L267: How sensitive is performance to the chunk budget $B$, and is there a stable operating point that balances gradient signal with per-step speedup?
L268: 
L269:   * RQ4
L270: 
L271: Is variance-aware probabilistic selection necessary, or do simpler masking strategies achieve comparable performance under the same chunk budget?

L273: #### RQ1: Learning Dynamics.
L274:
