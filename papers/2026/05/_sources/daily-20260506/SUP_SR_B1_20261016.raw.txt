L235: These conditions, however, are insufficiently studied by previous meta-learning methods except Text-to-LoRA^{1}^{1} 1 However, their experiment solely explores the adaptation on task-wise description..
L236: To learn-to-learn on such conditions, we reformulate such datasets with the format of $(x,y,z)$, where $z$ is the auxiliary annotation of those conditions. During the meta-training, $z$ is first inserted into different instructional prompts (depending on the condition type), then is used to adapt the model. During inference, the model produces the best output conditioned on the test $z$: $\arg\max{P(y^{\star}|x^{\star},z^{\star})}$.
L237: In this formulation, $z$ can semantically bridge the meta information between training and target tasks. Table cite125†2 summarizes different condition types, possible attributes, example prompts, and typical relative datasets.
L238: ## 5 Method
L239: 
L240: For problem proposed in Section cite20†4.2 , we introduce a novel meta-gating architecture, which is initialized from the original self-gating, driven by a hypernetwork with condition inputs. Post-hoc adaptation is then conducted on meta-tasks.

L242: ### 5.1 Meta-Gating
L243: #### Beta-SwiGLU.
L244: 
L245: To provide an extra DoF (degree of freedom) for meta-control, we reactivate the $\beta$ of SiLU ($\text{Swish}_{\beta=1}$ function (Eq (cite126†3 )); accordingly, the SwiGLU (Eq (cite127†4 )) becomes $\beta\text{-SwiGLU}$, with $\text{Swish}_{1}$ replaced by $\text{Swish}_{\beta}$:
L246: 
L247:  |  | $\displaystyle y=W_{\text{down}}\left(\text{Swish}_{\beta}\left(W_{\text{gate}}x\right)\otimes(W_{\text{up}}x)\right),\beta>0$  |  | (8)
L248: 
L249: where $\beta$ dynamically adjusts the slope of the Swish function.
L250: Apparently, when $\beta=1$, $\beta\text{-SwiGLU}$ decays to the original SwiGLU. Also, the gradient of $\text{Swish}_{\beta}$ is bounded by $1/4|x|^{2}$ with detailed derivation in Appendix cite51†A.2 . This property ensures that training $\beta\text{-SwiGLU}$ is friendly.
L251: #### The hypernetwork.
L252: We implement a lightweight hypernetwork which projects the textual condition $z$ into $\beta$ on each layer, denoting $\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(z,l)$. We first replace the placeholder of prompt $p$ (examples in Table cite125†2 ) with $z$, then process it with the tokenizer and embedding layer of the LLM itself. $\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(z)$ then implement a cross attention with a reduced dimension $R$, added with layer embedding, and finally feed into a one-layer, bias-free, MLP.
L253: In more details, on the $l$-th layer, the corresponding $\beta^{l}$ is generated by
L254:  | $\displaystyle z=$  | $\displaystyle\text{emb}(p(z))$  |  | (9)
L255:  | $\displaystyle e^{z}=$  | $\displaystyle\text{cross-attn}(\text{q=}W^{q}z,\text{k=}W^{k}e^{l},\text{v=}W^{v}e^{l})$  |
L256:  | $\displaystyle\beta^{l}=$  | $\displaystyle W(e^{z}+\text{layer-emb}(l)),l=1,2,\cdots,L$  |
L257:  |  | $\displaystyle W^{q},W^{k},W^{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{D\times R},W\in\mathcal{R}^{R\times C}$  |
L258: 
L259: where MLP is one-layer, bias-free, has an input-output dimension of $(R,C)$, and includes a final activation of $tanh$.
L260: ### 5.2 The Entire Model
L261: #### Re-formularized LLM.
L262: 
L263: We replace the SwiGLU of LLM with our $\beta\text{-SwiGLU}(x)$, with $\beta$ dynamically determined by the hypernetwork. The model can then be expressed as
L264: 
L265:  | $\displaystyle y=\mathcal{G}_{\{w,\beta^{l}=\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(z,l)\}}(x)$  |  | (10)

L374: ### 6.2 Datasets
L375: 
L376: MeGan is trained by meta-learning on a mixture of datasets, conditioned on different textual information:
L377: i) Persona: we use Persona-Chat (cite130†Zhang et al., 2018 ), which conditions the dialogue on unseen persona profiles.
L378: ii) Domain: AdaptSum (cite131†Yu et al., 2021 ) is employed to conduct the summarization task on 6 domains: debate, dialogue, email, movie review, science, social media, with low-resource training samples.
L379: iii) Task: we first adopt the experimental setting in (cite92†Min et al., 2022 ; cite93†Sinha et al., 2024 ) which employ the mixture of CrossFit (cite132†Ye et al., 2021 ) and UnifiedQA (cite133†Khashabi et al., 2020 ), covering a variety of tasks, and is classified into six transfer settings: HR$\rightarrow$LR, Class$\rightarrow$Class, non-Class$\rightarrow$Class, QA$\rightarrow$QA, non-QA$\rightarrow$QA, non-NLI$\rightarrow$NLI, and non-Para$\rightarrow$Para; the second experiment setting refers to (cite108†Charakorn et al., 2025 ), which uses the Super-NaturalInstructions (SNI) (cite134†Wang et al., 2022a ) as training and validation sets, while tests the model on ten generalized mainstream benchmarks, including Arc-challenge (ArcC), Arc-easy (ArcE), BoolQ (BQ), Hellaswag (HS), OpenbookQA (OQA), PIQA, Winogrande (WG), GSM8K, HumanEval (HE), and MBPP.

L424: ## 8 Conclusion
L425: In this paper, we propose MeGan, which conducts meta-control on LLMs with FFN gating adaptive to different conditions, such as task, domain, persona, style, and sentiment. We replace the SiLU activation in LLM by $\text{Swish}_{\beta}$, resulting in the new FFN block called $\beta$-SwiGLU. We further implement a hypernetwork which automatically converts textual conditions into $\beta$, resulting a meta-gating mechanism, adjusting the nonlinearity of activations upon different situations.
L426: This hypernetwork is then generalized on target tasks with dynamic condition comprehension. MeGan exhibits state-of-the-art performance on meta-learning.

L429: This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of meta-learning on LLMs. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.
