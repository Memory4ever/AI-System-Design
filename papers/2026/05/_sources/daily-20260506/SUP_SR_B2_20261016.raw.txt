L115: To avoid ambiguity, we distinguish the two diagonal operators used throughout the paper. The operator $\mathrm{Diag}\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ maps a vector $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ to the diagonal matrix whose $i$-th diagonal entry equals $a_{i}$ (with all off-diagonal entries equal to zero).
L116: Conversely, $\mathrm{diag}\!:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}$ extracts the diagonal of a matrix, i.e., $\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A})\triangleq[A_{11},A_{22},\ldots,A_{nn}]^{\top},$ where $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $A_{ii}$ denotes the $(i,i)$-th entry of $\mathbf{A}$. L117: ### 2.1 Hutchinson Trace and Diagonal Estimators
L118: ###### Definition 1 (Rademacher Vectors).
L119: 
L120: A random vector $\mathbf{z}=[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]^{\top}$ is a Rademacher vector if its entries $z_{i}$ are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d) random variables taking values in $\{-1,+1\}$, with probability $\Pr(z_{i}=\pm 1)=\tfrac{1}{2}.$
L121: 
L122: Let $\mathbf{z}\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}$ be a Rademacher random vector. Hutchinson’s trace estimator $T^{(s)}$ for a matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ is defined as:
L123:  | $$T^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{k=1}^{s}(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})^{\top}\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{z}^{(k)}.$$  |
L124: 
L125: It is well-established that $\mathbb{E}[T^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})]=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{A})$ and $\mathrm{Var}[T^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})]=\frac{2}{s}\,\|\bar{\mathbf{A}}\|_{F}^{2},$ where $\bar{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}-\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A}))$ denotes the matrix obtained from $\mathbf{A}$ by setting its diagonal entries to zero.
L126: The Hutchinson diagonal estimator can be understood as a localized extension of the trace estimator. While the trace estimator $T^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})$ for a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ collapses the quadratic form $\mathbf{z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}$ into a single scalar, the diagonal estimator preserves the entry-wise structure of the operator.
L127: Following the formulation of (cite54†Bekas et al., 2007 ), the diagonal of a matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ can be estimated by considering the Hadamard product of a probe vector $\mathbf{z}$ and its transformation $\mathbf{Az}$. Formally, given a sequence of $s$ number of i.i.d. Rademacher probe vectors $\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{s}$, Hutchinson’s diagonal estimator ${\mathrm{D}}^{(s)}$ is:
L128:  | $$\mathrm{D}^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{k=1}^{s}\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\odot(\mathbf{Az}^{(k)}),$$  |  | (2)
L129: 
L130: where $\odot$ denotes the element-wise (Hadamard) product.
L131: ### 2.2 Auxiliary Definitions
L132: 
L133: ###### Definition 2 (Riemann Zeta Function).
L134: 
L135: The Riemann zeta function is defined for real $s>1$ as
L136: 
L137:  | $$\zeta(s)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{s}}.$$  |
L138: 
L139: It is classical that the series converges for all $s>1$.
L140: ###### Definition 3 (Generalized harmonic number / partial zeta sum).
L141: 
L142: For $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}$, the generalized harmonic number is defined by
L143: 
L144:  | $$H_{n,s}\;:=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{s}}.$$  |
L145: 
L146: $H_{n,s}$ increases monotonically in $n$. $H_{n,s}$ converges to $\zeta(s)$ as $n\to\infty$ for every $s>1$.
L147: ###### Lemma 4 (Entrywise Spectral Decomposition).
L148: 
L149: Let $\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ be a symmetric matrix. Then there exists a set of orthonormal eigenvectors $\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}$ with corresponding eigenvalues $\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}$ such that $\mathbf{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\top}.$ In particular, the entries of $\mathbf{H}$ satisfy $H_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}u_{ki}u_{kj}$ where $u_{ki}$ is the $i$-th element of the $k$-th eigenvector.
L150: ## 3 Basis Selection with Importance (BSI)
L151: Our BSI framework establishes a principled selection rule that minimizes the expected loss increase incurred by dropping bases. It links the basis selection problem to the local geometry of the loss landscape by deriving an importance metric from the first- and second-order derivatives of the loss $\mathcal{\ell}$ with respect to the singular values. We first describe the framework design in Section cite14†3.1 , and then provide a detailed theoretical analysis in Section cite17†3.2 .

L300: For a general fixed matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, the Hutchinson diagonal estimator is unbiased, and its mean-square error satisfies $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|{\mathrm{D}}^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})-\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{A})\|_{2}^{2}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Var}\!\big({\mathrm{D}}^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})_{i}\big)\leq\frac{1}{s}\|\mathbf{A}\|_{F}^{2},$ which in general grows with the ambient dimension $n$.
L301: Consequently, worst-case guarantees for diagonal estimation typically deteriorate as the matrix dimension increases, unless additional structure information is imposed. L302: #### 3.2.3 A Probabilistic Guarantee Of Hessian Diagonal Estimator
L303: Variance analyses such as those in Section cite19†3.2.2 characterize convergence in expectation, but can be sensitive to large deviations (outliers) in the stochastic estimates. In this section, we derive an $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ probabilistic guarantee on the number of samples needed to estimate the Hessian diagonal. Definition cite66†10 formalizes this notion by requiring that the relative error exceeds $\varepsilon$ with probability at most $\delta$.
L304: Theorem cite67†11 bounds the number of samples $s$ needed for $\mathrm{D}^{(s)}$ to be a relative $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-approximator in terms of Hessian spectrum structure. Corollary cite68†12 presents a bound on the minimum number of samples necessary to reliably estimate the diagonal of a positive semidefinite Hessian.
L305: ###### Definition 10 (Relative $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-approximator).
L306: For any $\varepsilon>0$ and $0<\delta<1$, the diagonal estimator $\mathrm{D}^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})$ is a relative $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-approximator if $\text{ }\Pr\!\left(\left\|\mathrm{D}^{(s)}(\mathbf{A})-\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{A})\right\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{A})\right\|_{2}\right)\geq 1-\delta,$ where $s$ is the number of samples and $\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{A})$ denotes the diagonal of the matrix $\mathbf{A}$.
L307: ###### Theorem 11 (Probabilistic bound for Hessian Diagonal).
L308: 
L309: Assume $\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ is symmetric, $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{H})\neq 0$, and the power-law decay condition (Assumption cite64†8 ) holds, then the Rademacher diagonal estimator $\mathrm{D}^{(s)}(\mathbf{H})$ is a relative $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-approximator if
