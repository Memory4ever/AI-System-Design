23:  3 The Proposed Method 
24:  This section presents EvoJail, a novel multi-objective evolutionary framework fundamentally different from existing heuristic jailbreak-generation approaches. Unlike prior methods that rely on manually crafted transformations or differentiable surrogate models, EvoJail is designed to automatically uncover long-tail, hard-to-detect jailbreak strategies. To tackle the intrinsic challenges of (i) the complex representation of encryption-decryption logic, and (ii) the conflicting objectives of attack effectiveness and output quality, EvoJail introduces a unified multi-objective evolutionary framework for jailbreak prompt generation. It first formulates jailbreak generation as a bi-objective optimization problem that jointly maximizes attack effectiveness and minimizes output perplexity by adopting a semantic-algorithmic solution representation capable of capturing both high-level meaning and low-level structural transformations, and then integrates LLM-assisted operators into a population-based multi-objective evolutionary search, enabling adaptive and semantically informed mutation and recombination. The overall framework is illustrated in Fig. 1 . 
25:  3.1 Formulation of Long-Tail Distribution Attacks 
26:  From an optimization perspective, long-tail distribution attacks exhibit an inherent conflict between attack effectiveness and output quality, making them naturally amenable to a multi-objective formulation. However, unlike conventional multi-objective optimization problems studied in evolutionary computation, the decision variables in this setting are neither low-dimensional nor numerically parameterized, but instead consist of intertwined natural-language semantics and algorithmic transformation logic. 
27:  In particular, the core of an encoded jailbreak attack lies in constructing a pair of encryption and decryption functions ( E , D ) (E,D) that jointly satisfy three properties: 1) semantic obfuscation, where the encrypted query appears benign or nonsensical at the surface level; 2) algorithmic consistency, requiring exact recoverability of the original malicious intent; and 3) distributional deviation, where the resulting prompt lies in the long-tail region of the model’s training distribution. These requirements give rise to highly discrete, non-linear, and non-differentiable transformation spaces. For example, practical encryption-decryption schemes often involve conditional branching, sequence re-grouping, index-dependent operations, and rule-based reversals, as illustrated in Fig. 2 . Such structures cannot be faithfully captured by traditional vector-based encodings or parametric representations commonly assumed in classical multi-objective optimization. 
28:  As a consequence, directly applying standard mathematical formulations with explicitly defined decision variables severely restricts the expressive power of the search space, making it difficult to uncover structurally novel and semantically coherent jailbreak strategies. This limitation motivates the need for a representation that can model what the transformation intends to achieve and how it is algorithmically realized. 
29:  3.1.1 End-to-End Attack Pipeline 
30:  The long-tail distribution attacks is modeled as an end-to-end transformation pipeline, in which malicious intent is concealed through algorithmic obfuscation and later reconstructed within the model’s internal reasoning process. 
31:  Let q q denote the original malicious query. An encryption function E E and a corresponding decryption function D D constitute the core components of the attack. For the approach to be valid, the decoding process is required faithfully recover the original query, i.e., 
32:  D ⁡ ( E ⁡ ( q ) ) = q . D(E(q))=q. 
33:  The encrypted query q ′ = E ⁡ ( q ) q^{\prime}=E(q) and the decryption logic D D are embedded into a predefined prompt template T T , yielding the adversarial prompt T ⁡ [ q ′ , D ] T[q^{\prime},D] . This construction aligns with prior observations that template-based contextual embedding provides a more reliable interface for guiding model behavior [ 23 , 35 , 15 , 48 ] . 
34:  Formally, the long-tail distribution attack pipeline consists of four sequential stages: 
35:  Encryption: transform q q into an obfuscated query q ′ = E ⁡ ( q ) q^{\prime}=E(q) to conceal malicious semantics; 
36:  Decryption specification: provide explicit or implicit instructions D D to recover the original semantics; 
37:  Prompt construction: embed ( q ′ , D ) (q^{\prime},D) into a template T T to establish contextual coherence; 
38:  Model invocation: submit T ⁡ [ q ′ , D ] T[q^{\prime},D] to the target LLM to elicit responses that reveal the original intent without triggering safety mechanisms. 
39:  This pipeline highlights that attack success emerges from the joint interaction of semantic context and algorithmic structure, rather than from isolated perturbations.

44:  where 
45:  h h is a natural-language heuristic describing the design principle of the encryption-decryption scheme, 
46:  e e and d d denote the concrete implementations of the encryption and decryption functions, respectively, 
47:  t t is a prompt template sampled from a predefined template pool 𝒯 prompts \mathcal{T}_{\text{prompts}} . 
48:  The natural-language heuristic h h captures abstract strategies such as reordering, symbolic substitution, or conditional decoding, which are difficult to enumerate explicitly yet critical for exploring diverse long-tail attack patterns. The algorithmic components ( e , d ) (e,d) ensure functional correctness and reproducibility, while the template component t t is decoupled from optimization to constrain search complexity. 
49:  Under this representation, the attack instances constitute a hybrid semantic-algorithmic search space that cannot be expressed as a conventional Euclidean decision space.

60:  3.2 LLM-Assisted Evolutionary Multi-Objective Optimization-Based Long-Tail Distribution Attacks 
61:  As discussed in the previous section, the design of long-tail distribution attacks exhibits two fundamental characteristics: the presence of multiple conflicting objectives, and an open-ended, highly structured solution space defined by natural-language semantics and programmatic encryption-decryption logic. Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) provide a natural optimization paradigm for such problem [ 50 ] . 
62:  However, conventional MOEAs are not directly applicable in this setting as their effectiveness critically depends on the compatibility between the evolutionary operators and the underlying solution representation. Classical mutation and crossover operators are typically defined over numeric vectors, permutations, or fixed-length symbolic strings, and rely on random perturbations or predefined recombination rules. In contrast, individuals in long-tail distribution attacks consist of tightly coupled natural-language semantics and executable encryption-decryption programs subject to strict reversibility constraints. Blind random perturbations are highly likely to break syntactic validity or logical reversibility, while handcrafted rule-based operators struggle to produce semantically meaningful and structurally novel long-tail behaviors, leading to inefficient exploration and heavy reliance on human expertise. 
63:  Recently, LLMs have introduced new opportunities for evolutionary computation [ 41 ] . Prior studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of LLM-assisted evolutionary optimization in various domains such as autonomous MOEAs [ 12 ] , recommendation systems [ 8 ] , automated programming [ 17 ] , and protein design [ 38 ] . A common insight across these works is that embedding LLMs into evolutionary operators allows the search process to be guided by semantic understanding and domain knowledge, thereby improving efficiency and solution quality in highly structured, non-numeric search spaces. 
64:  Inspired by these advancements, we propose an LLM-assisted evolutionary algorithm, termed EvoJail, which retains the population-based multi-objective optimization structure of MOEAs while embedding LLMs into the design of evolutionary operators. By leveraging the semantic understanding and program synthesis capabilities of LLMs, EvoJail enables adaptive, structure-aware generation, mutation, crossover, and repair of candidate attack individuals, thereby transforming MOEA into a semantically informed evolutionary search process. The algorithm is illustrated in Fig. 1 , with the corresponding pseudocode given in Algorithm 1 . 
65:  3.2.1 LLM-Assisted Generation Operators 
66:  A central component of the EvoJail algorithm is the use of a unified LLM-assisted generation procedure that serves as the core operator for initialization, mutation, and crossover. The pseudo-code is given in Algorithm 2 . Rather than relying on handcrafted genetic operators, EvoJail leverages a design model θ d \theta_{d} to generate structurally valid and semantically meaningful individuals under different prompt configurations, where p b ​ u ​ i ​ l ​ d ∈ { p i ​ n ​ i ​ t ​ i ​ a ​ l ​ i ​ z ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n , p m ​ u ​ t ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n , p c ​ r ​ o ​ s ​ s ​ o ​ v ​ e ​ r } p_{build}\in\{p_{initialization},p_{mutation},p_{crossover}\} 
67:  The initialization prompt p i ​ n ​ i ​ t ​ i ​ a ​ l ​ i ​ z ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n p_{initialization} instructs the design model to generate a new individual that preserves the stylistic structure of an ancestor while introducing a distinct encoding logic, as shown in Fig. 3 . The output is constrained to a syntactically correct Python dictionary containing a heuristic description and paired encode-decode algorithms. This design yields several benefits. Firstly, by encouraging variations in word order, sorting criteria, data structures, and metadata, the initial population spans a broad region of the structural search space, thereby introducing early structural diversity. Secondly, the code-level constraints reduce the prevalence of ill-formed individuals in early generations. Thirdly, throughout the search process, character-level encodings (e.g., Base64 or Rot13) are explicitly excluded, as such transformations are known to degrade attack effectiveness on smaller models due to their limited capability to interpret low-level symbol manipulations [ 51 ] . 
68:  Mutation and crossover, p m ​ u ​ t ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n p_{mutation} and p c ​ r ​ o ​ s ​ s ​ o ​ v ​ e ​ r p_{crossover} , are performed using a shared genetic prompt, as shown in Fig. 4 , distinguished by their operational context that the mutation operates on a single parent, while crossover combines two. The evolutionary feedback is explicitly injected into the generation process. Each parent individual is annotated with a parent improvement status (Better, Equal, Worse, Mixed, or Unknown) based on changes in its expected performance relative to its predecessors. This information enables the design model to reason about which structural components are beneficial and which are potentially harmful. The prompt further provides refined and advanced structural guidance, encouraging the model to retain effective components while increasing token distribution unpredictability, introduce structure-aware reordering, grouping, or indexing strategies, embed lightweight, reversible metadata, and exploit data structures such as stacks, trees, or probabilistic groupings. As a result, mutation and crossover become adaptive, semantics-aware transformations rather than random perturbations, substantially improving search efficiency in the highly structured solution space. 
69:  Besides, since strict reversibility is difficult to guarantee in natural language-driven generation, EvoJail incorporates a verification-repair loop p r ​ e ​ p ​ a ​ i ​ r p_{repair} , as shown in Fig. 5 . For each candidate, the encryption-decryption pair is tested against a reference input. If reversibility fails, the design model is allowed up to R R targeted repair attempts using a dedicated repair context. Importantly, individuals that remain irreversible after repair may still be retained if the decoded output is executable, preventing excessive contraction of the search space. This mechanism balances structural rigor with exploratory flexibility and is critical for discovering long-tail strategies. Finally, a template t t is sampled at random from the predefined template pool T prompts {T}_{\text{prompts}} , extended from [ 35 , 23 ] , and combined with the encryption-decryption logic to form a final candidate attack. 
70:  3.2.2 Details of the Evolutionary Search 
71:  On the basis of the LLM-assisted generation, EvoJail operates within a standard multi-objective evolutionary optimization loop. The algorithm starts from an ancestor set 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , which contains representative long-tail distribution strategies [ 23 , 51 ] , including the Queue, OddEven, Length, Stack, and Reverse. The Queue employs the first-in-first-out principle with a cyclic shift and rotates the words forward by one position, OddEven segregates words by their index parity by placeing all odd-positioned words first, followed by even-positioned words, Length orders words by their character count while preserving original indices, Stack operates on the last-in-first-out principle and encrypts the sentence by reversing the word order, and Reverse reverses the entire word sequence. With the ancestor set, an initial population P 0 P_{0} of size N N is generated using the LLM-assisted generation with p i ​ n ​ i ​ t ​ i ​ a ​ l ​ i ​ z ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n p_{initialization} and p r ​ e ​ p ​ a ​ i ​ r p_{repair} , where Deepseek-v3 [ 18 ] is adopted as the designer model θ d \theta_{d} . After the initialization, candidate individuals are iteratively generated, evaluated, selected, and transformed to optimize both attack effectiveness and output quality. 
72:  At each generation, individuals are evaluated under two objectives: attack effectiveness and output perplexity. Parent selection follows a hybrid strategy to balance exploitation and exploration: with equal probability, either the top-d individuals ranked by attack effectiveness are selected, or a non-dominated binary tournament based on Pareto dominance is applied. Variation is introduced via the LLM-assisted mutation and crossover operators with p m ​ u ​ t ​ a ​ t ​ i ​ o ​ n p_{mutation} , p c ​ r ​ o ​ s ​ s ​ o ​ v ​ e ​ r p_{crossover} and p r ​ e ​ p ​ a ​ i ​ r p_{repair} . Population update is performed using fast non-dominated sorting [ 5 ] . After T T generations, the algorithm outputs an optimized population P ∗ P^{*} , prioritizing individuals on the first Pareto front and ranking them by attack effectiveness.

83:  4.2 Results and Discussions 
84:  Based on the HV results shown in Table 1 , the proposed EvoJail algorithm outperforms baseline methods in 15 out of the 18 test scenarios. Specifically, in all scenarios involving LLaMA series models, EvoJail’s HV values consistently led by a large margin. On the LLaMA8B, EvoJail repeatedly surpassed 0.700 (e.g., LLaMA8B-G2 reached 0.770), while methods such as FlipAttack and CodeAttack showed jailbreak failure that their ASR is nearly 1, and although CodeChameleon was relatively stable, it did not reach optimal levels. 85:  To illustrate the diversity of the algorithms on the Pareto front, Fig. 6 shows the dominance diagram of the solution set corresponding to the median HV performance from multiple experiments. As can be seen from this figure, there is a clear conflict between the ASR and the PPL. Specifically, the solutions generated by the baseline methods or EvoJail exhibit a mutually balancing distribution in the target space. Regarding Pareto dominance and coverage, the solution set obtained by EvoJail dominates all baselines in the G1-G3 scenarios of LLaMA-7B, occupying the optimal lower-left region, and again achieves complete dominance in LLaMA-8B-J3. Even in scenarios without strict dominance (such as J1, J2 and the G series of LLaMA-7B/8B), its solution set still shows a wider distribution in the target space, particularly excelling in maintaining PPL smaller than 10. EvoJail demonstrates superior performance in balancing attack effectiveness and output quality: maintaining high ASR and extremely lower PPL in LLaMA-8B-G, while CodeAttack achieves similar higher ASR but significantly higher PPL. These subplots collectively show that EvoJail exhibits advantages in the diversity and optimality of the Pareto front comparing with these baselines. In the remaining scenarios outside of LLaMA-7B, EvoJail and solutions on the baseline, such as CodeAttack, together constitute the fitting results of the current algorithm to the approximate Pareto front in the encryption/decryption design space. These results suggest that EvoJail can systematically generate high-quality and diverse attack strategies during design phases, and can successfully explore different and equally optimal regions on the Pareto front in jailbreak encryption-decryption algorithms design.

When the integration scale is small, the sampled strategies may excel in only one objective, leading to a non-dominant relationship with the baseline (i.e., occasional victories and defeats). Because these above results suggest that the model’s architecture and training fine-tuning strategies also decided the performance of different jailbreak algorithms [ 37 , 10 , 29 ] . 
108:  As shown in Fig. 11 , with the increase in the number of integration strategies (from 3 to 16), EvoJail demonstrates significant and robust performance improvement, which suggests the superiority of its methodology. First, the ASR shows a monotonic increasing trend across all experimental groups, with the growth rate gradually slowing down. On the one hand, this intuitively reflects the submodular benefit pattern of the integration strategies; on the other hand, and more importantly, it reveals that EvoJail, through its multi-objective evolutionary search framework, generates attack strategies with high diversity and complementarity. The strategies introduced early on significantly broaden the attack surface, quickly increasing ASR; as the integration scale expands, although the marginal gains between strategies decrease, the ASR continues to grow, which further proves the richness of the strategy pool—i.e., EvoJail can systematically discover a large number of heterogeneous and effective attack patterns.Although PPL fluctuates in certain scenarios (such as LLaMA-7B-J and GPT-G), there is an overall downward trend. This indicates that during the integration process, high-ASR but high-PPL strategies are gradually supplemented or replaced by those with better attack effectiveness and output fluency. 
109:  In contrast, EvoJail generates a set of strategies automatically, with the advantage of not relying on manual priors and being able to adaptively generate diverse strategies across different scenarios. Therefore, this result does not negate the value of the automated framework but also more deeply confirms EvoJail’s core capability as an automated attack framework: it can continuously generate a large number of diverse and high-quality Pareto-optimal strategies, providing a solid foundation for building a highly adaptive integrated attack system. 
110:  5 Conclusion 
111:  In this work, we introduced EvoJail, the first automated framework for generating long-tail distribution attacks against LLMs using multi-objective evolutionary search. By eliminating reliance on handcrafted rules, EvoJail enables systematic evaluation of LLM security and privacy vulnerabilities across diverse user-generated inputs in real-world Web environments. The framework combines semantic and algorithmic representations with LLM-assisted operators to efficiently explore the encryption-decryption prompt space, producing adaptive, interpretable, and diverse attack strategies. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EvoJail achieves competitive attack effectiveness and superior output quality while providing a Pareto front of strategies that support flexible and robust evaluation. Our findings highlight the importance of automated, multi-objective approaches for assessing and improving the safety and robustness of deployed LLMs, offering actionable insights for the development of more resilient defense mechanisms. Looking forward, future work could leverage insights from EvoJail to develop adaptive defenses against long-tail attacks, improving model robustness through targeted mitigation [ 49 ] and learning-based optimization strategies [ 36 , 32 , 30 ] that evolve alongside emerging threats.
