2026\nL66: # Latent Compaction with Test-Time Training\nL67: \nL68: Yingfa Chen    Xingyu Shen    Chaojun Xiao    Xu Han Affiliation: NLP Group, DCST, IAI, BNRIST, Tsinghua University, Beijing, China Email: chenyingfa1999@gmail.com\nL69: ###### Abstract\nL70: Long contexts enlarge key–value (KV) caches, increasing memory use and attention costs during generation. Compressing these caches must preserve information for unseen questions. We introduce Latent Compaction, which learns a compact document-specific cache through test-time training. We initialize cache entries using reconstruction importance, teacher attention, and key-space diversity.\nL71: Training first fits attention outputs and log-normalizers to those of the full cache, with reconstruction-selected entries held fixed. It then jointly optimizes the entire cache through end-to-end distillation from the full-cache model. Model weights remain frozen throughout, and training uses only generic prompts about the document, without access to downstream questions or answers.\nL72: Experiments on RULER, LongBench, and Phonebook Lookup demonstrate substantial gains over budget-matched compression baselines. On a RULER, for example, our method achieves 96.7% accuracy at $32\\times$ compression, compared with 40.0% for KVZip+. The method also transfers across model scales and families, with retrieval gains on both Qwen3 and MiniCPM5.\nL73: These results show that treating the KV cache as a neural network and optimizing it with test-time training can preserve retrieval accuracy under aggressive compression.^{1}^{1} 1 The code is released at cite54†https://www.github.com/THUNLP/latent-compaction†www.github.com L74: ## 1 Introduction\nL75: \nL76: Large key–value (KV) caches are a major bottleneck to efficient LLM inference: long contexts limit batch sizes and increase memory-bandwidth demands at each decoding step (cite55†Zhang et al., 2023 ; cite56†Liu et al., 2024 ), motivating context compression.\nL77: Frontier-model platforms already offer context compaction to sustain conversations beyond a single context window (cite57†Anthropic, 2026 ; cite58†OpenAI, 2026 ). Claude, for example, replaces older context with a text summary. Summaries can omit details whose importance only becomes clear later (cite59†Anthropic, 2025 ), motivating compression of the model’s KV representation directly.\nL78: We study document compression before downstream questions arrive: the cache must preserve information for unseen queries, while preparation costs can be amortized across repeated use.\nL79: Eviction methods select original KV entries using attention or reconstruction (cite60†Li et al., 2024 ; cite61†Cai et al., 2025 ; cite62†Kim et al., 2025 ). Cartridges learns compact caches through self-study (cite63†Eyuboglu et al., 2025 ), while Attention Matching fits attention outputs and mass (cite64†Zweiger et al., 2026 ).\nL80: These approaches motivate test-time training of document memory (cite65†Sun et al., 2020 ): can gradient descent on a compact KV cache preserve document information under an aggressive memory budget?\nL81: We introduce Latent Compaction, which trains a document-specific KV cache with frozen model weights. Initialization combines globally selected preservers with anchors and diversifiers. Stage 1 fixes preservers and fits the other entries to full-cache attention outputs and log-normalizers. Stage 2 jointly optimizes all entries using the model’s predictions on generic document prompts. Downstream questions and answers are excluded; the trained cache is fixed during reuse.\nL82: For a 4K document at $32\\times$ compression, each layer/head averages 96 preservers and 32 anchors and diversifiers.\nL83: Experiments on RULER, LongBench, and Phonebook Lookup demonstrate the benefits of Latent Compaction across synthetic retrieval and natural-document understanding tasks. The method substantially improves retrieval accuracy over budget-matched compression baselines across context lengths and compression ratios, while LongBench results show gains on broader long-context tasks.\nL84: Additional Phonebook Lookup experiments demonstrate transfer across model scales and families: the retrieval advantages extend to multiple Qwen3 model sizes and MiniCPM5 models. Ablations identify the roles of initialization, both training stages, and attention normalization. These results concern logical KV-pair budgets rather than measured memory savings or inference speedups.\nL85: Our contributions are:\nL86: \nL87:   * •\nL88: \nL89: A novel method for learning compact context representation trained with gradient descent and initialized through complementary selection rules.\nL90: \nL91:   * •\nL92: \nL93: A document-specific test-time training procedure that learns from the frozen model’s attention and predictions without access to downstream questions or answers.\nL94: \nL95:   * •\nL96: Superior performance on a wide range of empirical downstream evaluation across three benchmarks and multiple model scales and families, with ablations of initialization, training, and attention normalization.\nL97: \nL98: Figure 1: Latent Compaction and baselines across compression ratios and context lengths on RULER and Phonebook Lookup with Qwen3-1.7B.\nL99: ## 2 Related Work\nL100: #### KV-Cache Eviction and Budget Allocation.\nL101: Eviction methods retain selected original KV pairs. H_{2}O combines recent and high-attention tokens (cite55†Zhang et al., 2023 ), while SnapKV selects positions using an observation window (cite60†Li et al., 2024 ). PyramidKV varies budgets across layers (cite61†Cai et al., 2025 ), and Ada-KV adapts budgets across heads (cite66†Feng et al., 2025 ). KVZip scores each entry by its maximum attention weight during context reconstruction (cite62†Kim et al., 2025 ).\nL102: KVZip+, introduced by cite67†Jégou and Jeblick (2026) , rescales each weight by the projected value norm divided by the input hidden-state norm before taking the maximum. This accounts for each value’s relative contribution to the residual stream. Their KVzap method learns lightweight predictors of these scores to accelerate pruning. We use KVZip+ scores to initialize preservers before cache optimization. We evaluate query-agnostic SnapKV and PyramidKV adaptations that withhold downstream questions.\nL103: #### Learned Context Representations.\nL104: Prefix-tuning optimizes continuous prefixes with frozen model weights (cite68†Li and Liang, 2021 ). AutoCompressors and the In-context Autoencoder learn compression mechanisms across examples, producing compact summary vectors or memory slots (cite69†Chevalier et al., 2023 ; cite70†Ge et al., 2024 ). Cartridges instead optimizes a compact KV cache for an individual corpus through self-study and context distillation (cite63†Eyuboglu et al., 2025 ).\nL105: Attention Matching fits full-cache attention outputs and mass through key selection, biases, and values (cite64†Zweiger et al., 2026 ). Our related stage-1 loss is followed by end-to-end refinement of all entries. RestoreKV generates restoration entries using a shared LoRA mechanism (cite71†Baek et al., 2026 ); we optimize document-specific slots directly.\nL106: #### Test-Time Training.\nL107: Test-time training (TTT) adapts model parameters using a self-supervised objective on an unlabeled test input before prediction (cite65†Sun et al., 2020 ). For language models, cite72†Hardt and Sun (2024) fine-tune model weights on retrieved nearest-neighbor text for each test input.\nL108: TTT layers make an online-learned model the recurrent hidden state (cite73†Sun et al., 2025 ), while TTT-E2E updates Transformer weights on the input context through next-token prediction, with a meta-learned initialization (cite74†Tandon et al., 2025 ). Latent Compaction instead updates document-specific KV slots in an existing frozen Transformer, using full-cache teacher attention and predictions.\nL109: It prepares the compact cache before downstream questions are known and reuses that cache across questions without further optimization.\nL110: Figure 2: Latent Compaction training pipeline. Preservers, anchors, and diversifiers are initialized from the original cache. Stage 1 freezes preservers and fits anchors and diversifiers to full-cache attention outputs. Stage 2 refines all entries through end-to-end distillation.\nL111: ## 3 Method: Latent Compaction\nL112: \nL113: Latent Compaction constructs a compact, trainable KV cache for a document before downstream questions arrive. Model weights remain frozen throughout, and the optimized cache is reused across questions.\nL114: In what follows, Section cite13†3.1 defines the memory architecture and its interaction with subsequent tokens. Section cite16†3.2 explains the preserver, anchor, and diversifier initialization and budget allocation. Sections cite18†3.3 and cite19†3.4 describe attention fitting and end-to-end refinement, respectively. Section cite20†3.5 details the generic teacher rollouts that provide supervision for both stages.\nL115: #### Preliminaries: Attention and KV Caching.\nL116: \nL117: Consider a query head and its associated KV head in one Transformer layer. Let $q,k_{i},v_{i}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote the query and the key and value at cache entry $i$, where $d$ is the head dimension. Let $\\mathcal{I}$ index entries causally visible to $q$: document entries plus any question or generated-token entries at or before its position. The attention output $A(q)\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and scalar normalizer $Z(q)>0$ are\nL118:  | $$A(q)=\\frac{\\sum_{i\\in\\mathcal{I}}\\exp\\left(q^{\\top}k_{i}/\\sqrt{d}\\right)v_{i}}{Z(q)},\\qquad Z(q)=\\sum_{i\\in\\mathcal{I}}\\exp\\left(q^{\\top}k_{i}/\\sqrt{d}\\right),$$  |  | (1)\nL119: \nL120: Queries and keys include positional encoding. An $N$-token document stores $2LHNd$ key/value scalars across $L$ layers and $H$ KV heads; grouped-query attention shares each KV head among multiple query heads. We compress this document cache for reuse across questions. Below, $s=1/\\sqrt{d}$ is the attention scale.\nL121: ### 3.1 Memory Architecture\nL122: \nL123: Given an average budget of $B$ KV pairs per layer and KV head, we initialize $LHB$ KV slots in total. The number of slots may vary across layer–head pairs. Each slot also carries a scalar attention bias to compensate for the change in attention mass caused by compression.\nL124: #### Attention Biases.\nL125: \nL126: For one layer and KV head, let $n$ be its number of compacted document entries. Omitting layer/head indices, let $K_{\\mathrm{cmp}},V_{\\mathrm{cmp}}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n\\times d}$ contain their keys and values as rows, and let $b_{\\mathrm{cmp}}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n}$ contain their biases, initialized to zero. For a query head associated with this KV head, $q\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$. Following Attention Matching (cite64†Zweiger et al., 2026 ), biases adjust attention mass:\nL127:  | $\\displaystyle\\ell(q)$  | $\\displaystyle=sK_{\\mathrm{cmp}}q+b_{\\mathrm{cmp}},$  |  | (2)\nL128:  | $\\displaystyle A_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q)$  | $\\displaystyle=V_{\\mathrm{cmp}}^{\\top}\\operatorname{softmax}(\\ell(q)).$  |  | (3)\nL129: The score vector $\\ell(q)\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and its softmax weights have one component per compacted entry; $A_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q)\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the resulting per-head attention output. A bias $b_{j}\\in\\mathbb{R}$ multiplies entry $j$’s unnormalized weight by $e^{b_{j}}$, adjusting both its relative contribution and the document’s total attention mass. Cache parameters are learned per document and fixed during downstream inference.\nL130: #### Handling Subsequent Tokens.\nL131: \nL132: Question and generated tokens append ordinary, unbiased KV pairs directly after the document, outside its budget and without renumbering positions. For a query $q_{t}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ at token position $t$, define the scalar compressed-cache attention normalizer\nL133: \nL134:  | $$Z_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q_{t})=\\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\\top}\\exp(\\ell(q_{t})).$$  |  | (4)\nL135: Here $\\mathbf{1}_{n}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is the all-ones vector and exponentiation is elementwise. Let $A_{\\mathrm{new},t}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $Z_{\\mathrm{new},t}\\geq 0$ be the attention output and scalar normalizer over causally accessible question and generated-token entries for $q_{t}$ (taking both as zero if there are none). The combined output $A_{t}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is\nL136:  | $$A_{t}=\\frac{Z_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q_{t})A_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q_{t})+Z_{\\mathrm{new},t}A_{\\mathrm{new},t}}{Z_{\\mathrm{cmp}}(q_{t})+Z_{\\mathrm{new},t}}.$$  |  | (5)\nL137: \nL138: This equals a single softmax over compressed and subsequent entries, with learned biases applied only to the former. Normalizers and mixture weights are computed in log-space.\nL139: ### 3.2 Initialization\nL140: \nL141: We initialize compact caches from original KV pairs using three complementary kinds of entries:\nL142: \nL143:   * •\nL144: \nL145: Preserver. We rank the KVZip+ reconstruction scores of all original KV pairs from every layer and KV head together (cite62†Kim et al., 2025 ; cite67†Jégou and Jeblick, 2026 ). The highest-scoring pairs fill one total preserver budget, so layer–head pairs can receive different counts.\nL146: \nL147:   * •\nL148: Anchor. One quarter of the anchor–diversifier budget uses original pairs with high normalized teacher attention. We also include the first document position as a potential attention sink (cite75†Xiao et al., 2024 ).\nL149: \nL150:   * •\nL151: \nL152: Diversifier. In each layer and KV head, choose each new key farthest from its nearest anchor or previously selected diversifier. Preservers are excluded from the distance reference set.\nL153: Preservers are frozen in stage 1; all entries train in stage 2. Anchors and diversifiers also train in stage 1. The rules combine high-scoring original entries with attention-informed seeds and broad key-space coverage.\nL154: #### Budget Allocation\nL155: \nL156: With an average budget of $B$ entries per layer and KV head, we reserve $M=B/4$ per head for anchors and diversifiers. The other $LH(B-M)$ slots come from one ranking of KVZip+ scores across all layers, KV heads, and document positions. One quarter of each head’s $M$ slots are anchors: the first document entry and the highest-scoring remaining entries under maximum teacher attention,\nL157: \nL158:  | $$a_{i}=\\max_{q\\in\\mathcal{Q}}\\left[\\operatorname{softmax}(sKq)\\right]_{i},$$  |  | (6)\nL159: where $i\\in\\{1,\\ldots,N\\}$ indexes the original document positions, $K\\in\\mathbb{R}^{N\\times d}$ contains their keys for the corresponding layer and KV head, $s=1/\\sqrt{d}$, and $\\mathcal{Q}\\subset\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ contains teacher queries from the associated query heads (Section cite20†3.5 ). Thus $a_{i}$ is a scalar score for document position $i$. The first entry counts as an anchor.\nL160: The remaining entries are diversifiers, which are selected by farthest-first traversal (cite76†Gonzalez, 1985 ). Starting with the set of anchor indices $S\\subseteq\\{1,\\ldots,N\\}$, we repeatedly add\nL161: \nL162:  | $$i^{\\star}=\\arg\\max_{i\\notin S}\\min_{j\\in S}\\|k_{i}-k_{j}\\|_{2}^{2}$$  |  | (7)\nL163: \nL164: until $|S|=M$, where $k_{i}$ is row $i$ of $K$. The set $S$ starts with anchors and grows only with diversifiers, never with preserver selections. Selected keys keep their original values; stage 2 optimizes all entries.\nL165: ### 3.3 Training Stage 1: Attention Fitting\nL166: \nL167: We fit each layer independently using captured teacher queries. The attention outputs and log-normalizers are computed over the complete document cache, including the frozen preservers:\nL168: \nL169:  | $$\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{attn}}=\\operatorname{MSE}\\left(\\widehat{A}_{\\mathrm{cmp}},\\widehat{A}_{\\mathrm{full}}\\right)+\\operatorname{MSE}\\left(\\widehat{\\log Z}_{\\mathrm{cmp}},\\widehat{\\log Z}_{\\mathrm{full}}\\right).$$  |  | (8)\nL170: For each captured query, $A_{\\mathrm{cmp}},A_{\\mathrm{full}}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ are compressed- and full-document attention outputs, while $\\log Z_{\\mathrm{cmp}},\\log Z_{\\mathrm{full}}\\in\\mathbb{R}$ are their log-normalizers. MSE averages over captured queries and the relevant output dimensions. Hats denote standardization using fixed teacher statistics per query head: output statistics are computed over query positions and channels, and log-normalizer statistics over query positions.\nL171: Standard deviations are floored at $10^{-4}$. Matching log-normalizers calibrates document attention relative to subsequent tokens. Anchor and diversifier keys, values, and biases receive updates; preserver keys, values, and biases remain frozen in stage 1.\nL172: ### 3.4 Training Stage 2: End-to-End Refinement\nL173: We refine all cache entries jointly. At step $t\\in\\{1,\\ldots,T\\}$ of a length-$T$ teacher continuation, $p_{t},r_{t}\\in[0,1]^{|\\mathcal{V}|}$ are the full-cache and compressed-cache probability vectors over vocabulary $\\mathcal{V}$ under the same text prefix; $p_{t,v}$ and $r_{t,v}$ denote their components for token $v$.\nL174: We retain the teacher’s top-20 token set $\\mathcal{T}_{t}\\subset\\mathcal{V}$ individually and group the rest into a tail category with probabilities $p_{t,\\mathrm{tail}}=1-\\sum_{v\\in\\mathcal{T}_{t}}p_{t,v}$ and $r_{t,\\mathrm{tail}}=1-\\sum_{v\\in\\mathcal{T}_{t}}r_{t,v}$. Both models use the teacher’s token set, without renormalizing the top-20 probabilities. We minimize\nL175:  | $\\displaystyle\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{KL}}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\frac{1}{T}\\sum_{t=1}^{T}\\left[\\sum_{v\\in\\mathcal{T}_{t}}p_{t,v}\\log\\frac{p_{t,v}}{r_{t,v}}+p_{t,\\mathrm{tail}}\\log\\frac{p_{t,\\mathrm{tail}}}{r_{t,\\mathrm{tail}}}\\right],$  |  | (9)\nL176:  | $\\displaystyle\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{LM}}$  | $\\displaystyle=-\\frac{1}{T}\\sum_{t=1}^{T}\\log r_{t,c_{t}},\\qquad\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{stage2}}=\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{KL}}+\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{LM}}.$  |  | (10)\nL177: The two terms inside the brackets match the retained tokens and the total tail mass, respectively; individual tail tokens are not distinguished. The standard LM loss $\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{LM}}$ increases the compressed-cache model’s full-vocabulary probability of the teacher’s greedy token $c_{t}=\\arg\\max_{v}p_{t,v}$. The two losses have equal weight and are averaged across sequences; cache size remains fixed.\nL178: ### 3.5 Teacher Rollouts for Document-Specific Supervision\nL179: \nL180: A teacher rollout is the frozen model’s response to a generic prompt about document $D$. Let $C_{D}^{\\mathrm{full}}$ be the cache obtained by prefilling $D$, and let $\\{u_{j}\\}_{j=1}^{J}$ be the fixed bank of $J=480$ text prompts (24 document requests paired with 20 response instructions; Appendix cite51†D ). The prompts exclude downstream evaluation questions and answers.\nL181: #### Generating Rollouts.\nL182: \nL183: Each prompt starts from an independent fork of $C_{D}^{\\mathrm{full}}$. With frozen model weights $\\theta$, the teacher distribution and greedy continuation are\nL184: \nL185:  | $\\displaystyle p_{j,t}(v)$  | $\\displaystyle=P_{\\theta}(v\\mid C_{D}^{\\mathrm{full}},u_{j},y_{j,<t}),$  |  | (11)\nL186:  | $\\displaystyle y_{j,t}$  | $\\displaystyle=\\arg\\max_{v}p_{j,t}(v),\\qquad 1\\leq t\\leq T_{j}\\leq 24.$  |\nL187: Generation stops at an end-of-sequence token or after 24 tokens; prompts never share continuation history.\nL188: #### Using the Rollouts.\nL189: \nL190: Let $q_{\\ell,h}^{(j,s)}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the teacher query at position $s$ while processing prompt $u_{j}$ and continuation $y_{j}$ in layer $\\ell$ and query head $h$. Stage 1 collects\nL191: \nL192:  | $$\\mathcal{Q}_{\\ell,h}(D)=\\{q_{\\ell,h}^{(j,s)}:1\\leq j\\leq J,\\ 1\\leq s\\leq|u_{j}|+T_{j}\\}.$$  |  | (12)\nL193: It matches full- and compressed-document attention outputs and log-normalizers on these queries using Equation cite77†8 , with preservers frozen. In stage 2, the compressed cache $C_{D}^{\\mathrm{cmp}}$ predicts under the same teacher-generated prefix:\nL194: \nL195:  | $$r_{j,t}(v)=P_{\\theta}(v\\mid C_{D}^{\\mathrm{cmp}},u_{j},y_{j,<t}),\\qquad\\mathcal{L}_{D}=\\frac{1}{J}\\sum_{j=1}^{J}\\mathcal{L}_{\\mathrm{stage2}}\\bigl((p_{j,t},r_{j,t})_{t=1}^{T_{j}}\\bigr).$$  |  | (13)\nL196: Thus the student receives teacher tokens rather than its own predictions (teacher forcing). Equation cite78†10 defines each rollout loss. Stored teacher top-20 logits and the vocabulary log-normalizer recover its top-20 probabilities and tail mass; both stages use the same fixed rollouts.\nL197: Figure 3: Comparison of compression methods on LongBench.
