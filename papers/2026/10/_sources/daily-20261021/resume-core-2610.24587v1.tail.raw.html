Appendix C Perturbation Catalogue

This section supplements the LIBERO-Pro2.0 results with definitions of all 22 static and 20 dynamic perturbations and the task partition used in the controlled eight-task studies. The examples in Appendix B illustrate these definitions; the nominal, unperturbed task is the BASE reference.

Design scope.

Perturbations cover object and scene geometry, appearance, sensing, temporal alignment, action execution, robot state, and language. Each type shares a semantic definition across suites; affected objects, magnitudes, directions, and event settings are specified per task. These parameter choices do not define additional perturbation types.

Task validity.

The original goal and success predicate are preserved. Geometry and state changes retain reachable, collision-free configurations and feasible task completion. Planar pose changes combine horizontal translation and yaw; pitch and roll are excluded from this category. Physical parameters such as mass, inertia, friction, stiffness, and damping are not varied. Grasp loss and robot displacement are represented by injected outcome states or bounded control interventions, rather than simulated impact or falling forces.

Initialization and runtime events.

Static perturbations are introduced at reset; altered scene or fault settings persist while normal robot and object motion remains possible. Dynamic perturbations are triggered at task-relevant phases, such as approach, grasp, transport, or placement. Trigger conditions, duration, and release rules are specified by each case. Restoring a sensor or control channel does not imply rolling back the complete scene. Experiment-specific freeze or waiting durations are distinct from these general mechanism definitions.

C.1 Category Definitions and Assignment Principle

We assign each perturbation to one category according to its primary intervention target. The categories organize perturbations by their dominant operational effect and are not mutually exclusive descriptions of every downstream consequence. Condition perturbations modify task-relevant object or receiver configurations, relative layouts, or geometric constraints on approach and placement. Environment perturbations modify object instances or scene context, including clutter, distractors, occlusion, visual-domain appearance, support-surface appearance, and external visual conditions such as camera motion or lighting changes. Observation perturbations modify the formation, quality, availability, or synchronization of the inputs delivered to the policy. Execution perturbations modify control timing or action execution. Robot perturbations modify robot initialization, calibration, or robot-related state and manipulation outcomes. Language perturbations modify instruction wording while preserving task semantics. Some perturbations may affect multiple aspects of task execution; each is assigned according to its primary intervention target. This mapping is used consistently to compute the category averages in Tables 1–3.

C.5 Evaluation Protocol

We evaluate all policies from fixed checkpoints under identical benchmark settings. Each policy receives observation modalities matching its architecture, while environment settings, perturbation parameters, and task success criteria are held constant across all models. We retain each policy’s original action representation and prediction horizon unless explicitly altered in diagnostic ablation experiments. No privileged information about the perturbation is available to the policy in standard evaluation runs.

An episode is marked successful only if the agent fulfills the original task completion requirements within the evaluation time horizon. Episodes that end due to timeout or failed manipulation are counted as failures.

Our main evaluation metric is task success rate (SR). For model mm and perturbation case cc, we define

C.6 Training Setup

Training Data.

We fine-tune π0.5\pi_{0.5} using a mixture of perturbation-augmented demonstrations and original LIBERO demonstrations. For each of the ten perturbation cases across five static–dynamic families, we select two tasks from each of the four LIBERO suites, yielding eight training tasks per case. We collect ten demonstrations per task, resulting in 10×8×10=80010\times 8\times 10=800 perturbation-augmented trajectories. To preserve the policy’s original manipulation capabilities, we additionally include standard LIBERO demonstrations. Specifically, we sample 20 demonstrations from each of the 40 original tasks across the four suites, yielding another 40×20=80040\times 20=800 trajectories. The two datasets are mixed at a 1:1 ratio, resulting in a total of 1,600 training trajectories.

Fine-tuning Configuration.

We adopt Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of π0.5\pi_{0.5}. The LoRA rank is set to 32 for the Gemma vision-language model (VLM) and 16 for the Gemma action head. We use an effective batch size of 144 and train for one epoch over the mixed dataset.
