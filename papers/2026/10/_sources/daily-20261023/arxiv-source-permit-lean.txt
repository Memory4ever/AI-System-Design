E Scope of the Lean 4 Verification
The Lean artifact is available upon request and will be released with the code when the paper is published. The artifact contains the executable single-user interpreter, proofs, build assertions, dependency manifest, and axiom report. It pins Lean 4 and Mathlib to v4.31.0 (Mathlib revision fabf563a ); lake exe cache get && lake build reproduces the check. Supplement S9 gives the complete correspondence table, ten modeling deltas, and the specification corrections discovered during mechanization.
What is checked.
action_integrity and action_integrity_from establish the single-user action guarantee from the empty endorsed store and from any state satisfying the mechanized invariant. reasoning_integrity and reasoning_integrity_from prove the transcript-conditioned comparison from empty or shared endorsed seeds, for arbitrary agent strategies and independently chosen attacks. Their conclusions include equal sink logs, operations, replies, and final protected views. Paste presence and content can differ. Supporting results check reply isolation, mandatory handle gating, and attack-surface confinement. A standalone batch layer checks displayed-element flow, rejection behavior, and transcript determinacy; executable assertions cover the baseline counterexample and eight scenarios.
What remains a paper proof.
The run-level integration of batched iteration, durable endorsements from accepted positions of an aborted invocation, arbitrary mid-run matched starting states, and the reduction from reactive actors to event-indexed oracles are proved in the supplement, outside the checked interpreter. Options O1 – O2 are checked at the gate level only; their wider integration, Option O3 , the federated protocol, probability bounds, and confirmation-budget results are not mechanized. The checked interpreter returns one selected element at a direct collection-handle call and records additional transcript-determined selection information. These representation differences and their correspondence arguments are explicit in S9 .
Trusted base and artifact.
The artifact’s clean-build axiom report lists only propext and Quot.sound for the top-level theorems, with no sorryAx ; the executable assertions additionally trust Lean’s evaluator. Kernel checking certifies the mechanized statements, not the correspondence between that model and a deployed harness or the reliability of a verifier. The artifact includes the full source, CORRESPONDENCE.md , pinned build instructions, the build log, and source checksums. Thus the concise statements here remain auditable against both the complete paper proofs and the precise mechanized scope.

Appendix S1 Structure of the Proof, Notation, and Conventions
S1.1 Organisation and register conventions
Sections S1 – S8 contain the formal
development for PerMIT, with a machine-checked single-user core
(§ S9 ) and register-separated normative, proof,
and rationale layers; the empirical appendices are indexed in the guide
in Appendix A of the main paper.
The material is organised into three strictly separated registers.
Normative register (Appendices S2 , S4 ).
The memory-interface semantics, the interpreter and protocol rules
( R1 – R14 ), and the assumptions ( A1 – A9 ; the budget-model assumptions
A10 – A13 are stated with the model they govern). Every rule clause states what
the harness does, tersely; no rule contains its own justification. A rule
clause states normative behaviour tersely, relying only on the
normative definitions and conventions it cites; rationale and
empirical interpretation are never needed to implement it.
Proof register (Appendices S3 , S5 ,
S6 ).
Definitions, lemmas, and theorems. Proof-register text
cites the normative register and the named invariants of
§ S1.4 ; it never cites the non-normative register.
The single-user results of Appendix S3 are additionally
machine-checked in Lean 4 over the map-free operation algebra, with the
batch-confirmation surface of Rule R9 mechanized as a
standalone layer; the store-level integration of 𝗆𝖺𝗉 \mathsf{map} into the
run-level interpreter and Option O3 remain outside the mechanized
surface, under the disclosed deltas recorded in § S9 .
Non-normative register (Appendix S7 and commentary
blocks).
Design rationale (Notes N1 – N18 ), the small-type commentary
blocks defined below, and a security and robustness analysis
(Analyses S1 – S18 ): an indexed catalogue of
attack vectors and failure modes, each with its disposition
by formal fact, and its status: disposed (under stated
assumptions; a formal fact disposes
of it) or residual (a genuine limitation, disclosed); and
either entering only the budget constants or not used in
any proof . Four rationale remarks whose content adjoins specific results
are set as numbered Remarks adjacent to the results they annotate,
Remarks S4.7 ,
S5.19 , S5.5 , and S5.26 . Nothing normative
lives in this register; no proof depends on it.
Commentary blocks. Throughout the normative and proof
registers, short passages set in small type under the heading
Commentary (non-normative) carry the rationale, epistemic
status, and validation pointers for the material they adjoin, so that a
rule or assumption and its justification can be read together without
the justification entering the register. Commentary blocks belong to
the non-normative register in function: no rule reads them, no proof cites
them, and a reader who skips every commentary block loses no
hypothesis and no step of any proof.
Statement discipline. A theorem, lemma, proposition,
corollary, definition, or invariant environment contains
hypotheses, the conclusion, and bare pointers (“established by”,
“see Remark”); interpretation, comparison, special cases, and design
justification are set in a numbered remark adjacent to the result they
concern.
Cross-referencing convention. The formal development
may point
into the non-normative register only by a parenthetical key, e.g. “(rationale:
N2 ; analysis: S11 )”; the non-normative register may cite the
formal development freely. Consequently the formal development is self-contained: a reader who
skips Appendix S7 entirely loses no hypothesis and no
step of any proof. A reader with a specific concern should consult
the attack and limitation index (Table S3 ), which
maps concerns to analysis entries and to the formal facts that govern
them.
