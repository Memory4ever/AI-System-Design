Appendix A Method Details
This section complements the method description in the main paper.
We first formalize the pointwise alignment, then describe the practical construction of the relational objectives and the training workflow.
 A.1 Pointwise Alignment
We implement pointwise feature alignment as a controlled alternative to RGF, following the representation-alignment principle of REPA  ( Yu et al. 2024 ) .
Let
 d_{\mathrm{cos}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=1-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{y}}{\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_{2}\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{2}} 
 (A.1) 
denote cosine distance.
For video alignment, the VGGT patch grid is bilinearly resampled to the corresponding WAM feature grid.
The pointwise video objective is
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{v}=\mathbb{E}_{t,v}\left[\frac{1}{N_{v}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{v}}d_{\mathrm{cos}}\left(g_{\mathrm{vid}}^{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell,t,v,i}^{v}),\widetilde{\mathbf{p}}_{t,v,i}\right)\right], 
 (A.2) 
where \widetilde{\mathbf{p}}_{t,v,i} is the geometric target at the same spatial location.
For action alignment, we use only spatially pooled patch features, excluding VGGT camera tokens.
Each action step t is paired with the observation at the same step, and the geometric target for view v is
 \mathbf{q}_{t}^{v}=\operatorname{Pool}(\mathbf{P}_{t}^{v}) .
The action projector output is reshaped to V\times d_{g} , yielding one vector per view:
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{a}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V}d_{\mathrm{cos}}\left(\left[g_{\mathrm{act}}^{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell,t}^{a})\right]_{v},\mathbf{q}_{t}^{v}\right)\right]. 
 (A.3) 
When enabled, the video and action pointwise terms each use a loss weight of 0.5 .
The video-only control disables \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pw}}^{a} while keeping all other settings unchanged.
For the video-only DINOv2 pointwise control, final-layer ViT-L patch features replace VGGT features, and the video projector output dimension is adjusted accordingly  ( Oquab et al. 2024 ) .
 A.2 Relational Objectives
We extract final-layer features from a frozen VGGT and cache them before WAM training  ( Wang et al. 2025 ) .
For video supervision, cached VGGT features are selected at RGB frames corresponding to the VAE latent timesteps, and their patch grids are bilinearly resampled to the WAM grid.
The projected WAM features and geometric targets are normalized along the feature dimension before constructing the full cosine relation matrices.
For intra-frame relations, we compute all feature pairs within each camera view and remove self-relations on the diagonal.
For cross-view relations, we enumerate every unique view pair v<u and retain all cross-view entries.
For action-temporal supervision, the 32-step action sequence is paired with 32 pooled scene descriptors obtained from the corresponding consecutive observations.
This construction is independent of the nine-frame video subsampling and therefore requires no temporal interpolation.
Rows and columns associated with padded action steps are masked, and action self-relations are removed.
For an entry-wise residual e , we use the smooth L1 (Huber) penalty
 \rho_{\beta}(e)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{2}}{2\beta},&|e|<\beta,\\[3.0pt]
|e|-\dfrac{\beta}{2},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\beta=1. 
 (A.4) 
The masked relation loss is
 \mathcal{D}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{R}^{s},\mathbf{R}^{g})=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}\rho_{1}\!\left(R_{ij}^{s}-R_{ij}^{g}\right), 
 (A.5) 
where \Omega contains the valid entries defined above.
The loss is averaged over valid entries, views, timesteps, and samples.
The intra-frame, cross-view, and action-temporal terms are equally weighted, and the overall RGF weight is \lambda_{\mathrm{RGF}}=0.5 .

 Appendix B Implementation Details
This section documents the model configurations and training settings used in all experiments.
 B.1 Model Architecture
All models are initialized from the pretrained Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion model  ( Wan et al. 2025 ) .
The pretrained video DiT serves as the video expert, while the action expert is initialized by linearly interpolating the video-DiT weights to the smaller hidden dimension.

RGF uses separate two-layer MLP projection heads with a SiLU activation.
The video and action heads map 3072\!\rightarrow\!2048 and 1024\!\rightarrow\!2048 , respectively.
For the DINOv2 control, the video projection dimension is changed to 1024 to match the ViT-L features.
All projection heads are removed at inference.
We evaluate three WAM paradigms under this shared backbone  ( Yuan et al. 2026 ) .
The Uncond paradigm predicts actions directly from the current visual context, while retaining video co-training.
The Joint paradigm jointly denoises future video and action representations.
The IDM paradigm first predicts future video representations and then conditions action prediction on them.
 B.2 Training Hyperparameters
We use AdamW with a learning rate of 1\times 10^{-4} , weight decay of 10^{-2} , and a cosine schedule following a linear warm-up over the first 5% of training.
Training uses bf16 mixed precision, a per-device batch size of 16, and gradient clipping at 1.0.
LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0 models are trained for 10 and 5 epochs, respectively.
At inference, all models use 10 denoising steps and a classifier-free guidance scale of 1.0.
 B.3 Compute Usage
LIBERO experiments are trained on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs and require approximately 16 hours per run.
RoboTwin 2.0 experiments use 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs and require approximately 65 hours per run.
