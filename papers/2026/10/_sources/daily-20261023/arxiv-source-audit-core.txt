5 The refresh trap: acting on audit collapse
Proposition 2 describes what the aggregate audit prefers. Deployment makes the
problem operational: a low monitor value can trigger collection of fresh policy samples and a
verifier refresh. The mixture law predicts two distinct hazards. Label mismatch: if a
component verifier is refreshed against the full outcome y y , optimization explicitly moves it
toward the audit’s preferred entangled predictor. Support mismatch: even with the correct
component labels F F , a saturated policy may provide almost no F F -failures, so the refresh data
contain little or no information about the decision boundary the verifier must retain. The second
hazard means the problem is not fixed simply by choosing the “right” label.

Empirical conclusion.
Across the study, five of six registered predictions held. More importantly, the evidence follows
the mechanism at four levels: the audit rewards entanglement, optimization removes own-failure
support, refresh causes held-out and cross-generator component forgetting, and that forgetting
raises downstream violations. The failed prediction is informative rather than cosmetic: the
monitored AUC is not reliably repaired by refresh, strengthening the narrower conclusion that it
is an unsuitable control signal. The successful rescue identifies the actionable fix used in the
next section: preserve or deliberately reconstruct the component’s own failure distribution.
