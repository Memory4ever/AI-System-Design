 Reader–Writer Coupling in Transformer MLPs 
 
 
 Giyeong Oh
  
     
 Sunghee Ahn
  
     
 Youngjae Yu
   
  Affiliation:  Seoul National University
 
  Email:    {hard2251,snusnowhite,youngjaeyu}@snu.ac.kr  
     

  Abstract 
    
 A transformer MLP gives each hidden channel two roles: a reader that
decides when the channel responds, and a writer that decides what it adds
back to the residual stream. Read as a key–value
memory, the layer can be measured one factor set at a time: the pairing of readers to writers is carried only by their shared index, invisible
to any quantity computed from one factor set alone. Yet training couples the two factors through their joint update,
so we ask what reading them together shows. The
block supplies the joint instrument: the MLP writes back into the
very stream it reads, so the exact Jacobian of its update is square,
and its agreement with its own transpose resolves into reader–writer
terms whose inner products give each channel, read as a  couple , a signed  pairing
coordinate . Ranked by this coordinate, each block we measure has a small
 core  of strongly coupled channels; where initialization gives either sign equally, the core is nearly all opposed: each writer points against the direction its reader responds to. This
opposed core recurs from the smallest models trained from scratch to
released checkpoints; its sign is in place
within a few percent of training and holds while the coupling grows.
Read as couples, most channels return their contribution against the
reader that fired it, and the readers of later blocks take it up; few
show in the logits. Training holds the couples in place: flipping the
opposed writers moves the output toward common tokens, and a writer
permuted onto another reader regrows to fit that reader while the reader
stays. 
  
 
 
 
 
 
     
 
 
 
 
  1  Introduction 

 
 
  “Neurons that fire together, wire together.”  
 — Attributed to   Hebb (1949)   
 
 
 
 A transformer MLP is a short detour out of the residual stream: an
expansion reads the current state into a bank of hidden
features  ( Elhage et al., 2022 ;  Bricken et al., 2023 ) , and a
contraction writes their activated contributions back. Described as a
key–value memory, each channel    h  h    carries a reader     u  h   u_{h}    that decides when
it fires and a writer     v  h   v_{h}    that says what it
adds  ( Geva et al., 2021 ;  Geva et al., 2022 ;  Geva et al., 2023 ) .
Values projected to the vocabulary agree with their keys’ trigger contexts
far above chance  ( Geva et al., 2021 ;  Dai et al., 2022 ) , and editing
methods refine the contraction as one associative
memory  ( Meng et al., 2022 ;  Meng et al., 2023 ) . What all of these works see
and refine are the two factor sets
while reader is bound to specific writer through the shared index. 
 
 
 That binding is the MLP’s own structural coordinate. Attention
remakes its pairings by content  ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ) , but the
channelwise assignment is written into the weights once and shared across
positions and inputs. Every gradient step that reaches a channel updates
both of its factors at once, so whatever training leaves in a channel
it leaves in the pair, and an analysis that takes one factor at a
time discards what lies between them, the  coupling ,
by an identity of the parameterization: a quantity computed from one
factor set alone, reader norms, writer singular values, their histograms, is
unchanged by any re-pairing of writers to
readers  ( Phuong & Lampert, 2020 ;  Şimşek et al., 2021 ) . The joint quantities on
record  ( Du et al., 2018 ;  Ji & Telgarsky, 2018 ;  Gurnee et al., 2024 ;  Gerstner et al., 2026 ) 
are read pair by pair or against mismatched pairs; the comparison here
places the whole assignment among its re-pairings (  Section   2  ). At initialization, the
channels are drawn independently, so the assignment is exchangeable
and the parameterization supplies its own reference ensemble
(  Section   3  ).  Is which reader is bound to which
writer a learned fact about the trained network, or only the index
it is stored under?  
 
 
 Answering it takes both factor sets together with the direction a reader took in and the one its writer put back. The block hands over exactly that in how its update changes when the
state it read is nudged, the exact Jacobian     J  ⁡   (  x  )    J(x)    of the
update  ( Elhage et al., 2021 ) : the weight product prior work reads,
taken at the input the block saw. In the pre-norm blocks we measure  ( Xiong et al., 2020 ) ,    J  J   
factors into the two learned sets and the normalization alone
(  Section   3  ). Because the block adds to the stream it
reads,    J  J    is square, and comparing the operator with its own
transpose, the symmetric–skew split of an update
map  ( Bhatia et al., 2012 ;  Im et al., 2016 ) , scores how
readers and writers are paired in one scalar that is blind to polarity. Channel by channel, the trace carries the signed
pairing coordinate     m  h   m_{h}   , the writer pointing with or against its own
reader (  Figure   1  ). 
 
 
 We trained three sizes of pre-norm decoders on FineWeb-EDU  ( Penedo et al., 2024 ) ,  tiny  (29M),
 small  (124M) and  medium  (405M), three seeds each, and measured them beside released
models (  Section   5.1  ). Read as couples, a reader and its
writer are a pair that training made.
The trained pairing lies beyond every sampled re-pairing
(  Section   5.2  ), permuted writers regrow toward their own readers
(  Section   5.3  ).
The strongest pairs of a block, the  core , are opposed, and
amplifying members appear only at the ends of the stack (  Section   5.5  ). On the models trained here the sign is readable from the weights alone, and it covers the population, not only the core (  Section   5.4  ). The sign appears within the first few percent of training, block by block in an order
and at steps shared across seeds, before the coupling has grown; it forms on natural language and not on the same tokens with their order removed (  Sections   5.5   and   5.6  ).
Most channels are wired against what fires them: the contribution returns
against the reader that fired it and is taken up by the readers of later
blocks, and the vocabulary projection describes
the few that reach the
logits, most of them in the last blocks (  Section   5.7  ).
In summary, this paper contributes: 
 
  •  
 
  A couple view of MLPs.  We expose the learned
reader–writer correspondence by comparing assignments of fixed factor
sets (  Section   4  ). 
  
  •  
 
  Opposed strongest couples.  Trained assignments carry
excess Jacobian symmetry, and their strongest couples are opposed, the
writer pointing against its own reader. 
  
  •  
 
  Formation before growth.  The sign precedes the growth
of the coupling, in an order and at steps shared across seeds, and does
not form without the corpus’s token order. 
  
  •  
 
  A census of couples.  Every channel of the medium seeds
is read as a couple; most act through the readers of later blocks, and
flipping the opposed writers shifts outputs toward common tokens.

  2  Related Work 

 
 MLP keys, values, and neurons. 

 
 Transformer MLPs are read as key–value memories: expansion rows select
contexts, contraction columns write concept
directions  ( Geva et al., 2021 ;  Geva et al., 2022 ;  Geva et al., 2023 ;  Dai et al., 2022 ) , and editing methods update the contraction map as one
linear associative memory  ( Meng et al., 2022 ;  Meng et al., 2023 ) .
Value projections include concepts and function
words  ( Geva et al., 2021 ;  Geva et al., 2022 ) . Neuron censuses add writers
that oppose their own triggers  ( Voita et al., 2024 ) , writers graded
by token frequency  ( Stolfo et al., 2024 ) , and families of
prediction, suppression, alphabet, and entropy neurons labelled with
language-model assistance  ( Bills et al., 2023 ;  Gurnee et al., 2024 ) , the vocabulary our census uses (  Section   5.7  ). 
 
 
 
 Both factors at once. 

 
 Same-index input–output cosine is reported for GPT-2 and Pythia,
with most high-frequency neurons near-opposed and
unexplained  ( Gurnee et al., 2024 ) ; on gated models the same
weight-level cosine, with the gate’s sign gauged away, sorts neurons into
strengthening and weakening against mismatched
pairs  ( Gerstner et al., 2026 ) , or reads the gate row, whose
activation is mostly positive  ( Avrahamy et al., 2026 ) . Related
readings include same-index vocabulary agreement of
  Geva et al. (2021)  , whole-projection
products  ( Thakur & Khoury, 2026 ) , balancedness of deep-linear
factors  ( Du et al., 2018 ;  Ji & Telgarsky, 2018 ) , untied autoencoder Jacobian
symmetry  ( Im et al., 2016 ) , and path-geometry
invariants  ( Neyshabur et al., 2015 ) . All read weights or mismatched pairs;
the operator statistic places the whole assignment among bijective
re-pairings. 
 
 
 
 Hidden symmetries and model matching. 

 
 Hidden-unit permutations yield equivalent parameterizations and
structure loss landscapes  ( Phuong & Lampert, 2020 ;  Şimşek et al., 2021 ) ;
matching them enables model comparison, linear mode
connectivity, and merging  ( Garipov et al., 2018 ;  Draxler et al., 2018 ;  Entezari et al., 2022 ;  Frankle et al., 2020 ;  Ainsworth et al., 2023 ;  Singh & Jaggi, 2020 ;  Tatro et al., 2020 ) . Rather than quotienting relabeling away, we use the
common permutation as an exact negative control, and the one-sided
ensemble to probe relations within one model (  Section   4.1  ). 
 
 
 
 Local Jacobian and update geometry. 

 
 Modern Hopfield networks read attention as an energy
update  ( Ramsauer et al., 2021 ) ; Energy Transformers and
dynamical-network designs construct symmetric or antisymmetric operators  ( Hoover et al., 2023 ;  Chang et al., 2019 ;  Erichson et al., 2021 ) . The symmetric–skew split describes local update
geometry  ( Bhatia et al., 2012 )  and is traced across
training for self-attention  ( Saponati et al., 2025 ) ;
residual-stream studies report depth-structured spectral
geometry  ( Fernando & Guitchounts, 2026 ;  Elhage et al., 2021 ;  Shai et al., 2024 ) , and a corpus-averaged Jacobian lens reads
which tokens an activation promotes  ( Gurnee et al., 2026 ) . These
take the operator’s symmetry as given; we measure what the trained
assignment contributes to it.

  4  MLP Coupling 

 
 The coupling of an MLP is what training leaves between each reader
and its own writer. We look at it four ways: as a property of the whole
update, compared with its own transpose (  Section   4.1  ) and

as a signed pairing coordinate on each channel, which ranks a block’s core
(  Section   4.3  ); and as what a channel does at its
firing token, read through its writer’s vocabulary projection and its
effect on later readers (  Section   4.4  ). Appendix   A   summarizes the notation
used throughout the paper.

  6  Conclusion and Discussion 

 
 An MLP channel in a pre-norm transformer is a pair that training makes,
not a key and a value that happen to share an index. The trained
assignment stands apart from every sampled re-pairing of the same factors,
in the models we trained and in released ones, and permuted writers regrow
toward their own new readers. The pair carries a sign, and for most
channels the sign is opposed: the contribution runs back against the
reader that fired it, and the readers of the next blocks take it up. The
block’s update pushes back along most directions, the strongest pairs are
also opposed through most of the stack, and the sign is set in the first
steps, block by block in an order shared across seeds, before the coupling
has grown, and only when the corpus keeps its token order. Few channels
show in the next-token logits, and those that do sit in the last blocks; the
vocabulary projection describes them alone, and the rest are read by the
readers of later blocks. What remains as the unit is the pair: one reader,
one writer, and the sign between them. 
 
 
 Read this way, several directions open. A feature can be read on both
sides at once, and a dictionary latent is itself such a pair, an encoder row
and a decoder column under one
index  ( Bricken et al., 2023 ;  Huben et al., 2024 ) ; whether a
transcoder trained to replace the block inherits the sign of the couples it
replaces, or loses it, is a test this measurement makes available. Along the residual
path, stream couples hand their contribution to the readers of later blocks
(  Appendix   G  ), with stream work in the middle blocks and
vocabulary work at the end, as in the workspace-to-motor order of the
Jacobian lens  ( Gurnee et al., 2026 ) . The pair is also a candidate unit for
pruning  ( LeCun et al., 1989 ;  Sun et al., 2024 )  and editing  ( Meng et al., 2022 ) : a
few percent of channels carry half of a block’s squared coupling, and an edit
to a writer alone can change it. Along training most early
cores keep their sign while their vocabulary content keeps turning over
(  Section   F.2  ), a handle for following channels across
checkpoints  ( Olsson et al., 2022 ;  Biderman et al., 2023 ) . One thing stays
strange: a block’s strongest pairs work against their own
readers. Why training settles there, and what it does for the block that
reads next, we leave open.

 Transported-path check of the stream register. 

 
 The stream register predicts each later reader’s response along the residual
path alone. We check that prediction against the full model: the channel’s
activation at the firing position is scaled by     1  ±  0.5   1\pm 0.5   , and the central
difference of every later block’s pre-activations is the transported
first-order response, which includes the paths through the intervening
attention and MLP blocks (curvature below     2  %   2\%    of the response; the numbers
are unchanged at step sizes    0.25  0.25    and    0.125  0.125   ).
At the next block the transported response is the predicted one (cosine
   0.99  0.99   , with most of the strongest responders shared).
With distance the transported response keeps the predicted direction while
its coefficient on the prediction shrinks, to about    0.6  0.6    four blocks on and
about a fifth at twelve, never turning negative; summed over all later
blocks the predicted energy matches the transported total at the median.
The strongest in-context pushers keep their projection because they sit in
the last blocks; the core, which sits early, is attenuated the most.
The pattern is consistent with net attenuation of the contribution by the
intervening computation; which blocks attenuate it is not identified here.
Run on every channel of the three seeds (  Figure   7(b)  ), the
distance profile is the same in each seed to within    0.01  0.01    and the same for
every family and class of the census.

  Appendix I  Couple Census Protocol 

 
 The couple census of   Section   5.7   labels every channel of the three
medium seeds with a fixed taxonomy.
The labels are annotations produced by language models under a frozen
protocol; the quantities that enter any share, denominator, or class boundary
are computed from the measurements of   Appendix   C   and never read by a
model.
This section states the protocol end to end: what evidence each channel
receives, which model reads it at each stage, how the taxonomy was built and
frozen, how it was applied, and how its reliability is reported.
  Figure   26   gives the flow from the stored profiles to the final
classes. 
 
  
  Figure 26:   From profiles to classes. 
Stored channel profiles (activation contexts, replayed forward) yield three
registers per channel: the input that fires it, the contribution’s
first-order response on later readers, and its direct push on the
next-token logits, at the strongest firings and at typical activations.
Grey stages are measured; orange stages are language-model annotation,
with the engine of each stage under its column. 
 
 
  I.1  Evidence per channel 

 
 Each channel receives one evidence block in three registers.
The input register lists the 48 highest conditional-mean input tokens of the
channel profile and 42 activation contexts at ranks 1–32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 192,
256, 384, 512, 768, and 1000 of the channel’s strongest firings, with the
firing token marked, followed by 28 further contexts drawn uniformly from the
activation regimes below the top 1,000 (eight each from the 50th–90th,
90th–99th, and 99th-percentile-to-top-1000 bands of the channel’s positive
activations, and four from the floor regime at or below     −  0.15   -0.15   ), with the
channel’s population statistics.
The stream register reports, at those positions, the contribution’s return on
its own reader, the later readers it most often raises and lowers with the reader
labels of the first stage, the share of the response energy carried by the 32 strongest readers,
and the same quantities by regime (  Appendix   G   for the
validity of the first-order response).
The output register reports, per context, the actual next token with its
probability and the channel’s direct push, the eight largest next-token
logits with their pushes, the tokens most consistently raised and lowered across contexts,
and the largest push as a share of the decision gap, by regime.
All contexts come from an exact replay of the profile forward on the first
    10  7   10^{7}    tokens of the evaluation panel (256-token windows; replayed activations
match the stored profile values to     5  ×   10   −  5     5\times 10^{-5}   ), and every register’s
arrays are checked slot by slot against the replayed activations before any
model reads them. 
 
 
 
  I.2  Stage 1: open reading 

 
 One call per channel to Qwen3.6-35B-A3B  ( Qwen Team, 2026 )  (BF16,  vLLM 0.27.1 ,
temperature 0, at most 512 output tokens; evidence 20.4k tokens at the median,
maximum model length 22,387).
Records carry no channel identity, and the prompt asks for ten fields (what
fires the couple; the stream action and its kind; the output action and its
kind; the regime action and its kind; a couple name; one evidence sentence;
confidence) and no other output.
Rows that fail to parse (1.2–1.3% per seed) are retained as
 unparsed . 
 
 
 
  I.3  Stage 2: taxonomy 

 
 Numeric classes are computed first, from the measurements alone: the sign
strength of the contribution’s return on its own reader, the concentration of the
downstream response, the output push size against the decision gap, its
direction, and whether each persists at typical activations. Relative to
the decision gap, the output push is negligible below    0.10  0.10   , weak from
   0.10  0.10    to    0.25  0.25   , material from    0.25  0.25    to    1  1   , and decisive above    1  1   .
Moving the    0.25  0.25    threshold to    0.20  0.20    or    0.30  0.30    changes the share of channels
above it from     10.6  %   10.6\%    to     16.8  %   16.8\%    or     7.0  %   7.0\%   , respectively; each seed’s
shares are within    0.2  0.2    percentage points of these values. The remaining
cut points are in the released code.
A discovery sample of 4,000 channels per seed, stratified by output size and
self-erasure class with equal counts per cell, is read by Claude Opus in 40
groups of 300; each group proposes 8–20 functional classes with definitions,
examples, and counts.
One synthesis merges the 40 proposals into at most 24 classes with a
single-label rule, three validators apply the result to 300 rows each that
were not used for discovery and report overlaps, missing classes, and
undecidable rows, and one revision adopts the changes on which at least two
validators agree.
The revised taxonomy (24 classes plus  other  and  ambiguous )
is frozen and released as a document;  ambiguous  is reserved for
rows whose open reading contradicts its own numeric class, and the
labelling prompt also allows it when two classes fit equally.
The classes fall into four families: stream couples, whose contribution is read
by later readers and moves no next-token logit directly; logit couples,
whose contribution moves the next-token logits at its own firing positions; nudges,
between the two; and subject-fixed couples, defined by numeric, temporal,
bibliographic, or non-Latin material at any output size. 
 
 
 
  I.4  Stage 3: application and audit 

 
 Claude Sonnet 5 applies the frozen taxonomy to every channel in batches of 400
records; a record carries the open reading, the numeric classes, and the
tokens the contribution most raises and lowers, and the model returns one class, a
token class for the output, a reader family where the downstream response is
concentrated, and a confidence.
Channels whose open reading failed receive  not applicable ; channels
whose input label was a token list or shorter than three words (1.8–1.9%)
are re-read from six activation contexts.
A second annotator, Claude Opus 5.5, applies the same frozen taxonomy to
1,000 channels per seed, drawn with the discovery stratification and
disjoint from the discovery rows; its labels are adopted as the reference
for that sample, and the agreement of the population labels with it is
reported with a bootstrap standard error at the class level and at the level
of the four families.
Every share uses the fixed denominators of 98,304 channels per seed and
294,912 pooled;  not applicable , which includes unparsed
responses, and  ambiguous  appear as rows of every table, and
 other  holds no channel in the three seeds.
