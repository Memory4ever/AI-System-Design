Abstract
Predictive representations for robot manipulation should connect scene geometry with the state changes induced by actions. Our intuition is that understanding what an action will change starts with understanding where things are: complementary RGB and depth observations ground a common scene representation from which to infer those changes. Guided by this intuition, we introduce DepState-JEPA , a dual-stream RGB–Depth joint-embedding predictive architecture that connects spatial understanding with action-conditioned prediction through a shared latent state, DepState .
Built on V-JEPA 2.1, the framework uses depth both as a complementary observation stream and to ground both modalities in metric 3D space, aggregating shared information into DepState while retaining modality-private features for decoding.
Ray-conditioned readouts predict local features without target depth; after an action-conditioned transition, the same decoders predict future RGB and depth features using the predicted state and fixed initial private features.
Robot-state readouts provide recursive proprioceptive inputs; object-trajectory supervision and calibrated forward kinematics shape state learning through motion targets and joint–TCP consistency.
Comparisons show stronger RGB-encoder transfer after RGB-D adaptation, feature completion, conditioned visual forecasting, and manipulation success. Within-model ablations identify contributions from depth content, metric coordinates, and future-state matching, with additional control gains from FK regularization.
 1 Introduction
Learning visual world models enables anticipation of action outcomes ( Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018 ; Maes et al., 2026 ) .
For manipulation in real-world space, this requires relating the current spatial arrangement to
the changes an action may cause. Consider a robot reaching for a cup:
RGB reveals its appearance and context, while depth locates the cup and
gripper relative to the table in three-dimensional space. Anticipating the reach then requires
reasoning about how this arrangement will change, not merely recognizing
the cup. This raises a broader question: how can the geometry revealed by depth inform a robot’s predictions of how its actions will change the scene?

This depth-grounded state connects spatial queries with action-conditioned prediction of future visual features, robot state, and object motion.
 RGB JEPAs predict representations rather than pixels ( Assran et al., 2023 ; Bardes et al., 2024 ) :
V-JEPA 2 supports action-conditioned planning ( Assran et al., 2025 ) ,
while V-JEPA 2.1 strengthens dense visual features ( Mur-Labadia et al., 2026 ) .
 Depth-related JEPAs offer complementary designs. LeMuMo-JEPA combines RGB
and aligned depth features within a common Transformer for multimodal
perception
 ( Cornelissen et al., 2026 ) .
Locate 3D’s 3D-JEPA predicts masked point-cloud features enriched by lifted
2D features, rather than maintaining separate RGB–depth streams
 ( Arnaud et al., 2025 ) ; JEPADepth predicts depth from RGB with auxiliary
latent supervision, rather than encoding measured depth ( Grigore & Popa, 2026 ) .
These designs target multimodal perception or geometric estimation, but do not establish a common state interface between spatial understanding and action-driven evolution. This motivates our work:
 Can complementary observations ground a spatially queryable, temporally predictive shared state representation while retaining modality-private features in separate decoding paths? 
To answer this question, we introduce DepState-JEPA (Fig.  1 ),
a joint-embedding predictive architecture centered on DepState ,
a depth-grounded shared latent state connecting RGB–depth observations,
spatial readout, and action-conditioned transition. Built on V-JEPA 2.1, an independent
Depth-Video branch provides complementary observations and metric
grounding for both streams. DepState aggregates shared information,
while private paths preserve modality-specific detail for decoding
rather than forming separate evolving states. This common interface
supports reading missing spatial content and forecasting representations
after robot actions.
Representation learning uses images and video; robot prediction
initializes DepState from a single RGB-D frame and evolves it under
actions and proprioception, without accessing future RGB-D images.
Specifically, visible metric depth and camera intrinsics place both streams in metric 3D
coordinates; shared–private projections and learned aggregation form
a compact, geometrically grounded DepState. Ray-conditioned readouts
query local shared features without target depth; private features
supply modality-specific decoding detail. After action-conditioned transitions,
the same decoders predict future RGB and depth features with fixed
initial private memory. Robot-state readouts supply recursive proprioception;
object-motion supervision shapes the state. Calibrated forward kinematics
couples independent joint and end-effector predictions, propagating
consistency gradients into the transition alongside direct physical supervision.
Training proceeds in two stages: 1) RGB-D adaptation learns
DepState from images and video clips via masked shared-feature completion
and dense modality-feature prediction, exploiting spatial and temporal
context. 2) Robot post-training learns
action-conditioned transitions and physical readouts using future-state
and feature targets, physical supervision, and kinematic regularization,
while RGB-D replay retains the adaptation objectives. Evaluation
(Sec.  4 ) spans
spatial understanding, multi-step forecasting, and manipulation, with
controlled ablations testing the roles of geometry, feature sharing,
and kinematic consistency.
Our contributions are threefold:
 • 
 Dual-stream RGB–Depth Video JEPA framework. 
We introduce DepState-JEPA , which connects spatial understanding and action-conditioned prediction through a shared latent state, DepState . Independent RGB and depth encoders capture complementary observations, while metric depth grounds both streams in a common 3D coordinate frame.
 • 
 Unified DepState construction and prediction pipeline. 
We aggregate shared RGB–depth features into DepState, retaining private features for modality-specific decoding. Ray-conditioned readouts predict missing features without target depth and are reused after action-conditioned transitions to predict future features, with initial private memory fixed throughout rollout. Robot-state readouts supply recursive proprioception; object-trajectory supervision and joint–TCP consistency provide complementary training signals for state evolution.
 • 
 Empirical gains and controlled component analysis. 
RGB-D adaptation improves RGB-encoder transfer; the full pipeline supports feature prediction and manipulation. Within-model ablations identify contributions from depth and metric coordinates, private decoding for visual accuracy, and future-state matching for control performance.
 2 Related Work
 2.1 Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures
Joint-embedding predictive learning uses target representations, rather than
pixels, as the objects of prediction. I-JEPA learns image representations by
predicting masked regions from visible context ( Assran et al., 2023 ) , while
V-JEPA extends this approach to spatiotemporal video features
 ( Bardes et al., 2024 ) . V-JEPA 2 scales video pretraining and connects learned
features to robot prediction and planning ( Assran et al., 2025 ) .
V-JEPA 2.1 further develops dense and multi-level supervision, strengthening
the spatial detail retained by video representations
 ( Mur-Labadia et al., 2026 ) . A complementary direction changes the unit of
prediction: Causal-JEPA masks object-level latents and infers them from
surrounding context to encourage interaction-dependent prediction
 ( Nam et al., 2026 ) . These approaches illustrate how prediction targets
and representation structure shape what a model learns. DepState-JEPA
extends the RGB-centric video JEPA framework along the modality axis,
introducing an independent Depth-Video stream. We retain V-JEPA 2.1’s dense
feature-learning design while making measured geometry an explicit input
to shared-state construction, complementing spatial cues learned from RGB.
 2.2 Depth and 3D Representation Learning for JEPAs
Depth-related JEPAs differ in whether depth is predicted, used for
supervision, or encoded as geometric input.
JEPADepth combines masked latent prediction with photometric supervision
for monocular depth estimation ( Grigore & Popa, 2026 ) .
Depth-Regularized JEPA aligns RGB features with depth features during
training while retaining RGB-only inference ( Khan, 2026 ) .
Le MuMo JEPA fuses RGB and camera-aligned LiDAR depth through learnable
fusion tokens within a shared Transformer ( Cornelissen et al., 2026 ) .
3D-JEPA in Locate 3D learns masked point-cloud representations from
RGB-D-derived geometry enriched with lifted 2D foundation-model features
 ( Arnaud et al., 2025 ) .
SR-JEPA predicts missing-entity features in native 3D scenes at supplied
target locations ( Zhou et al., 2026 ) .
DepState-JEPA jointly encodes RGB and measured depth in independent video
streams, using visible metric depth to ground both streams in camera
coordinates. Shared–private factorization retains modality-specific
features alongside the shared state, whereas ray-conditioned readout
predicts missing content without target depth or a supplied 3D location.
The same state evolves under actions to predict future RGB-D features,
connecting geometric representation learning with spatial completion and
temporal prediction through a common interface.
 2.3 Action-Conditioned World Models for Robotics
Latent world models support robot control by predicting how representations
change under actions. DINO-WM learns transitions over frozen DINOv2 patch
features and optimizes action sequences toward visual goals
 ( Zhou et al., 2024 ) ; PLDM studies JEPA-based planning from reward-free
offline trajectories ( Sobal et al., 2025 ) . V-JEPA 2 adapts pretrained video
features through action-conditioned robot post-training
 ( Assran et al., 2025 ) , whereas LeWorldModel jointly learns its encoder
and dynamics from pixels with a distributional regularizer
 ( Maes et al., 2026 ) . A related question is how physical information
should shape the representation. MCR uses proprioceptive state–action
alignment for robot representation learning ( Jiang et al., 2024 ) ;
PSG-JEPA grounds individual states and state pairs in physical quantities
 ( Yan et al., 2026 ) , and physically grounded JEPA combines inverse dynamics
with state alignment ( Liu et al., 2026b ) .
DepState-JEPA studies consistency among predicted physical quantities in
addition to their direct supervision. Calibrated forward kinematics
couples independent joint and end-effector readouts of the predicted state;
object motion is supervised through increment-based pose trajectories.
The kinematic constraint regularizes the depth-grounded state transition without
imposing hard guarantees of physically correct dynamics.
 Concurrent work. Closely related recent preprints advance geometric
supervision and spatial prediction ( Khan, 2026 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) ,
including geometry-conditioned video modeling
 ( Kim et al., 2026 ; DreamX Team et al., 2026 ) ; physical state grounding and
physics-conditioned latent dynamics
 ( Yan et al., 2026 ; Liu et al., 2026a ; Liu et al., 2026b ) ;
and joint world modeling and action generation ( Lin et al., 2026 ) .
These directions complement our use of an explicit depth stream,
depth-lifted positions, and selectively shared RGB-D features for spatial,
temporal, and robot prediction.
 3 Method
 3.1 Problem Setup and JEPA Objective
 Problem Setup. Given registered RGB I , metric depth D , and
intrinsics K , we construct DepState S , a shared latent state that
supports local feature readout and action-conditioned evolution
(Figure  2 ). Robot-state and object-motion
heads connect predicted states to physical supervision.
RGB-D adaptation uses images or video clips; robot prediction constructs
 S_{t} from the current frame only. Future RGB-D observations supply training
targets, never rollout inputs. S summarizes visible scene information
for the specified readout and transition tasks.
 JEPA Objective. JEPAs learn by predicting target representations
 ( Assran et al., 2023 ) . We adopt V-JEPA 2.1’s dense, multi-level objective
 ( Mur-Labadia et al., 2026 ) for both RGB and depth, denoted throughout by
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}} :
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}=\underset{m,\ell}{\operatorname{mean}}\!\left[\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}_{m}}e_{i}^{m,\ell}}{|\mathcal{M}_{m}|}+\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{m}}w_{i}e_{i}^{m,\ell}}{|\mathcal{V}_{m}|}\right]. 
 (1) 
Here m and \ell index modalities and encoder levels;
 \mathcal{M}_{m},\mathcal{V}_{m} contain valid masked and visible targets.
The error e_{i}^{m,\ell} is channel-averaged L1 between layer-normalized
predictions and stop-gradient teacher features; w_{i} weights visible
targets by distance to masked positions (Appendix  C.1 ).
The teacher follows \bar{\theta}\leftarrow\tau\bar{\theta}+(1-\tau)\theta ,
where \theta are online representation parameters and \tau is EMA retention.
This inherited objective supervises visual feature prediction; our state
construction and readout define the interface through which it is learned.

Spatial queries read local content; action-conditioned transitions advance

Physical readouts predict robot state and object motion, with FK
regularizing independent joint/TCP outputs.
 3.2 Depth-Grounded Shared-State Construction
 Depth-Lifted Spatial Encoding. 
V-JEPA 2.1 RGB and depth encoders with separate weights produce
multi-level features, fused per modality into z_{i}^{m} ,
 m\in\{\mathrm{rgb},\mathrm{dep}\} . For a visible patch center
 \mathbf{p}_{i}=(u_{i},v_{i},1)^{\top} with valid metric depth d_{i} ,
 \mathbf{x}_{i}=d_{i}K^{-1}\mathbf{p}_{i},\qquad\widetilde{z}_{i}^{m}=z_{i}^{m}+\operatorname{PE}_{3D}(\mathbf{x}_{i}). 
 (2) 
Here \mathbf{x}_{i} is the patch’s metric position in the camera frame,
and \operatorname{PE}_{3D} is a learned projection of Fourier-encoded
coordinates ( Mildenhall et al., 2020 ) . Adding it to both streams places
corresponding RGB and depth features in the same metric frame, separately
from inherited spatiotemporal RoPE ( Su et al., 2021 ) .
Masked tokens are removed before encoder attention, and only
visible depth supplies online coordinates. Invalid depths receive an
unknown-geometry embedding
(App.  B.1 ).
 Shared–Private Feature Projection. 
To aggregate common information without forcing all modality detail into
 S , independent MLPs project the grounded tokens \widetilde{Z}^{m} into
shared features and private decoder inputs:
 H^{m}=F_{s}^{m}(\widetilde{Z}^{m}),\qquad P^{m}=F_{p}^{m}(\widetilde{Z}^{m}),\qquad P^{\mathrm{rgb}}=A,\quad P^{\mathrm{dep}}=R. 
 (3) 
Here F_{s}^{m} and F_{p}^{m} are modality-specific shared and private
projections, with output widths d_{s} and d_{p} , respectively.
Only H^{m} enters the recurrent state and control interface; A and R 
bypass it to supply RGB- and depth-specific content to the visual decoders.
The state and private paths thus have distinct computational roles.
 Depth-Grounded Shared-State Aggregation. 
Learned queries compress the shared observation features into a
fixed-size state:
 S=G_{\psi}\!\left(Q_{s},[H^{\mathrm{rgb}};H^{\mathrm{dep}}]\right). 
 (4) 
The attention aggregator G_{\psi} uses 64 learned queries Q_{s} of width
384 to read shared keys/values with modality/time embeddings
 ( Jaegle et al., 2021 ) ; [\cdot;\cdot] denotes concatenation.
The resulting state varies with the observation. Query indices are generic
slots, not predefined robot joints or object identities.
 Selective-Sharing Regularization. 
Alignment encourages cross-modal agreement, decorrelation discourages
shared–private redundancy, and a variance floor opposes collapse:
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fac}}=\underset{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{valid}}}{\operatorname{mean}}d_{c}(H_{i}^{\mathrm{rgb}},H_{i}^{\mathrm{dep}})+\lambda_{\mathrm{decorr}}\sum_{m}\frac{\|C(H^{m},P^{m})\|_{F}^{2}}{d_{s}d_{p}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{var}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}}. 
 (5) 
Here d_{c}(a,b)=1-a^{\top}b/(\|a\|_{2}\|b\|_{2}) is cosine distance, and
 \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{valid}} contains geometrically valid visible
correspondences. C is cross-covariance after channel standardization
over valid batch–position pairs; \|\cdot\|_{F} is the Frobenius norm,
and d_{s}d_{p} normalizes the number of shared–private channel pairs.
Both online branches receive gradients. The variance-floor term
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{var}} ( Bardes et al., 2022 ) regularizes features
and across-sample state variation; its definition and the fixed weights
are given in Appendix  C.1 .
Cross-modal alignment acts on shared features, while the private paths
retain separate modality-specific decoding objectives.
 3.3 Ray-Conditioned Spatial Prediction
 Ray-Conditioned State Readout. 
Whereas aggregation writes observations into S , the spatial decoder
 h_{\eta} reads local shared content from S alone. A target pixel supplies
a ray direction r_{i} , not its distance, and relative clip time \nu_{i} 
(zero for images):
 \begin{gathered}r_{i}=K^{-1}\mathbf{p}_{i}/\|K^{-1}\mathbf{p}_{i}\|_{2},\quad\hat{h}_{i}=h_{\eta}(S,\operatorname{PE}_{\mathrm{ray}}(r_{i},\nu_{i})),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}}=\underset{i\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{valid}}}{\operatorname{mean}}\,d_{c}\!\left(\hat{h}_{i},\operatorname{sg}(h_{i}^{\star})\right).\end{gathered} 
 (6) 
Here \operatorname{PE}_{\mathrm{ray}} embeds direction and relative time;
 \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{valid}} selects valid masked targets for the loss,
without supplying target depth to the online query.
The target
 h_{i}^{\star}=\operatorname{norm}(\operatorname{norm}(\bar{H}_{i}^{\mathrm{rgb}})+\operatorname{norm}(\bar{H}_{i}^{\mathrm{dep}})) uses full-observation
momentum features and L2 normalization; \operatorname{sg} stops gradients.
 Private-Conditioned Feature Prediction. 
Modality decoders g_{m}^{\ell} combine \hat{h}_{i} with visible A or R to predict
encoder-level RGB/depth features at both masked and visible positions,
supervised by the inherited dense JEPA objective
in Eq. ( 1 ).
Together these define the spatial prediction objective
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{spatial}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{comp}} :
completion supervises local shared features, and dense prediction anchors
modality-specific encoder features. Both operate in representation space.
 3.4 Action-Conditioned State Prediction
 Action-Conditioned State Transition. 
A temporal Transformer advances S_{t} using a joint command u_{t} ,
configuration q_{t} , instantaneous velocity \dot{q}_{t} , and
physical time interval \Delta t_{t} , distinct from clip time \nu_{i} .
The existing visual readouts decode the predicted state:
 \begin{gathered}\hat{S}_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\phi}(S_{t},u_{t},q_{t},\dot{q}_{t};\Delta t_{t}),\quad\hat{Z}_{t+1}^{m,\ell}=g_{m}^{\ell}\!\left(h_{\eta}(\hat{S}_{t+1},Q_{\mathrm{ray}}),P_{t}^{m}\right).\end{gathered} 
 (7) 
Here Q_{\mathrm{ray}} contains the known image-grid ray embeddings,
with \nu_{i}=0 for each predicted frame. P_{t}^{m} is extracted from the
initial observation and held fixed across the rollout, serving visual
decoding only. Spatial and future prediction share h_{\eta},g_{m}^{\ell} ,
not the temporal Transformer.
Future-feature matching supervises the content read from the predicted
state; state matching anchors the representation reused at the next step:
 \begin{gathered}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temporal}}=\underset{m,\ell,i}{\operatorname{mean}}\,\frac{1}{c}\left\|\operatorname{LN}(\hat{Z}_{t+1,i}^{m,\ell})-\operatorname{sg}(\operatorname{LN}(\bar{Z}_{t+1,i}^{m,\ell}))\right\|_{1}+\underset{k}{\operatorname{mean}}\,d_{c}(\hat{S}_{t+1,k},\operatorname{sg}(\bar{S}_{t+1,k})).\end{gathered} 
 (8) 
Here \bar{Z} and \bar{S} are momentum encoder features and aggregated
states; i indexes valid target positions and k corresponding state
slots. LN is non-affine layer normalization and c the feature width.
Both components are averaged over samples and supervised rollout steps
(Appendix  C.1 ).
A fixed calibrated external camera supplies future rays without future
depth. Control and ablation rollouts use predicted states and endpoint
proprioception; A,R stay fixed at the initial observation.
 Robot-State and Object-Motion Readouts. 
Three independent endpoint heads read mean-pooled \hat{S}_{t+1} to predict
joint configuration \hat{q}_{t+1} , instantaneous velocity
 \widehat{\dot{q}}_{t+1} , and tool-center-point (TCP) pose \hat{T}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{tcp}} .
The predicted configuration and velocity condition the next rollout step.
One object-motion head reads the pooled state pair (S_{t},\hat{S}_{t+1}) 
and its difference to predict a finite body-frame pose increment
 \widehat{\Delta\xi}_{t}^{o} , not a velocity, for each annotated object or part o :
 \hat{T}_{t+1}^{o}=\tilde{T}_{t}^{o}\operatorname{Exp}([\widehat{\Delta\xi}_{t}^{o}]^{\wedge}). 
 (9) 
Here \operatorname{Exp} maps the Lie-algebra matrix
 [\widehat{\Delta\xi}_{t}^{o}]^{\wedge} to an SE(3) transform acting on the right.
The trajectory starts from a supplied object pose and thereafter uses
predicted poses; this accumulator does not condition the visual state,
transition, or readout heads. The endpoint readouts and integrated object
poses receive valid-label supervision, grouped as \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}} 
(Appendix  C.1 ).
 Joint–TCP Kinematic Consistency. 
Independent joint and TCP predictions should describe the same robot
configuration. Direct labels anchor each output to measurements;
fixed differentiable forward kinematics (FK) constrains their agreement:
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FK}}=d_{\mathrm{pose}}(\mathrm{FK}_{\mathrm{tcp}}(\hat{q}_{t+1}),\hat{T}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{tcp}}). 
 (10) 
Here d_{\mathrm{pose}} combines unit-normalized translation and SO(3)
geodesic errors. \mathrm{FK}_{\mathrm{tcp}} includes the calibrated chain,
base transform, and tool offset; gradients through both heads
reach the latent transition. This consistency term complements direct
joint/TCP supervision by coupling the two output heads through the same
predicted state and known robot geometry.
 3.5 Training and Rollout Protocol
RGB-D adaptation trains state construction and readout on images and
video clips using spatial and temporal context, not causal next-frame
rollouts. Robot post-training adds action-conditioned transitions and
physical heads; image/video replay continues the adaptation objective:
 \begin{gathered}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adapt}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{spatial}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fac}},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{robot}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{temporal}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fac}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{FK}}\gamma(n)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FK}}.\end{gathered} 
 (11) 
The schedule \gamma(n) gradually enables FK over robot updates n ;
supervision uses valid labels. The stopped EMA teacher tracks the
representation stack, including factorization and aggregation, but not
predictors or physical heads. Control and ablation rollouts recursively
use predicted states and proprioception with fixed initial private features;
Table  2 uses recorded proprioception instead.

Appendix  B.2 details the modules, and
Algorithms  1 – 2 
specify training and rollout.

 4.2 Spatial Representation Transfer and Prediction
 Spatial Transfer with Frozen Encoders.

Following DreamerV3 ( Hafner et al., 2025 ) , we train an actor–critic

Evaluation directly executes the actor. We use 20 DMControl (DMC) and
50 Meta-World (MW) tasks with matched interaction budgets. Tab.  3 reports task-averaged episode
return (the sum of rewards) for DMC and averaged success for MW.

 Latent Space Receding-Horizon Planning. 
Following TD-MPC2 ( Hansen et al., 2024 ) , we use model predictive control
(MPC): optimize action sequences through the learned latent transition
using predicted rewards and terminal values, execute the first action,
and replan at every step. Task sets, interaction budgets, and search budgets
are matched across JEPA models. Tab.  3 reports
both metrics; App.  E.6.2 specifies value learning
and trajectory optimization.

Simulation-trained models remain frozen for drawer and cup visual-goal MPC.

 4.5 Ablation Study
Ex.1–Ex.15 vary components against the full Ex.16 configuration.
App.  E.7 defines interventions and disaggregates completion,
Drawer/Cup prediction, and controller-specific success.
 Effect of Depth Content and Metric Coordinates. 
Ex.1–Ex.3 and Ex.16 isolate depth content and metric coordinates under fixed
backbones and supervision. Combining both reduces L1 from 0.408 to 0.340 and
raises SR from 76.8% to 83.4% (Fig.  6 (a)). Each factor
improves prediction and control, although their joint gains are subadditive at the reported means.
 Effect of Selective Sharing and Spatial Readout. 
Ex.4–Ex.8 vary private decoding, alignment, aggregation, queries, and completion
(Fig.  6 (b)). Removing private decoding raises L1 from 0.340 to 0.390,
with SR at 83.2% versus 83.4%. Mean aggregation gives L1 0.440 and SR 77.0%.
Pixel/ray queries tie in feature scores; private decoding primarily benefits visual readout.
 Effect of Temporal Modeling and Physical Supervision. 
Ex.9–Ex.15 vary temporal targets, physical supervision, memory, and decoders
(Fig.  6 (c)). Removing future-state matching lowers SR
from 83.4% to 79.5%; removing FK gives 82.5% with direct labels retained.
Separate decoders reach L1 0.333 and SR 83.4% with added capacity.
App.  E.7 relates these outcomes to each intervention.
 5 Conclusion
DepState-JEPA connects RGB–depth geometry, shared-state evolution, and private decoding
for feature completion, conditioned forecasting, and manipulation. RGB-D adaptation improves
encoder transfer; ablations identify contributions from geometry, local state content, and
future-state supervision. App.  F discusses the remaining scope.
