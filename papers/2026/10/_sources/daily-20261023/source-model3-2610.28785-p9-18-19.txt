6 RELATEDWORK
Task selection and rollout allocation.Adaptive curricula change which tasks an agent encounters.
PLR prioritizes levels using TD error (Jiang et al., 2021), while problem-level RL replay uses
empirical intermediate success rates (Fatemi, 2026). Other selectors use reward dynamics (Zheng
et al., 2025b), task difficulty (Qu et al., 2025; 2026; Gao et al., 2025), estimated learning impact (Li
et al., 2025), or replay priorities (Sun et al., 2025). For group-relative training, DAPO filters all-equal
groups after generation (Yu et al., 2025). VIP allocates rollout counts by predicted gradient variance
(Nguyen et al., 2026); DUET also allocates token budgets (Hu et al., 2026), and TRACE predicts
mixed outcomes at prompts and interaction prefixes using an independent rollout model (Zou et al.,
2026). BRACEinstead selects tasks for fixed-size groups using capped historical evidence, posterior
uncertainty, and a correlated reward model. The correlation correction improves forecast calibration
while preserving known-probability task ranks at fixed group size (Proposition 1); historical evidence
and relative weights therefore matter to the resulting allocation. Policy-gradient estimators (Liu et al.,
2025; Ahmadian et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025a) address a separate choice.
Tool-using agents and harness search.Multi-turn training studies delayed rewards and tool
interaction (Zhou et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b; Jin et al., 2025; Qian et al., 2025). MCP work
studies protocol architecture and overhead, tool discovery, and agent evaluation (Hou et al., 2026;
Anjum et al., 2026; Fei et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025a), while benchmarks measure
tool execution and function composition (Patil et al., 2025; Basu et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2023; Yao
et al., 2024). Harness-search systems propose edits to prompts, workflows, or agent code (Hu et al.,
2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Agrawal et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026; Zelikman et al., 2024; Zhang et al.,
2026). DISCORDaddresses the subsequent retention decision with exact conditional paired inference
(McNemar, 1947) and decision-preserving curtailment (Alling, 1963); alternative matched-pair tests
include exact unconditional procedures (Suissa & Shuster, 1991).
Statistical foundations.Beta–Binomial models describe clustered binary outcomes (Kleinman, 1973;
Crowder, 1978; Williams, 1975; Ridout et al., 1999) and motivate BRACE’s within-group forecast.
Its task selection is related to active learning (Settles, 2009; Houlsby et al., 2011) and adaptive replay
(Schaul et al., 2016), with non-degenerate group probability as the allocation objective.
7 CONCLUSION
Reward disagreement provides a common lens for allocating evaluation and training computation.
DISCORDuses exact paired inference and decision-preserving stopping to retain harness edits when
observed disagreements support improvement. BRACEuses capped historical evidence, posterior
uncertainty, and reward dependence to forecast mixed-reward groups before generation. Across three
model scales, BRACEachieves higher observed held-out MCP solve rates than the compared methods,
reaches their strongest plotted peaks with fewer new rollouts, and transfers to BFCL and NESTFUL
without further training. Together, the methods direct interaction budget toward harness comparisons
that can change retention and rollout groups that can supply a group-relative training signal.

C.4 CONTROLLED EVOLUTION LOOPS
The controlled loops use the BFCL v3 (Patil et al., 2025) live multiple split, restricted to 200
single-turn tasks with binary syntax-tree matching. Callers are Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (Bai et al.,
2025), Granite-3.3-8B-Instruct (IBM Granite Team, 2025), Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Mistral AI,
2024), and Falcon3-7B-Instruct (Technology Innovation Institute, 2024), sampled at temperature0.7
with eight seeds per task unless otherwise specified in the run records.
Each loop evaluates eight rounds of three proposed edits. Edits add or remove blocks of advertised
function names. Withholding names at initialization creates a recoverable handicap; starting from
the full surface instead produces a regime in which proposals are predominantly harmful. The
three retention rules consume matched episode budgets and identical proposals. The untested rule
retains a strict empirical improvement; the single-round rule compares disjoint task halves with a
label-permutation test; DISCORDuses paired outcomes and its exact conditional test.
DISCORDrecovers more of the injected handicap in 18 of 21 paired comparisons (Figure 3). The
one-sided paired t-test gives p= 0.00015 (t= 4.37 , 20 degrees of freedom); enumerating all221 sign
assignments gives an exact sign-flip value of8.5×10 −5 under the corresponding symmetry null. The
single-round gate overstates realized effects by 1.8–17×. In the harmful-proposal regime, DISCORD
preserves performance in all 17 matched seeds, whereas strict-improvement retention degrades in 9
of 17. The paired contingency table has nine seeds with degradation only under strict-improvement
retention, none with degradation only under DISCORD, and eight with no degradation under either
method. The two-sided exact McNemar value is 2(1/2)9 = 0.00390625. Mean changes are small, so
this result concerns the frequency of degradation rather than a large difference in mean improvement.
C.5 LIVEMCPCOMPARISON
The live comparison uses 320 AWM (Wang et al., 2026) tasks across 158 servers, with a shared
proposal stream and a full-surface reference objective of 0.2813. The shallow regime withholds 10%
of tool names, producing a recoverable effect of 2.2 pp; the deep regime withholds 40%, producing
12.7pp. Table 6 reports final objective values, acceptance counts, and episode cost.
The deployed rule reaches a higher final objective with fewer evaluation episodes but overstates it

objective achieved by a search; the method does not dominate both criteria.

D BRACE:POSTERIOR COMPUTATION AND IMPLEMENTATION
D.1 POSTERIOR EVIDENCE AND SAMPLING
A task’s success and failure evidence defines the working Beta approximationpi ∼Beta(a i, bi) with
ai = 1 +s i and bi = 1 +f i. At initialization, the implementation caps historical evidence separately
at 20 successful and 40 failed episodes per task. The bank is incorporated once; subsequent cycles
add local outcomes and apply the same evidence-discount rule. The registered configuration uses
γ= 1 and retains evidence without decay. Setting γ <1 would reduce the influence of outcomes
from older policies.

A plug-in estimator substitutes a single estimated solve probability into the availability score. Posterior
integration averages over uncertainty in that probability. Two tasks with the same posterior mean can
therefore receive different scores when their evidence counts differ. This is especially relevant early
in training, before the current run has collected substantial task-specific evidence.
D.2 BANK PROVENANCE AND COST ACCOUNTING
The default bank reads other runs’ episode logs once at initialization and excludes the current arm’s
own log, which supplies its live evidence. Historical outcomes come from policies at 2B, 4B, and
8B. Thus initialization can transfer evidence about task difficulty across model scales, including
from larger to smaller models. The cumulative audit inventory contains 195 episode files: 107
from 8B, 47 from 2B, and 41 from 4B policies. This inventory differs from an individual run’s
initialization snapshot, which depends on the files available at launch. Only eligible training-task
outcomes contribute to initialization.
The VIP-score (Nguyen et al., 2026) and correlation-removal ablations use the same capped per-task
starting counts as full BRACE. Their bank-read totals can differ because the available files and
redundant observations differ, while capping produces the same retained evidence. Consequently,
more parsed episodes do not imply a larger effective prior in these comparisons.
The historical corpus and the capped evidence have different sizes. For example, the recorded bank
read for the 4B arm q4bT2 parses 651,804 episodes from 115 files and primes 1,023 tasks. Separate
caps of 20 successes and 40 failures bound its historical contribution by 61,380 counts, or 9.4%
of the parsed episode count. This is an upper bound on retained historical counts; it is neither the
fraction of eligible episodes retained nor a compute-saving estimate.
The bank reuses episodes generated by other experimental runs; no dedicated collection run was
launched to build it. This avoids additional probing for the receiving run while retaining the historical
collection cost and the benefit of additional data. A current allocation cycle requests256×5 = 1,280
episodes. Historical generation, current-run generation, and the one-time bank read are separate costs.
