  C.2  Generation Prompts and Faithfulness Regimes
 Faithfulness scores in this paper come from two distinct generation
regimes that use different prompt templates. Both are scored by the
same RAGAS v0.4 / GPT-5.1 judge with identical decoding
(  Section   C.1.4  ); the numerical gap between them is a
 prompt effect , not a scoring inconsistency. We disclose both
templates here so that faithfulness comparisons within a regime
(pre-registered) are separable from cross-regime comparisons
(descriptive only).

 Agentic-prompt scope.
 The CRAG grader prompt, Self-RAG reflection prompts, and
Speculative-RAG draft-and-verify prompts are held fixed at the
versions frozen at design time (recorded alongside the manifest at the paths referenced in   Table   58   and available from the authors on request). Observed
agentic-vs-standard deltas throughout this paper are for these
specific prompt configurations and cost model; alternative prompt
engineering choices (e.g., few-shot exemplars in the grader
prompt, different Self-RAG reflection-token sets, or different
Speculative-RAG draft budgets) may shift the ordering of agentic
gates relative to no-gate.   Section   I.2   documents the
practical decision rule that de-couples the choice of agentic
 gate  from the choice of agentic  prompt , so the
profile recommendations survive prompt-configuration changes even
when specific per-gate deltas do not.
  C.3  Length-Controlled Faithfulness Rerun
 A natural concern is that faithfulness gains under our
concise-answer prompting may partly reflect brevity, shorter answers
make fewer atomic claims and thus fewer opportunities for the RAGAS
judge to flag a claim as unsupported, rather than genuinely superior
grounding. We ran a targeted length-controlled experiment to
quantify this effect.
 Design.
 We drew a stratified sample of 50 questions across three benchmarks
(SQuAD v2: 22, MuSiQue: 18, HotpotQA: 10) and re-generated answers
under three prompt regimes derived from a common instruction skeleton
that varies only length guidance: (A)  concise  (“answer in one
short sentence”), (B)  neutral  (“answer based on the
context”), and (C)  verbose  (“answer in detail with supporting
facts”). Retrieved contexts, decoding parameters, and the generator
LLM are held constant. All      50  ×  3   =  150   50\times 3=150    answer–context pairs
are re-scored under the canonical GPT-5.1 faithfulness judge. Raw data ( paper/data/length_controlled_faith.jsonl ) and the analysis script ( paper/scripts/m6_length_analysis.py ) are available from the authors on request.

 What this changes and what it does not.
 A portion of the raw faithfulness advantage
reported for concise-regen configurations reflects brevity rather
than superior grounding, so we report a length-residualized
column alongside raw faithfulness for any cross-regime comparison
(see the length-controlled entry in   Table   58  ).  Within-regime 
comparisons (the 122-configuration ANOVA and the scaling slopes)
hold prompt length constant by construction and are therefore
unaffected: the
