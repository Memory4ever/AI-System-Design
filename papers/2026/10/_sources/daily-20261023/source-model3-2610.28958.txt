Abstract
Transformers are deployed as a special model class. Neural processing units (NPU) add dedicated operators for attention and normalization, and quantization relies on Transformer-specific algorithms. We show the computation does not force this. Every Transformer block, with attention, LayerNorm and a GELU MLP, compiles exactly into a generic deep neural network built from fixed affine maps, routing and the square, sigmoid and inverse square root, plus GELU or its sigmoid approximation. Without activation–activation products, every transform-wise compilation adds at least N 2 N^{2} scalar nonlinear evaluations per head for dense scores. Keeping that one operation type removes this cost; its elementwise form, present on every surveyed NPU, suffices with the sigmoid and inverse square root. For top- k k attention with exchangeable tail scores, we prove that exact or conditional-mean tail mass gives an unbiased, minimum-mean-square-error estimate of the attention distribution, while tail drop is conditionally biased. When queries or agents cluster, centroids choose the keys first, reducing the number of computed scores from N 2 N^{2} to N ​ k Nk per head. On DeiT-Tiny, MambaIRv2-Light and Pythia-70M the compiled models match their references with all keys, standard post-training quantization applies, and the exact tail-mass estimate beats tail dropping with half as many keys on DeiT-Tiny and, for k ≤ 8 k\leq 8 , on Pythia-70M.
Introduction
Transformers [ 1 ] are treated as a model class apart from the deep neural networks (DNNs) that accelerators and tools were first built for. This view has two practical consequences.
The first is hardware. Accelerators built for convolutional networks run a fixed operator set and hand everything else to the host. The Edge TPU compiler cuts the model at the first unsupported operation and runs the rest on the CPU [ 2 ] . NVIDIA’s Jetson DLA has no matrix-multiply layer [ 3 ] . On phones, llm.npu runs the linear layers on the NPU and keeps LayerNorm and attention on the CPU or GPU [ 4 ] . Vendors answer with Transformer-specific operators and units for attention, softmax and normalization [ 5 , 6 , 7 ] .
The second is quantization. Post-training quantization (PTQ) of Transformers relies on dedicated algorithms. Liu et al. search quantization scales with a ranking-aware objective [ 8 ] , and PTQ4ViT adds twin-uniform quantizers for the post-softmax and post-GELU activations [ 9 ] . FQ-ViT adds power-of-two factors for LayerNorm and a log-integer softmax [ 10 ] . RepQ-ViT reparameterizes quantization scales [ 11 ] , IGQ-ViT groups channels per instance [ 12 ] , I-BERT replaces the nonlinearities by integer polynomials [ 13 ] , and SmoothQuant moves activation outliers into the weights of language models [ 14 ] .
We show that the computation does not force this split. The argument has four steps.
Exact compilation. Every Transformer block, with LayerNorm, scaled dot-product attention, softmax and a GELU MLP, compiles exactly into a generic DNN of fixed affine maps, scalar nonlinearities and routing, with no product of two activations (Theorem 1 ). We call it the strict graph. Squares replace products, through a ​ b = 1 2 ​ [ ( a + b ) 2 − a 2 − b 2 ] ab=\tfrac{1}{2}[(a+b)^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}] where the squares of the factors are shared and through polarization otherwise, and no exponential or logarithm is needed: squares, the sigmoid and the inverse square root suffice, with GELU or its approximation x ​ σ ​ ( 1.702 ​ x ) x\,\sigma(1.702x) as the MLP activation.
The cost of strictness. Since the strict graph has no dedicated layers, standard DNN tools apply to it. But removing products of two activations has a price. For free arguments, any compilation without them adds at least N 2 N^{2} scalar nonlinear evaluations per head at Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} and N ​ d Nd at P ​ V PV , whatever the scalar functions, depth or width (Theorem 2 ).
One more operation type. Adding activation–activation products removes the price. Their elementwise form, the Hadamard product, is on every NPU we survey, and it suffices: with it, a block needs only the sigmoid and the inverse square root besides its MLP activation, and only softmax adds work, 2 ​ N 2 2N^{2} evaluations per head (Proposition 1 ). With the exponential and the reciprocal it adds no pointwise work (Proposition 2 ), and with matrix multiplication of two activations the extended compilation adds no work of either kind (Theorem 3 ). On a GPU, for k ≤ 64 k\leq 64 , the Hadamard and strict graphs run at 1.0 1.0 to 1.3 1.3 and 1.2 1.2 to 1.5 1.5 times the latency of this extended graph. Theorems 2 and 3 make this operation type necessary and sufficient for compilation without added pointwise work. Practice already uses this operation set. ONNX defines LayerNormalization and Softmax as functions over it [ 15 , 16 ] , current NPUs expose it [ 6 , 17 , 18 ] , and convolutional networks with squeeze-and-excitation gating already use the elementwise product [ 19 , 20 ] .
Clustered attention. The attention weights of a query cluster on a few keys [ 21 , 22 , 23 ] when its scores have a gap (Lemma 1 ). Queries cluster too [ 24 , 25 ] , and queries close to one point share their top keys (Lemma 2 ). Top- k k attention keeps the k k largest scores of each query [ 21 , 22 ] , the topological rule of bird flocks [ 26 ] . We add an estimate of the omitted probability mass, spread uniformly over the dropped keys. For exchangeable tail scores, exact or conditional-mean tail mass gives the unbiased, minimum-mean-square-error attention-distribution estimate; tail drop is conditionally biased (Theorem 4 ). Centroids of the query clusters [ 25 ] choose the keys before the scores, so the strict graph forms only N ​ k Nk scores per head (Corollary 2 , Proposition 5 ). One step of the Motsch–Tadmor flocking model is one attention head (Proposition 6 ).
We test the compilation on three model families: DeiT-Tiny [ 27 ] for image classification on Oxford-IIIT Pet [ 28 ] and ImageNet-1k [ 29 ] , MambaIRv2-Light [ 30 ] for super-resolution, and Pythia-70M [ 31 ] for language modeling.
Related work. Universal approximation gives some network within each error on a compact set [ 32 , 33 ] . Forming products from squares is classical: tables of quarter-squares multiply by polarization [ 34 ] , Winograd’s inner product forms the self-terms of each row once and halves the products of a matrix multiplication [ 35 ] , and expressivity proofs for ReLU networks use a ​ b = 1 2 ​ [ ( a + b ) 2 − a 2 − b 2 ] ab=\tfrac{1}{2}[(a+b)^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}] [ 36 ] . Compilers such as Glow map a model onto the operators a device provides [ 37 ] . Privacy-preserving inference decomposes attention, softmax and LayerNorm into primitive operations for cryptographic protocols, usually with polynomial approximations [ 38 , 39 , 40 ] . Multiplication-free training replaces every product by an approximate piecewise-affine operation and trains on that arithmetic [ 41 ] . Training-time alternatives simplify LayerNorm [ 42 ] or remove it [ 43 , 44 ] . Top- k k attention saves memory on long contexts [ 21 , 22 , 23 ] , usually by renormalizing the kept scores. Scatterbrain adds a low-rank estimate of the non-selected attention to a sparse one [ 45 ] , Combiner attends to the rest of the sequence through region summaries [ 46 ] , and MagicPIG replaces top- k k selection by sampling to avoid its bias [ 47 ] . Clustered attention chooses the kept keys by query centroids and gives the other keys the weights of the centroid [ 25 ] ; we use its key selection with calibrated centroids. Lookup tables, piecewise-linear units and integer approximations implement scalar nonlinearities in hardware [ 48 , 49 , 7 ] .
What is new. Unlike universal approximation, the compiled graph is exact on all inputs, reuses the trained weights and has a known size. Unlike compilers and privacy-preserving decompositions, we ask which small operator set expresses a whole block exactly. New are the lower bound that prices product-free compilation and the extended construction that removes the price. Unlike multiplication-free training, we compile a trained model, keep LayerNorm, and remove only products of two activations. Our tail-mass estimate is simpler than those of Scatterbrain, Combiner and clustered attention. It is a fixed affine map for a constant mass. For clustered queries, new are the cost bounds for the selected scores and the flocking step as one attention head.

A generic DNN vocabulary
We describe a DNN by the types of operations its nodes perform. Three types cover plain convolutional networks and multilayer perceptrons.

Convolutions, fully connected layers, batch normalization at inference, residual sums and average pooling are fixed affine maps; max pooling is routing. ReLU, GELU and other activations are unary maps. Gating, as in squeeze-and-excitation blocks [ 19 ] , multiplies two activations and is not in this basis. The set Φ \Phi is a parameter. Strict compilation pays an unavoidable pointwise cost
A multiply–accumulate (MAC) array computes products of matrices and reuses each loaded number many times. A unary map takes one number and returns one, so it runs on a separate, slower scalar, vector or lookup-table unit. We count the two kinds of work separately.
Definition 4 (Pointwise, contraction and added work) .
For a graph G G , the pointwise work PW ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{PW}(G) is the number of scalar evaluations of unary maps in one forward pass; it counts evaluations, not nodes. The contraction work MAC ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{MAC}(G) is the number of multiply–accumulate steps in its fixed affine nodes and in its products of two activations. The source graph of a transform T T is the graph of its defining formula in Definition 3 , with each product of two activations as one product node; PW ⁡ ( T ) \mathrm{PW}(T) and MAC ⁡ ( T ) \mathrm{MAC}(T) are its work. Score \mathrm{Score} is one product node with PW = 0 \mathrm{PW}=0 and MAC = N 2 ​ d \mathrm{MAC}=N^{2}d per head, LayerNorm forms its second moment with a product node and pays one inverse square root per token, and softmax pays one exponential per entry and one reciprocal per row. If a transform-wise compilation replaces T T by the graph G T G_{T} , its added work is Δ ​ PW ​ ( T ) = PW ⁡ ( G T ) − PW ⁡ ( T ) \Delta\mathrm{PW}(T)=\mathrm{PW}(G_{T})-\mathrm{PW}(T) and Δ ​ MAC ​ ( T ) = MAC ⁡ ( G T ) − MAC ⁡ ( T ) \Delta\mathrm{MAC}(T)=\mathrm{MAC}(G_{T})-\mathrm{MAC}(T) .

Activation–activation products remove the cost
Theorem 2 names the missing operation type: the product of two activations. Every NPU we survey provides it as an elementwise product, together with the sigmoid (Supplementary Table S1 ).

Attention weights cluster on a few keys

Top- k k attention keeps the support I = TopK ⁡ ( s , k ) I=\mathrm{TopK}(s,k) and drops the other m = N − k m=N-k keys, the set J J . Centroids factor the selection scores
Exact top- k k forms all N 2 N^{2} scores before it selects, so its strict graph makes at least N 2 N^{2} unary evaluations per head (Theorem 2 ). When the queries cluster, the keys can be chosen before the scores, and only the k k kept scores of each query are formed.
Corollary 2 (Cost of selected scores) .
Fix one head and 1 ≤ k ≤ N 1\leq k\leq N , and let each query select k k keys by a rule that is constant on some open set of inputs. Every product-free graph that computes the N ​ k Nk selected scores has PW ≥ N ​ k \mathrm{PW}\geq Nk . Given the selected key indices, a graph over ℬ strict ​ ( Φ strict ) \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{strict}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{strict}}) computes them with PW = N ​ k ​ d + 2 ​ N ​ d \mathrm{PW}=Nkd+2Nd .
For each head, k-means on calibration queries gives C C centroids c 1 , … , c C c_{1},\dots,c_{C} , the rows of W ∈ ℝ C × d W\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times d} . Query i i goes to its nearest centroid c r ⁡ ( i ) c_{r(i)} , the one that maximizes the affine score q i ⋅ c r − 1 2 ​ ‖ c r ‖ 2 q_{i}\cdot c_{r}-\tfrac{1}{2}\|c_{r}\|^{2} , and the assignment E ∈ { 0 , 1 } N × C E\in\{0,1\}^{N\times C} factors the queries as Q ≈ E ​ W Q\approx EW . The selection scores E ​ W ​ K ⊤ / d EWK^{\top}/\sqrt{d} , a gather of the centroid scores W ​ K ⊤ / d WK^{\top}/\sqrt{d} , replace Q ​ K ⊤ / d QK^{\top}/\sqrt{d} , and the routed support of query i i is the top k k keys of its row. It is the exact top- k k support of q i q_{i} whenever the gap condition of Lemma 2 holds for c = c r ⁡ ( i ) c=c_{r(i)} . Like TopK \mathrm{TopK} of the scores, it is constant on an open set of inputs, so Corollary 2 bounds its scores. The source graph of the routed map is the all-values form ( 1 ) on the routed support.
Proposition 5 (Routed support) .
For fixed centroids and an h h -head block, tail-mass attention with a constant tail mass on the routed supports is computed exactly by a graph over ℬ strict ​ ( Φ strict ) \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{strict}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{strict}}) whose Score \mathrm{Score} and Mix \mathrm{Mix} subgraphs make h ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ​ k ​ d + N ​ k + 3 ​ N ​ d ) h(2Nkd+Nk+3Nd) unary evaluations, and by a graph over ℬ ext ​ ( Φ ext ) \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}) with Δ ​ PW = 0 \Delta\mathrm{PW}=0 .
In both graphs the assignment scores and the centroid scores are fixed affine maps with 2 ​ h ​ C ​ N ​ d 2hCNd multiply–accumulate steps, and the assignment and the selection are routing, so the router adds no unary evaluation. The compiled models match their references
We compile three pretrained models with the extended graph and top- k k tail-mass attention, and evaluate them before training. With all keys kept, the compiled models match their references: 91.36 % 91.36\% against 91.36 % 91.36\% on Pet, 72.01 % 72.01\% against 72.02 % 72.02\% on ImageNet, 32.51 32.51 dB against 32.51 32.51 dB on Set5, and a token negative log-likelihood (NLL) of 4.5496 4.5496 against 4.5496 4.5496 on WikiText-2. The strict and extended graphs compute the same model exactly (Theorems 1 and 3 ). Only model changes, such as top- k k attention, and rounding change accuracy.

NPUs provide the Hadamard basis
Supplementary Table S1 shows where each graph runs on ten edge accelerators by documented operator support, with the approximation of GELU where GELU is missing. The extended graph runs entirely on five; the toolchains of the other four have no matrix product of two activations and place it on the host. Standard quantization applies to the compiled graph
We apply the post-training quantization (PTQ) used for convolutional networks [ 53 , 54 ] : symmetric max-abs scales from calibration data, with no training or reconstruction and no Transformer-specific quantizer. The only fitted quantities are least-squares output maps (Methods).

Quantizers cut each graph into units with integer inputs and outputs (Methods). Weights, the logits and the inputs of linear, convolution and product nodes take 8 bits; the fixed affine nodes of the strict graph take 12-bit inputs, since these include squares and sums of squares.

The bf16 runs evaluate the reference and every compiled graph under PyTorch CUDA autocast, which keeps means and sums in fp32; a node that adds several inputs stores its result in the precision of its first input. In float64, against the reference with its weights rounded to bf16 the same way, the extended and strict DeiT-Tiny graphs and the Hadamard graph with exact GELU, all with all keys, reproduce the reference logits on 16 Pet test images to 6 × 10 − 8 6\times 10^{-8} . The differences between the compiled and reference models in Table 2 therefore come from bf16 weights and arithmetic. The implementation of the strict graph evaluates ( x + ε ) − 1 / 2 (x+\varepsilon)^{-1/2} as a single kernel; it equals the inverse square root after a fixed affine map.

S1. Tightness and scope of the bound
Squares only. With Φ = { ( ⋅ ) 2 } \Phi=\{(\cdot)^{2}\} and one nonlinear layer, PW \mathrm{PW} is within a factor two of the bilinear rank of the matrix multiplication tensor, since polarization turns each product into two squares and each square is one product. Fast matrix multiplication [ 64 , 65 ] then gives strict graphs with PW = O ⁡ ( n 2.373 ) \mathrm{PW}=O(n^{2.373}) for n × n n\times n products, so no Ω ⁡ ( N 2 ​ d ) \Omega(N^{2}d) bound holds for this Φ \Phi , and the factor d + 2 ​ d / N d+2d/N between the strict graph of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 is not forced. Conversely, on the set of Lemma 8 a graph over { ( ⋅ ) 2 } \{(\cdot)^{2}\} with PW \mathrm{PW} squarings is an arithmetic circuit with PW \mathrm{PW} nonscalar multiplications, and nonscalar multiplications never exceed the total arithmetic cost [ 66 ] . For d = N d=N , a lower bound of order N 2 + δ N^{2+\delta} for this Φ \Phi would therefore prove that the exponent of matrix multiplication is at least 2 + δ 2+\delta , an open problem. For fixed d d , N 2 ​ d = O ⁡ ( N 2 ) N^{2}d=O(N^{2}) and the theorem is tight in order. MAC arrays and tensor units run the classical product, and the strict graph follows it.
Unrestricted Φ \Phi . On a bounded box, Score \mathrm{Score} is computed by a product-free graph with N 2 + 2 ​ N ​ d N^{2}+2Nd unary evaluations, so no argument that ignores the regularity of Φ \Phi can improve Theorem 2 beyond the additive 2 ​ N ​ d 2Nd . Map the n = 2 ​ N ​ d n=2Nd inputs affinely into [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) . For p = 1 , … , n p=1,\dots,n let ψ p \psi_{p} send x ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) x\in[0,1) , written in binary with infinitely many zero digits, to the number whose digit at position r ​ n + p rn+p is the r r -th digit of x x and whose other digits are zero. The affine sum z = ∑ p ψ p ​ ( x p ) z=\sum_{p}\psi_{p}(x_{p}) interleaves the digits of all inputs, has infinitely many zero digits, and so determines every x p x_{p} . One further unary map per output, ϕ i ​ j ​ ( z ) = S i ​ j \phi_{ij}(z)=S_{ij} , decodes the score. The total is n + N 2 n+N^{2} evaluations. The construction reaches the bound by first encoding its inputs; one evaluation of a linear form alone cannot compute a product on an open set, because ϕ ⁡ ( a ⋅ x + b ) \phi(a\cdot x+b) is constant on hyperplanes.
Transform-wise scope. Applied to a whole block as one map, the nonlinear rank is at most the number of outputs, N ​ h ​ d = 3.78 × 10 4 Nhd=3.78\times 10^{4} for DeiT-Tiny, below the pointwise work of the source block. A compilation that never forms Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} is therefore outside Theorem 2 . The theorem concerns the attention contractions as transforms, which is the form in which every Transformer implementation and every NPU operator set presents them.
S2. NPU operator support
Table S1 answers, for ten edge accelerators and from their documented operator support, whether the dense strict, Hadamard and extended graphs run entirely on the NPU. “Whole” means the documentation lists every operation of the graph as supported on the accelerator; otherwise the cell lists the operations left to the host CPU or GPU, or, on R-Car, to the on-chip DSP. MatMul is a matrix product of two activations, as in Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} and P ​ V PV , and Rsqrt is the inverse square root. Where the compiler or runtime is open source we read the placement from its code at the commit given in the reference; for the three closed toolchains, Rockchip, Jetson DLA and Edge TPU, we use the vendor documentation. A square runs as the elementwise product x ⊙ x x\odot x , which every accelerator in the table supports. A softmax counts as on the NPU when the NPU runs it as a native row-wise operator or runs both the exponential and the reciprocal. On the Jetson DLA the inverse square root runs as an INT8 power and the reciprocal as an INT8 division, each with a constant operand, and softmax runs on the NPU on Orin. The R-Car CNN-IP lacks the inverse square root and runs it on the on-chip DSP. The open-source NVDLA IP and Ethos-N78 lack the inverse square root too and leave it, with the LayerNorm and the reciprocal that need it, to the host. The extended graph runs whole on Ethos-U, VeriSilicon, the TDA4VM and the RK3588, and on R-Car with the DSP. The toolchains of the other four have no matrix product of two activations and place the MatMul on the host, on three of them also softmax, and on two also the inverse square root of LayerNorm. The Edge TPU compiler adds a limit of its own: it splits a model only once, so the first unsupported operation and everything after it run on the CPU [ 2 ] . The strict graph runs whole there, and the extended graph moves to the CPU from the first attention score on. On NVIDIA these limits come from the toolchains, not the hardware: the open-source NVDLA IP, which the Jetson DLA is based on, has a programmable lookup table with linear interpolation, two tables of 65 and 257 entries, in its activation and normalization units [ 75 ] . It can hold every function of Φ strict \Phi_{\mathrm{strict}} and Φ ext \Phi_{\mathrm{ext}} , but both NVIDIA toolchains fill it only for the functions in the table.
Every accelerator multiplies two activations elementwise, so none is product-free in the sense of Definition 1 : a CNN-class NPU provides the Hadamard basis ℬ ⊙ \mathcal{B}_{\odot} . There, Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} and P ​ V PV need no pointwise work, since a broadcast Hadamard product followed by a fixed sum computes them (Proposition 2 ). With the sigmoid and the inverse square root, the Hadamard basis also runs the rest of the block (Proposition 1 ). The Hadamard graph takes the operations of the strict graph, with the elementwise product between two activations where the strict graph multiplies an activation by itself, so it runs on the accelerators where the strict graph runs. Where the toolchain multiplies only tensors of equal shape, as on the Ethos-N78, broadcast routing expands both operands first, as the strict graph does for its polarization operands. The Ethos-U55 compiler takes this route: it turns a matrix multiplication between activations into an elementwise product into an int32 buffer followed by a sum, one output column at a time. The Ethos-U85 compiler runs the same operation on its MAC array. TopK \mathrm{TopK} runs on the TDA4VM accelerator and on the R-Car DSP. On the other accelerators top- k k attention and the routed support keep the selection on the host; Ethos-U and VeriSilicon can instead replace TopK \mathrm{TopK} by k k arg-max passes with masking.
S3. Why the bound counts pointwise work
Write IO L ​ ( G ) \mathrm{IO}_{L}(G) for the minimum number of words moved between a fast memory of L L words and slow memory, over all schedules of a graph G G . With bandwidth β \beta , every schedule takes time
T ⁡ ( G ) ≥ max ⁡ ( IO L ​ ( G ) / β , MAC ⁡ ( G ) / R mac , PW ⁡ ( G ) / R pw ) . T(G)\ \geq\ \max\Bigl(\mathrm{IO}_{L}(G)/\beta,\ \mathrm{MAC}(G)/R_{\mathrm{mac}},\ \mathrm{PW}(G)/R_{\mathrm{pw}}\Bigr).
The unfused strict graph writes the N 2 ​ d N^{2}d values q i ​ f + k j ​ f q_{if}+k_{jf} to memory (Supplementary Section S10), so this one schedule has arithmetic intensity Θ ⁡ ( 1 ) \Theta(1) and moves far more than IO L \mathrm{IO}_{L} . These writes are a property of the schedule, not of the basis. The strict expansion of Score \mathrm{Score} in Lemma 5 has the access pattern of the matrix product it replaces, so the tiling of the matrix product applies. With square output tiles of side Θ ⁡ ( L ) \Theta(\sqrt{L}) , streaming over the reduction index, and L L small compared with the operands, a fused schedule moves
IO L = O ⁡ ( N 2 ​ d L + N 2 + N ​ d ) \mathrm{IO}_{L}=O\Bigl(\frac{N^{2}d}{\sqrt{L}}+N^{2}+Nd\Bigr)
words per head, where the last two terms are the compulsory traffic of writing the scores and reading Q Q and K K ; for classical (non-Strassen) schedules of the native product the first term is also a lower bound in this regime [ 76 ] . A fused strict kernel therefore has the same traffic order as native Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} . Fusion changes neither the number of unary evaluations nor the unit that must perform them. That is why Theorem 2 bounds PW \mathrm{PW} and Corollary 1 rests on the third term. The same tiling applies to a Hadamard product followed by a fixed reduction (Proposition 2 ): fused, it has the access pattern of the matrix product, so the Hadamard and extended bases differ only in the schedule a compiler chooses.

S5. Polarization and squares
The strict graph forms each product of two activations from squares, by one of two classical identities:
a ​ b = 1 4 ​ [ ( a + b ) 2 − ( a − b ) 2 ] , a ​ b = 1 2 ​ [ ( a + b ) 2 − a 2 − b 2 ] . ab=\tfrac{1}{4}\bigl[(a+b)^{2}-(a-b)^{2}\bigr],\qquad ab=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[(a+b)^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}\bigr].
The first, polarization, is the method of quarter-squares [ 34 ] . The second, the norm expansion, forms products in expressivity proofs for ReLU networks [ 36 ] .
Cost. Polarization pays two squares per product. The norm expansion pays one square per product plus the squares a 2 a^{2} and b 2 b^{2} of the factors, so an isolated product costs two squares by polarization and three by the norm expansion. In a contraction each factor enters many products, and its square is formed once. Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} of two N × d N\times d matrices then costs 2 ​ N 2 ​ d 2N^{2}d squares by polarization and N 2 ​ d + 2 ​ N ​ d N^{2}d+2Nd by the norm expansion, and the ratio to the bound of Theorem 2 falls from 2 ​ d 2d to d + 2 ​ d / N d+2d/N . This is Winograd’s precomputation of the self-terms of each row [ 35 ] , with squares in place of products. The strict graph uses the norm expansion where the squares of the factors are shared, in Q ​ K ⊤ QK^{\top} , P ​ V PV , LayerNorm and the exact tail term, and polarization elsewhere, in softmax and the GELU approximation.
Exactness. Both identities are exact in real arithmetic and, without overflow or requantization, in integer arithmetic (Methods).
Rounding. Both identities recover a small product as the difference of large squares. In the value mix the result y = ∑ k p k ​ v k y=\sum_{k}p_{k}v_{k} is a weighted mean of the values, while the squares sum to about ∑ k v k 2 \sum_{k}v_{k}^{2} ; on DeiT-Tiny this ratio is 65 65 at k = 16 k=16 and 543 543 with all keys. In bf16 the order of the operations then sets the error (Table S3 ). Rounding each partial sum before the subtraction, 1 2 ​ [ ∑ k ( p k + v k ) 2 − ∑ k p k 2 − ∑ k v k 2 ] \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\sum_{k}(p_{k}+v_{k})^{2}-\sum_{k}p_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k}v_{k}^{2}\bigr] , gives 57 % 57\% relative error with all keys. Subtracting the two close squares per term inside the node that sums over keys gives 14 % 14\% for either identity: 1 4 ​ ∑ k [ ( p k + v k ) 2 − ( p k − v k ) 2 ] \tfrac{1}{4}\sum_{k}\bigl[(p_{k}+v_{k})^{2}-(p_{k}-v_{k})^{2}\bigr] by polarization and 1 2 ​ [ ∑ k ( v k 2 − ( v k − p k ) 2 ) + ∑ k p k 2 ] \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\sum_{k}\bigl(v_{k}^{2}-(v_{k}-p_{k})^{2}\bigr)+\sum_{k}p_{k}^{2}\bigr] by the norm expansion. With this order the two identities differ in cost, not in precision. As Brent showed for Winograd’s identity [ 77 ] , scaling the operands helps further: the fixed affine maps p → λ ​ p p\to\lambda p and v → v / λ v\to v/\lambda with λ 2 = ‖ v ‖ / ‖ p ‖ \lambda^{2}=\|v\|/\|p\| cut the error to 2.7 % 2.7\% .
Scaling and PTQ. Brent’s scaling and the PTQ scales are the same choice. PTQ gives each tensor a grid with step s = max ⁡ | x | / ( 2 b − 1 − 1 ) s=\max|x|/(2^{b-1}-1) for b b bits. The node that forms a ± b a\pm b adds two tensors, so in integers both operands sit on one grid, and with the step set by the larger operand the smaller keeps few levels: in the mix the weights p ≤ 1 p\leq 1 share the grid of values several times larger. The scaling a → λ ​ a a\to\lambda a , b → b / λ b\to b/\lambda leaves the product unchanged and gives both operands the same range, so one grid fits both. It also makes the squares as small as they can be, since λ 2 ​ a 2 + b 2 / λ 2 ≥ 2 ​ | a ​ b | \lambda^{2}a^{2}+b^{2}/\lambda^{2}\geq 2|ab| with equality at λ 2 = | b | / | a | \lambda^{2}=|b|/|a| , and the size of the squares sets the step of the node that subtracts them. PTQ calibration already measures the ranges, so λ 2 = max ⁡ | b | / max ⁡ | a | \lambda^{2}=\max|b|/\max|a| follows from the same statistics, and λ \lambda folds into the fixed affine maps that produce a a and b b and into the output maps, so it costs nothing at run time. A node that combines several inputs, as the norm expansion does, quantizes each input on its own grid and rescales it before the sum (Methods); this takes care of the subtraction, but the balance of the operands inside a ± b a\pm b still sets the number of bits. The strict graph in this paper applies no λ \lambda , and its fixed affine nodes take 12-bit input codes. Brent’s scaling can reduce this bit width.
