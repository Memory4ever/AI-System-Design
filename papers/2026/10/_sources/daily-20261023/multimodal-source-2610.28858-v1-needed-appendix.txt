 Appendix A Limitations and future work
We see three main limitations.
 Idealized analysis. Our analysis in Section   3.3 assumes optimal heads and a fixed reward head. In practice, the heads are only approximately optimal, and the reward head is refit on samples from the current generator, so the target moves during training. We also have no convergence guarantee for the alternating updates.
 Learned reward. The generator is trained against the reward head, not the reward itself, so it inherits both the errors of the head and the flaws of the external reward. The reference head keeps samples close to the reference pool, but it cannot tell when the generator is exploiting the reward.
 Reference pool. Fidelity is measured against the pool, so the generator also picks up properties that the reward ignores, such as style and composition ( Section   4.3 ).
Several directions remain open. A convergence analysis of the alternating updates would put the method on firmer ground. The reward head also deserves a wider study, covering its architecture design, its backbone features analysis, the noise levels it sees, and so on. Richer reference pools, with several references per prompt or with real images, are another natural extension. Practically, DualHeads could be applied during distillation, at higher resolutions, and to few-step video generators.
