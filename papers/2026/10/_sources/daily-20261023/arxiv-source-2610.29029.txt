Abstract
Verifier stacks increasingly sit inside post-training and test-time compute loops. We study a
specific control loop: a component verifier is evaluated on fresh policy outputs against the
full success label and refreshed when that aggregate score falls. For a conjunctive target
y = F ∧ M y=F\wedge M , its on-policy AUC is ( 1 − q ) ​ A own + q ​ A cross (1-q)A_{\rm own}+qA_{\rm cross} , where
q = Pr ⁡ [ F = 1 ∣ y = 0 ] q=\Pr[F=1\mid y=0] . As the policy masters F F , q q can rise toward one, removing weight from
F F -failures and rewarding information about M M . We call this on-policy audit collapse .
We derive a sharp identified set, a complete-erasure threshold, and the exact point at which the
audit can prefer a worse component verifier; its population optimum is the full-outcome predictor,
not the component oracle. An exact oracle exhibits the collapse, a natural multi-label text control
shows inversion can select a head with lower canonical F F -AUC at the observed failure mixture,
and a prediction-registered ECG study shows that acting on the audit can reduce off-policy F F -AUC,
transfer the damage to an unseen generator, and increase downstream constraint violations. Preserving
own-failure support prevents the damage. Matching label-complexity bounds and a controlled-mixture
audit give the corresponding repair: audit the component on its own failures, and keep those failures
in the refresh distribution.
1 Introduction
Verifier stacks increasingly sit inside the optimization loop. Post-training systems combine
outcome, process, rubric and constraint signals, while test-time compute uses verifier scores to
search, rank and select candidates
( Cobbe et al., 2021 ; Lightman et al., 2024 ; Brown et al., 2024 ; Snell et al., 2025 ; Mahmoud et al., 2026 ; Wu et al., 2023 ; Zhou et al., 2024 ; Moskovitz et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2026 ; Bai et al., 2026 ) . A natural but unsafe control loop is to monitor a component verifier on fresh policy samples
against the full task outcome; if that aggregate score drops, collect new on-policy data and refresh
the verifier. Policy-interaction data are in fact used to refine reward models and iterative RLHF
pipelines repeatedly update preference models on fresh policy data ( Bai et al., 2022 ; Sun et al., 2025 ) .
This paper shows that this loop can become systematically wrong because the policy improved .

failures, some violate F F and others already satisfy F F but fail only M M . Pairwise additivity
of AUC gives

separates the two estimands explicitly. A cross A_{\rm cross} compares successes with failures that already satisfy F F ;
an exact F F -oracle has A cross = 1 / 2 A_{\rm cross}=1/2 by construction. When optimization increases satisfaction of F F faster than the remaining conjunct—in particular as F F saturates while M M remains imperfect— q q rises. Consequently, the standard aggregate monitor progressively removes weight from
the verifier’s own failure mode and places it on information about the other conjunct.
The paradox is therefore directional: the more completely the policy masters a component,
the less the usual on-policy audit measures that component. We call this on-policy audit
collapse : optimization removes the very failure mode needed to evaluate the module that learned it.
This creates a concrete failure chain. First, the monitor loses identification: a falling AUC need
not imply a worse component verifier. Second, it can become actively anti-aligned with the audit’s intended subproblem: at a calculable
q ∗ q^{\ast} it prefers a head with worse A own A_{\rm own} because that head leaks information about
M M ; in our text and engine controls this can also select a head with worse canonical F F -AUC. Third, the usual response to a bad monitor—on-policy refresh—can remove component
competence. Once optimization has depleted F F -failures, refresh data alone no longer identify the
boundary the component verifier must retain; outcome labels can actively entangle it, while even
correct F F labels cannot reconstruct failures absent from support. The result can look benign on the
monitored distribution while becoming worse exactly on the off-support failures a constraint checker
must catch after a generator, prompt distribution, or policy shift.

Viewed as a learning system, this is a control-plane failure: optimizing the policy changes the data
used to decide whether its verifier should be replaced or refreshed.
When does the failure apply?
The central result requires three ingredients: a head intended for component F F , an on-policy
audit against the full outcome y y rather than a component-specific label, and use of that audit to
compare, early-stop or trigger refreshes. If trusted F F labels are available and the component is
audited directly (preferably on a reference distribution that preserves F F -failures), the collapse in
Eq. 1 is avoided. Our claim is therefore about this specific but natural interface,
not about every verifier evaluation protocol.
Why this interface is current.
Its ingredients already coexist in modern post-training systems. Rubric RL independently scores
prompt-specific criteria and aggregates them into a scalar on-policy reward
( Gunjal et al., 2026 ; Mahmoud et al., 2026 ) ; policy-aware rubric work reports that substantial
criterion mass is already saturated or dead under current policies ( Tyagi et al., 2026 ) ; and
fresh policy-interaction data are used to refine reward models or preference models
( Sun et al., 2025 ; Bai et al., 2022 ) . We do not claim that these systems implement our exact
audit-trigger-refresh controller end to end. They establish that criterion-level verifiers, policy-induced
saturation, scalar aggregation and on-policy refinement are contemporary ingredients; Appendix AD.3
maps the interfaces explicitly.
Our experiments are organized to test this chain rather than to accumulate unrelated benchmarks.
The engine domain gives an exact component oracle, allowing us to separate real verifier failure
from a case-mix artifact. GSM8K supplies a familiar language-domain negative control for ranking
inversion. A natural multi-label GoEmotions stress test uses separately annotated semantic criteria to test
audit collapse, inversion and refresh under support depletion; a constructed AG News control is
reported in the appendix. ECG then stresses the operational
consequence on a frozen external benchmark with three generator families, subject-held-out splits,
two refresh model classes, downstream best-of- 16 16 selection, and a precommitted set of
predictions. Across these settings the observations line up with the mechanism: exact-oracle AUC
collapses, the analytic inversion threshold predicts where inversion does and does not occur,
optimization removes own-failure support, outcome refresh damages off-policy canonical F F -AUC and
downstream constraints in every paired split, and deliberately restoring failure support prevents
the damage.
Contributions.
(1) On-policy audit collapse. We derive the mixture law, a sharp identified set, and the
complete-erasure threshold q ≥ max ⁡ ( A , 1 − A ) q\geq\max(A,1-A) (Section 4 ).
(2) Audit inversion. We give the threshold q ∗ q^{\ast} at which cross-component signal
overtakes own-failure separability and show that the aggregate audit’s population maximizer is the
full-outcome predictor, not the component oracle (Proposition 2 ).
(3) The refresh trap. A controlled semantic-language stress test and a prediction-registered
ECG study separate label mismatch from support mismatch and connect refresh to downstream harm
(Section 5 ).
(4) Support-preserving repair. We give matching passive label-complexity bounds and a
designed two-mixture audit whose error does not depend on the deployed policy’s saturation
(Section 6 ). Downstream selection/training-interface results are deliberately kept as a
boundary analysis in the appendix.

5 The refresh trap: acting on audit collapse
Proposition 2 describes what the aggregate audit prefers. Deployment makes the
problem operational: a low monitor value can trigger collection of fresh policy samples and a
verifier refresh. The mixture law predicts two distinct hazards. Label mismatch: if a
component verifier is refreshed against the full outcome y y , optimization explicitly moves it
toward the audit’s preferred entangled predictor. Support mismatch: even with the correct
component labels F F , a saturated policy may provide almost no F F -failures, so the refresh data
contain little or no information about the decision boundary the verifier must retain. The second
hazard means the problem is not fixed simply by choosing the “right” label.

Empirical conclusion.
Across the study, five of six registered predictions held. More importantly, the evidence follows
the mechanism at four levels: the audit rewards entanglement, optimization removes own-failure
support, refresh causes held-out and cross-generator component forgetting, and that forgetting
raises downstream violations. The failed prediction is informative rather than cosmetic: the
monitored AUC is not reliably repaired by refresh, strengthening the narrower conclusion that it
is an unsuitable control signal. The successful rescue identifies the actionable fix used in the
next section: preserve or deliberately reconstruct the component’s own failure distribution.

7 Scope: what the results do and do not establish
The AUC symptom does not identify its cause: an exact oracle collapses and an unbounded
autoencoder genuinely exploits the critic (feasibility 0.9999 0.9999 predicted, 1.8 % 1.8\% actual;
Appendix F ); this is not a claim that in-bounds exploitation is absent.
Proposition 2 is exact, but its engine and GSM8K checks reuse published
quantities and are post-hoc. The GoEmotions study is a post-hoc natural multi-label text stress test
with a small bag-of-words verifier; the AG News experiment is a more constructed control with
24 deterministic robustness splits rather than independent replications. Together they establish that
the mechanism is not specific to engineered numeric features, not that our refresh effect has been
measured with an LLM-scale semantic verifier. Contemporary rubric-RL work independently shows
criterion-level saturation and scalar aggregation at LLM scale ( Gunjal et al., 2026 ; Tyagi et al., 2026 ) ;
we use that literature only to establish interface relevance, not as validation of our inversion or refresh
claims. The ECG refresh study is CPU-only and uses one benchmark whose component is a deterministic
resource constraint, two head classes and ten subject splits; we did not refresh an LLM-scale verifier,
did not study online RL with a refreshed verifier, and our prediction that refresh repairs the monitor failed. Engine and GSM8K policies are Qwen2.5, the
strongest training result is offline DPO with three seeds, and ECG training is informative in one
family. The audit requires trusted component labels (proxy bins change the estimand); the engine
oracle is simplified, and ECG is a signal-processing benchmark, not a clinical validation.
8 Related work
Modern post-training makes the component/aggregate interface concrete. Rubric RL scores
prompt-specific criteria separately and then aggregates them into scalar on-policy rewards
( Gunjal et al., 2026 ; Mahmoud et al., 2026 ) ; policy-aware rubric work further shows that criterion
informativeness changes with the policy because many criteria become saturated or dead
( Tyagi et al., 2026 ) . Fresh policy interactions are also used to refine reward or preference
models ( Sun et al., 2025 ; Bai et al., 2022 ) . Two adjacent loop failures are especially relevant:
on-policy optimization against one verifier can remove behaviors needed for later objectives
( Wei et al., 2026 ) , and a verifier useful on one task can regress a learner after task shift
( Lin, 2026 ) . We instead study the audit interface : full-outcome AUC can stop
identifying one component, prefer an entangled verifier, and induce a harmful refresh even when the
component verifier was initially correct.
The statistical ingredients are classical. ROC behavior depends on case mix
( Sadatsafavi et al., 2022 ) ; partial identification and trimming bounds describe incomplete
observables ( Manski, 2003 ; Tamer, 2010 ; Horowitz and Manski, 1995 ) ; deliberately
varying mixture weights is a linear special case of finite-mixture identification
( Henry et al., 2014 ) ; the within/cross split parallels xAUC ( Kallus and Zhou, 2019 ) ; and the
population AUC maximizer is the classical optimal ranker ( Clémençon et al., 2008 ) . We do not
claim these ingredients as new. The new consequence is their combination under policy-induced
semantic failure mixtures: complete loss of component identification, a calculable
verifier-ranking inversion, and an audit-driven maintenance failure.

Mixture-shift versus ordering-drift decomposition.
Across two policies, the AUC change can also be decomposed into mixture shift and
conditional-ordering drift:
A t − A s = \displaystyle A_{t}-A_{s}={}
( q t − q s ) ​ ( A cross s − A own s ) \displaystyle(q_{t}-q_{s})(A_{\rm cross}^{s}-A_{\rm own}^{s})
(7)
+ ( 1 − q t ) ​ ( A own t − A own s ) + q t ​ ( A cross t − A cross s ) . \displaystyle+(1-q_{t})(A_{\rm own}^{t}-A_{\rm own}^{s})+q_{t}(A_{\rm cross}^{t}-A_{\rm cross}^{s}).
The first term changes only composition; the last two change conditional ordering.
Both mechanisms can therefore coexist. The identity extends to any disjoint
failure-bin partition and requires no independence assumptions.
Rules that reduce the saturated component’s influence recover selection.

Appendix AE Selection interface: audit equivalence does not imply equal influence
The audit in Section 4 uses only the ordering within one component

s F ↦ g ⁡ ( s F ) s_{F}\mapsto g(s_{F}) . A multi-component selector also consumes the scale of s F s_{F}

Proposition 5 (Selection ambiguity is the Pareto set) .
Fix a pool with component scores ( s F , s M ) (s_{F},s_{M}) and distinct score pairs (duplicates
are merged; Appendix Y ). As g g ranges over strictly
increasing maps—all of which leave every ROC/AUC of s F s_{F} unchanged—the set of

pool in ( s F , s M ) (s_{F},s_{M}) . Over positive rescalings g ⁡ ( t ) = c ​ t g(t)=ct it is the subset of
Par \operatorname{Par} on the upper convex hull. Hence an audit statistic invariant to
monotone reparameterization determines the composite decision iff
| Par | = 1 |\operatorname{Par}|=1 , and determines whether that decision is on target iff all
Pareto candidates share one oracle label.
The proof is constructive (Appendix Y ): a dominated candidate loses
under every increasing g g , and each Pareto candidate wins under a g g that jumps at its
own s F s_{F} . This
is the classical correspondence between Pareto optimality and monotone scalarization
( Miettinen, 1999 ) , read here as a statement about what an audit leaves open.
Its size is predictable.

Takeaway.
Inference scalarization is a decision rule; pre-DPO scalarization is a
data-construction operator . The evidence is one-directional and thin
(Figure 16 b, Appendix AB ): all three rules with ≥ 79 % \geq 79\% gate
overlap reached the gate’s endpoint, consistent with Proposition 7 ,
but only Phi’s comes from a family whose gate is informative; low overlap left the
endpoint undetermined. A weak bottleneck contrast accompanied all four
informative misses, but Phi’s pair weight also missed with balanced contrasts, a residual
that two contrast coordinates cannot see. In practice: when a tuned selection weight trains poorly, check
its pair overlap with the rule it should emulate; if overlap is low, neither overlap
nor mean contrasts certify it, and the reference rule’s own pairs are the safe default.
