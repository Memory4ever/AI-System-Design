 C.1 Full statement and proof of Theorem  1 
 Theorem  1 (Exponential locality of GMRF; full statement) . 
 Suppose X_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q^{-1}) , where Q\in\mathbb{R}^{Md_{p}\times Md_{p}} is symmetric positive definite and its off-diagonal patch blocks vanish outside the edges of G_{0}=([M],E_{0}) .
Let X_{t}=a_{t}X_{0}+b_{t}\varepsilon with a_{t},b_{t}>0 and independent \varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{Md_{p}}) , and set Y_{t}=X_{t}/a_{t} and \sigma_{t}=b_{t}/a_{t} .
For B_{r}(m)=\{l:d_{G_{0}}(m,l)\leq r\} , define 
 D_{m,t}^{\ast}(y)=\mathbb{E}[X_{0,m}\mid Y_{t}=y,t],\qquad D_{m,t}^{\ast,r}(y)=\mathbb{E}[X_{0,m}\mid Y_{t,B_{r}(m)}=y_{B_{r}(m)},t]. 
 Let q_{-}:=\lambda_{\min}(Q) and q_{+}:=\lambda_{\max}(Q) satisfy 0<q_{-}<q_{+}<\infty .
Then for every patch m and every integer radius r\geq 0 , 
 \displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-D_{m,t}^{\ast,r}(Y_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{d_{p}}{q_{-}}\left[\frac{\left(1-1/\sqrt{\chi_{t}}\right)^{2}}{1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-}}\right]\rho_{t}^{2r}, 
 (15) 
 where \chi_{t}=(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+})/(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-}) is the condition number of I+\sigma_{t}^{2}Q and \rho_{t}=(\sqrt{\chi_{t}}-1)/(\sqrt{\chi_{t}}+1) is the Chebyshev convergence factor.
In the notation of Assump.  1 , the bound is C_{t}\exp(-c_{t}r) with 
 C_{t}=\frac{d_{p}(1-1/\sqrt{\chi_{t}})^{2}}{q_{-}(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-})},\qquad c_{t}=2\log(1/\rho_{t})>0. 
 If q_{-}=q_{+} , then Q=q_{-}I and radius zero is exact. 
Proof.
 Since Y_{t}=X_{0}+\sigma_{t}\varepsilon , Bayes’ rule gives posterior precision
 Q+\sigma_{t}^{-2}I=\sigma_{t}^{-2}(I+\sigma_{t}^{2}Q) and posterior mean 
 \displaystyle D_{m,t}^{\ast}(y)=\left[(I+\sigma_{t}^{2}Q)^{-1}y\right]_{m}. 
 Let H_{t}:=I+\sigma_{t}^{2}Q . The matrix H_{t} is real symmetric positive definite, and 
 \displaystyle\spec(H_{t})\subseteq[1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-},1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+}]. 
 Write its spectral decomposition as H_{t}=U\Lambda_{t}U^{\top} , where U is orthogonal and \Lambda_{t} is diagonal.
We first approximate the scalar inverse function with the degree- r polynomial in ( 14 ), and then apply that polynomial to H_{t} . 
 Take h_{-,t}:=1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-} and h_{+,t}:=1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+} , so that \chi_{t}=h_{+,t}/h_{-,t} .
Then by the Chebyshev approximation in ( 13 ), the degree- r Chebyshev polynomial \psi_{r,t}(x) as given in ( 14 ) (with h_{-} and h_{+} replaced by h_{-,t} and h_{+,t} ) satisfies 
 \displaystyle\|H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\|_{2} 
 \displaystyle=\|U\left(\Lambda_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(\Lambda_{t})\right)U^{\top}\|_{2} 
 \displaystyle=\max_{\lambda\in\spec(H_{t})}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}-\psi_{r,t}(\lambda)\right| 
 \displaystyle\leq\frac{2\rho_{t}^{r+1}}{\sqrt{h_{-,t}h_{+,t}}(1-\rho_{t})}. 
 On the other hand, H_{t} has the same off-diagonal block sparsity pattern as Q , so, for every integer k\geq 0 , [H_{t}^{k}]_{ml}=0 whenever d_{G_{0}}(m,l)>k .
Indeed, each nonzero term in the blockwise expansion of [H_{t}^{k}]_{ml} corresponds to a length- k walk from m to l , with diagonal blocks allowing the walk to remain at its current vertex.
Because \psi_{r,t} has degree at most r , the predictor 
 \displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{m,t}^{r}(y):=[\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})y]_{m} 
 depends only on y_{B_{r}(m)} and is therefore measurable with respect to Y_{t,B_{r}(m)} .
Graph connectivity determines which patches lie within each radius, while the conditioning of I+\sigma_{t}^{2}Q controls the decay rate: worse conditioning slows the certified decay and can require broader context. 
 By the tower property,
 D_{m,t}^{\ast,r}(Y_{t})=\mathbb{E}[D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})\mid Y_{t,B_{r}(m)},t] .
The projection property of conditional expectation therefore implies 
 \displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-D_{m,t}^{\ast,r}(Y_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\|D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-\widetilde{D}_{m,t}^{r}(Y_{t})\|_{2}^{2}. 
 Construct P_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p}\times Md_{p}} so that it selects patch m of a noisy image y\in\mathbb{R}^{Md_{p}} , i.e., P_{m}y=y_{m} .
Then D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-\widetilde{D}_{m,t}^{r}(Y_{t})=P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)Y_{t} .
Since Y_{t} is zero-mean Gaussian with covariance Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I , we have 
 \displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-\widetilde{D}_{m,t}^{r}(Y_{t})\|_{2}^{2} 
 \displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\bigg(P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)(Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I)\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)P_{m}^{\top}\bigg) 
 \displaystyle=\left\|P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)(Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I)^{1/2}\right\|_{F}^{2} 
 \displaystyle\leq\|Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I\|_{2}\left\|P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}. 
 Since P_{m}(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p}\times Md_{p}} has d_{p} rows, the Frobenius–operator norm inequality and the Chebyshev approximation bound give 
 \displaystyle\left\|P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)\right\|_{F}^{2} 
 \displaystyle\leq d_{p}\left\|P_{m}\left(H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right)\right\|_{2}^{2} 
 \displaystyle\leq d_{p}\left\|H_{t}^{-1}-\psi_{r,t}(H_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2} 
 \displaystyle\leq d_{p}\frac{4\rho_{t}^{2r+2}}{h_{-,t}h_{+,t}(1-\rho_{t})^{2}}. 
 Also, since Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I is real symmetric positive definite, 
 \displaystyle\|Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I\|_{2}=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(Q^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}I)=q_{-}^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}. 
 Combining the above bounds, we have 
 \displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|D_{m,t}^{\ast}(Y_{t})-\widetilde{D}_{m,t}^{r}(Y_{t})\|_{2}^{2} 
 \displaystyle\leq d_{p}(q_{-}^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2})\frac{4\rho_{t}^{2r+2}}{h_{-,t}h_{+,t}(1-\rho_{t})^{2}}. 
 Finally, the identities 
 \displaystyle q_{-}^{-1}+\sigma_{t}^{2}=\frac{h_{-,t}}{q_{-}},\qquad\frac{2\rho_{t}}{\sqrt{h_{-,t}h_{+,t}}(1-\rho_{t})}=\frac{1-1/\sqrt{\chi_{t}}}{h_{-,t}} 
 reduce the preceding display to ( 15 ).
∎ 
 C.2 Full statement and proof of Corollary  2 
 Corollary  2 (Bounded-range monotonicity of the locality radius; full statement) . 
 Under the hypotheses of the full Thm.  1 above, with 0<q_{-}<q_{+} ,
define \sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}^{2}:=(q_{-}^{1/3}+q_{+}^{1/3})/(q_{-}^{2/3}q_{+}^{2/3}) .
For \epsilon>0 and 0<\sigma_{t}\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}} , the smallest radius r_{\epsilon}(\sigma_{t}) such that the upper bound in ( 15 ) is at most \epsilon is the least nonnegative integer satisfying 
 \displaystyle r_{\epsilon}(\sigma_{t})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{d_{p}\left(1-1/\sqrt{\chi_{t}}\right)^{2}}{\epsilon q_{-}(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-})}\right)\bigg/\log\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{t}}\right) 
 (16) 
 The resulting r_{\epsilon}(\sigma_{t}) is nondecreasing in \sigma_{t} on the same range. At \sigma_{t}=0 , radius zero is exact. 
Proof.
 We show that the upper bound in ( 15 )
is nondecreasing in \sigma_{t} for 0<\sigma_{t}\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}} .
First, for \sigma_{t}>0 , 
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{\chi_{t}}\frac{d\chi_{t}}{d\sigma_{t}}=\frac{2\sigma_{t}(q_{+}-q_{-})}{(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+})(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-})}\geq 0, 
 and hence d\chi_{t}/d\sigma_{t}\geq 0 .
Next, 
 \displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma_{t}}\log\left[\frac{\left(1-1/\sqrt{\chi_{t}}\right)^{2}}{(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-})}\right] 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\chi_{t}}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\chi_{t}}\frac{d\chi_{t}}{d\sigma_{t}}\right)-\frac{2\sigma_{t}q_{-}}{1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-}} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{2\sigma_{t}}{1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-}}\left[\frac{q_{+}-q_{-}}{(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+})(\sqrt{\chi_{t}}-1)}-q_{-}\right] 
 is nonnegative when q_{+}-q_{-}\geq q_{-}(1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+})(\sqrt{\chi_{t}}-1) . Using 
 \displaystyle 1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{+}=\chi_{t}\frac{q_{+}-q_{-}}{q_{+}-q_{-}\chi_{t}},\qquad q_{+}-q_{-}\chi_{t}=\frac{q_{+}-q_{-}}{1+\sigma_{t}^{2}q_{-}}, 
 this condition is equivalent to q_{+}\geq q_{-}\chi_{t}^{3/2} , which simplifes to \sigma_{t}^{2}\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}^{2} . 
 For the rest of the term in the upper bound, it is straightforward to see that d\rho_{t}/d\chi_{t}\geq 0 , and since d\chi_{t}/d\sigma_{t}\geq 0 , we have d\rho_{t}/d\sigma_{t}\geq 0 .
Consequently, if 0<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}} , every radius whose upper bound is at most \epsilon at \sigma_{2} also has upper bound at most \epsilon at \sigma_{1} . Hence r_{\epsilon}(\sigma_{2})\geq r_{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}) .
∎
