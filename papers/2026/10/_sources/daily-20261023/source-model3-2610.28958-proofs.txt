Lemma 8 (Fixed routing on a set dense in a ball) .
Let G G be a finite product-free graph with inputs in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , and let U ⊆ ℝ n U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n} be a nonempty open set. There is a set D ⊆ U D\subseteq U and an open ball B ⊆ U B\subseteq U such that D D is dense in B B and every routing decision of G G is the same at every point of D D .
Proof.
G G has finitely many routing nodes, and each, with its fixed tie-breaking rule, has finitely many outcomes: an order of finitely many compared values, or a selection among finitely many entries. The joint outcome therefore takes finitely many values, and the sets of points of U U with a given joint outcome form a finite partition of U U . A finite union of nowhere dense sets is nowhere dense, and U U is not, so some part D ′ D^{\prime} is not nowhere dense: its closure contains an open ball B B , which we may take inside U U . Then D = D ′ ∩ B D=D^{\prime}\cap B is dense in B B .
∎
No continuity is used: the outcome sets may be arbitrary, so Φ \Phi needs no continuity or measurability.

Lemma 11 (Rank of quadratic forms) .

Proof.
Suppose ∑ s λ s ​ F s \sum_{s}\lambda_{s}F_{s} agrees on D D with an affine function. Both sides are continuous and D D is dense in B B , so they agree on B B .
