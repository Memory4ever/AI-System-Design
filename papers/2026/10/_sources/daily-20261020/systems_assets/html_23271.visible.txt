Abstract
Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models promise generalist robotic
agents, yet consolidating skills learned across heterogeneous tasks and
environments remains difficult: training data is fragmented across
platforms, joint multi-task training suffers from interference, and model
merging tends to introduce parameter conflicts or require architectural
changes. We instead advocate a paradigm in which skills are first learned
independently and then consolidated post hoc. To this end, we propose
NestedVLA , which replaces parameter aggregation with
parameter generation : a nested hypernetwork, conditioned on the
target task, synthesizes task-adaptive action-expert parameters that compose
with a frozen base VLA. Task-specific compressed VLM residuals are retained
separately. This formulation enables flexible task-conditioned skill reuse
while mitigating interference. Across three RL benchmarks (MetaWorld,
ManiSkill, CALVIN), NestedVLA outperforms the strongest model-merging
baseline by    41  41    pp on average, matches per-task fine-tuning within
1  1    pp on ManiSkill, and substantially closes the gap on CALVIN, pointing
toward a storage-efficient route to skill consolidation. Code is available
at  https://github.com/WanyuGroup/NeurIPS2026-NestedVLA .
1  Introduction
Vision-language-action (VLA) models combine visual and language understanding
with embodied control  [ 2 ,  71 ,  26 ,  1 ,  40 ] . Independently
fine-tuning a common backbone produces policies specialized for different
robotic benchmarks, but storing one full checkpoint per benchmark duplicates
most parameters. Joint multi-task training can avoid this duplication when
data from all benchmarks can be pooled. We instead study post-hoc
consolidation of policies independently fine-tuned from the same backbone.
We seek to consolidate their task-specific parameter updates into a compact
representation without pooling the original training data.
Figure 1:   Motivation and overview of NestedVLA.
(a) Model merging combines independently fine-tuned VLA weights into a
shared model, where incompatible updates can impair the source tasks.
(b) NestedVLA retains a compressed task-specific VLM update and uses an
embedded task-conditioned hypernetwork to generate the action-expert update.
Both updates are applied to a shared base to materialize the selected VLA.
A natural post-hoc approach is to merge the parameter updates of the
fine-tuned policies into a single checkpoint. In VLA models, however,
independently learned updates can interfere strongly when combined: in our
experiments, simple averaging loses most of the performance of the separate
policies. Our weight-space analysis finds weak alignment across major
parameter groups, an incompatibility that is particularly consequential in
the action expert because it directly governs control
(Appendix   E  ). This observation is consistent with
MergeVLA  [ 16 ] , which likewise identifies dependencies
distributed across action-expert layers as a major obstacle to cross-skill
merging. These findings motivate a consolidation strategy that preserves
policy-specific parameter structure rather than collapsing all updates into
one set of weights.
We propose  NestedVLA , a framework that shifts the paradigm from
parameter aggregation to parameter generation. A hypernetwork embedded in
the VLA generates sparse action-expert updates conditioned on the target
task, while compressed task-specific VLM residuals are retained separately.
The selected updates compose with a shared base to instantiate a
task-specific VLA without changing its forward architecture or introducing
routing supervision. We evaluate NestedVLA on MetaWorld  [ 35 ] ,
ManiSkill  [ 45 ] , and CALVIN  [ 36 ]  using the open-source
π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}     [ 40 ]  backbone. NestedVLA outperforms the strongest
model-merging baseline by    41  41    pp on average, matches per-task fine-tuning
within    1  1    pp on ManiSkill, and substantially closes the gap on CALVIN.
2  Related Work
Vision-Language-Action models.
Recent VLAs cast robotic control as instruction-following on top of
pretrained vision-language backbones  [ 2 ,  71 ,  26 ,  24 ,  1 ,  40 ] . OpenVLA  [ 26 ]  discretizes actions into rare
vocabulary tokens for autoregressive prediction;     π  0   \pi_{0}     [ 1 ]
pairs the VLM with a separate  action expert  trained via flow
matching for continuous action chunks;     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}     [ 40 ]
extends this design to interleave sub-task narration and low-level
control. Lighter variants such as VLA-Adapter  [ 51 ]
preserve multi-task competence at sub-billion-parameter scales. VLAct
uses representation-centric continued pre-training to preserve VLM priors
and promote shared action semantics across
embodiments  [ 63 ] . Across this family, multi-task ability
is learned from pooled data, requiring co-located datasets and predefined
skills. We target the regime where these assumptions break: skills are
acquired  independently  and consolidated  after the fact .
Parameter-efficient visual adaptation.
Prompt- and adapter-based methods offer lightweight alternatives to full
fine-tuning of vision backbones. Recent work makes prompts instance-aware,
injects structured visual semantics, or searches for layer-specific prompt
fusion rules  [ 57 ,  58 ,  59 ] .
Structured low-rank adaptation further filters task-irrelevant directions
and coordinates updates across layers  [ 61 ] , while visual
alignment methods address over-reliance on language priors in multimodal
models  [ 60 ] .   Meng et al. [38]   propose AHRA, a
hierarchical shared–private expert framework that aligns semantic levels
while preserving modality-specific evidence. Such methods adapt or organize
multimodal representations; NestedVLA instead starts from independently trained
VLA policies and consolidates their parameter updates post hoc.
Hypernetworks.
Using one network to predict the parameters of another dates back to
HyperNetworks  [ 20 ] , with later applications to
continual learning  [ 46 ]  and multi-task
NLP  [ 25 ] . Recent parameter generators use latent
diffusion to synthesize neural-network weights  [ 48 ] , scale
this process to full models with recurrent generation  [ 49 ] ,
map task prompts directly to LoRA weights  [ 31 ] , or produce
instance-conditioned weight updates as functional memory  [ 56 ] .
NestedVLA inherits this task-conditioned generation premise, but targets a
sparse top-   K  K    delta on a frozen billion-scale VLA backbone via patch-based
tokenization, and consolidates independently fine-tuned RL policies rather
than learning skills from scratch.
Reusable task updates.
Related work on language models compresses full-model task updates into
modular SkillPacks for heterogeneous knowledge fusion and multi-domain
specialization  [ 13 ,  30 ] . HoE instead extracts
LoRA-form experts from task vectors and routes them for multi-objective
alignment  [ 29 ] . These studies concern text-only language models
and retain or select task modules. NestedVLA studies closed-loop VLA policies
and generates the action-expert update with an embedded hypernetwork.
Model merging.
Model merging combines independently trained checkpoints into a single
deployable model. Recent surveys organize model fusion across parameter,
representation, and behavior levels  [ 3 ,  69 ,  70 ] .  Static merging  produces one fixed delta via
weighted averaging  [ 55 ,  34 ,  22 ,  11 ] ,
conflict-handling  [ 62 ,  65 ,  5 ,  10 ,  14 ] , or low-rank
sub-spaces  [ 17 ,  44 ,  33 ,  32 ,  12 ] , with adapter
counterparts  [ 66 ,  68 ] .  Dynamic
merging  retains per-task masks, scalars, residuals, or
frequency-domain corrections at inference  [ 47 ,  21 ,  67 ,  27 ,  7 ] . Recent adaptive methods build reusable
component-level expert libraries for input-aware recombination  [ 42 ]
or evolve experts with manifold-aware, training-free routing for continual
merging  [ 43 ] . Across both regimes the operative verb is
select : every output value is an interpolation, mask, or
projection of values already present in      {    θ  m   −   θ  0    }   m   \{\theta_{m}-\theta_{0}\}_{m}   .
Complementary studies make expert training merge-aware  [ 19 ] ,
calibrate or quantize post-merge models  [ 18 ,  50 ] ,
and analyze scaling laws or expert-read budgets for weight-space
merging  [ 53 ,  52 ] .
NestedVLA  generates  the per-skill delta from a single shared
hypernetwork instead. Embodied applications of merging remain largely
unexplored; ReVLA  [ 6 ]  mitigates single-skill domain
forgetting rather than multi-skill consolidation.

3.3  Skill Acquisition
The acquisition stage produces, for each task, a sparse skill
representation     z  m   z_{m}    that the hypernetwork can later be trained against.
It comprises two steps: independent fine-tuning to obtain     τ  m   \tau_{m}   , and
drop-and-rescale compression to turn     τ  m   \tau_{m}    into the compact     z  m   z_{m}   .
Independent fine-tuning.
For each     𝒟  m   \mathcal{D}_{m}   , we fine-tune     Θ  0   \Theta_{0}    to obtain     Θ  m   \Theta_{m}
and the task vector      τ  m   =    Θ  m   −   Θ  0     \tau_{m}=\Theta_{m}-\Theta_{0}   . We make no assumption
on the training signal:     Θ  m   \Theta_{m}    may be obtained via supervised
fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or any combination thereof, as
long as all     {   Θ  m   }   \{\Theta_{m}\}    are derived from the same     Θ  0   \Theta_{0}   . This
decoupling matches the data-silo realities of robotics, where
specialized skills are often trained independently in their native
pipelines.
Drop-and-rescale compression.
A naive option is to use the dense     τ  m   \tau_{m}    as the hypernetwork target,
but this is prohibitive in both memory and learnability for VLA
backbones with billions of parameters. We observe that VLA fine-tuning
deltas exhibit a  sparse-magnitude-dominated  structure: a small
fraction of elements concentrate the bulk of the delta’s     ℓ  2   \ell_{2}
energy, while the rest behave like low-amplitude noise; in contrast,
LLM SFT deltas are typically denser and benefit from
quantization-plus-low-rank schemes such as D-QRELO  [ 28 ] . We
compared low-bit quantization, low-rank decomposition, and
drop-and-rescale on VLA deltas and found drop-and-rescale dominates
the fidelity-versus-compression trade-off
(§  D  ); we adopt it throughout NestedVLA.

3.4.1  Patch-based Parameter Tokenization
A direct regression       𝒢  ϕ   ​   (  m  )    →   z  m    \mathcal{G}_{\phi}(m)\to z_{m}    over a billion-element
output is infeasible. We tokenize the parameter space into a 2D grid

fixed-size      H  patch   ×   W  patch    H_{\text{patch}}\times W_{\text{patch}}    tiles (large tensors

factorization exposes a structured spatial layout to the generator
while keeping the per-token output dimension bounded. Concrete patch
counts and tile sizes are reported in §  4.1  .

4  Experiments
We evaluate NestedVLA on three closed-loop manipulation benchmarks and
ask three questions: (i) can a single nested hypernetwork consolidate a
heterogeneous skill set without storing a full checkpoint per skill
(§  4.2  )? (ii) which design choices matter
(§  4.3  )? (iii) where does the remaining gap to per-task
fine-tuning come from (§  4.4  )?
4.1  Setup
Backbone and skills.
We instantiate     Θ  0   \Theta_{0}    as     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}     [ 40 ]  and collect three
skills by independently fine-tuning with FlowSDE  [ 4 ]  on
MetaWorld  [ 35 ] , ManiSkill  [ 45 ] , and
CALVIN  [ 36 ] , yielding task vectors      {   τ  m   }    m  =  1   3   \{\tau_{m}\}_{m=1}^{3}   .
The backbone factorizes into a VLM half (   ∼  \sim      3.21  3.21    B params) and
an action expert (   ∼  \sim      0.40  0.40    B params); we confine the hypernetwork

Static merging.
None of the static merging baselines is competitive on cross-benchmark
VLA fusion. Plain parameter averaging reaches     14.8  %   14.8\%    average success;
TIES  [ 62 ] , WUDI  [ 5 ] , and TSV-Merging  [ 17 ]  each
underperform  averaging despite incorporating sign-conflict
resolution, weighted updates, or singular-vector compression. All four
methods drive CALVIN to     0  %   0\%    chain completion, consistent with our
hypothesis (§  1  ) that VLA representations are more
sensitive to parameter perturbations than the SFT deltas these methods
were primarily evaluated on.
Dynamic merging.
Retaining per-task information at inference time helps, but only
modestly. TALL-Mask  [ 47 ]  achieves     22.6  %   22.6\%    average success
with per-task binary masks at     2.09  ×   2.09\times    storage.
EMR-Merging  [ 21 ]  adds per-task sign modulators and scalars and
reaches     23.0  %   23.0\%    at     2.89  ×   2.89\times    storage, the highest budget of any
baseline. Twin-Merging  [ 32 ]  attaches per-task low-rank
residuals at     2.25  ×   2.25\times    and reaches     25.3  %   25.3\%   .
Free-Merging  [ 67 ] , the strongest dynamic baseline,
applies frequency-domain filtering on per-task deltas and reaches
25.6  %   25.6\%    at     2.16  ×   2.16\times   . Despite their parameter budgets ranging from
2.09  ×   2.09\times    to     2.89  ×   2.89\times   , the four methods cluster within a
3  3    pp band, suggesting the bottleneck is not storage but the
underlying paradigm of  selecting  from a fixed set of base
values.
Storage analysis.
NestedVLA’s     1.5  ×   1.5\times    overall budget decomposes asymmetrically across
the two halves of the backbone. On top of the     1.0  ×   1.0\times    shared base,
the VLM half (   ∼  \sim      3.21  3.21    B parameters,    ∼  \sim       89  %   89\%    of the backbone)
is stored per-task as a top-    K  =   10  %    K{=}10\%    compressed delta (a    1  1   -bit
mask plus the kept values), contributing    ∼  \sim       0.35  ×   0.35\times    across the
three tasks. The action expert (   ∼  \sim      0.40  0.40    B parameters,
∼  \sim       11  %   11\%   ) is  generated  by the hypernetwork at inference and
stores only a precomputed mask per task (   ∼  \sim       0.01  ×   0.01\times   ); the
hypernetwork itself (   ∼  \sim      0.43  0.43    B parameters) adds
∼  \sim       0.12  ×   0.12\times   . The detailed per-component derivation is given in
App.   C  . The trainable capacity is therefore
concentrated on the action side, the harder consolidation target
where per-task deltas are denser and more conflicting, precisely
where it is needed. This contrasts sharply with the dynamic baselines:
EMR-Merging stores per-task masks  and  per-task scaling factors
uniformly across the entire backbone at     2.89  ×   2.89\times    to reach only
23.0  %   23.0\%   , while Twin-Merging and Free-Merging both pay
∼  \sim       2.2  ×   2.2\times    for per-task residuals or frequency-domain
corrections without distinguishing where additional capacity is
useful. NestedVLA’s storage advantage at the evaluated three-skill setting
comes from concentrating generation capacity on the action expert. Total
storage still grows with the number of skills because compressed VLM
residuals, masks, and task-specific generator components must be retained,
but each skill adds substantially less than a full VLA checkpoint.

5  Conclusion
We studied skill consolidation in VLA models when training data is
fragmented across benchmark-specific tasks. Standard model-merging methods degrade catastrophically
on cross-benchmark VLA fusion: the strongest baseline reaches only
25.6  %   25.6\%    average success across MetaWorld, ManiSkill, and CALVIN. We
introduced NestedVLA, which replaces action-expert parameter
selection  with parameter  generation  via a nested
hypernetwork, reaching
66.7  %   66.7\%    average success at     1.5  ×   1.5\times    storage (    +  41.1   +41.1    pp over
the strongest dynamic baseline), and matching per-task fine-tuning
within    1  1    pp on ManiSkill while reaching the     K  =   10  %    K{=}10\%
sparsity-budget ceiling on CALVIN. The MetaWorld gap is bounded by
the top-   K  K    ideal itself, with adaptive sparsity the natural next
direction.

Static merging.
•
Averaging   [ 55 ,  15 ]  merges the three task
vectors by element-wise averaging,
τ  ¯   =    1  M   ​    ∑  m    τ  m      \bar{\tau}=\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{m}\tau_{m}   , and adds the result to the

•

our     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    task vectors.
•
WUDI-Merging   [ 5 ]  formulates merging as an

the merged delta and the per-task deltas, iteratively refining the

•
TSV-Merging   [ 17 ]  (Task-Singular-Vector
Merging) defines a measure of task interference based on the

their joint top subspace. The output is a single fused delta at
1.00  ×   1.00\times    storage; we retain the rank that captures
∼  \sim       95  %   95\%    of the per-task spectral energy.

Appendix F  VLM Static Merging Sanity Test
The main paper confines the hypernetwork to the action expert and
keeps per-task VLM storage. This section provides the empirical
evidence supporting that design choice. We ran a sanity test in which
only the VLM half is consolidated across tasks (via TIES or WUDI),
while the action expert remains the GT per-task FlowSDE checkpoint.
This isolates the cost of VLM consolidation and answers whether the
VLM is consolidatable in principle.
F.1  Setup
For each of the three benchmarks, we constructed a sanity checkpoint
by replacing only the VLM portion of the per-task FlowSDE
checkpoint with a 3-task-merged VLM. The action expert is the
unmodified GT per-task FlowSDE expert. Two merge strategies were
tested:
•
TIES VLM:  TIES-Merging  [ 62 ]  applied with
trim-rate     k  =  0.8   k{=}0.8    and scaling    0.5  0.5   , applied to the VLM half only.
•

optimization iterations on the VLM half.

isolate the cost of static VLM consolidation alone. One technical
exception:     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    uses tied embedding for
language_model.embed_tokens , which is not stored in the
merged VLM checkpoint; we fall back to per-task FlowSDE for this
single tensor (representing     0.17  %   0.17\%    of VLM parameters). Each sanity
checkpoint is evaluated only on its corresponding benchmark; no

We use v2 to denote a possible extension in which the addressed parameter
blocks cover both the VLM and the action expert, so the hypernetwork
generates both updates for the selected task. This removes the stored VLM
values but substantially enlarges the output space and may require additional
generator capacity and metadata. We do not evaluate this extension here;
all reported results and storage measurements correspond to v1.

Appendix H  Limitations
The current evaluation has four limitations that we mark as future
work.  (i) Skill-set scale.  All experiments consolidate
M  =  3   M{=}3    skills; we have not measured how training time, hypernetwork
capacity, or output quality scale as    M  M    grows by an order of
magnitude.  (ii) Adding new skills.  A new skill currently
requires extending the per-task patch-embedding table, the per-task
multi-head output, and re-training the hypernetwork; we have not
demonstrated incremental or low-cost addition of an unseen skill.
(iii) VLM consolidation.  The VLM half is kept per-task as a

Appendix I  Broader Impact
NestedVLA reduces the per-skill storage and serving cost of multi-task
robotic policies by replacing per-task fine-tuned checkpoints with a
single shared parameter generator. Like other efficient deployment
methods, lowering this cost can amplify both beneficial uses
(low-resource personalisation, on-device multi-skill manipulation,
faster transfer between robot platforms) and risks already present
in the underlying VLA stack, such as dual-use of robotic policies in
unintended physical environments. Our hypernetwork operates entirely
on parameter deltas of an existing publicly released backbone
(    π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}   ); we introduce no new training data or labels, and the
deployed behaviour inherits the biases and failure modes of the
source FlowSDE checkpoints. All our experiments are simulated; no
physical robot is operated in this study. We see no direct path from
the parameter-generation framework to a novel misuse scenario beyond
those already discussed in the broader VLA literature.
