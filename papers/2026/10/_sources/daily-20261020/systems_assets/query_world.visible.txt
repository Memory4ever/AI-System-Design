SARAM: Sparse Adapter Allocation with Reuse and Merging for Lifelong Robot Learning
2026-10-17T18:14:37Z
Lifelong robot learning requires acquiring new tasks sequentially without forgetting earlier ones. With large pre-trained vision-language-action (VLA) backbones, adapter-based methods effectively mitigate this challenge. However, their task-specific adapters and selection modules accumulate with the task stream, creating a scalability challenge in on-disk storage and deployment cost under fixed onboard resource budgets. We propose SARAM, a lifelong learning framework that limits storage growth while keeping GPU memory and per-step inference cost independent of the task stream length. For each new task, SARAM reuses frozen adapters from previous tasks and introduces new adapters only at sparsely selected layers, with a duplication penalty encouraging them to capture information not already represented by the adapter library. At deployment, a compact signature derived from backbone activation statistics identifies the task once per episode, and the corresponding task update is merged into the backbone, leaving only the backbone in the control loop. GPU memory usage and control frequency remain comparable to those of the backbone as the task stream grows. Experiments on a real UR5 arm with a different backbone further demonstrate retention throughout a sequential task stream.

Three dimensional modeling for autonomous driving must continuously estimate road geometry, obstacle occupancy, traffic participant behavior, and observational uncertainty under limited computational resources. Existing reconstruction methods can synthesize realistic novel views, but visual fidelity does not sufficiently characterize the geometric reliability required for driving. Generative world models can expand scenarios and predict future observations, yet may introduce content unsupported by measurements. We propose DriveWorld3D, a framework combining temporal video learning, measurement constraints from multiple sensors, and offline generative simulation. Its online component maintains a unified world state comprising road surfaces, volumetric occupancy, dynamic entities, and road relations, while separately representing physical occupancy, observability, and evidence provenance. Action conditioned future prediction and risk sensitive planning connect this representation to driving decisions. Its offline component reconstructs recorded scenes using neural radiance fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting, then expands long tail conditions using generative models while restricting the use of generated content as geometric ground truth. We specify data partitioning, sensor ablations, fault injection, paired closed loop testing, and real to simulation consistency assessment. The central hypothesis is that explicit evidence management can reduce dangerous false free space predictions and occlusion risk within a matched computational budget while retaining acceptable driving efficiency. All performance claims remain subject to experimental validation. Keywords: autonomous driving; 3D occupancy; sensor fusion; uncertainty; generative world models; NeRF; 3D Gaussian Splatting; closed loop simulation

Pathology foundation models for training-free case retrieval in salivary gland tumors
2026-10-15T15:40:48Z
In pathology, pattern recognition relies on a frame of reference built through extensive training and experience, which may still fall short when encountering rare cancer subtypes. Archives of previously diagnosed tumors often contain relevant precedents, yet identifying the most relevant cases remains challenging. Here we explore whether content-based retrieval with current pathology foundation models facilitates rare cancer diagnostics without any task-specific training. Pathologists can query a digitized archive using a region of interest (ROI) from a whole slide image (WSI) and retrieve morphologically similar cases, along with their ICD-O codes and corresponding WSIs. The ROI can be selected manually or automatically using a gated attention-based multiple instance learning model. We evaluate our approach by leave-one-case-out on an imbalanced dataset of 137 salivary gland tumor cases spanning 12 ICD-O morphology categories, comparing four pathology foundation models (Atlas2, Atlas2-B, Atlas2-S, and UNI2h). Top-1/3/5 retrieval was 62.5%/76.7%/81.3% for Atlas2, 50.2/66.6/72.9% for UNI2h, 52.3%/71.5%/77.6% for Atlas2-B and 54.4/65.2/73.7% for Atlas2-S. Macro-averaged F1 ranged from 69.0% for the smallest model Atlas2-S to 76.6% for the largest model Atlas2 across the five classes with at least ten cases. Our modified Chamfer similarity separated correct from incorrect matches among the Top-5 more reliably for Atlas2 (AUC 0.754) than for Atlas2-B (0.679, p = 0.020) and Atlas2-S (0.653, p = 0.003), but not UNI2h (0.697, p = 0.196). Requiring no additional training or manual annotation, this approach makes departmental archives searchable with pathology foundation models, providing a low-barrier route to case-based differential diagnosis and extending the individual frame of reference to the collective experience of the department.

Memory as Weights: Internalizing Long-Term History for Streaming Videos
2026-10-15T15:29:57Z
Streaming vision-language models (VLMs) must continuously integrate information from the past while remaining responsive to current observations and user queries. Existing memory mechanisms typically compress, retrieve, or cache historical observations and reintroduce them into the input context, forcing historical memory and current observations to compete for limited context. We propose MaLoW, a streaming memory framework that instead internalizes historical context into evolving lightweight parameter updates. MaLoW uses a recurrent HyperNetwork to incrementally consolidate incoming video observations into fixed-size LoRA weights, allowing historical information to be carried forward without revisiting the full video stream. In doing so, MaLoW represents history as weights, while keeping the current observation as input. To support training, we further construct MaLoW-Instruct, a large-scale streaming video dataset containing 381K question-answer pairs over 56K videos. We conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate that MaLoW achieves strong performance on OVO-Bench, StreamingBench, and StreamArena. On the backward split of OVO-Bench, MaLoW outperforms prior memory approaches by 3.9% to 11.8% across different video backbones while incurring only modest latency overhead. Beyond streaming video understanding, MaLoW also generalizes to vision-language-action models, improving the action success rate on Simpler WidowX by 9.3%.

Event-STVG: Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding for Long-Form Event Reasoning
2026-10-15T15:13:54Z
Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG) aims to jointly localize a target in space and time given a natural-language query. Despite continued advances, existing STVG benchmarks remain largely centered on localizing targets through instance-specific descriptions, leaving them largely decoupled from long-form reasoning increasingly emphasized in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). In this paper, we introduce Event-STVG, a task that shifts STVG from locating what is described toward event-level reasoning by unifying long-form context, repeated-event reasoning, event localization, role inference, and multi-target grounding. To facilitate research on Event-STVG, we construct a large-scale benchmark comprising 4,016 annotated event occurrences across soccer and seven additional domains. Experiments with state-of-the-art MLLMs reveal substantial difficulty, with the strongest model achieving only 5.4% m-vIoU at the 10-minute horizon. Across domains, we consistently observe confusion among repeated same-type events, spatial grounding bottlenecks, and rare success in jointly grounding all required roles. Event-STVG establishes a new challenge for visual understanding in MLLMs, calling for future models that can jointly reason about complex events across space and time. Code and annotations are available at https://github.com/THU-KEG/EventSTVG
