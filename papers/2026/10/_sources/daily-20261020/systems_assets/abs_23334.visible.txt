Title: LAW: Unifying Language, Action, and World Modeling for Agentic Physical Intelligence
Authors:  Jiajun Cao ,  Kai Tang ,  Bohua Zhang ,  Zezhong Qian ,  Ying Li ,  Xiaozhu Ju ,  Xiaobao Wei ,  Jiaming Liu ,  Yang Ding ,  Sixiang Chen ,  Yiding Sun ,  Siyuan Qian ,  Tinghao Wang ,  Jixian Wu ,  Yichen Guo ,  Yi Zhang ,  Shilong Zou ,  Yong Dai ,  Jian Tang ,  Shanghang Zhang
View a PDF of the paper titled LAW: Unifying Language, Action, and World Modeling for Agentic Physical Intelligence, by Jiajun Cao and 19 other authors
View PDF
HTML (experimental)
Abstract: World-action models jointly predict actions and future world states, but long-horizon manipulation further requires language reasoning to plan subtask objectives, assess execution progress, and guide action selection. We present LAW, a closed-loop VLM--WAM framework that unifies language reasoning, action generation, and world modeling through semantic subtask progress. Within the LAW agent, a hybrid-expert vision-language model plans the remaining subtasks, estimates the values of and fuses actions proposed by a world-action model, and asynchronously assesses execution status to support continued execution and subtask transitions. Within the world-action model, Mixture-of-Transformer Experts integrates modality-specific and shared computation for joint video--action modeling. Dual-stream hierarchical visual memory maintains temporal context for both models, while subtask-local value learning aligns action evaluation with semantic objectives and preserves supervision from successful prefixes of failed trajectories. LAW achieves 94.21% average success across 50 RoboTwin tasks. Replacing the WAM backbone with OpenWAM-$alpha$ further raises the average success rate to 95.56%, establishing a new open-source state of the art on RoboTwin. Despite having only 2B parameters, our VLM also outperforms variants using Embodied-R1.5 and GPT-5.5 High. On three long-horizon real-robot tasks, LAW achieves 65% average success, compared with 28.33% for $pi_{0.5}$ and 25% for Fast-WAM.
