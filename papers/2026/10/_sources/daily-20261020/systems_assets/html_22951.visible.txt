Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong promise for generalist robotic manipulation, but their inference cost remains a major bottleneck for real-time control. This limitation is especially pronounced in flow-matching VLAs such as     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}   , where the action expert is evaluated repeatedly during iterative denoising. We introduce MambaAct, an efficient Mamba action expert for flow-matching VLA policies. MambaAct retains the pretrained visual-language prefix encoder of     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}   , while replacing its transformer-based action expert with a compact Mamba-based action expert. Within this expert, Mamba serves as the action mixer that models dependencies across the noisy continuous-action sequence. To preserve visual-language conditioning without repeated token-level cross-attention, MambaAct uses a lightweight Context Compressor to map Vision-language model (VLM) prefix tokens into a compact conditioning signal that gates and modulates each Mamba action block. MambaAct uses a     10  ×   10\times    smaller action expert and reduces flow-matching denoising latency by     64.8  %   64.8\%    on LIBERO and     58.8  %   58.8\%    in real-world deployment, while improving mean LIBERO success by    1.08  1.08    percentage points across three independent fine-tuning seeds and maintaining gains on LIBERO-Plus and real-world benchmarks. Our code and model weights are publicly available at  https://github.com/giakhuyendihoc/MambaAct .
I   Introduction
Recent flow-matching VLAs such as     π  0   \pi_{0}     [ 1 ]  and     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}     [ 2 ]  achieve strong manipulation performance, but their iterative action-generation process introduces substantial inference cost. At every policy query, the visual-language prefix can be computed once, whereas the action expert must be evaluated repeatedly across multiple flow-matching denoising steps. As a result, action generation can become the dominant source of latency even when the visual-language backbone itself is relatively compact.
Recent efforts to improve VLA efficiency have largely reduced this cost by decreasing the number of sampling steps, compressing or pruning pretrained networks, reducing visual-token processing, or redesigning the policy around smaller architectures. While effective, these approaches primarily accelerate inference by reducing how often existing components are evaluated or by simplifying the visual-language pathway and overall model. In contrast, the architecture of the repeatedly evaluated action expert itself remains comparatively underexplored as an efficiency axis. This motivates us to ask whether flow-matching inference can instead be accelerated by replacing the action-generation module while preserving the pretrained visual-language representations and standard flow-matching formulation.
We address this question with  MambaAct , a compact Mamba  [ 3 ]  action expert for flow-matching VLA policies. MambaAct retains the pretrained     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    visual-language encoder and replaces its large Transformer-based action expert with a lightweight Mamba sequence mixer. Mamba is attractive in this setting because its selective state-space formulation provides a compact inductive bias for modeling ordered, strongly correlated action sequences. As we show empirically, this design enables aggressive action-expert compression while preserving policy performance. To efficiently condition the newly initialized expert on the pretrained VLM, we introduce a Context Compressor and gated FiLM  [ 4 ]  interface that transform the visual-language prefix into a compact representation reused throughout flow-matching denoising. The resulting expert contains only    29.7  29.7   M parameters, compared with    311.5  311.5   M for the original     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    action expert.
Our main contributions are:
•
We introduce  MambaAct , establishing action-expert replacement as a complementary efficiency axis for flow-matching VLAs. MambaAct replaces the repeatedly evaluated Transformer action expert with a compact Mamba expert while retaining the pretrained visual-language backbone and standard flow-matching formulation, without additional pretraining or distillation.
•
We show that Mamba enables aggressive action-expert compression: MambaAct uses over     10  ×   10\times    fewer action-expert parameters than     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    while improving mean LIBERO  [ 5 ]  success by    1.08  1.08    percentage points across three fine-tuning seeds.
•
Through controlled ablations, we examine the contributions of the Mamba action mixer and conditioning interface to the efficiency-performance trade-off. We further demonstrate that MambaAct composes with VLM-depth reduction and transfers to SmolVLA  [ 6 ] , indicating that action-expert replacement generalizes across flow-matching VLA backbones.
II   Related Work
II-A    Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models
The growing computational cost of VLA models has motivated a range of approaches that reduce inference cost at different stages of the policy. One direction focuses on accelerating iterative action generation. SnapFlow  [ 7 ] , for example, uses progressive self-distillation to compress iterative flow matching into a single action-expert evaluation, thereby reducing the number of sampling steps required at inference time. Complementary approaches instead reduce the cost of each network evaluation by simplifying the underlying architecture. Shallow-   π  \pi     [ 8 ]  distills shallower visual-language and action transformers, while CLP  [ 9 ]  identifies and removes redundant layers from pretrained VLA models.
Another line of work targets the visual-language pathway, where long visual-token sequences can dominate computation. General VLM pruning methods such as FastV  [ 10 ]  and DivPrune  [ 11 ]  preserve early visual processing while removing redundant tokens before later transformer layers, and have subsequently been evaluated in VLA policies  [ 9 ] . VLA-specific methods further adapt this idea to robotic control: LightVLA  [ 12 ] , ADP  [ 13 ] , and SpecPrune-VLA  [ 14 ]  selectively reduce visual tokens while retaining task-relevant information for action prediction. Beyond pruning individual components, several works pursue efficiency through compact architectures or coordinated reductions across multiple computational axes. SmolVLA  [ 6 ]  reduces the overall model scale, whereas CogVLA  [ 15 ]  combines instruction-conditioned visual aggregation, token sparsification, and a redesigned action decoder. Similarly, EfficientVLA  [ 16 ]  combines token and layer reduction with feature reuse across action-generation steps.
MambaAct targets a different design point: it retains the pretrained     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    backbone and standard flow-matching formulation, but replaces the transformer action expert with a compact Mamba-based expert trained jointly during task fine-tuning, without teacher models, distillation objectives, or additional pretraining.
II-B    Mamba in Vision-Language-Action Models
Mamba  [ 3 ]  replaces pairwise self-attention with selective state-space recurrence, providing a compact mechanism for modeling ordered sequences. This makes it attractive for repeatedly evaluated action experts operating under tight parameter budgets. Mamba has been explored across sequence modeling and computer vision  [ 17 ] , with recent extensions to robotic perception and control  [ 18 ,  19 ,  20 ] .
Within VLA modeling, RoboMamba  [ 18 ]  uses Mamba primarily as the multimodal visual-language reasoning backbone, targeting efficient perception and reasoning rather than dense continuous action generation.
Mamba has also been explored for continuous control. Mamba Policy  [ 19 ]  applies Mamba to continuous-action diffusion, demonstrating selective state-space models as compact action-denoising networks. However, it is not designed as an action expert conditioned on representations from a pretrained vision-language backbone. More recently, SUREFlow  [ 20 ]  develops a lightweight Mamba-based multimodal policy with uncertainty-aware residual flow matching, jointly predicting action velocity and uncertainty to selectively refine unreliable actions. Unlike MambaAct, SUREFlow constructs a compact end-to-end multimodal policy rather than preserving a pretrained foundation VLM and replacing only its action expert.
MambaAct targets this complementary setting: accelerating the action-generation pathway of a pretrained flow-matching VLA while preserving its learned visual-language representations. It retains the     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    VLM and standard flow-matching objective, while replacing only the repeatedly evaluated Transformer action expert with a compact Mamba expert conditioned through compressed VLM context and gated FiLM modulation. We further show that this action-expert replacement transfers to SmolVLA.

III-C    Experimental Setup
We evaluate whether replacing the transformer-based action expert with a lightweight Mamba action expert can preserve or improve policy performance while reducing inference cost. We use LIBERO to measure simulated task performance and latency, LIBERO-Plus to assess zero-shot robustness, and two real-world UR5 tasks - tube placing and nut pouring - to evaluate physical manipulation performance and deployment efficiency.
Base policy and model configuration
We use OpenPI     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}     [ 2 ]  as both the base model and the controlled baseline. MambaAct retains the pretrained PaliGemma VLM backbone  [ 21 ]  including SigLIP vision encoder and Gemma visual-language prefix encoder, while replacing its transformer-based action expert with a lightweight Mamba action expert.
All     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}   -based variants are initialized from the same base checkpoint and fine-tuned using a single-stage full-model recipe, in which the vision encoder, executed VLM layers, context-compression module, and action expert are optimized jointly. The MambaAct model executes all 18 VLM layers and uses a four-block Mamba action expert with width    512  512   , state size    64  64   , expansion factor    1  1   , and head dimension    64  64   . The Context Compressor produces     K  =  64   K=64    context tokens, comprising 44 visual tokens, 16 language tokens, and 4 learned-query global summary tokens.

Simulation benchmarks
We evaluate each policy on the standard LIBERO benchmark  [ 5 ] , including the  Spatial ,  Object ,  Goal , and  LIBERO-10  (later denoted as L-10) suites. Each suite contains ten tasks. We run 50 trials per task using evaluation seed    7  7    and report the success rate for each suite together with the unweighted average across all four suites. LIBERO inference uses image resolution     224  ×  224   224\times 224   , action horizon     H  =  10   H=10   , ten flow-matching denoising steps, and replanning every five environment steps.
For the main LIBERO comparison, we report mean and standard deviation across
these three fine-tuning seeds.
However, standard LIBERO performance may mask overfitting to benchmark-specific visual and environmental conditions, as policies can degrade under small perturbations. We therefore evaluate MambaAct and the matched     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    baseline on LIBERO-Plus  [ 22 ] , which contains    10,030  10{,}030    test-only task instances across seven perturbation categories: CAMERA, ROBOT, LANGUAGE, LIGHT, BACKGROUND, NOISE, and LAYOUT. Following the official protocol, we run one rollout per task instance using a fixed evaluation seed and report category-level and overall success rates.

Our setup uses two UR5 robots (Fig.   3  ): the left robot performs manipulation and carries a wrist-mounted RealSense camera, while the right robot remains fixed and carries a second RealSense camera providing the third-person view. For physical deployment, the policies predict delta-joint action chunks with horizon     H  =  50   H=50    from the visual-language observation. IV-B    Ablation Studies
We conduct three ablation studies to assess the source of gain, composability, and transferability of MambaAct.
Unless otherwise stated, all ablations use a fine-tuning seed of    42  42    and an evaluation
seed of    7  7   .
Ablation 1: Architectural source of gain.  We isolate the contributions of the action mixer and conditioning interface using a     2  ×  2   2\times 2    comparison of Mamba and Transformer mixers with gated and cross-attention conditioning (Table   V  ).
Under gated conditioning, Mamba achieves     98.4  %   98.4\%    average success, substantially outperforming the size-matched Transformer at     85.7  %   85.7\%   . The same trend holds with cross-attention (    96.7  %   96.7\%    vs.     75.6  %   75.6\%   ), suggesting that the Mamba action mixer is important for preserving performance under aggressive action-expert compression. We hypothesize that selective state-space mixing provides a useful inductive bias for flow-matching action generation: Mamba propagates information along the ordered, strongly correlated action-token sequence through a selective recurrent state rather than unrestricted pairwise token interactions. This may allow a compact expert to model action-token dynamics more effectively, consistent with prior results on Mamba-based diffusion policies  [ 19 ] .
Gated conditioning also consistently outperforms cross-attention: with Mamba, it improves average success from     96.7  %   96.7\%    to     98.4  %   98.4\%    while reducing latency from    119.66  119.66    to    107.61  107.61    ms; with Transformer, it improves success from     75.6  %   75.6\%    to     85.7  %   85.7\%   . These results suggest that repeated token-level attention to the compressed context is not necessary for strong action generation in the evaluated setting. However, the gated models use larger action experts (   30  30   M vs.    17  17   -   18  18   M parameters), so the improvement cannot be attributed to conditioning alone.
One possible explanation is the optimization setting of MambaAct. The action expert and conditioning interface are initialized from scratch, while the VLM starts from pretrained     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    weights and is jointly fine-tuned. We hypothesize that gated FiLM provides a constrained interface for aligning these components: feature-wise scale and shift adapt the action representation to the visual-language condition, while the residual gate controls the magnitude of the conditioned update. This interpretation is consistent with prior findings on FiLM  [ 4 ]  and adaptive modulation in diffusion transformers  [ 24 ] . Overall, the ablation suggests that Mamba provides a stronger performance-capacity trade-off under aggressive action-expert compression. Gated variants further improve the observed efficiency-performance trade-off, although the accuracy contribution of the conditioning interface remains partially confounded by expert size.

Ablation 2: Composability.  Here, we study whether MambaAct can compose with complementary prefix-side VLA acceleration. Efficient VLA systems can be accelerated along multiple axes, including reducing denoising steps, pruning or compressing visual tokens, truncating VLM depth, and replacing the action expert. Because MambaAct primarily reduces computation in the repeatedly evaluated action pathway, its acceleration mechanism is distinct from methods that reduce computation in the visual-language prefix. We test this composability using VLM-depth reduction as a representative prefix-side acceleration. As a controlled efficiency–accuracy trade-off, we combine MambaAct with VLM-depth reduction by executing only the first    12  12    VLM layers instead of all    18  18    layers before passing the resulting context representation to the Mamba action expert.
Tables   VI   and   VII   show that MambaAct remains effective when combined with prefix-side acceleration. On LIBERO, the L12 variant reduces training time from    13.78  13.78    to    10.81  10.81    hours and lowers E2E latency from    107.6  107.6    to    102.91  102.91    ms, while maintaining     97.5  %   97.5\%    average success compared with     98.4  %   98.4\%    for the full model. This suggests that the Mamba action expert can operate effectively on intermediate VLM representations, rather than requiring the full-depth visual-language prefix.

These results indicate that the Mamba action-expert replacement transfers beyond the    π  \pi   -family, reducing action-expert size while improving average task success on a second flow-matching VLA backbone.
In summary, the ablations support three observations. First, Mamba substantially outperforms size-matched Transformer mixers under both evaluated conditioning interfaces, suggesting that the action mixer itself is important for preserving performance under aggressive expert compression. Second, gated conditioning provides an efficient interface between the pretrained VLM and the newly initialized action expert, although its accuracy advantage over cross-attention is partially confounded by model capacity. Third, the resulting Mamba action expert remains effective under VLM-depth reduction and transfers to a second flow-matching VLA architecture. Together, these results are consistent with benefits from selective action
sequence modeling and lightweight visual-language conditioning beyond
compression alone.
V   Conclusion
We present and publicly release MambaAct, a lightweight Mamba-based action expert tailored for flow-matching vision-language-action models. Rather than altering the pretrained vision-language backbone or truncating denoising steps, MambaAct directly optimizes the frequently evaluated action-generation pathway. By substituting the Transformer action expert in     π  0.5   \pi_{0.5}    with a Mamba mixer, MambaAct enables aggressive compression of the repeatedly evaluated action pathway, reducing the core action-expert parameter count from    311.5  311.5   M to    29.7  29.7   M (    ∼  10  ×   \sim 10\times   ) while substantially lowering denoising latency.
Crucially, this compression improves mean LIBERO performance across three
independent fine-tuning seeds, while retaining strong robustness on
LIBERO-Plus and on real-world robotic tasks. Furthermore, MambaAct seamlessly integrates with VLM-depth reduction and generalizes effectively when transferred to SmolVLA.
Despite these strengths, several limitations remain. First, our real-world experiments are currently restricted to two UR5 tasks under primary spatial distribution shifts. In addition, while MambaAct composes with VLM-depth reduction, its compatibility with other efficiency techniques, such as visual-token pruning and denoising-step compression, remains to be evaluated. We also leave deeper analysis of the gated visual-language conditioning and selective Mamba state dynamics to future work.
