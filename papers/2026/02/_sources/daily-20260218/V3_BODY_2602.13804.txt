Abstract
Long-context decoding in large language models is widely believed to be inherently quadratic or, at best, linear in the context length due to dense attention. We challenge this assumption by introducing a geometric framework showing that attention is intrinsically sparse under mild non-degeneracy conditions.
We prove that an entropically-regularized projection onto the convex hull of key vectors admits a two-term decomposition: a linear tangential perturbation and an exponentially small off-face leakage governed by a support gap parameter Δ \Delta . When Δ > 0 \Delta>0 , the effective active support remains stable and small, independently of the total context size.
This theoretical insight motivates Sparse Paged Attention , an IO-aware attention mechanism combining page routing, convex sparse solving, and fused CUDA kernels. We show that per-token decode complexity reduces from O ⁡ ( T ​ D ) O(TD) to O ⁡ ( P ​ D + K c ​ D ) O(PD+K_{c}D) , where P P and K c K_{c} are context-independent constants. Empirically, on Llama-3-8B with vLLM and H100 GPUs, we observe near-constant decode latency up to 128k context length with quality controlled by an explicit exponential leakage bound; this submission focuses on system latency and scalability measurements.
We emphasize scope: our “constant time” claim is constant in context length T T in the certified face-stable regime (positive support gap), with an explicit diagnostic and a safe dense fallback when the gap is small.
Code and artifacts. Our reference implementation and scripts are available at
https://github.com/DatarConsulting/Vashista-Sparse-Attention .
1 Overview and Key Contributions
TL;DR.
Long-context decoding does not need dense attention . Under mild non-degeneracy (a positive support gap Δ \Delta ),
the optimal context selection lies on a low-dimensional face of a convex hull, and entropic/softmax attention places
exponentially small mass outside that face. This yields a principled path to constant-size effective context and
near-constant decode cost.
What is new.
•
A geometric theory of sparsity in attention. We cast context selection as projection onto a convex polytope
and show that face stability controls sparsity ( Sections 4 , 5 and 6 ).
•
Deterministic main theorem (face stability ⇒ \Rightarrow exponential leakage).
Our main result ( Theorem 1 ) decomposes the approximation error into an O ⁡ ( ε ) O(\varepsilon) term on the active face
and an exp ( − Δ / ε ) \exp(-\Delta/\varepsilon) leakage term off-face.
•
KKT certificate for constant-size concentration. A simple KKT-based support gap quantity
Δ ⁡ ( q ) = min j ∉ I ⁡ μ j ∗ \Delta(q)=\min_{j\notin I}\mu_{j}^{\ast} certifies that only a constant-size active set matters
( Lemmas 1 and 1 ).
•
System implication: certified truncation via paging. We translate the theorem into a sparse paged decoding
primitive with predictable quality–latency trade-offs ( Section 7 ).
•
Diagnostics and empirical validation. We measure gap proxies and leakage in industrial serving settings and
report end-to-end wins ( Sections 8 and 9 ).
Roadmap.
Section 4 sets up the geometry; Section 5 introduces entropic (softmax-like) regularization;
Section 6 states assumptions and the main theorem; Section 7 gives the decoding primitive and complexity;
Section 8 provides diagnostics for when gaps appear; Section 9 validates the theory empirically.
Full proofs appear in Appendices A–E.
2 Introduction
Transformer attention becomes memory-bandwidth bound during decoding when the context length T T is large. Standard dense attention (e.g., SDPA/FlashAttention) must read essentially all keys/values for each decoded token, inducing per-token cost that scales at least linearly with T T .
We ask: is long-context decoding fundamentally dense ?
Scope. Throughout, “constant time” means constant in the context length T T given fixed budgets ( P , K c ) (P,K_{c}) and in the certified regime where the support gap is non-negligible; the method exposes diagnostics and can safely revert to dense attention when the gap is small.
This paper answers negatively under a geometric stability condition. We show that the entropically-regularized convex projection onto the convex hull of keys concentrates on a small exposed face when a support gap Δ \Delta is positive. This yields an exponentially small off-face leakage, enabling sparse decoding with context-independent candidate size under typical regimes.
Contributions.
•
A geometric theorem: face stability with exponential leakage (Theorem 1 ).
•
An IO-aware sparse decoding mechanism (Vashista Sparse Attention) achieving O ⁡ ( P ​ D + K c ​ D ) O(PD+K_{c}D) per-token complexity under stable-face regimes.
•
Statistical evidence/arguments that Δ \Delta is typically bounded away from 0 0 in practical LLM decoding (Section 8 ).
•
A complete appendix: full proof, second-order asymptotics, and lower bounds (Appendices A–D).

7 Vashista Sparse Attention and Decode Complexity
We connect the theorem to an industrial decoding architecture.
7.1 Paged KV and routing
Assume a paged KV cache: keys/values are stored in pages of size B sz B_{\text{sz}} (block size), and each sequence maintains a block table mapping logical blocks to physical pages (as in vLLM). At decode time:
1.
Page routing : score coarse page summaries k page k_{\mathrm{page}} to select P P candidate pages.
2.
Token routing : score tokens within those pages to select K c K_{c} candidate tokens.
3.
Convex sparse solve : compute α \alpha on the candidates (e.g., Frank–Wolfe with small iterations and certificates).
4.
Fused gather : compute ∑ k ≤ K c α k ​ V ​ [ page k , off k ] \sum_{k\leq K_{c}}\alpha_{k}\,V[\mathrm{page}_{k},\mathrm{off}_{k}] via a fused CUDA kernel (no materialization).
7.2 Implementation corollary
Corollary 3 (Implementation complexity: O ⁡ ( P ​ D + K c ​ D ) O(PD+K_{c}D) decode) .
Let D D be head dimension. Suppose the routed candidate sizes P P and K c K_{c} are bounded independently of context length T T .
Then the per-token decode complexity (and KV reads) of Vashista Sparse Attention is
O ⁡ ( P ​ D + K c ​ D ) , O(PD+K_{c}D),
compared to dense attention cost O ⁡ ( T ​ D ) O(TD) .

10 Analysis: Quality Guarantees, Baselines, and Failure Modes
This section makes the theoretical guarantees operational for practitioners by discussing accuracy evidence, baseline breadth, and failure modes.
10.1 Accuracy and retrieval quality: what is guaranteed and what must be reported
Our theoretical results bound the deviation between entropic attention and the unregularized projection solution. In particular, under the support-gap condition Δ ⁡ ( q ) > 0 \Delta(q)>0 , the probability mass assigned to off-face tokens decays exponentially in Δ ⁡ ( q ) / ε \Delta(q)/\varepsilon (Corollary 1 ), while the on-face perturbation scales linearly with ε \varepsilon (Theorem 1 ). These two terms provide an explicit accuracy-control knob :
‖ y ε − y ∗ ‖ ≤ C lin ​ ε + C exp ​ exp ⁡ ( − Δ ⁡ ( q ) c ​ ε ) , \|y_{\varepsilon}-y^{\ast}\|\ \leq\ C_{\mathrm{lin}}\,\varepsilon\;+\;C_{\mathrm{exp}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\Delta(q)}{c\,\varepsilon}\Big),
(10.1)
so choosing ε ≪ Δ ⁡ ( q ) \varepsilon\ll\Delta(q) makes any information discarded outside the active face mathematically negligible.
Recommended evaluation protocol.
Because Table 2 and Table 3 focus on latency, a complete evaluation should additionally report (i) perplexity deltas on standard language modeling benchmarks and (ii) long-context retrieval accuracy on benchmarks such as Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) and RULER, at matched context lengths. These metrics should be reported as a function of the operating point ( P , K c , ε ) (P,K_{c},\varepsilon) and alongside the leakage diagnostic Δ ^ \widehat{\Delta} from Section 8 . The theorem predicts that regimes with larger Δ ^ \widehat{\Delta} tolerate more aggressive sparsification with minimal quality impact.
10.2 Baseline comparisons beyond dense SDPA
Our system measurements compare against dense SDPA (FlashAttention) because it is the dominant production baseline in vLLM/sglang serving. For positioning, it is useful to contrast with other long-context approximations:
Heuristic retention and streaming methods.
Approaches such as token eviction (e.g., H2O-like policies) and streaming attention maintain a fixed budget by heuristic selection. We also relate to streaming/eviction approaches such as StreamingLLM, which maintain a running memory but do not provide a per-query geometric certificate comparable to our KKT support gap. These methods can be effective but typically do not provide a deterministic certificate that the discarded tokens are irrelevant for a given query.
Geometric certificate as a moat.
In contrast, Vashista Sparse Attention is tied to a convex-analytic optimum and exposes a dual certificate: the KKT multipliers induce the support gap Δ ⁡ ( q ) \Delta(q) (Lemma 1 ). When Δ ⁡ ( q ) > 0 \Delta(q)>0 , the exponential leakage bound of Corollary 1 quantifies, per query, how much probability mass can lie outside the active face at temperature ε \varepsilon . This is a qualitatively different guarantee from heuristic baselines.
10.3 Failure modes: the Δ ⁡ ( q ) ≈ 0 \Delta(q)\approx 0 regime
The face-stability guarantee requires Δ ⁡ ( q ) > 0 \Delta(q)>0 . Degenerate cases (ties or near-ties) can produce Δ ⁡ ( q ) ≈ 0 \Delta(q)\approx 0 , in which case exponential concentration may not hold and attention can become effectively dense.
System behavior.
In these regimes, two defensible policies exist: (i) fallback-to-dense for a small fraction of queries flagged by a near-zero Δ ^ \widehat{\Delta} diagnostic, preserving quality at the cost of occasional dense steps; or (ii) cap-compute at ( P , K c ) (P,K_{c}) , accepting that accuracy may degrade when the diagnostic indicates an ambiguous active face. The current implementation is compatible with either policy; the choice is deployment-dependent.
Why Δ ⁡ ( q ) ≈ 0 \Delta(q)\approx 0 is often rare in practice.
Section 8 argues that in high-dimensional embeddings, exact degeneracies are statistically unlikely and the empirical gap proxy typically scales slowly with M M (informally, 𝔼 ⁡ [ Δ ^ ] ≈ 1 / log ⁡ M \mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]\approx 1/\log M ). This supports the engineering assumption that fallback events are uncommon, but the diagnostic should be reported and monitored.
10.4 Latency breakdown: routing vs. solver vs. kernel
A natural concern is whether the convex sparse solve (e.g., Frank–Wolfe style updates) dominates runtime. The end-to-end latency reported in Table 2 includes: (a) page routing (selecting P P pages), (b) the sparse solver (producing a K c K_{c} -sparse mixture), and (c) the fused attention kernel over the selected candidates. In bandwidth-limited long-context decoding, the dominant savings come from avoiding O ⁡ ( T ​ D ) O(TD) reads, so the key requirement is that (a)+(b) remain small compared to the avoided memory traffic.
Latency breakdown.
A profiler-based breakdown (routing/solver/kernel) as percentages of the “Sparse ms” column directly addresses this concern. The expected outcome is that solver overhead is amortized by the reduced IO, especially at large T T where dense attention becomes memory bound.
10.5 Sensitivity to the temperature / regularization parameter ε \varepsilon
The parameter ε \varepsilon controls a trade-off: large ε \varepsilon increases leakage (less sparsity), while very small ε \varepsilon can make optimization numerically stiff. The leakage bound suggests a principled operating heuristic:
target leakage ​ δ ⇒ ε ≲ Δ ⁡ ( q ) c ​ log ⁡ ( ( M − | I | ) / δ ) . \text{target leakage }\delta\ \Rightarrow\ \varepsilon\ \lesssim\ \frac{\Delta(q)}{c\,\log((M-|I|)/\delta)}.
(10.2)
In practice, deployments can choose a conservative global ε \varepsilon and optionally adapt it using the diagnostic Δ ^ \widehat{\Delta} , keeping ε \varepsilon within a stable range while enforcing a desired leakage level.
10.6 Why the “constants” P P and K c K_{c} need not grow with T T
The complexity claim O ⁡ ( P ​ D + K c ​ D ) O(PD+K_{c}D) hinges on the observation that effective attention support is governed by the active face size and by how many semantic regions must be searched to locate that face. The face-stability theorem implies that, once the relevant face is found, increasing T T adds mostly off-face keys whose contribution is exponentially suppressed when Δ ⁡ ( q ) \Delta(q) stays bounded away from zero. This motivates treating K c K_{c} as a context-independent budget in the stable regime.
For P P , the routing mechanism targets a bounded number of pages expected to contain the active face (Section 7 ). The theoretical link is that the number of relevant faces for a fixed query does not grow linearly with T T under face stability; the diagnostic Δ ^ \widehat{\Delta} provides a way to detect and handle the rare cases where this assumption breaks.
11 Deployment Roadmap and Enterprise Impact
This work is motivated by a common enterprise constraint: long-context serving must be fast , predictable ,
and often privacy-preserving (e.g., on-prem or air-gapped environments).
Beyond the theoretical guarantees and benchmarks in Section 9 , we outline how the proposed
mechanism is intended to be adopted in production systems and why it matters for business-critical workloads.
11.1 Next steps: a drop-in attention replacement for vLLM and sglang
Datar Labs is currently engineering a drop-in replacement for standard attention modules that allows
operators to interchange attention mechanisms inside popular serving stacks, notably vLLM and sglang ,
without changing model weights or application code.
The goal is to expose Vashista Sparse Attention as a backend that can be enabled via configuration, while preserving:
(i) compatibility with common decoder-only Transformer checkpoints,
(ii) existing KV-cache formats and batching/scheduling logic, and
(iii) deterministic behavior required for reproducible offline evaluation.
11.2 Why air-gapped and confidentiality-first deployments benefit
In air-gapped or confidentiality-first settings (regulated industries, sensitive IP, internal knowledge bases),
deployments typically cannot rely on external retrieval services, hosted vector databases, or third-party telemetry.
A constant-size effective-context mechanism is valuable because it reduces the compute and memory footprint per token
while keeping the full context local to the deployment.
This directly improves:
•
Cost predictability: bounded per-token attention work makes latency and throughput more stable under long inputs.
•
Privacy posture: all scoring and selection can run on-prem, avoiding data egress and simplifying compliance.
•
Operational simplicity: fewer moving parts than multi-service retrieval pipelines in restricted environments.
11.3 Enterprise advantages for RAG
In Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), the dominant failure modes are often retrieval noise and context bloat :
too many retrieved chunks degrade answer quality and increase serving cost.
Vashista Sparse Attention offers an additional, model-native filtering layer:
•
Noise suppression: the support/face-gap perspective predicts that irrelevant tokens receive exponentially small mass
once a stable active face is formed, improving robustness to imperfect retrieval.
•
Budget control: attention can be bounded to a constant candidate set even when upstream retrieval returns many chunks.
•
Faster iteration: teams can tune a small set of serving knobs (e.g., paging parameters and the entropic temperature)
to trade off quality and latency with a principled safety margin (gap diagnostics).
Practically, this means organizations can keep larger corpora and longer prompts available while paying only for the
small portion that is actually used by the model at decode time.
11.4 Other high-value use cases
The same properties are attractive in:
•
Long-horizon agents and tool use: where histories grow quickly and bounded attention avoids quadratic slowdowns.
•
Enterprise copilots over code and logs: where inputs are long, noisy, and private.
•
Multi-document summarization and compliance review: where full-document retention matters but compute budgets are fixed.
Takeaway.
The core theoretical message of this paper— constant-size effective attention under a verifiable support-gap condition —is
not only a mathematical statement but also a deployment principle: keep all context available locally, yet pay only for what
the model provably needs.
12 Limitations and Responsible Use
•
Guarantees require a positive gap Δ ⁡ ( q ) > 0 \Delta(q)>0 ; quasi-ties weaken exponential leakage.
•
The analysis is local in q q and assumes tangential nondegeneracy on the active face.
•
The strongest system gains rely on paged KV layout and IO-aware kernels.
•
Adversarial contexts may force large active supports.

We provided a geometric theory explaining why long-context decoding can be intrinsically sparse under stable-face regimes, and we outlined an IO-aware sparse paged attention mechanism aligned with modern serving engines.

(v) the on-face displacement is linear in ε \varepsilon with a constant governed by tangential conditioning.

Assume Δ ⁡ ( q ) = 0 \Delta(q)=0 and that the set of active faces is not locally constant at q q .
Then there exist sequences ε n ↓ 0 \varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0 and queries q n → q q_{n}\to q such that

for some constant c > 0 c>0 (depending on local curvature and the tie structure).
Proof.
When Δ ⁡ ( q ) = 0 \Delta(q)=0 , the dual multipliers corresponding to at least one inactive atom can approach 0 0 along perturbations of q q .
In the entropic KKT conditions, inactive mass satisfies α ε , j = exp ( − μ ε , j / ε ) \alpha_{\varepsilon,j}=\exp(-\mu_{\varepsilon,j}/\varepsilon) up to normalization.
If μ ε , j = O ⁡ ( ε ) \mu_{\varepsilon,j}=O(\varepsilon) along such perturbations (as happens near ties), then α ε , j = Θ ⁡ ( 1 ) \alpha_{\varepsilon,j}=\Theta(1) .
Even when normalization dampens the effect, one can choose perturbations so that μ ε , j ≍ ε \mu_{\varepsilon,j}\asymp\varepsilon for a set of indices,
forcing total inactive mass at least on the order of ε \varepsilon (and often constant).
∎
D.3 Implication for sparse decoding
Theorem 1 is therefore inherently a stable-face result: it explains why sparsity is
typical when gaps are positive and supports are isolated, but it cannot rule out dense regimes at (or near)
queries that induce ties, plateaus, or rapidly changing active faces.

E.4 Practical screening interpretation
In decoding, one can use cheap scores (e.g. approximate logits) to propose a candidate set S S ,
run FW on Δ S \Delta_{S} , and monitor g t g_{t} . If g t g_{t} is below the certificate threshold, the active face is likely correct
and sparse decoding is justified. Otherwise, one enlarges S S (paged expansion) until the certificate passes.
