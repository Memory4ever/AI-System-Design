Abstract
Neural scaling laws provide a predictable recipe for AI advancement: reducing numerical precision should linearly improve computational efficiency and energy profile ( E ∝ bits E\propto\text{bits} ). In this paper, we demonstrate that this scaling law breaks in the context of multi-hop reasoning. We reveal a ’quantization trap’ where reducing precision from 16-bit to 8/4-bit paradoxically increases more net energy consumption while degrading reasoning accuracy. We provide a rigorous theoretical decomposition that attributes this failure to hardware casting overhead, the hidden latency cost of dequantization kernels, which becomes a dominant bottleneck in sequential reasoning chains, as well as to a sequential energy amortization failure. As a result, scaling law breaking is unavoidable in practice. Our findings suggest that the industry’s “smaller-is-better” heuristic is mathematically counterproductive for complex reasoning tasks.
Keywords: Scaling law, Sustainability, Quantization, LLM
1 Introduction
The ”Scaling Law” is the foundational dogma of modern Large Language Models (LLMs) [ 1 ] . It posits that performance is a predictable function of compute and by extension, that quantization is almost a ”free lunch” for efficiency: reducing bits b b should yield a proportional reduction in energy E E and memory V V .
Consequently, the industry has widely adopted aggressive compression techniques to facilitate wider LLM deployment. For instance, the transition from 16-bit floating point (FP16) to 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) or GPTQ-weighted quantization [ 2 , 3 ] has become the standard protocol for serving 70B-parameter models on single consumer-grade GPUs. These methods are built on the empirical success of ’near-lossless’ quantization in general linguistic tasks, where the marginal increase in perplexity is viewed as a negligible price for the 4 × \times reduction in memory footprint and the theoretical 2–4 × \times gain in throughput [ 2 , 4 ] . This ’linear scaling law’ paradigm assumes that efficiency gains are monotonic: as the bit-width b b decreases, the inference cost of LLM (functionally the ’cost-to-reason’ in complex tasks) should follow a downward linear trajectory.
However, we identify a fundamental breakdown of this linear scaling paradigm within multi-hop reasoning. Multi-hop reasoning [ 5 ] is the process of decomposing a complex problem into a sequential chain of intermediate logical steps, where each deduction acts as a strict prerequisite for the next. Here, the sequential accumulation of quantization noise across logical chains triggers a violation of the scaling law and we term this as Quantization Trap , where energy consumption increases and reasoning efficiency decreases for low-bit models, irrespective of model scale.
Technically, the scaling law violation (the mechanism) drives the model into this trap (the outcome), where the compressed model is strictly dominated by the original baseline.
We attribute this failure to the hardware-level ’Casting Overhead,’ where the cumulative micro-latency of de-quantizing weights back to native precision at each reasoning hop outweighs the memory bandwidth gains of compression besides rigorous analysis.
Our specific contributions:
1.
Sustainability Index (SI) framework: We develop a Sustainability Index (SI) framework to evaluate AI across the three pillars of sustainability: Economic Efficiency ( E S ​ I E_{SI} ), Social Trust ( T S ​ I T_{SI} ), and Environmental Energy ( S S ​ I S_{SI} ) to detect the Quantization Trap, in a mathematically rigorous way, which is a sub-optimal AI configurations ( Sustainability Inversion ).
2.
Quantization Trap in multi-hop reasoning: We empirically identify and characterize a fundamental breaking of linear scaling laws in multi-hop reasoning. Benchmarking diverse models (e.g., Mistral-7B [ 6 ] , Qwen-3 (0.6B)) on multi-hop reasoning-heavy datasets (e.g., GSM8K and MathQA) [ 7 , 8 ] , across NVIDIA L4, A100, H100, and Blackwell RTX 6000-pro architectures.
3.
Theoretical foundations of the Quantization Trap: We prove a Sequential Amortization Failure theorem establishing that the Quantization Trap is structurally unavoidable in multi-hop reasoning, and demonstrate that standard mitigation strategies are mathematically incapable of restoring reasoning trust.
By exposing the breakdown of scaling laws, our findings provide a rigorous mathematical bridge between hardware telemetry and cognitive performance, enabling developers to identify and avoid Quantization Traps before committing expensive GPU resources. Ultimately, this discovery will force a fundamental shift in LLM development away from brute-force compression toward precision-aware scaling that is tailored to the actual complexity of the reasoning task, besides a mitigation path.
2 Blind spots in LLM scaling laws
The current understanding of LLM scaling is dominated by the power-law relationships established by [ 1 ] and [ 9 ] , implicitly assuming that reducing numerical precision yields a guaranteed, linear reduction in hardware cost. While these scaling laws provide a recipe for pre-training, they treat ”efficiency” as a one-dimensional variable (typically FLOPs or memory footprint), effectively blinding practitioners to the non-linear latency and energy penalties incurred by hardware-level de-quantization.
Consequently, the subsequent explosion in quantization research [ 2 , 3 ] has focused almost exclusively on preserving model accuracy at lower bit-widths, assuming that hardware efficiency (e.g., on GPU) improves monotonically with bit-reduction.
However, existing scaling laws and evaluation frameworks suffer from at least two critical blind spots when applied to multi-hop reasoning :
1.
Sequential Deductive Fragility: They ignore the sequential dependencies of reasoning tasks, where quantization noise compounds at every reasoning step. This accumulation makes multi-hop reasoning uniquely fragile, as minor errors in early reasoning hops act as false premises for subsequent steps, causing irreversible logical divergence. This drives the model into the ’Quantization Trap’ , which remains invisible to standard single-turn benchmarks [ 10 ] .
2.
Hardware-Software Disconnect: They ignore the ’Casting Overhead’ [ 11 ] , the physical energy cost of de-quantizing low-bit weights back into native precision (FP16/BF16) during execution. This disconnect is particularly acute on widespread architectures like NVIDIA Ampere (A100) and Hopper (H100) ; while powerful, these architectures lack native arithmetic support for non-standard compressed formats (e.g., NF4), necessitating a mandatory, high-energy de-quantization step for every inference operation [ 2 , 12 ] . As a result, the linear scaling intuition (“fewer bits ⇒ \Rightarrow lower cost”) may not hold. The extra de-quantization (“casting”) work can outweigh the bandwidth savings, so using fewer bits does not reliably reduce latency or energy, and can even increase them.
To address these blind spots, we need a sustainability-focused evaluation that goes beyond single-number accuracy or perplexity. In particular, we require a unified, quantitative framework that jointly measures an LLM’s economic efficiency (throughput and deployability), trustworthiness (task performance under reasoning stress), and environmental cost (energy per query). Such a framework makes it possible to systematically detect the Quantization Trap , when apparent efficiency optimization, especially aggressive low-bit quantization-collapse under real deployment constraints and push the system into a quantization trap.
Section 3 introduces the framework of Sustainability Index (SI) to formally quantify these trade-offs.

Unlike arithmetic or geometric means, the harmonic formulation is bottleneck-sensitive . It strictly penalizes deficiencies in either dimension, preventing ’extreme-case bias’ where a model might achieve high throughput simply by consuming excessive memory (or vice-versa). By explicitly including ρ \rho , our economic sustainability index penalizes the systemic ’compute divide’ [ 13 ] .

3.2 The Sustainability Manifold: Aggregation and Scaling Laws
We define the global Sustainability Index (SI) as the scalar projection of the vector 𝐯 ⁡ ( θ ) \mathbf{v}(\theta) through a policy function ℱ : ℝ 3 → [ 0 , 1 ] \mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1] . While traditional scaling laws focus on a 1D trajectory (e.g., bits vs. loss), our framework evaluates model configurations as points on a 3D Sustainability Manifold .
In this work, we instantiate ℱ \mathcal{F} as a Compensatory (Linear) Policy , defined as:
S ​ I ​ ( θ ) = 𝐰 ⊤ ​ 𝐯 ​ ( θ ) , where ​ ∑ i ∈ { T , E , S } w i = 1 SI(\theta)=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{v}(\theta),\quad\text{where }\sum_{i\in\{T,E,S\}}w_{i}=1
(6)
This formulation enables us to quantify the marginal rate of substitution between Economic efficiency, Energy, and Trust within the global sustainability score. While the weight vector 𝐰 \mathbf{w} can be tuned to reflect specific task priorities, we adopt a balanced configuration for reasoning tasks ( w T = 0.34 , w E = 0.33 , w S = 0.33 w_{T}=0.34,w_{E}=0.33,w_{S}=0.33 ) to ensure equal prioritization of all three pillars.
Remark 3.1 .
While we employ a linear formulation to map the reasoning trade-off manifold, we acknowledge that safety-critical domains may necessitate a bottleneck-intolerant geometric aggregation ( S ​ I = ∏ v i w i SI=\prod v_{i}^{w_{i}} ) to prevent efficiency gains from masking catastrophic failure.
The Scaling Monotonicity Hypothesis
The prevailing scaling paradigm relies on the Monotonicity Hypothesis : the assumption that reducing precision p p yields a monotonic increase in the scalar utility of the AI system, i.e., ∂ S ​ I ∂ p < 0 \frac{\partial SI}{\partial p}<0 .
In multi-hop reasoning, however, reducing p p causes the Trust ( T S ​ I T_{SI} ) and Energy ( S S ​ I S_{SI} ) indices to degrade (see the next Section). This forces their partial derivatives with respect to precision to be positive ( ∂ T S ​ I ∂ p , ∂ S S ​ I ∂ p > 0 \frac{\partial T_{SI}}{\partial p},\frac{\partial S_{SI}}{\partial p}>0 ). When these penalties outweigh the marginal economic gains of compression ( ∂ E S ​ I ∂ p < 0 \frac{\partial E_{SI}}{\partial p}<0 ), the global gradient ∇ p S ​ I \nabla_{\!p}SI flips sign, mathematically detecting the Scaling Law Divergence inherent in a Quantization Trap.
Proposition 3.2 (Scaling Law Divergence) .
Let S ​ I ​ ( θ ) SI(\theta) be a function of precision (bit-width) p p for a configuration θ ∈ Θ \theta\in\Theta . A Quantization Trap is identified when: ∂ S ​ I ∂ p > 0 \frac{\partial SI}{\partial p}>0 signifying a fundamental breakdown of the linear scaling law. (proof in appendix)

4 Scaling Law Breaking in reasoning
In this section, we demonstrate that for multi-hopping reasoning tasks, the relationship between bit-precision and efficiency and energy is not merely sub-linear, but non-monotonic, leading to a structural ’Sustainability Inversion’: the scaling law is broken in a Quantization Trap.
4.1 Multi-Hop Reasoning and casting overhead
Multi-hop reasoning is distinguished from standard autoregressive generation by its requirement for deductive fidelity across a sequence of K K intermediate logical states, { h 1 , h 2 , … , h K } \{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{K}\} . In this regime, each reasoning ”hop” h k h_{k} is a logical precursor to h k + 1 h_{k+1} , creating a dependency chain where the probability of reaching the correct ground-truth solution y y is the joint product of successful intermediate transitions:
P ⁡ ( y ∣ x , θ ) = ∏ k = 1 K P ⁡ ( h k ∣ h < k , x , θ ) P(y\mid x,\theta)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}P(h_{k}\mid h_{<k},x,\theta)
(7)
This sequential structure makes reasoning uniquely sensitive to Scaling Law Divergence through two primary mechanisms under quantization.
Deductive Fragility. Quantization errors compound across sequential hops, where a minor shift in an early premise amplifies into ”deductive drift” and logical divergence. This may cause reasoning Trust to decrease more rapidly than in single-turn tasks, driving the positive trust gradient ∂ T S ​ I ∂ p > 0 \frac{\partial T_{SI}}{\partial p}>0 .
Temporal Accumulation.
Total latency 𝒟 total ≈ ∑ k = 1 K ( τ comp + τ cast ) \mathcal{D}_{\text{total}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\tau_{\text{comp}}+\tau_{\text{cast}}) aggregates native compute time τ comp \tau_{\text{comp}} and de-quantization overhead τ cast \tau_{\text{cast}} across all K K sequential logical hops. Here, a hop is the atomic autoregressive forward pass (token generation). This cumulative penalty bottlenecks throughput and inflates power draw, systematically degrading both the economic ( E S ​ I E_{SI} ) and energy ( S S ​ I S_{SI} ) sustainability pillars.
Logical vs. Atomic Hops.
While the literature [ 18 ] often defines ”multi-hop reasoning” by its M M high-level logical milestones (e.g., 8–9 arithmetic operations), these milestones are physically materialized on hardware through a much longer sequence of K K atomic hops (autoregressive token generations), where K ≫ M K\gg M , specifically in the context of multi-hop reasoning [ 19 ] . We anchor our analysis at the atomic hop level, because it is the fundamental unit of hardware execution where the casting overhead τ cast \tau_{\text{cast}} is incurred and compounded.

We formalize the hardware bottleneck via defining the Casting Overhead Ratio ( C ​ O ​ R COR ) ” the ratio of de-quantization latency to native compute time:

4.2 Evaluation Models, Data and Hardware
To probe the limits of scaling laws, we evaluate two primary reasoning-optimized models: Mistral-7B-v0.3 , a high-performance model with strong deductive capabilities, and Qwen-3-0.6B , an ultra-thin architecture designed for extreme resource-constrained deployment. We further corroborate our findings using Falcon-3B [ 21 ] to ensure that the observed Quantization Trap is a consistent phenomenon across mid-tier parameter scales.
GSM8K and MathQA [ 8 ] benchmarks: These models are tested on GSM8K (7.5K training / 1K test samples), consisting of multi-step grade-school arithmetic, and MathQA (29.8K training / 2.9K test samples), which focuses on complex algebraic transformationsc.
GSM8K consists of 8.5K high-quality, multi-step elementary school math word problems. Unlike simple question-answering, GSM8K requires models to generate a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) involving 2 to 8 arithmetic reasoning steps to arrive at a final verifiable answer. It serves as our primary probe for arithmetic noise propagation , where a single precision-induced error in an early intermediate step (e.g., a carry-over mistake) inevitably causes the entire logical chain to diverge from the ground truth.
MathQA demands significantly higher deductive depth in reasoning. MathQA stress-tests Scaling Law Divergence through complex algebraic transformations. Its extended logical chains amplify both Casting Overhead accumulation and the compounding effects of quantization noise .
Hardware Instrumentation. We mainly conduct our experiments on NVIDIA L4, A100, and H100 GPUs, spanning the Ada Lovelace, Ampere, and Hopper architectures, respectively. These platforms represent the dominant compute paths in contemporary AI deployment, ensuring that our characterization of Scaling Law Divergence reflects the physical reality of modern hardware constraints.

4.3.1 The Size Paradox: Ultra-Thin Models
One might dismiss the Scaling law breaking in Quantization Trap as a byproduct of the massive memory bandwidth requirements inherent to 7B-scale models; however, by evaluating the ultra-thin Qwen-3 (0.6B), we reveal a Size Paradox : the trap is not merely a symptom of model scale, but an inescapable architectural law that becomes structurally deeper as the parameter footprint shrinks.
As illustrated in Figure 2 , the 0.6B model provides the definitive evidence for Scaling Law Divergence on the GSM8K data. In Panel A, we observe a terminal Reasoning Collapse at 4-bit precision; while 8-bit maintains trust, the 4-bit variant exhibits a catastrophic drop in reasoning accuracy across all hardware.
The mechanical cause is isolated in Panel B. At the 0.6B parameter scale, τ comp \tau_{\text{comp}} becomes negligible. At the 0.6B scale, the actual matrix math is so minimal that it finishes within the GPU’s fixed latency floor for kernel dispatch [ 19 ] . 4.4 The scaling law breaking Driver: Amortization Failure
The scaling law breaking in Quantization Trap is a mechanistic consequence of Amortization Failure within the GPU’s execution pipeline. On architectures (L4/A100/H100), Tensor Cores lack a native hardware logic chain for sub-8-bit arithmetic [ 3 , 4 ] , necessitating the de-quantization of weights back into native precision ( F ​ P ​ 16 FP16 ) before any mathematical operation can occur.
This architectural void creates a high Casting Overhead Ratio (COR). For Mistral-7B reasoning on GSM8K, the COR on H100 hardware reaches a staggering 2.45 for 8-bit precision and 1.50 for 4-bit precision. This implies that for every joule spent on actual inference, the system wastes up to 2.45 joules simply translating bits.
In high-concurrency scenarios, this overhead is ”amortized” across the batch. However, multi-hop reasoning is operationally forced into a B = 1 B=1 regime to satisfy sequential logic dependencies. In this state, the COR is never shared. While 4-bit (NF4) achieves a 75% bandwidth benefit, the fixed COR ensures that the cumulative casting energy Φ = ∑ k = 1 K C ​ O ​ R \Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{K}COR grows linearly with the reasoning depth K K , eventually outpacing all memory savings. COR ⁡ ( p , B ) = a cast ​ ( p ) a comp ​ ( p ) ⋅ B ⟹ ∂ COR ∂ B = − a cast ​ ( p ) a comp ​ ( p ) ⋅ B 2 ≤ 0 \mathrm{COR}(p,B)=\frac{a_{\mathrm{cast}}(p)}{a_{\mathrm{comp}}(p)\cdot B}\implies\frac{\partial\mathrm{COR}}{\partial B}=-\frac{a_{\mathrm{cast}}(p)}{a_{\mathrm{comp}}(p)\cdot B^{2}}\leq 0

Because the total energy to move and de-quantize weights is constant, processing more queries simultaneously reduces the energy ’bill’ for each individual query.
2. Proving Trust Invariance:
Reasoning accuracy T ⁡ ( p , B ) T(p,B) is determined by the model’s logits, which are a function of the precision p p . For a fixed decoding algorithm, the output y ^ \hat{y} for an input x x is:
y ^ = f ⁡ ( x , p ) \hat{y}=f(x,p)
(24)
Since f ⁡ ( x , p ) f(x,p) has no dependency on the batch size B B (Assumption of Semantic Independence), the prediction for any query in the batch remains identical regardless of parallelization:
T ⁡ ( p , B ) = 1 N ​ ∑ i = 1 N 𝕀 ⁡ ( y ^ p , i = y i ∗ ) ⟹ ∂ T ∂ B = 0 T(p,B)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{p,i}=y^{*}_{i})\implies\frac{\partial T}{\partial B}=0

This proves the decoupling. Batching makes the hardware faster, but it does not make the model smarter. The reasoning errors caused by 4-bit quantization are a permanent property of the bits ( p p ), not the batch ( B B ). Mitigation via batching fixes the energy ’Trap’ but leaves the ’Deductive Fragility’ untouched.
