3 Problem Setup
Given a dataset {(𝐱i,yi)}i=1n\left\{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{i},y_{i}\right)\right\}_{i=1}^{n}, we are interested in training a neural network f⁡(𝜽,⋅)f\left(\bm{\theta},\cdot\right) that maps each input 𝐱i∈ℝd{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}’s to an output f⁡(𝜽,𝐱i)f\left(\bm{\theta},{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right) that fits the labels yi∈ℝy_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.
We consider f⁡(𝜽,⋅)f\left(\bm{\theta},\cdot\right) as a two-layer ReLU activated Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) under the NTK scaling:
f⁡(𝜽,𝐱)=1m​∑r=1mar​σ​(𝐰r⊤​𝐱),f\left(\bm{\theta},{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\sigma\left({\mathbf{w}}_{r}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}\right),
where 𝜽=({𝐰r}r=1m,{ar}r=1m)\bm{\theta}=\left(\left\{{\mathbf{w}}_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{m},\left\{a_{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{m}\right) denotes the neural network parameters, and 𝝈⁡(⋅)=max⁡{0,⋅}\bm{\sigma}\left(\cdot\right)=\max\{0,\cdot\} denotes the ReLU activation function.
We assume that the second-layer weights ar∈{±1}a_{r}\in\{\pm 1\} are fixed, and only the first layer weights 𝐰r{\mathbf{w}}_{r}’s are trainable.
Thus, we will be using f⁡(𝐖,𝐱)≡f⁡(𝜽,𝐱)f(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{x})\equiv f(\bm{\theta},\mathbf{x}) where 𝐖∈ℝm×d\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}, unless otherwise stated.
This neural network set-up is studied widely in previous works (Du et al., 2018).
We consider the training of the neural network by minimizing the MSE loss ℒ⁡(𝐖){\mathcal{L}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right) over the dataset {(𝐱i,yi)}i=1n\left\{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{i},y_{i}\right)\right\}_{i=1}^{n}:
ℒ⁡(𝐖)=12​∑i=1n(f⁡(𝐖,𝐱i)−yi)2.{\mathcal{L}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right)-y_{i}\right)^{2}.
With the influence of the ReLU activation, the loss is both non-convex and non-smooth.
However, a line of previous works (Du et al., 2018; Song and
Yang, 2020; Oymak and
Soltanolkotabi, 2019) proves a linear convergence rate of the loss function under the assumption that the number of hidden neurons is sufficiently large by adopting an NTK-based analysis (Jacot et al., 2018).
While there have been theoretical approaches and assumptions that go beyond the NTK assumption, our focus is on a generalized scenario where the input data may be corrupted in each iteration under a multiplicative Gaussian noise:
Let 𝐜∼𝒩⁡(𝟏d,κ2​𝐈d){\mathbf{c}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{1}_{d},\kappa^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{d}\right) be an isotropic Gaussian random vector centered at the all-one vector 𝟏d\bm{1}_{d}, the neural network output is given by f⁡(𝐖,𝐱⊙𝐜)f\left({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{x}}\odot{\mathbf{c}}\right),
where ⊙\odot denotes the Hadamard (element-wise) product between two vectors.
Under the multiplicative noise, the neural network is trained with gradient descent where each gradient is computed based on the surrogate loss ℒ𝐂​(𝐖){\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbf{C}}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right) defined over the neural network with the masked input:
ℒ𝐂​(𝐖)=12​∑i=1n(f⁡(𝐖,𝐱i⊙𝐜i)−yi)2.{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbf{C}}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\odot{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\right)-y_{i}\right)^{2}.
Here 𝐂={𝐜i}i=1n{\mathbf{C}}=\{{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} denotes the collection of the masks for all input 𝐱i{\mathbf{x}}_{i}.
We assume that 𝐜i{\mathbf{c}}_{i}’s are independent.
In real-world applications, this scheme can be considered as training on an imprecise hardware, where each input data point is read-in with noise. Alternatively, one could view each 𝐱i{\mathbf{x}}_{i} as the output of a pre-trained large model, and our training scheme can be considered as fine-tuning the last two layers with the Gaussian drop-out (Wang and Manning, 2013; Kingma et al., 2015; Rey and Mnih, 2021) in the intermediate layer.
Let {𝐖k}k=1K\left\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K} be generated from the stochastic gradient descent given by:
𝐖k+1=𝐖k−η​∇𝐖ℒ𝐂k​(𝐖k),\mathbf{W}_{k+1}=\mathbf{W}_{k}-\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right),
(1)
where 𝐂k{\mathbf{C}}_{k} is sampled independently in every iteration of the gradient descent.
Our goal is to study the convergence of the loss sequence {ℒ⁡(𝐖k)}k=1∞\left\{{\mathcal{L}}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}.
Notice that the loss involved in the weight-update is the surrogate loss ℒ𝐂k​(𝐖k){\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}\left(\mathbf{W}_{k}\right), but the loss we aim to show convergence is the original loss ℒ⁡(𝐖){\mathcal{L}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right).
Our set-up marks some differences from previous works.
First, our set-up is distinct from unbiased estimators in current literature; our setup does not have such favorable property, since the randomness is applied at the input level of the neural network.
Second, although there is a line of work that analyzes the convergence of vanilla drop-out tranining on two-layer neural networks (Liao and Kyrillidis, 2022; Mianjy and Arora, 2020), in their analysis the mask is applied to the hidden neurons after the activation function.
On the contrary, our mask is applied directly to the input, which is contained in the non-linear function.
Therefore, any analysis of the mask randomness must go through the ReLU function, which brings technical difficulty. We assume the following property for the training data.
Assumption 3.1.
The training dataset {(𝐱i,yi)}i=1n\{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{i},y_{i}\right)\}_{i=1}^{n} satisfies ‖𝐱i‖2≤1,|yi|≤O⁡(1)\left\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq 1,\left|y_{i}\right|\leq O\left(1\right), and for any pair i≠ji\neq j, there exists no real number qq such that 𝐱i=q⋅𝐱j{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=q\cdot{\mathbf{x}}_{j}.
This assumption guarantees the boundedness of the dataset, and that the input data are non-degenerate, which is a standard assumption in Du et al. (2018); Song and
Yang (2020); Liao and Kyrillidis (2022).

Remark 4.3.
The core of our analysis of the expected loss involves understanding how the ReLU activation behaves under the multiplicative Gaussian input mask. Lemma D.14 in the appendix provides the analytical form for the expectation of a truncated Gaussian random variable.
This leads to the definition of a smoothed activation function, as presented in Theorem 4.2.
