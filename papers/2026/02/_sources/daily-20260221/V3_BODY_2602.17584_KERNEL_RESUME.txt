5.3 Linear Alignment of Contrastive Models
In the previous section, we showed that independently trained contrastive models can induce multimodal kernels that agree up to an additive constant, despite differences in data and modeling choices. We now assume this kernel agreement holds on domains of interest ΩX⊆𝒳\Omega_{X}\subseteq\mathcal{X} and ΩY⊆𝒴\Omega_{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{Y} (e.g., a downstream image-text dataset) and analyze its geometric consequences. Our first main result shows that matching kernels on a small set of anchor points suffices to determine a linear map relating the two embedding spaces.
Let the contrastive model pairs (f,g)(f,g) and (f~,g~)(\tilde{f},\tilde{g}) map inputs to 𝕊d−1⊂ℝd\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} and 𝕊d~−1⊂ℝd~\mathbb{S}^{\tilde{d}-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}} respectively, where d≤d~d\leq\tilde{d}, without loss of generality. 5.4 Isometric Alignment of Contrastive Models
Theorem 5.3 shows that kernel matching identifies the representation up to a linear map AA. But, contrastive encoders normalize embeddings to the unit hypersphere 𝕊d−1\mathbb{S}^{d-1}, forcing ‖f~​(x)‖2=‖A​f​(x)‖2=1\|\tilde{f}(x)\|_{2}=\|Af(x)\|_{2}=1 everywhere. This forces AA to be an isometry (A⊤​A=IdA^{\top}A=I_{d}) only if the data is sufficiently diverse to probe the matrix in all directions. We formalize this diversity via the following condition.
Definition 5.4.

Equivalently: if M∈Sym⁡(d)M\in\mathrm{Sym}(d) and x⊤​M​x=0x^{\top}Mx=0 for all x∈Sx\in S, then M=0M=0. This equivalence follows from the identity x⊤​M​x=⟨x​x⊤,M⟩x^{\top}Mx=\langle xx^{\top},M\rangle.

(Orthogonal Identifiability, proof in Section C.5.2).
