2 Background: Geometric Framework for Grokking
We review the geometric framework introduced in our prior work [Xu, 2026b] for studying grokking through the lens of loss-landscape curvature. This section defines the key mathematical objects and establishes the theoretical context for the present work.
2.1 Grokking
Power et al. [2022] first reported grokking on modular arithmetic tasks, showing that small transformers can memorize training data in hundreds of steps but require thousands to generalize. Subsequent work has shown grokking in diverse settings including group operations [Nanda et al., 2023], boolean functions, sparse parities [Barak et al., 2022], and compositional generalization [Murty et al., 2023]. The phenomenon is sensitive to regularization strength [Liu et al., 2022], dataset size [Liu et al., 2023], and learning rate.

2.3 The Commutator Defect
The commutator defect quantifies the non-commutativity of gradient updates from different mini-batches, providing a local probe of loss-landscape curvature that requires no Hessian computation.

A large defect signals that the order of gradient updates matters—the loss landscape has developed curvature structure that breaks path-independence.
2.4 Manifold Projection and Invariance
To determine whether curvature lives inside or outside the execution manifold, we decompose the commutator vector δ\delta relative to the PCA basis [Xu, 2026b].
Definition 3 (Invariance measure).
Let B∈ℝP×KB\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times K} be an orthonormal basis for the PCA subspace embedded in the full parameter space (PP total parameters). Given commutator vector δ=θA​B−θB​A\delta=\theta_{AB}-\theta_{BA}, decompose:
δ=B​B⊤​δ⏟δ∥​(projected)+(δ−B​B⊤​δ)⏟δ⟂​(residual).\delta=\underbrace{BB^{\top}\delta}_{\delta_{\parallel}\ (\text{projected})}+\underbrace{(\delta-BB^{\top}\delta)}_{\delta_{\perp}\ (\text{residual})}.
(6)
The invariance measure is the residual fraction:
ρ=∥δ⟂∥∥δ∥.\rho=\frac{\lVert\delta_{\perp}\rVert}{\lVert\delta\rVert}.
(7)
If ρ≈1\rho\approx 1, the commutator is orthogonal to the execution manifold—curvature is confined to the normal bundle—and the manifold exhibits empirical invariance under the optimization dynamics. If ρ≈0\rho\approx 0, curvature lies within the learned subspace.
Definition 4 (Transverse decoupling).
The optimization dynamics are transversely decoupled on the execution manifold ℳ\mathcal{M} if commutator defect vectors are confined to its normal bundle (ρ≈1\rho\approx 1). In this regime, curvature-induced perturbations act almost entirely orthogonally to ℳ\mathcal{M}, so that the observed trajectory remains confined to the learned subspace over training.
In modular arithmetic, we found ρ≈1.000\rho\approx 1.000 within numerical precision across all 36 conditions [Xu, 2026b] (6 operations ×\times 2 weight-decay settings ×\times 3 seeds), with the exec/random projection ratio at 1.8–2.9×\times, confirming that the near-zero parallel component is geometrically structured rather than a dimensionality artifact.
2.5 Random Subspace Control
Because any KK-dimensional subspace of ℝP\mathbb{R}^{P} captures ∼K/P\sim\sqrt{K/P} of a random vector, near-unity ρ\rho alone does not confirm geometric structure. Following Xu [2026b], we compare the PCA-basis projection against random baselines: for each commutator vector δ\delta, we compute the projection fraction onto Nrand=5N_{\text{rand}}=5 random KK-dimensional orthonormal bases and report the exec/random ratio projexec/projrand\text{proj}_{\text{exec}}/\text{proj}_{\text{rand}}. Values significantly above 1.0 confirm that the PCA subspace captures more commutator energy than expected by chance.
2.6 Scaling Law
We established [Xu, 2026b] that the lead time between defect onset and grokking obeys a power law:
Δ​t=C⋅tgrokα,\Delta t=C\cdot t_{\text{grok}}^{\alpha},
(8)
where Δ​t=tgrok−tonset\Delta t=t_{\text{grok}}-t_{\text{onset}} is the lead time, tgrokt_{\text{grok}} is the grokking step, and tonsett_{\text{onset}} is the defect onset step. The lead fraction Δ​t/tgrok\Delta t/t_{\text{grok}} expresses lead time as a proportion of total training time. In modular arithmetic, α=1.27±0.03\alpha=1.27\pm 0.03 (R2=0.97R^{2}=0.97, n=43n=43) across a 300×\times learning-rate sweep: because α>1\alpha>1, the predictive window grows super-linearly with grokking timescale.
3 Experimental Setup
3.1 Tasks and Datasets
SCAN Compositional Generalization.
We use the SCAN simple split [Lake and Baroni, 2018], randomly sampling Ntrain=2,048N_{\text{train}}=2{,}048 command–action pairs from the full dataset of approximately 14,000 examples. The remaining examples form the test set (∼9,000{\sim}9{,}000). The command vocabulary contains 21 tokens and the action vocabulary 7 tokens. Maximum command length is 8 tokens and maximum action length is 13 tokens (both plus boundary tokens). The data split is fixed across all runs (data seed = 0).
Dyck-1 Depth Prediction.
We generate Dyck-1 sequences of length 24 (maximum nesting depth 12) with a fixed random seed. The model must predict the current stack depth at each parenthesis position: a 13-class classification problem (depths 0–12). The vocabulary contains 3 tokens: open parenthesis, close parenthesis, and padding. We use an extreme data-scarcity regime: Ntrain=50N_{\text{train}}=50 sequences, Ntest=5,000N_{\text{test}}=5{,}000 sequences. This large train–test ratio amplifies the grokking effect.
3.2 Model Architectures
SCAN.
We use a standard encoder–decoder transformer with dmodel=256d_{\text{model}}=256, nlayers=3n_{\text{layers}}=3 (each for encoder and decoder), nheads=4n_{\text{heads}}=4, dff=512d_{\text{ff}}=512, and no dropout. The decoder uses causal masking and cross-attention to the encoder output. Each decoder layer has four sets of projection matrices: self-attention (WQ,WK,WV,WOW_{Q},W_{K},W_{V},W_{O}), cross-attention (WQ×,WK×,WV×,WO×W^{\times}_{Q},W^{\times}_{K},W^{\times}_{V},W^{\times}_{O}), and feedforward (Wup,WdownW_{\text{up}},W_{\text{down}}).
Dyck.
We use a causal (decoder-only) transformer with dmodel=128d_{\text{model}}=128, nlayers=2n_{\text{layers}}=2, nheads=4n_{\text{heads}}=4, dff=256d_{\text{ff}}=256, and no dropout. Each layer has self-attention (WQ,WK,WV,WOW_{Q},W_{K},W_{V},W_{O}) and feedforward (Wup,WdownW_{\text{up}},W_{\text{down}}) projections. This architecture matches the encoder used for modular arithmetic in Xu [2026b], providing a controlled comparison.

3.3 Training Protocol
Both tasks are trained with AdamW [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019] (β1=0.9\beta_{1}=0.9, β2=0.98\beta_{2}=0.98) and gradient clipping at norm 1.0. Weight decay is fixed at λ=1.0\lambda=1.0 throughout—the strong regularization is necessary to induce grokking within tractable training budgets. This matches the fast-regime configuration of Xu [2026b].
We sweep learning rates across two or more orders of magnitude for each task:
•
SCAN: η∈{10−5,5×10−5,10−4,5×10−4,10−3}\eta\in\{10^{-5},5{\times}10^{-5},10^{-4},5{\times}10^{-4},10^{-3}\}, with 13 runs total (seeds 42, 137, 2024 at each of the four higher LRs, plus seed 42 at η=10−5\eta=10^{-5}).
•
Dyck: η∈{3×10−5,10−4,5×10−4,10−3,3×10−3,10−2}\eta\in\{3{\times}10^{-5},10^{-4},5{\times}10^{-4},10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-3},10^{-2}\}, with 16 runs total (seeds 42, 137, 2024 at each of the five higher LRs, plus seed 42 at η=3×10−5\eta=3{\times}10^{-5}).
Maximum training steps are learning-rate-dependent (longer budgets for slower LRs), ranging from 20K steps at η=10−2\eta=10^{-2} to 200K steps at η=10−5\eta=10^{-5} (SCAN) and 300K steps at η=3×10−5\eta=3\times 10^{-5} (Dyck).
3.4 Attention Weight Logging
Following Xu [2026b], we log attention weight matrices at regular intervals during training. For SCAN, we log encoder and decoder self-attention and cross-attention projections. For Dyck, we log the self-attention projections (WQ,WK,WV,WOW_{Q},W_{K},W_{V},W_{O}) and feedforward weights (Wup,WdownW_{\text{up}},W_{\text{down}}) every 200 steps, yielding ∼\sim101 snapshots per seed at η=10−3\eta=10^{-3}.
4 Methods
4.1 Commutator Defect Measurement
At regular intervals (every 100–500 steps, depending on LR), we compute K=5K=5 independent commutator defect measurements by sampling fresh random mini-batch pairs (A,B)(A,B) from the training set. The perturbation step size is ηcomm=10−3\eta_{\text{comm}}=10^{-3}, matching Xu [2026b]. Adaptive scaling is applied if gradient norms are too small for float32 precision. We report the median, 25th, and 75th percentiles across the KK measurements.
The defect is computed via Equations 2, 3 and 4: we perform two forward-backward passes (once in order A→BA\to B, once in order B→AB\to A) and measure how much the final parameter vectors diverge, normalized by the gradient magnitudes. Each measurement thus requires 4 forward-backward passes total.
4.2 Grokking Detection
We define the grokking step tgrokt_{\text{grok}} as the first training step at which test accuracy exceeds a threshold for nsustained=3n_{\text{sustained}}=3 consecutive evaluations:
•
SCAN: test sequence-level accuracy ≥0.98\geq 0.98
•
Dyck: test token-level accuracy ≥0.98\geq 0.98
The sustained criterion is essential at high learning rates, where accuracy can oscillate rapidly above and below the threshold without stabilizing (see Section 7). This matches the early-stopping criterion of Xu [2026b], who defined grokking as test accuracy ≥98%\geq 98\% for 3 consecutive evaluations.
4.3 Defect Onset Detection
We define the defect onset step tonsett_{\text{onset}} as the first step where the median defect exceeds both:
1.
10×10\times the baseline (median of the first 3 measurements), and
2.
An absolute floor of 20.
This dual criterion, introduced in Xu [2026b], avoids false positives from noise at early steps when the defect baseline may be near zero.
4.4 PCA Eigenanalysis
For weight snapshots at η=10−3\eta=10^{-3} (Dyck) and η=10−4\eta=10^{-4} (SCAN), we compute the expanding-window PC1 variance ratio following the procedure of Section 2.2: at each training step tt, we form the trajectory matrix XX from weight displacements Δ​W​(τ)=W⁡(τ)−W⁡(0)\Delta W(\tau)=W(\tau)-W(0) for τ=0,…,t\tau=0,\ldots,t and compute the fraction of variance explained by the first principal component.
We compare grokking runs (λ=1.0\lambda=1.0) against no-weight-decay controls (λ=0\lambda=0), and compute z-scores relative to a random-walk null model (synthetic trajectories with matched per-step displacement norms but randomized directions).
4.5 Three-Basis Integrability Decomposition
To understand where in parameter space the non-commutativity arises, we decompose the commutator vector δ\delta into components aligned with three learned bases, extending the analysis of Xu [2026b]. At each phase of training (early, memorization, pre-grok, post-grok), we construct three orthonormal bases:
1.
Weight SVD: top-kk singular directions of the current weight matrix W⁡(t)W(t).
2.
Δ​W\Delta W SVD: top-kk singular directions of the weight displacement W⁡(t)−W⁡(0)W(t)-W(0).
3.
Gradient SVD: top-kk singular directions of the accumulated gradient matrix.
For each basis, we compute the exec/random ratio: the projection fraction onto the learned basis from actual training (“exec”) versus a random basis of matched dimensionality. A ratio near 1.0 indicates the commutator is randomly oriented; a ratio above 1.0 indicates structured alignment with the learning dynamics.
