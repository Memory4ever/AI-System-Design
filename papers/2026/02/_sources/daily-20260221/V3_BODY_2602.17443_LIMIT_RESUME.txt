Appendix I Limitations
Automated Adjudication Validity.
Our evaluation relies on an LLM-based Arbiter to determine semantic leakage and rule violations. Although low-temperature sampling (T=0.01T=0.01) reduces variance, automated semantic equivalence judgments may miss subtle implicatures, indirect confirmations, or pragmatic cues. Conversely, borderline paraphrases may be flagged as leakage. While this approach ensures scalability and consistency, it does not replace human expert validation.
Atomic Secret Design.
In AIDG-I, secrets are single, atomic propositions. This isolates containment and extraction dynamics but does not capture multi-fact or relational secrets (e.g., distributed knowledge, conditional dependencies). Real-world information protection often involves compositional structure, where leakage may occur incrementally across turns. Our findings may not directly extrapolate to such settings.
Closed Ontology in AIDG-II.
The 100-word ontology enables controlled entropy-reduction analysis but restricts hypothesis space structure. Real-world deduction often operates over open or dynamically expanding domains. Models may behave differently when the hypothesis space is not explicitly bounded.
Tournament Size and Statistical Power.
Although 439 games provide structured coverage, the number of games per role per model remains moderate. Some per-model comparisons (e.g., disqualification variance or Mode B win rates near 0%) may be sensitive to small sample effects. Larger-scale replications would strengthen confidence in fine-grained ranking differences.
Parameter Count as Proxy for Capability.
We report model scale (parameter count) as a descriptive variable; however, parameter count is an imperfect proxy for training data quality, alignment procedures, architectural innovations, and post-training optimization. Observed correlations (or lack thereof) between scale and performance should therefore be interpreted cautiously.
Prompt and Framing Sensitivity.
Game dynamics depend on system prompt formulation. Minor wording changes can alter alignment priors or reasoning behavior. While we release prompts verbatim for reproducibility, alternative phrasings or role descriptions could influence results.
Temperature and Decoding Choices.
Seeker agents operate at T=0.7T=0.7 to induce strategic diversity. Different decoding parameters (e.g., top-pp, deterministic decoding) may yield different strategic trajectories. Our conclusions are therefore conditioned on this decoding regime.
Turn Horizon Constraints.
Maximum turn limits (10 for AIDG-I, 16 for AIDG-II) impose a fixed interaction horizon. Very long-horizon strategies, gradual probing, or delayed leakage patterns are not captured. The turn-decay multiplier in AIDG-II further incentivizes earlier locking behavior, potentially shaping strategic exploration.
Model-to-Model Interaction.
All games are model-to-model. Human interlocutors exhibit richer pragmatic adaptation, social signaling, and long-term memory. Consequently, observed asymmetries may differ in mixed human–model or human–human adversarial settings.
Distributional Alignment Bias.
Instruction-tuned models are optimized for helpfulness and safety alignment. The observed asymmetry between containment and deduction may partially reflect alignment priors rather than core architectural constraints. Alternative training objectives could produce different asymmetry magnitudes.
Generality Across Domains and Languages.
Our evaluation focuses on English-language, general-purpose frontier models. Multilingual models, domain-specialized systems, or non-instruction-tuned base models may exhibit different asymmetry profiles.
ELO Modeling Assumptions.
The Dual-ELO system assumes transitive, stationary skill levels and independent game outcomes. In reality, strategic adaptation or meta-learning across games could violate these assumptions. Additionally, efficiency-weighted updates introduce a modeling choice that emphasizes early success in AIDG-II; alternative weighting schemes could yield slightly different rating magnitudes.
Appendix J Ethics Statement
This work studies adversarial dialogue capabilities in Large Language Models to better understand the boundaries of information containment and strategic inference. We recognize the dual-use nature of red-teaming and adversarial evaluation research and adopt safeguards to minimize risk.
•
Synthetic Secrets: All experiments use synthetic, atomic facts (e.g., “The coffee cup is empty”). We do not include personally identifiable information (PII), sensitive real-world data, or harmful domain knowledge.
•
Model-to-Model Interaction: All interactions occur between models. No human subjects are involved in dialogue generation or evaluation.
