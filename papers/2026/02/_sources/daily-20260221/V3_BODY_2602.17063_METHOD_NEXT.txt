3 Sign Lock-In Theory
The empirical results suggest that optimization rarely changes signs:
most coordinates keep the sign they received at initialization.
This section formalizes a simple mechanism behind this persistence.
A sign flip of a scalar coordinate can occur only if the trajectory crosses the boundary at 00.
If typical training dynamics keep coordinates at magnitudes bounded away from 00, then sign flips must be initiated by rare
excursions into a narrow boundary neighborhood.
We show that, under the standard training setting of deep learning, the number of effective outer-to-outer sign flips admits a geometric-tail bound.
3.1 Problem Setting
We analyze a single coordinate because sign patterns are defined entrywise and sign-storage cost is inherently coordinatewise.
The theory is stated for a one-dimensional adapted process; in experiments, we aggregate per-coordinate statistics across layers.

3.2 Assumption: Bounded Update and Re-Entry
The sign lock-in bound is stated for the abstract adapted process (wt)(w_{t}) and depends on two key components.
The first excludes “pathological” one-step sign flips. The second controls the likelihood that the process returns to the boundary neighborhood after moving back into the outer region.
Assumption 3.3 (Bounded update).
Fix T∈ℕT\in{\mathbb{N}}.
There exist deterministic constants Δ>0\Delta>0 and δupd∈[0,1)\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}\in[0,1) such that, for every stopping time
θ\theta satisfying θ≤T−1\theta\leq T-1,
ℙ⁡[ℰΔ​(θ)|ℱθ]≥1−δupd​a.s.,{\mathbb{P}}\left[{\mathcal{E}}_{\Delta}(\theta)|{\mathcal{F}}_{\theta}\right]\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}~~\mathrm{a.s.},
where the good event ℰΔ​(θ){\mathcal{E}}_{\Delta}(\theta) is
ℰΔ(θ)≔{maxθ≤t≤T−1|wt+1−wt|≤Δ}.{\mathcal{E}}_{\Delta}(\theta)\coloneqq\left\{\max_{\theta\leq t\leq T-1}|w_{t+1}-w_{t}|\leq\Delta\right\}.
In particular, on ℰΔ≔ℰΔ​(0){\mathcal{E}}_{\Delta}\coloneqq{\mathcal{E}}_{\Delta}(0) we have |wt+1−wt|≤Δ|w_{t+1}-w_{t}|\leq\Delta for all 0≤t≤T−10\leq t\leq T-1, and the special case δupd=0\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}=0 yields an almost-sure uniform increment bound.
Assumption 3.3 prevents “pathological” one-step sign flips that jump across the origin while remaining in the outer region.
With ϵ≥Δ\epsilon\geq\Delta (Definition 3.1), any outer-to-outer sign flip must pass through 𝖡𝖽⁡(ϵ)\mathsf{Bd}(\epsilon).
Assumption 3.4 (Re-entry bound condition).
There exists gT∈(0,1)g_{T}\in(0,1) such that for all k≥0k\geq 0,
ℙ[τk+1≤T|ℱσk]≤gTa.s.on{σk≤T}.{\mathbb{P}}[\tau_{k+1}\leq T|{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma_{k}}]\leq g_{T}~~\mathrm{a.s.}~\mathrm{on}~\{\sigma_{k}\leq T\}.
Rather than being a technical convenience, Assumption 3.4
delineates the boundary between a stable training regime, where meaningful weights persist,
and a degenerate floating regime (Appendix D.6)
in which the boundary becomes attractive and weights collapse toward zero.
The latter requires inward drift strong enough to distort task loss and is therefore non-standard.
Under standard smoothness and bounded-noise conditions, this assumption is satisfied for scheduled SGD
(Proposition D.10).
The proof is based on Lemma D.8 that the inward drift toward the sign boundary—even if present—is bounded by the finite cumulative gradient norm ensured by standard descent theory.
Proposition 3.5 (Informal version of Proposition D.10: re-entry bound in SGD).

there exists an explicit upper bound gTSGDg_{T}^{\mathrm{SGD}} such that for all k≥0k\geq 0,
ℙ⁡[τk+1≤T∣ℱσk]≤gTSGD.{\mathbb{P}}[\tau_{k+1}\leq T\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma_{k}}]\leq g_{T}^{\mathrm{SGD}}.
Moreover, gTSGDg_{T}^{\mathrm{SGD}} decreases when (i) the boundary margin ρ−ϵ\rho-\epsilon grows,
(ii) step sizes decay so that ∑t<Tηt2\sum_{t<T}\eta_{t}^{2} is small, and (iii) mini-batch noise is moderate.
Consequently, Assumption 3.4 holds with gT:=gTSGDg_{T}:=g_{T}^{\mathrm{SGD}}, yielding the geometric-tail bound in

3.3 Geometric Tail for Effective Sign Flips
An outer-to-outer sign flip can occur only if the trajectory (i) exits the outer region, (ii) enters a neighborhood of the boundary, and (iii) re-enters the outer region from the opposite side. Assumption 3.4 uniformly controls step (ii) over time, while Assumption 3.3 prevents single update transitions from (i) to (iii). Taken together, these conditions yield a geometric-tail bound on the number of effective outer-to-outer sign flips.
Theorem 3.6 (Sign Lock-in Theorem).
Under Assumptions 3.3–3.4,
define the effective outer-to-outer flip count up to time TT by
KTeff​(ρ)≔∑k≥1𝟏​[σk≤T,sign⁡(wσk)≠sign⁡(wσk−1)],K^{\mathrm{eff}}_{T}(\rho)\coloneqq\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{1}\Bigl[\sigma_{k}\leq T,\ \sign(w_{\sigma_{k}})\neq\sign(w_{\sigma_{k-1}})\Bigr],
which is well-defined since |wσk|≥ρ>0|w_{\sigma_{k}}|\geq\rho>0 on {σk≤T}\{\sigma_{k}\leq T\}.
Let hT:=ℙ[τ1≤T]∈[0,1]h_{T}:={\mathbb{P}}[\tau_{1}\leq T]\in[0,1].
Then for all integers k≥1k\geq 1,
ℙ[τk≤T]≤hTgTk−1,{\mathbb{P}}[\tau_{k}\leq T]\leq h_{T}\,g_{T}^{k-1},
and consequently,
ℙ\ab[KTeff(ρ)≥k]≤hTgTk−1+δupd.{\mathbb{P}}\ab[K^{\mathrm{eff}}_{T}(\rho)\geq k]\leq h_{T}g_{T}^{k-1}+\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}.
Remark 3.7.
The sign lock-in theorem relies solely on Assumptions 3.3 and 3.4, making it optimizer-agnostic.
The uniform re-entry condition of Proposition 3.5 is also not tied to SGD.
SGD variant optimizers follow the same arguments underlying Proposition D.10.
The theorem formalizes the excursion picture: sign changes are initiated only by boundary hits,
and repeated effective outer-to-outer sign flips are exponentially unlikely.
To connect this excursion-based perspective to the most common empirical notion of sign persistence, we next demonstrate a complementary fact: if the sign at time TT differs from the initial sign, then the trajectory must have entered a small Δ\Delta-neighborhood of the origin at least once, provided that one-step updates are bounded.
