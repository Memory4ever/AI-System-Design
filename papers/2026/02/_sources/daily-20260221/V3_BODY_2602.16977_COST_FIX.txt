Our method requires approximately two forward and two backward passes per training step, as we process safety and utility batches separately to enable conditional ablation on safety samples only.
Additionally, RDO introduces overhead at the end of each epoch, though this cost is amortized across training steps and is negligible relative to the per-step cost.
We note that this could be reduced to ∼\sim1 forward and backward pass by batching safety and utility samples together with conditional ablation, which we leave as a future implementation optimization.
Among the baselines, Augmented RT is the most efficient, matching the cost of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) because it only modifies the training data.
DeepRefusal is comparably efficient, as it computes a single refusal feature prior to training and processes mixed batches in a single forward pass.
Notably, our method is ∼\sim5.5×\times more efficient than CAT and LAT, which incur high compute costs due to optimizing unique adversarial latent states across training steps.
Overall, our method strikes a practical balance between efficiency and robustness, incurring only a small constant-factor overhead over SFT while avoiding the prohibitive costs of per-step adversarial optimization, yet still achieving state-of-the-art robustness.
