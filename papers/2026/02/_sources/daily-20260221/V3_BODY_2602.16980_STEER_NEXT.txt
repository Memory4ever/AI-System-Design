PII extraction methodology.
Our full extraction pipeline combines the extraction strategy ℰ\mathcal{E} from §3.1 with the learned intervention.
Given a prompt pp produced by ℰ\mathcal{E}, we apply the steering intervention at the first input token and then decode a continuation conditioned on pp using ℰ\mathcal{E}’s decoding procedure.

4 Evaluation
4.1 Experimental Setup
Datasets.
We focus on two fine-tuning datasets known to contain substantial amounts of PII: Enron (Klimt and Yang, 2004) and TREC (Wu et al., 2006). The Enron dataset, composed of internal company emails made public following investigation into the scandal, includes over 500,000 emails belonging to 150 individuals. The TREC dataset composes over 174,000 emails between World Wide Web Consortium members from 2005.
We consider three classes of PII: email addresses, phone numbers, and personal names.
Models.
We leverage a diverse set of language models with publicly available pretrained checkpoints, including GPT-Neo-125M (Black et al., 2021), PHI-2 (Javaheripi et al., 2023), and LLaMA-3-8B (Dubey and others, 2024). These models span a broad range of pretraining corpora (both open and proprietary) and parameter scales (125M–8B). We finetune each pretrained model on the two datasets described above and use the resulting models as our experimental targets.
Baseline attacks.
We evaluate UniLeak against three representative untargeted PII extraction attacks:
•
Carlini et al. (2021) (BOS) generate texts by initializing generation with the BOS token and randomly sampling new tokens from the top-kk predictions. We set kk to 40.
•
Lukas et al. (2023) generate texts beginning with the empty prompt, and similar to the attack by Carlini et al. (2021), applies top-kk sampling; we also set kk to 40.
•
Keum et al. (2025) (Private Investigator) optimize a set of diverse single-token prompts to maximize PII generation using a surrogate model (GPT-Neo-125M).
Following the evaluation protocol used in these prior works, we generate 200,000 256-token sequences per method.
4.2 Effectiveness of UniLeak
Methodology.
We evaluate UniLeak in extracting PII from language models finetuned on Enron and TREC. We compare the number of unique PII items produced by each attack, which highlights the complementary advantage of ours over existing baselines. Because UniLeak operates by steering model activations during generation, we apply it on top of two existing extraction attacks: Carlini et al. (2021) and Keum et al. (2025).
