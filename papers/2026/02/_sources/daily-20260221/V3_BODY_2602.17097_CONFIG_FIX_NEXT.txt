Appendix D Inference Settings
This section details the inference parameters we used to obtain our results. For audio editing and storytelling (Sections 5.1 and 5.2), we sample with 150 diffusion steps. For our T2A ablation results, we use 100 diffusion steps for all models. For all audio generation tasks, we use Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) with a weight of 3.0, the Karras noise schedule (Karras et al., 2022) and DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2025). We use greedy search for all text generation results.
Appendix E Evaluation Settings
E.1 multiFLAM
multiFLAM evaluates if a generated multi-source audio scene contains all necessary elements. Let there be MM captions, one for each sound source in YY. multiFLAM is calculated as the average probability of a sound corresponding to caption XmX_{m} occurring in scene YY for all m∈Mm\in M. First, we use FLAM to output the probabilities of XmX_{m} for each timestep yt∈Yy_{t}\in Y, which are then aggregated by taking the maximum value over TT.
PFLAM​(Xm,Y)=MAX​(PFLAM​(Xm,yt)|t=1,…​T)P_{\text{FLAM}}(X_{m},Y)=\text{MAX}(P_{\text{FLAM}}(X_{m},y_{t})|t=1,...T)
(13)
We use the maximum instead of mean since the latter unfairly punishes detecting sounds that only occur in small portions of the audio. The average probability across sounds are then calculated following Equation 4, yielding the final result.
E.2 Δ\DeltamultiFLAM
Δ\DeltamultiFLAM evaluates the consistency an editing operation to its original audio. Given the sound to be edited e∈Xe\in X, we calculate the multiFLAM of YY and Y^\hat{Y} using X′=X−{e}X^{\prime}=X-\{e\}.
ΔmultiFLAM​(X′,Y^,Y)=|multiFLAM​(X′,Y)−multiFLAM​(X′,Y^)|\begin{split}\text{$\Delta$multiFLAM}(X^{\prime},\hat{Y},Y)=\\
|\text{multiFLAM}(X^{\prime},Y)-\text{multiFLAM}(X^{\prime},\hat{Y})|\end{split}
(14)
In other words, Δ\DeltamultiFLAM is the change in multiFLAM score for all sounds that should not be edited. Scores closer to 0 indicate better consistency with the original, whereas scores closer to 1.0 indicate more unnecessary changes.
E.3 editFLAM
editFLAM measures whether an audio editing operation itself was performed successfully. Generally, it is calculated as the difference in the FLAM probability between the input audio YY and the predicted audio Y^\hat{Y} (Equation 18). As mentioned in Section 3.3, the exact implementation depends on the task. In this section, we provide the implementation of each task that we evaluate using the metric.
Adding a sound: When adding a sound, the probability of caption XX occurring in Y^\hat{Y} should be higher than the probability of it occurring in YY:
editFLAM-ADD​(X,Y,Y^)=PFLAM​(X,Y^)−PFLAM​(X,Y)\textsc{editFLAM-ADD}(X,Y,\hat{Y})=P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,\hat{Y})-P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,Y)
(15)
A positive score indicates an absolute increase in the probability that the sound was successfully added. A score around 0 indicates that the sound was likely not added. A negative score indicates unintended distortions in the edited audio.
Removing a sound: When removing a sound, the probability of caption XX occurring in Y^\hat{Y} should be lower than the probability of it occurring in YY:
editFLAM-REMOVE​(X,Y,Y^)=PFLAM​(X,Y)−PFLAM​(X,Y^)\textsc{editFLAM-REMOVE}(X,Y,\hat{Y})=P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,Y)-P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,\hat{Y})
(16)
To normalize each task score so that higher is better, we invert the subtraction. A positive score indicates an absolute increase in the probability that the sound was successfully removed. A score of around 0 indicates that the sound was likely not removed. A negative score indicates unintended distortions in the edited audio, which may include making the target sound more prominent.
Changing a sound: One of the most complex operations to evaluate in editing is changing a sound X1X_{1} to X2X_{2}. We formulate this as a combination of two sub-tasks: removing X1X_{1} and adding X2X_{2}.
editFLAM-CHANGE​(X1,X2,Y,Y^)=editFLAM-ADD​(X2,Y,Y^)+editFLAM-REMOVE​(X1,Y,Y^)2\textsc{editFLAM-CHANGE}(X_{1},X_{2},Y,\hat{Y})=\frac{\textsc{editFLAM-ADD}(X_{2},Y,\hat{Y})+\textsc{editFLAM-REMOVE}(X_{1},Y,\hat{Y})}{2}
(17)
The final score is the average of the two sub-task scores. A positive scores indicates that both changes were likely to have occurred successfully. Scores closer to 0 indicate that there was likely no change in the sound. Negative scores indicate unintended distortions in the edited audio.
Changing acoustic level of a sound: When changing the acoustic level of a sound (such as volume or panning) and not the semantics, the probability of caption XX occurring in Y^\hat{Y} should be no different than the probability of it occurring in YY:
editFLAM-Acoustic​(X,Y,Y^)=1.0−|PFLAM​(X,Y^)−PFLAM​(X,Y)|\textsc{editFLAM-Acoustic}(X,Y,\hat{Y})=1.0-|P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,\hat{Y})-P_{\text{FLAM}}(X,Y)|
(18)
To normalize the task score so that higher is better, we compute the 1.0 minus the absolute value in the change in probability. A higher score indicates that it is likely that no semantic changes were made to the sound, while scores closer to 0 indicate significant changes.
