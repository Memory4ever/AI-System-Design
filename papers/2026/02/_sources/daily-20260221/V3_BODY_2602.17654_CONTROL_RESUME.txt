5.4. Ablation Studies
Model Architecture.
Regarding the model architecture, a Siamese encoder consistently outperforms an asymmetric encoder in both training stages, as shown in Tables 1 and 4.
Although an asymmetric encoder with untied parameters may allow the model to better fine tune for each expertise (query and item) (Xie et al., 2022), we observe that keeping the Siamese structure yields slightly better performance (see Table 1).
Hierarchical Item Taxonomy Enrichment.
The empirical results presented in Table 4 show that incorporating taxonomy information improves disambiguation in item category clustering, while more fine grained taxonomy hierarchies introduce noise without commensurate gains in the semantic space of e-commerce search.
Synthetic Query Augmentation.
We keep the proportion of synthetic queries generated based on item features relatively low similar to (Xie et al., 2022) as such queries generally exhibit higher than average lexical similarity to positive items; over representing these pairs would bias the model toward lexical matching.
We observe that injecting synthetic queries at a low ratio improves overall model performance on standard metrics but degrades separability.
However, when synthetic queries are selectively added only for items lacking positive query–item pairs, and this is combined with an enriched item landscape, the model achieves both improved performance and enhanced separability (see Table 1).

Spelling Augmentation of Training Queries.
We explored several strategies to improve the robustness of the model to spelling perturbations as presented in Table 1.
Regularizing between clean queries and their spelling variants is intended to encourage invariance by clustering similar embeddings; however, this approach leads to overfitting to query specific patterns and increased sensitivity to spelling noise. In place substitution similarly yields no measurable benefit.
We conjecture that spelling variants presented without their corresponding clean queries introduce predominantly noise, as they lack a well formed semantic structure from which the model can learn.
In contrast, additive augmentation, as described in Section 4.2, substantially improves the quality of the model without degrading the performance in the clean query set.

Hard Sample Mining.
The threshold k/2k/2 as described in Section 4.4 is chosen during the training of our model, but can be adjusted according to the capacity and performance of the model.
In particular, including the hardest negatives (e.g. items with label 00 in the top 1010) slightly improved performance in our case, in contrast to (Huang et al., 2020), likely because circle loss is more robust to hard negatives than triplet loss, reducing the risk of vanishing gradients or model divergence (Sun et al., 2020).
The definition of "hard positives" can also be adjusted depending on the interpretation of the label 11.
For narrow or niche queries with few relevant items, including label 11 samples helps the model learn the proper relative spacing between queries and items.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce several techniques for training data construction and model optimization that push the boundaries of state of the art small scale retrieval embedding models in the domain specific context of DoorDash.
In particular, the proposed "Mine and Refine" strategy, LLM supervised offline hard positive and negative sample mining with circle loss, accounts for the majority of the performance gains.
The proposed model demonstrates consistent improvements across both offline relevance evaluations of the embedding model and the end to end search pipeline, as well as in online experiments.
