4 Sign Lock-In Enhancement
The sign lock-in theory suggests that effective outer-to-outer sign flips are rare and exhibit a geometric tail. This result naturally raises the following question:
Can sign lock-in preserve a compressible initialization template while maintaining task quality?
To probe this question, we introduce a theory-guided approach that emphasizes the two quantities influencing boundary excursions in Theorem 3.6. Specifically, we aim to (i) reduce the probability of reaching the sign boundary, the initial-hit factor hTh_{T}, and (ii) decrease the probability of returning to the boundary after escaping, the re-entry ratio gTg_{T}. Each component below is designed to control one of these terms, offering a practical method to investigate whether a compressible sign template can be maintained without compromising task quality.
4.1 Theory-driven Heuristics
Beyond describing a naturally emerging phenomenon, sign lock-in can be actively controlled through artificial interventions.

To keep signs lock-in, we describe two lightweight components to control two lock-in parameters.
Compressible sign template.
We begin by specifying a compressible sign template during initialization.
For a layer ll with a weight matrix W(l)∈ℝm×nW^{(l)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}, we create a low-rank template by sampling two factor matrices
G∈ℝm×rG\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times r} and H∈ℝn×rH\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times r}, where r≪min⁡(m,n)r\ll\min(m,n), and then taking the elementwise sign of their product:

Gap Initialization.
To reduce early sign flips caused by weights drifting near zero, we initialize each weight with a margin explicitly away from the origin.
Let σinit>0\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}>0 denote the base initialization scale and define a gap threshold, ainit≔cgap​σinit,cgap>0a_{\mathrm{init}}\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{gap}}\sigma_{\mathrm{init}},c_{\mathrm{gap}}>0,
where cgapc_{\mathrm{gap}} is a user-chosen constant controlling the gap size.
For each entry, we sample z∼𝒩⁡(0,σinit2)z\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^{2}) and reject the draw if |z|<ainit|z|<a_{\mathrm{init}}, repeating until |z|≥ainit|z|\geq a_{\mathrm{init}}.
Let Z(l)∈ℝm×nZ^{(l)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n} be the resulting matrix with entries that are independently and identically distributed according to this rejection-sampling procedure.
Equivalently, each Zi​j(l)Z^{(l)}_{ij} follows a two-sided truncated Gaussian supported on

By construction, this initialization suppresses early excursions into the near-zero region, where sign changes are most likely.

Outer-drift regularization.
Even with a gap at initialization, individual weights may later drift back to zero, where sign changes are most probable.
To discourage such re-entries during early optimization, we introduce a lightweight log-barrier that penalizes small magnitudes.
For a weight matrix W∈ℝm×nW\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}, a gap threshold ainit>0a_{\mathrm{init}}>0, and a numerical stabilizer ϵlb>0\epsilon_{\mathrm{lb}}>0, we define the following:
RLB​(W,ainit,ϵlb)≔1m​n​∑i,jlog⁡(max⁡{1,ainit|Wi​j|+ϵlb}).R_{\mathrm{LB}}(W;a_{\mathrm{init}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{lb}})\\
\coloneqq\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i,j}\log\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{a_{\mathrm{init}}}{|W_{ij}|+\epsilon_{\mathrm{lb}}}\right\}\right).
This penalty is 00 whenever |Wi​j||W_{ij}| is safely outside the near-zero band, i.e., when |Wi​j|+ϵlb≥ainit|W_{ij}|+\epsilon_{\mathrm{lb}}\geq a_{\mathrm{init}}, and it increases smoothly as |Wi​j||W_{ij}| approaches 00.
Let θ\theta denote all model weight parameters, and let ℒtask​(θ){\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{task}}(\theta) be the task loss, e.g., negative log-likelihood. We apply the barrier to a selected set of layers ℳ{\mathcal{M}}, resulting in the time-dependent training objective at the optimization step tt:
ℒtotal​(θ,t)≔ℒtask​(θ)+λ⁡(t)​∑l∈ℳRLB​(W(l),ainit,ϵlb),{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{total}}(\theta;t)\coloneqq{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{task}}(\theta)+\lambda(t)\sum_{l\in{\mathcal{M}}}R_{\mathrm{LB}}(W^{(l)};a_{\mathrm{init}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{lb}}),
where λ⁡(t)≥0\lambda(t)\geq 0 controls the strength of the regularizer.
In practice, we keep λ⁡(t)\lambda(t) constant during an initial warmup phase and then reduce it to 00, so that the penalty primarily influences early dynamics. Overall, this regularizer biases optimization away from the near-zero region after weights have moved into the outer region |Wi​j(l)|≳ainit|W^{(l)}_{ij}|\gtrsim a_{\mathrm{init}}, reducing repeated boundary excursions and helping to preserve the intended sign structure.
Remark 4.1.
The strongest form of this approach enforces signs to match a deterministic template exactly, making sign storage effectively zero. We defer this extreme variant to Appendix E.3 to maintain the main text focus on minimal interventions.
