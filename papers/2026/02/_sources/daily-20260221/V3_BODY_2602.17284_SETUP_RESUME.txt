2.2 Dominating pair for random allocation
A key concept for characterizing the privacy guarantees of an algorithm is that of a dominating pair of distributions (Zhu et al., 2022).
Definition 2.5 (Dominating pair (Zhu et al., 2022)).
Given distributions P1,Q1P_{1},Q_{1} over some domain Ω1\Omega_{1}, and P2,Q2P_{2},Q_{2} over Ω2\Omega_{2}, we say LP1,Q1L_{P_{1},Q_{1}} dominate LP2,Q2L_{P_{2},Q_{2}} if for all κ≥0\kappa\geq 0 we have 𝑯κ(P2‖Q2)≤𝑯κ(P1‖Q1)\boldsymbol{H}_{\kappa}\left(P_{2}~\left\|~Q_{2}\right.\right)\leq\boldsymbol{H}_{\kappa}\left(P_{1}~\left\|~Q_{1}\right.\right).
If δ→M(ε)≤𝑯eε(P‖Q)\vec{\delta}_{M}(\varepsilon)\leq\boldsymbol{H}_{e^{\varepsilon}}\left(P~\left\|~Q\right.\right) for all ε∈ℝ\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}, we say (P,Q)(P,Q) is a dominating pair of distributions for MM in the remove direction. Similarly, if δ→M(ε)≤𝑯eε(P‖Q)\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\delta$}}$}_{M}(\varepsilon)\leq\boldsymbol{H}_{e^{\varepsilon}}\left(P~\left\|~Q\right.\right) for all ε∈ℝ\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}, we say (P,Q)(P,Q) is a dominating pair of distributions for MM in the add direction.
For example, a dominating pair of distributions for the Gaussian mechanism NσN_{\sigma} is simply 𝒩⁡(1,σ2)\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma^{2}) and 𝒩⁡(0,σ2)\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}) (Zhu et al., 2022), so the privacy loss of NσN_{\sigma} is ℓ⁡(x,𝒩⁡(1,σ2),𝒩⁡(0,σ2))=xσ2−12​σ2\ell\left(x;\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma^{2}),\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})\right)=\frac{x}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}} and its PLD is simply 𝒩⁡(12​σ2,1σ2)\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}},\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right).
The notion of dominating pair can be used to define a dominating randomizer, which captures the privacy guarantees of a tt-step algorithm independently of its algorithmic adaptive properties.
Definition 2.6.
[Dominating randomizer]
Let R:{⊥,∗}→𝒴R:\{\bot,*\}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y} be a randomizer and let PP and QQ denote its output distributions on ∗* and ⊥\bot, respectively. We say that a tt-step algorithm M:𝒳∗×𝒴<t→𝒴M\colon\mathcal{X}^{*}\times\mathcal{Y}^{<t}\to\mathcal{Y} is dominated by randomizer RR if (P,Q)(P,Q) are a dominating pair of distributions for M⁡(⋅,⋅)M(\cdot,\cdot) w.r.t. the remove direction over all indexes i∈[t]i\in[t] and input partial views 𝒗1:i−1\boldsymbol{v}_{1:i-1}.
When there exists a pair of datasets 𝒔≃𝒔′\boldsymbol{s}\simeq\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime} and a view 𝒗∈𝒴∗\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Y}^{*} such that P=M⁡(𝒔,𝒗)P=M(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{v}), Q=M⁡(𝒔′,𝒗)Q=M(\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{v}) then the privacy profile of RR is identical to that of MM.
The definition of the random allocation scheme naturally extends to the case where the internal algorithm is a randomizer, 𝒜t,k​(R):{∗,⊥}→𝒴t\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(R\right):\{*,\bot\}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{t}, and the domination of RR extends to this scheme, as formalized in the next claim.
Lemma 2.7 (Allocation reduction to randomizer (Feldman and Shenfeld, 2025)).
Given t∈ℕt\in\mathbb{N}; k∈[t]k\in[t] and an algorithm MM dominated by a randomizer RR, we have δ→𝒜t,k​(M)​(ε)≤δ→𝒜t,k​(R)​(ε)\vec{\delta}_{\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(M\right)}(\varepsilon)\leq\vec{\delta}_{\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(R\right)}(\varepsilon) and δ→𝒜t,k​(M)​(ε)≤δ→𝒜t,k​(R)​(ε)\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\delta$}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(M\right)}(\varepsilon)\leq\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\delta$}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(R\right)}(\varepsilon) for all ε∈ℝ\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}.
A special case of this result for Gaussian noise addition can also be found in (Chua et al., 2024a; Choquette-Choo et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025). The domination given by this reduction is tight for many natural choices of MM whenever RR is the tightly dominating randomizer for MM (see (Feldman and Shenfeld, 2025) for a more detailed discussion).
Random allocation of a randomizer with k>1k>1 can be further reduced to a composition of single allocations.
Lemma 2.8 (Reduction to a single allocation (Feldman and Shenfeld, 2025)).
For any k∈ℕk\in\mathbb{N}, ε>0\varepsilon>0 we have δ𝒜t,k​(R)​(ε)≤δ𝒜⌊t/k⌋​(R)⊗k​(ε)\delta_{\mathcal{A}_{t,k}\left(R\right)}(\varepsilon)\leq\delta_{\mathcal{A}_{\lfloor t/k\rfloor}\left(R\right)}^{\otimes k}(\varepsilon) for all ε∈ℝ\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}, where ⊗k{\otimes k} denotes the composition of kk runs of the algorithm or scheme which in our case is 𝒜⌊t/k⌋​(R)\mathcal{A}_{\lfloor t/k\rfloor}\left(R\right).
While this reduction is lossy, in particular when ⌊t/k⌋\lfloor t/k\rfloor is relatively small, we remark that an analogous reduction for Poisson sampling is exact. Namely, sampling independently at the rate of k/tk/t for tt steps is equivalent to sampling at the rate of k/tk/t for t/kt/k steps (which is the analog of 11-out-of-t/kt/k random allocation) composed kk times. Thus, ignoring the rounding error (in the ⌊t/k⌋\lfloor t/k\rfloor operation), this reduction implies that the relationship between kk-out-of-tt random allocation and tt rounds of k/tk/t-rate Poisson subsampling is the same as the relationship between 11-out-of-t/kt/k random allocation and t/kt/k rounds of 11-out-of-t/kt/k Poisson subsampling.
The dominating pair of distributions for random allocation with k=1k=1 has a simple explicit form (Feldman and Shenfeld, 2025) (for the special case of Gaussian noise addition this result can also be found in (Chua et al., 2024a; Dong et al., 2025)).
Claim 2.9 (Dominating pair of distributions for random allocation (Feldman and Shenfeld, 2025)).

Then we have that 𝒜t​(M,⊥)\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(M;\bot\right) is distributed as QtQ^{t} and 𝒜t​(M,∗)\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(M;*\right) is distributed as P¯t\bar{P}_{t}, which implies that for any ε>0\varepsilon>0
δ→𝒜t​(M)(ε)≤δ→𝒜t​(R)(ε)=𝑯eε(P¯t‖Qt)andδ→𝒜t​(M)(ε)≤δ→𝒜t​(R)(ε)=𝑯eε(Qt‖P¯t).\vec{\delta}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(M\right)}(\varepsilon)\leq\vec{\delta}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(R\right)}(\varepsilon)=\boldsymbol{H}_{e^{\varepsilon}}\left(\bar{P}_{t}~\left\|~Q^{t}\right.\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\delta$}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(M\right)}(\varepsilon)\leq\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\delta$}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(R\right)}(\varepsilon)=\boldsymbol{H}_{e^{\varepsilon}}\left(Q^{t}~\left\|~\bar{P}_{t}\right.\right).
Combining these results, the analysis of general algorithms with random allocation scheme reduces to the analysis of the (composition of) random allocation of a single pair of distributions with a single allocation, which we do in the next sections.
