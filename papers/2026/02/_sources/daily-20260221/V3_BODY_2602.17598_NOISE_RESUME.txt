4.4 Noise Robustness
Under multi-talker babble noise (MUSAN, 0–15 dB SNR), our Whisper-based cascades degrade substantially less than the E2E models tested.
At 0 dB, Cascade-S loses 0.5–4.2% accuracy across text-sufficient tasks, while E2E models show substantially larger drops on SST-2 and CSQA (5.9–12.7%; Figure 3).
This asymmetry is expected: Whisper, trained on 680,000 hours of diverse audio, acts as a noise-robust front-end that absorbs acoustic degradation before it reaches the LLM.
Gemini degrades fastest despite the best clean accuracy: SST-2 drops 10.210.2% at 0 dB (90.4% →\to 80.3%) vs. Cascade-S’s 2.6% drop.
This means that selecting a system based on clean-condition accuracy can reverse under deployment noise: a team choosing Gemini over Cascade-S for SST-2 would see a 7.6 percentage-point reversal at 0 dB (from ++2.0% clean advantage to −-5.6% disadvantage), invisible in standard benchmarks.

5 Mechanistic Evidence
We now move from behavioral observation to mechanistic analysis, building a four-level case: what is encoded (probing), how it emerges (logit lens), whether the emergent text carries task-relevant information (functional text test), and whether it is causally necessary (LEACE erasure).
5.1 Layer-wise Probing
Figure 4 shows acoustic and text probe results across layers for Qwen2-Audio, Ultravox, and Whisper.
Whisper serves as a control: as a dedicated ASR encoder, it provides a reference for how a model that is explicitly trained to convert speech to text organizes acoustic and textual information across layers.
Whisper’s CTC text decodability shows a non-monotonic pattern, dipping to 0.16 at layer 16 before recovering to 0.26 by layer 31, consistent with an encoder bottleneck where mid-layers focus on acoustic processing and later layers build text representations.
Acoustic information is retained but restructured.
Energy R2R^{2} remains high throughout: 0.92 →\to 0.86 for Qwen2-Audio, 0.86 →\to 0.83 for Ultravox, and 0.99 →\to 0.96 for Whisper.
However, pitch R2R^{2} declines more steeply (Qwen2-Audio: 0.63 →\to 0.48; Ultravox: 0.37 →\to 0.31), suggesting finer-grained acoustic features are progressively overwritten as the model builds text representations.
Text decodability reveals divergent architectures.
Ultravox shows progressive text emergence (CTC: 0.03 →\to 0.20), while Qwen2-Audio maintains stable text decodability (0.48–0.51) through layers 0–20 before a sharp decline in the final layers (0.43 at L24, 0.29 at L31); its cross-attention encoder already produces text-rich representations that the LLM reorganizes in its final layers rather than building from scratch.
This inversion reveals that Qwen2-Audio’s encoder has already solved the speech-to-text problem before the LLM layers begin; the LLM’s role is not to build text but to reason over it, mirroring a cascade’s division of labor.
The acoustic compression gradient maps onto behavioral agreement: Qwen2-Audio (steepest pitch decline, 0.63 →\to 0.48) shows the lowest κ\kappa with its cascade; Ultravox (milder decline, 0.37 →\to 0.31) shows higher κ\kappa.

5.2 Logit Lens: Visualizing Text Emergence
While probing requires an external classifier, the logit lens [26, 27] reveals text emergence through the model’s own unembedding matrix, requiring no training.
Figure 5 and Table 5 show bag-of-tokens precision (fraction of greedily decoded tokens appearing in the reference transcript) by layer.
Both models build text representations, but the degree differs markedly.
