4.3 The circuit monotonicity property and its impact on minimality
To address the issue of algorithms converging to “bad” local minima, we identify a key property of the faithfulness predicate Φ\Phi with crucial implications for stronger minimal subsets — monotonicity:
Definition 7.
We say that a circuit faithfulness predicate Φ\Phi is monotonic iff for any C⊆C′⊆GC\subseteq C^{\prime}\subseteq G it holds that if Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) is true, then Φ⁡(C′,G)\Phi(C^{\prime},G) is true.
Intuitively, monotonicity means that once Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) holds for a circuit CC (i.e., it is “faithful”), it will keep holding as elements are added. In other words, enlarging the circuit never breaks faithfulness. This property is essential for Algorithm 1, as it underpins the stronger minimality guarantee:
Proposition 4.
If Φ\Phi is monotonic, then for any model fGf_{G}, Algorithm 1 converges to a subset-minimal circuit CC concerning Φ\Phi.
The condition of monotonicity. Interestingly, we establish a novel connection between the guarantees on the input and patching domains outlined in Section 3 and the monotonic behavior of Φ\Phi:
Proposition 5.
Let Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) denote validating whether CC is input-robust concerning ⟨fG,𝒵⟩\langle f_{G},\mathcal{Z}\rangle (Def. 1), and simultaneously patching-robust concerning ⟨fG,𝒵′⟩\langle f_{G},\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}\rangle (Def. 2). Then if 𝒵⊆𝒵′\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{Z^{\prime}} and ℋG​(𝒵′)\mathcal{H}_{G}(\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}) is closed under concatenation, Φ\Phi is monotonic.
Intuitively, Proposition 5 shows that if the patching domain 𝒵′\mathcal{Z}^{\prime} subsumes the input domain 𝒵\mathcal{Z}, and the activation space ℋG​(𝒵′)\mathcal{H}_{G}(\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}) is closed under concatenation, i.e., concatenating any two partial activations remains within ℋG​(𝒵′)\mathcal{H}_{G}(\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}) — then the faithfulness predicate Φ\Phi is monotonic. This introduces a new class of evaluation conditions that are monotonic by construction, yielding stronger minimal circuits.
Proposition 6.
If the condition Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) is set to validating whether CC is input-robust concerning ⟨fG,𝒵⟩\langle f_{G},\mathcal{Z}\rangle (Def. 1), and also patching-robust with respect to ⟨fG,𝒵′⟩\langle f_{G},\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}\rangle (Def. 2), then if 𝒵⊆𝒵′\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{Z^{\prime}} and ℋG​(𝒵′)\mathcal{H}_{G}(\mathcal{Z^{\prime}}) is closed under concatenation, Algorithm 1 converges to a subset-minimal circuit.
4.4 From Subset-Minimal Circuits to Cardinally-Minimal Circuits
Although the monotonicity of Φ\Phi provides a stronger guarantee of subset-minimality, it still does not ensure convergence to the globally minimal circuit (i.e., a cardinally-minimal circuit). A naive approach to obtain such circuits is to enumerate all C⊆GC\subseteq G, verify Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G), and choose the one with the lowest cardinality |C||C|, but this quickly becomes intractable even for modestly sized graphs.
Exploiting circuit blocking-set duality for efficient approximation of cardinally-minimal circuits. In this subsection, we leverage the idea that neural networks often contain small “circuit blocking-sets” — subgraphs C′⊆GC^{\prime}\subseteq G whose altered activations break the faithfulness of any circuit CC that excludes them. We prove a duality between circuits (under monotone faithfulness predicates) and these blocking-sets, enabling a new algorithmic construction that approximates — and sometimes exactly recovers — cardinally minimal circuits far more efficiently than naive search. Formally, for fGf_{G} and Φ\Phi, a circuit blocking-set is any C′⊆GC^{\prime}\subseteq G such that Φ⁡(C∖C′,G)\Phi(C\setminus C^{\prime},G) fails for all C⊆GC\subseteq G. This yields a duality grounded in a minimum-hitting-set (MHS) relation between circuits and blocking-sets:
Proposition 7.
Given some model fGf_{G}, and a monotonic predicate Φ\Phi, the MHS of all circuit blocking-sets concerning Φ\Phi is a cardinally minimal circuit CC for which Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) is true. Moreover, the MHS of all circuits C⊆GC\subseteq G for which Φ⁡(C,G)\Phi(C,G) is true, is a cardinally minimal blocking-set w.r.t Φ\Phi.
