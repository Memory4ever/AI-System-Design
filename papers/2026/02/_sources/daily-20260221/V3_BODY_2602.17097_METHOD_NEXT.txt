4.1 Architecture
AudioChat consists of 2 modules: a continuous audio tokenizer that converts the waveform into latent embeddings and a multi-modal LLM. An overview of this architecture is shown in Figure 3, whereas Section 4.2 further details the training procedure for the multi-modal LLM.
Audio Tokenizer: We use a continuous audio tokenizer, similar to DAC-VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2014; Kumar et al., 2023; Polyak et al., 2024), to convert an input waveform into continuous latent embeddings and vice versa. It receives stereo 48kHz audio as input and encodes it into a 40Hz latent embedding. The audio tokenizer is frozen during training. More details can be found in Appendix B.1.
Multi-modal Language Model: We introduce a novel architecture for injecting multi-modal information into text-only LLMs, which we designate as the Self-Cascaded Transformer (SCT), inspired by recent advances in speech representation learning (Liu et al., 2025a; Chung et al., 2021). SCT sequentially splits the Transformer layers among two multi-modal tasks: understanding and generation. Given a KK-layer Transformer, the first UU layers are trained only by a LM head (Section X) for understanding. The remaining K−UK-U layers are also optimized by the diffusion objective (Section Y) for generation. The text embeddings, understanding layers, and LM head directly correspond to and are initialized from a base text-only LLM. This design is significantly simpler and more flexible than previous proposed approaches like MoT (Liang et al., 2025), which require copying the entire base LLM’s parameters and the use of multiple multi-modal representations (Deng et al., 2025). We empirically found that the SCT design significantly improved audio generation quality.
4.2 Audio Transfusion Forcing
We propose Audio Transfusion Forcing, a method to equip text-only LLMs with the ability to generate high-fidelity audio through continuous latent tokens within multi-turn interactions. Audio Transfusion Forcing consists of two key components: Transfusion and Diffusion Forcing.
Transfusion: Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2025) was originally proposed in computer vision to develop a model that can jointly perform generation and understanding tasks. It consists of two objectives: causal language modeling (ℒLM\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{LM}}) and diffusion (ℒDDPM\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{DDPM}}).
ℒtransfusion=ℒLM+λ​ℒDDPM\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{transfusion}}=\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{LM}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{DDPM}}
(7)
Where λ\lambda is a weighting term to control for the variable dynamic range between the loss functions. Our use of Transfusion differs in two respects: 1.) we are the first to apply it to audio and 2.) in addition to joint generation/understanding, we use it to train a model that can decompose complex tasks via text-to-text reasoning.
Language Modeling: We adopt the framework of causal language modeling (Brown et al., 2020) to perform CoT reasoning conditioned on the user’s input text. Given an LL-length input sequence of discrete text tokens X=(xl|l=1,…,L)X=(x_{l}|l=1,...,L), the LM is trained to model the product of conditional probabilities by optimizing the negative log-likelihood of P(xi|x1:i−1)P(x_{i}|x_{1:{i-1}}):
ℒLM=−log∏i=0lP(xi|x1:i−1)\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{LM}}=-\log\prod^{l}_{i=0}P(x_{i}|x_{1:{i-1}})
(8)
Which yields the causal language modeling loss ℒLM\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{LM}} to be used in stochastic gradient descent.
Diffusion: Diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) are a class of generative models that learn to iteratively reverse a noise addition process. Briefly, the forward process of diffusion is defined as a first-order Markov chain that gradually corrupts an input 𝐲\mathbf{y} with Gaussian noise over TT timesteps, yielding 𝐲1,𝐲2​…​𝐲T\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}...\mathbf{y}_{T}. The diffusion model thus learns the reverse process P⁡(𝐲t−1|𝐲t)P(\mathbf{y}_{t-1}|\mathbf{y}_{t}) by denoising the data at each timestep, where t∈Tt\in T is a randomly sampled timestep during training. In practice, this is accomplished by predicting the noise ϵ\epsilon added to yy at timestep tt, such that
ℒDDPM=‖ϵ−ϵθ​(𝐲t,t,𝐱)‖2\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{DDPM}}=||\epsilon-\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{y}_{t},t,\mathbf{x})||^{2}
(9)
Where ϵθ\epsilon_{\theta} is the predicted noise as a function of yty_{t}, tt, and additional context 𝐱\mathbf{x}, parameterized by the diffusion model. The overall optimization objective is therefore the mean-squared error between added noise ϵ\epsilon and the predicted noise ϵθ​(𝐲t,t,𝐱)\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{y}_{t},t,\mathbf{x}). Equation 9 can then be generalized to multi-turn training for audio editing:
ℒDDPM_multi=‖ϵ−ϵθ​(𝐲ts,t,𝐱,𝐲0​…​𝐲s−1)‖2\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{DDPM\_multi}}=||\epsilon-\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{y}_{t}^{s},t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{0}...\mathbf{y}^{s-1})||^{2}
(10)
Here, SS is the total number of conversation turns, 𝐲ts\mathbf{y}_{t}^{s} is the noised latent of the target audio at turn ss and 𝐲0​…​𝐲s−1\mathbf{y}^{0}...\mathbf{y}^{s-1} are the clean audio latents from the previous turns.
Diffusion Forcing: One challenge of applying the above diffusion formulation (Equation 10) in the audio editing task is the extremely high correlation between the latent representations of the input audio context 𝐲0​…​𝐲s−1\mathbf{y}^{0}...\mathbf{y}^{s-1} and the noisy target audio 𝐲ts\mathbf{y}_{t}^{s}. This makes the training objective a near-trivial task, since the model can just simply copy the input latents, leading to poor generalization. While this training problem can be addressed by also noising the input audio (yielding 𝐲t0​…​𝐲ts−1\mathbf{y}_{t}^{0}...\mathbf{y}_{t}^{s-1}), this leads to a spurious correlation where the timestep tt is always the same for all SS, breaking the inference process when t0​…​ts−1=0t_{0}...t_{s-1}=0 (no noise) and ts=Tt_{s}=T (full noise). To address this, we propose using Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024a), originally proposed for auto-regressive diffusion models, to independently noise 𝐲0​…​𝐲s−1\mathbf{y}^{0}...\mathbf{y}^{s-1} and 𝐲s\mathbf{y}^{s}. Specifically, we independently sample a different timestep tst_{s} for each generation turn in SS. Finally, this yields the overall audio generation objective by modifying Equation 10:
ℒDDPM_Force=‖ϵ−ϵθ​(𝐲ts,t,𝐱,𝐲t00​…​𝐲ts−1s−1)‖2\mathcal{L}_{\textsc{DDPM\_Force}}=||\epsilon-\epsilon_{\theta}(\mathbf{y}_{t}^{s},t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{0}_{t_{0}}...\mathbf{y}_{t_{s-1}}^{s-1})||^{2}
(11)
Diffusion Forcing forces the model to perform audio generation with a variable amount of conditional audio information at each training step, mitigating both training collapse and train-test mismatches.
This technique also allows us to simultaneously train the diffusion model with an arbitrary number of editing turns for each sample in parallel.
