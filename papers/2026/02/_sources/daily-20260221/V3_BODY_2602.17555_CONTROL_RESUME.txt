Applying GRPO alone yields moderate improvements, but combining it with our EVSG leads to significant gains across all metrics. 4.4 Qualitative Analysis
Fig. 4 provides a visual example. It shows that our method improves object and event relation modeling, enhances event localization, and reduces hallucinations during multi-step reasoning.
In this example, previous methods incorrectly interpret the scene as the man performing a dynamic warm-up, leading to the wrong answer (B). In contrast, our EVSG-enhanced model correctly identifies the detailed temporal sequence: the man first performs sit-ups on the bench, then gets off, folds a white towel on the ground, and finally begins rolling crunches with the ab wheel. By explicitly grounding these actions in the temporal context, our method produces the correct answer (C) with precise temporal localization ([0-49.1s]). This qualitative case illustrates that our method allows the model to better capture causal and sequential dependencies between events, yielding more consistent reasoning and alleviating hallucinations caused by implicit event relations.
5 Conclusion
This work introduces GraphThinker, a MLLM post-training method integrated with explicit event-level graphs for video reasoning, effectively reducing hallucinations during multi-step reasoning. To explicitly model intra-event and inter-event relations without manual annotations, we propose an Event-based Video Scene Graph (EVSG) generated by the MLLM itself. For better leveraging the event-level graph for video reasoning, we integrate it into the GRPO post-training framework and design a visual attention reward to effectively utilize EVSG and high-value visual cues. Our method shows strong effectiveness and achieves the state-of-the-art on Rextime and VidHalluc benchmarks.
