In Figure 3, we showcase a real-data example on the Air Quality dataset with a distribution shift between the training and test sets due to time-dependent effects.

While the results above guarantee a strict improvement at every nonstationary λ\lambda and require no distributional assumptions, they do not quantify the magnitude of the gain, i.e., how much smaller R𝗌𝖽⋆​(λ)R_{\mathsf{sd}}^{\star}(\lambda) can be than R⁡(λ)R(\lambda).
In the next section, we consider standard distributional assumptions in the proportional-asymptotics literature and derive deterministic equivalents for these risks and their improvements as explicit functions of the problem parameters.
3 Proportional Asymptotic Results
We now refine the structural identities from Section 2 by deriving deterministic limits for the optimal SD risk R𝗌𝖽⋆​(λ)R_{\mathsf{sd}}^{\star}(\lambda) and the optimal mixing weight ξ⋆​(λ)\xi^{\star}(\lambda) in the proportional asymptotics regime in which n,p→∞n,p\to\infty with p/n→γ∈(0,∞)p/n\to\gamma\in(0,\infty).
Our goals are to (i) obtain computable asymptotic expressions for R𝗌𝖽⋆​(λ)R_{\mathsf{sd}}^{\star}(\lambda) and its gain over the teacher risk R⁡(λ)R(\lambda), and (ii) quantify how these quantities depend on the aspect ratio γ\gamma, the signal-to-noise ratio snr, and signal–covariance alignment (to be defined below).
