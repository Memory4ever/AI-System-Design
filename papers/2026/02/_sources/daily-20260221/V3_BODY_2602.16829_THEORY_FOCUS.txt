1 Supplementary Derivation: Two-Timescale Gap
Below we work out the gap in closed form for the two-timescale learner, show that it is strictly positive whenever the timescales differ, and derive the peak magnitude.
1.1 Model definition
A learner tracks an environment state s∈{0,1}s\in\{0,1\} via two channels, each updated by an exponential moving average:
xfast​(t+1)\displaystyle x_{\text{fast}}(t+1)
=xfast​(t)+αfast⋅(o⁡(t)−xfast​(t))\displaystyle=x_{\text{fast}}(t)+\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot\bigl(o(t)-x_{\text{fast}}(t)\bigr)
(1)
xslow​(t+1)\displaystyle x_{\text{slow}}(t+1)
=xslow​(t)+αslow⋅(s⁡(t)−xslow​(t))\displaystyle=x_{\text{slow}}(t)+\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot\bigl(s(t)-x_{\text{slow}}(t)\bigr)
(2)
where o⁡(t)o(t) is the observed feedback signal (equal to s⁡(t)s(t) with probability 1−ε1-\varepsilon and 1−s⁡(t)1-s(t) with probability ε\varepsilon) and 0<αslow<αfast≤10<\alpha_{\text{slow}}<\alpha_{\text{fast}}\leq 1. In the continuous-time limit, these become exponential decay processes with rates αfast\alpha_{\text{fast}} and αslow\alpha_{\text{slow}} respectively.
1.2 Gap dynamics after a state change
Consider a state reversal of magnitude Δ\Delta at time t=0t=0. Both channels begin with the same initial displacement Δ\Delta from the new true state. In expectation, the residual error of each channel decays exponentially:
𝔼​[errorfast​(t)]\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\text{error}_{\text{fast}}(t)]
=Δ⋅e−αfast⋅t\displaystyle=\Delta\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t}
(3)
𝔼​[errorslow​(t)]\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\text{error}_{\text{slow}}(t)]
=Δ⋅e−αslow⋅t\displaystyle=\Delta\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t}
(4)
The feedback–truth gap is the difference in alignment between the two channels (equivalently, the difference in residual errors):
gap(t)=Δ⋅(e−αslow⋅t−e−αfast⋅t)\text{gap}(t)=\Delta\cdot\bigl(e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t}-e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t}\bigr)
(5)
1.3 Proof of strict positivity
Proposition 1. If αfast>αslow>0\alpha_{\text{fast}}>\alpha_{\text{slow}}>0, then gap​(t)>0\text{gap}(t)>0 for all t>0t>0.
Proof. Since αfast>αslow\alpha_{\text{fast}}>\alpha_{\text{slow}}, we have αfast⋅t>αslow⋅t\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t>\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t for all t>0t>0. Because f⁡(x)=e−xf(x)=e^{-x} is strictly decreasing, e−αfast⋅t<e−αslow⋅te^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t}<e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t}. Therefore e−αslow⋅t−e−αfast⋅t>0e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t}-e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t}>0, and since Δ>0\Delta>0, gap​(t)>0\text{gap}(t)>0. □\square
Corollary (Counterfactual). When αfast=αslow\alpha_{\text{fast}}=\alpha_{\text{slow}}, gap​(t)=0\text{gap}(t)=0 for all tt, regardless of noise level ε\varepsilon.
1.4 Peak timing
Differentiating Eq. (5) with respect to tt and setting the derivative to zero:
dd​tgap(t)=Δ⋅(−αslow⋅e−αslow⋅t+αfast⋅e−αfast⋅t)=0\frac{d}{dt}\,\text{gap}(t)=\Delta\cdot\bigl(-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t}+\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t}\bigr)=0
(6)
This yields:
αfast⋅e−αfast⋅t∗=αslow⋅e−αslow⋅t∗\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t^{*}}=\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t^{*}}
(7)
Taking logarithms:
ln⁡(αfastαslow)=(αfast−αslow)⋅t∗\ln\!\left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{fast}}}{\alpha_{\text{slow}}}\right)=(\alpha_{\text{fast}}-\alpha_{\text{slow}})\cdot t^{*}
(8)
Defining the timescale ratio r=αfast/αslowr=\alpha_{\text{fast}}/\alpha_{\text{slow}}:
t∗=ln⁡rαslow⋅(r−1)t^{*}=\frac{\ln r}{\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot(r-1)}
(9)
1.5 Peak magnitude
Substituting t∗t^{*} back into Eq. (5):
e−αslow⋅t∗\displaystyle e^{-\alpha_{\text{slow}}\cdot t^{*}}
=e−lnr/(r−1)=r−1/(r−1)\displaystyle=e^{-\ln r/(r-1)}=r^{-1/(r-1)}
(10)
e−αfast⋅t∗\displaystyle e^{-\alpha_{\text{fast}}\cdot t^{*}}
=e−r⋅lnr/(r−1)=r−r/(r−1)\displaystyle=e^{-r\cdot\ln r/(r-1)}=r^{-r/(r-1)}
(11)
Therefore:
gapmax=Δ⋅(r−1/(r−1)−r−r/(r−1))\text{gap}_{\max}=\Delta\cdot\bigl(r^{-1/(r-1)}-r^{-r/(r-1)}\bigr)
(12)
This can be simplified by noting that r−r/(r−1)=r−1/(r−1)⋅r−1r^{-r/(r-1)}=r^{-1/(r-1)}\cdot r^{-1}:
gapmax=Δ⋅r−1/(r−1)⋅(1−1r)\text{gap}_{\max}=\Delta\cdot r^{-1/(r-1)}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)
(13)
1.6 Limiting behavior
Large timescale mismatch (r→∞r\to\infty): As r→∞r\to\infty, r−1/(r−1)→1r^{-1/(r-1)}\to 1 and (1−1/r)→1(1-1/r)\to 1, so gapmax→Δ\text{gap}_{\max}\to\Delta. The peak gap approaches the full magnitude of the state change.
Matched timescales (r→1+r\to 1^{+}): Writing r=1+ϵr=1+\epsilon and expanding:
r−1/(r−1)=(1+ϵ)−1/ϵ→e−1as ϵ→0r^{-1/(r-1)}=(1+\epsilon)^{-1/\epsilon}\to e^{-1}\quad\text{as }\epsilon\to 0
(14)
while (1−1/r)=ϵ/(1+ϵ)→0(1-1/r)=\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)\to 0. Thus gapmax→0\text{gap}_{\max}\to 0, confirming that the gap vanishes continuously as timescales converge.
1.7 Cumulative gap
The cumulative positive gap, which corresponds to the AUGpos\text{AUG}_{\text{pos}} metric used in the main text, integrates Eq. (5) over the post-reversal period:
AUGpos=∫0Tgap​(t)​𝑑t=Δ⋅(1αslow−1αfast)⋅(1−residual​(T))\text{AUG}_{\text{pos}}=\int_{0}^{T}\text{gap}(t)\,dt=\Delta\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{\text{slow}}}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\text{fast}}}\right)\cdot\bigl(1-\text{residual}(T)\bigr)
(15)
where the residual term vanishes as T→∞T\to\infty. In the infinite-horizon limit:
AUGpos=Δ⋅r−1αfast\text{AUG}_{\text{pos}}=\Delta\cdot\frac{r-1}{\alpha_{\text{fast}}}
(16)
This confirms that cumulative over-commitment scales linearly with timescale ratio and inversely with the fast learning rate.

2.1.2 Architecture details
Dense baseline. Two hidden layers (128 and 64 units), ReLU, softmax output. Trained with Adam at lr=0.001\text{lr}=0.001, batch size 32, for 50 epochs. Nothing fancy.
Sparse-residual (ResEx). The first hidden layer is replaced by a residual block with a fixed sparse branch:
h1=x+α⋅σ⁡(Wsparse⋅x)h_{1}=x+\alpha\cdot\sigma(W_{\text{sparse}}\cdot x)
(17)
WsparseW_{\text{sparse}} is an expander-graph adjacency matrix – about 22% dense, with d=3d=3 random neighbours per node. When α=0\alpha=0 the block is a pass-through; turning α\alpha up lets the sparse branch reshape the representation. The point is to have a single knob that smoothly dials memorization capacity.
Controls. Three architectures isolate individual ingredients:
•
Dense+LS – the dense baseline plus label smoothing (ϵ=0.1\epsilon=0.1)
•
Dense+Residual – dense with an identity shortcut but no sparse bottleneck
•
Dense+StrongReg – dense with aggressive L2L_{2} (λ=0.01\lambda=0.01)
2.1.3 Memorization metric definitions
The generalization gap at epoch tt is simply training accuracy minus validation accuracy:
gap​(t)=acctrain​(t)−accval​(t)\text{gap}(t)=\text{acc}_{\text{train}}(t)-\text{acc}_{\text{val}}(t)
(18)
We then define two summary statistics. The first, AUGnorm\text{AUG}_{\text{norm}}, captures cumulative memorization pressure:
AUGnorm=1T​∑t=1Tmax⁡(0,gap​(t))\text{AUG}_{\text{norm}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max(0,\text{gap}(t))
(19)
where TT is the total number of epochs – so AUGnorm\text{AUG}_{\text{norm}} is an average over the whole run, not just the tail.
The second, T∗T^{*}, marks onset: the first epoch where the gap exceeds τ=0.05\tau=0.05 for three epochs running. If that never happens, we set T∗=TT^{*}=T (right-censored). Both metrics are computed per seed and averaged.
2.1.4 Alpha-grid causal intervention
The logic is straightforward. If the sparse branch really controls memorization, then increasing α\alpha should push both AUGnorm\text{AUG}_{\text{norm}} and T∗T^{*} up monotonically – but test accuracy should peak somewhere in the middle, because at high α\alpha the bottleneck starts hurting useful capacity too. We swept α∈{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.0}\alpha\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.0\} across all five benchmarks (10 seeds each, 200 runs). Spearman ρ\rho between α\alpha and the group-median metric was 1.0 for both AUGnorm\text{AUG}_{\text{norm}} and T∗T^{*} (p<10−3p<10^{-3}): perfect monotonic control. Test accuracy peaked at α=0.25\alpha=0.25, exactly the kind of inverted-U you would expect from a capacity–memorization tradeoff.
