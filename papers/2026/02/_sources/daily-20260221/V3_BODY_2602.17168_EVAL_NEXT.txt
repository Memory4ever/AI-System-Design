VI Experiments
VI-A Experimental Setup
Victim Models.
We follow BadCLIP [14] and take CLIP RN50 [26] as the clean pretrained model (trained on ∼\sim400M image-text pairs).
To test cross-model generalization, we additionally evaluate CLIP ViT-B/32 [26], ALBEF [3], FLAVA [2], and UniCL [29].
Datasets.
We poison 500k CC3M pairs [63] by injecting the target label “banana” into 1,500 samples.
Evaluation covers: (1) image-text retrieval on COCO [64] and SBU [65];
(2) zero-shot classification on ImageNet-1K [66], CIFAR-100 [67], and SVHN [68];
and (3) robustness under distribution shift on ImageNet-Sketch/V2/A/R [69, 70, 71, 72].
For defense baselines, we also sample CC3M subsets to align each method’s hyperparameters.
Attacks.
We compare four attack families:
(1) single-modal attacks that backdoor only one modality (e.g., BadNets [31], Blended [32]/SIG [34]/WaNet [33], SSBA [35]);
(2) multi-modal attacks that jointly tamper with vision and text (e.g., TrojVQA [57], MABA [38], VLTrojan [6]);
(3) poisoned-encoder attacks that directly corrupt CLIP’s encoder (e.g., BadEncoder [55], INACTIVE [37]); and
(4) CLIP-series attacks tailored to CLIP-like multimodal models (mmPoison [36], BadCLIP [14]).
Full trigger designs are deferred to the App. Sec. D.
Defenses.
We evaluate four defense categories:
(1) fine-tuning defenses that mitigate backdoors by retraining on clean data (FT [48], CleanCLIP [17], CleanerCLIP [18], TSC [49]);
(2) model-level detection methods that try to flag compromised models (DECREE [45], MM-BD [44], SEER [47]);
(3) inference-time defenses that identify or suppress triggered queries at deployment (STRIP [50], SCALE-UP [51], TeCo [52], BDetCLIP [53], DEDE [54]); and
(4) pre-training defenses, including data filtering (DAO [1], VDC [40], PSBD [39]) and trigger-avoidance strategies (ABL [41], UBT [15], RoCLIP [42], SafeCLIP [43]).
Algorithmic details and tuning protocols appear in the App. Sec. E.
Metrics.
We report Clean Accuracy (CA) and Attack Success Rate (ASR) as primary indicators of clean-task utility and backdoor effectiveness.
For linear-probe transfer, we use Misclassification Rate (MCR) and ASR-Proxy when the target class is absent but a semantically related superclass exists; otherwise, we report Dominant ASR (D-ASR), i.e., the fraction of triggered samples collapsing to the most frequent prediction.
For image-text retrieval, we measure R@1/R@10 on clean queries and ASR@1/ASR@10 on triggered queries.
For model-level defenses, we report Detection Success Rate (DSR) and Detection Margin (DM).
DM denotes the score gap between clean and backdoored samples, where a smaller DM implies more stealthy and harder-to-detect backdoors.
For data-filtering defenses, we report TPR, False Positive Rate (FPR).
We also use AUROC to summarize the TPR–FPR trade-off over all thresholds in inference-phase defenses.
Implementation.
Trigger patterns are optimized on a small CC3M subset with fixed hyperparameters (e.g., λT2T=0.1\lambda_{\text{T2T}}{=}0.1, λMPE=1\lambda_{\text{MPE}}{=}1) and standard Adam training.
We then poison 1,500 of 500k CC3M pairs and fine-tune with a 16×1616{\times}16 trigger block (≈\approx0.5% image area).
Unless otherwise stated, batch size, learning rate, iteration counts, and loss weights (e.g., λALIGN=0.008\lambda_{\text{ALIGN}}{=}0.008, λEWC=0.1\lambda_{\text{EWC}}{=}0.1) follow a unified recipe; full hyperparameters are given in the App. Sec. B.

VI-B Cross-Task and Cross-Model Evaluation of Attacks
This subsection evaluates BadCLIP++’s attack performance across multi-task and multi-model scenarios to validate its cross-task and cross-architecture generalization capabilities.
Zero-shot & robustness evaluation.
Tab. I compares BadCLIP++ with 12 representative backdoor attacks under the Zero-Shot setting, which evaluates the pretrained model’s intrinsic image–text alignment without downstream fine-tuning.
We report CA and ASR on ImageNet and their robustness across four test-domain variants, including ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-V2, and ImageNet-A.
The results of ImageNet-R in App. Sec. F-A.
From Tab. I, we conclude that:
❶ BadCLIP++ maintains the optimal attack-accuracy trade-off on standard ImageNet. Its ASR reaches 99.99% and CA is 58.92%, which is only 0.77% lower than that of the clean model (59.69%), indicating that a near-perfect attack success rate is achieved without sacrificing the performance of the main task.
❷ BadCLIP++ is still highly stable in distribution offset scenarios. In the four out-of-domain test sets of ImageNet-Sketch, V2, A & R, the ASR remains above 99.90%, which is an average improvement of about 1.0% compared with BadCLIP, indicating that the method is insensitive to out-of-domain feature changes and has strong trigger consistency.
