SMC sweep
Two adjustments to vanilla SMC sweep are required:
1.
In order for particle policies to be deterministic, an action
must be drawn from the proposal distribution on the first visit to each
visited state only. If a trajectory revisits a state, both the prior
and the proposal probability of the action assigned on the first visit
are 1 (and of any other action are 0).
2.
Because the stochastic estimate log⁡Z^\log\hat{Z} is induced by the
entire particle system rather than by individual particles,
stochasticity arising from environment dynamics must be coupled across
particles. In particular, random state transitions must be shared,
otherwise relative particle weights would be confounded by both
differences in their policies and independent realizations of the
transition noise. Concretely, whenever two particles visit the same
state ss and select the same action aa for the same kkth time,
they are required to transition to the same successor state s′s^{\prime}.
This can be implemented by lazily sampling a shared transition tensor
T^\hat{T}, where T^s,ak∼T(⋅∣s,a)\hat{T}_{s,a}^{k}\sim T(\cdot\mid s,a) is sampled once
and reused by all particles within a sweep. The environment dynamics
TT are then replaced, during inference, by this shared random
realization T^\hat{T} 22
2
This coupling of transition randomness
is related to the classical common random numbers variance
reduction technique (Mohamed et al., 2020), however here it is applied for
correctness rather than for variance reduction..
Optimization objective
The original VSMC formulation
assumes reparameterizable SMC proposal distributions and omits score
function terms associated with the non-differentiable
resampling operation due to their high variance and low
signal. In finite‐action MDPs, however, a categorical distribution
over actions cannot be reparameterized. Consequently, while the
resampling-induced score terms may still be dropped, the
score–function contribution arising from sampling actions from
the proposal must be retained in order to obtain meaningful
gradients. This yields the surrogate objective
