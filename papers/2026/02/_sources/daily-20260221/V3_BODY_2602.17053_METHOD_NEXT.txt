2 Reasoning Faithfulness
A faithful explanation should reflect a model’s internal reasoning process (Jacovi and Goldberg, 2020; Lyu et al., 2024), yet generated text is an external artifact and need not correspond to the model’s actual computation (Parcalabescu and Frank, 2023).
Since a truly faithful account would require interpreting incomprehensible attributes (e.g., all activation values of the model’s weights), and no consensus definition of faithfulness exists, a practical behavioral proxy is needed.
We therefore seek a user-facing, model-agnostic notion of faithfulness that can be evaluated solely from the model’s textual behavior (Appendix G.3).
We operationalize reasoning faithfulness via two verifiable properties of the output: stance consistency (internal logical integrity) and causal influence (whether the stated reasoning causally determines the ensuing output).
Concretely, stance consistency flags ornamental or self-contradictory chains even the answer is correct, whereas causal influence separates genuinely determinative reasons from post-hoc justifications.
We first formalize notions that track the canonical stance and its progression across a model’s output.
Definition 2.1 (Canonical Stance).
Let 𝒯\mathcal{T} denote the space of textual contexts and 𝒴\mathcal{Y} a finite set of stances (e.g., answer options).
The canonical stance of c∈𝒯c\in\mathcal{T} is S⁡(c)∈𝒴S(c)\in\mathcal{Y}, where extracted by the canonical stance extractor S:𝒯→𝒴S:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{Y}.
Definition 2.2 (Stance-Continuous).
For u,v∈𝒯u,v\in\mathcal{T} with concatenation c=(u,v)c=(u,v), let su=S⁡(u)s_{u}=S(u) and sv=S⁡(v)s_{v}=S(v).
The context cc is stance-continuous if su=svs_{u}=s_{v}, or if vv explicitly identifies (and justifies) a departure from sus_{u}.
Formally, the stance continuity indicator ι:𝒯×𝒯→{0,1}\iota:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\{0,1\} is
ι⁡(u,v):=𝟙​[(su=sv)∨(su≠sv∧Identified​(u,v))].\iota(u,v):=\mathbbm{1}\Big[(s_{u}=s_{v})\ \lor\ \big(s_{u}\neq s_{v}\land\textsc{Identified}(u,v)\big)\Big].
(1)
where Identified​(u,v)∈{0,1}\textsc{Identified}(u,v)\in\{0,1\} holds if and only if vv explicitly pinpoints a concrete rationale in uu (e.g., premise or step) to justify the change.
By convention, for the empty prefix ϵ\epsilon, ι⁡(ϵ,u)=1\iota(\epsilon,u)=1.
To apply this abstract notion of continuity to LRMs, we must first formalize the structural decomposition of their outputs into analyzable components.
Assumption 2.2.1.
Let oo be an LRM output decomposable (via model-specific delimiters) into components (r,e,a)(r,e,a), where r=(r1,…,rn)∈𝒯r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathcal{T} is the reasoning trace with ri∈𝒯r_{i}\in\mathcal{T}, e∈𝒯∪{∅}e\in\mathcal{T}\cup\{\varnothing\} is an optional explanation, and a∈𝒯a\in\mathcal{T} is the final answer.
Define the flattened sequence flat⁡(o)=(c1,…,cm)\mathrm{flat}(o)=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m}) with
flat⁡(o):={(r1,…,rn,a),if ​e=∅,(r1,…,rn,e,a)otherwise.\mathrm{flat}(o):=\begin{cases}(r_{1},\dots,r_{n},a),&\text{if }e=\varnothing,\\
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n},e,a)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
so each ci∈𝒯c_{i}\in\mathcal{T} and m∈{n+1,n+2}m\in\{n+1,n+2\}.
In our instantiated metric, we operate at the coarse (r,e,a)(r,e,a) granularity, treating rr as a single reasoning block, while the step-wise notation (r1,…,rn)(r_{1},\dots,r_{n}) keeps the formalism compatible with other per-step CoT extensions.

Leveraging this decomposition, we define the first primary condition of faithfulness: the entire output sequence must form a cohesive argumentative chain.
Definition 2.3 (Stance Consistency).
Given o=(r,e,a)o=(r,e,a) with flat⁡(o)=(c1,…,cm)\mathrm{flat}(o)=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m}), the output is stance-consistent if its flattened sequence forms a single unbroken chain of stance continuity:
χ(o):=⋀i=1mι(⟨c1:i−1⟩,ci)∈{0,1}.\chi(o):=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\iota\Big(\langle c_{1:i-1}\rangle,\,c_{i}\Big)\in\{0,1\}.
(2)
Thus any deviation—from a contradiction within rr to an unjustified transition between rr, ee, and aa—is counted as a failure of overall coherence.
While consistency ensures internal logic, it does not verify if the reasoning actually produced the answer. To address this, we introduce our second condition based on counterfactual intervention.

Definition 2.4 (Causal Influence).
Given model ℳ\mathcal{M} and input xx, let o=(r,e,a)∼ℳ(⋅∣x)o=(r,e,a)\sim\mathcal{M}(\cdot\mid x) and let o′=(rnew,e′,a′)∼ℳ(⋅∣x,r′)o^{\prime}=(r_{\text{new}},e^{\prime},a^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{M}(\cdot\mid x,r^{\prime}) be the output under an output-level counterfactual reasoning r′r^{\prime}.

κ(o,o′):=𝟙[S(rnew)≠S(r)]⏟Case 1: Reasoning Causality∨𝟙[S(a′)≠S(a)]⏟Case 2: Answer Causality∈{0,1}.\kappa(o,o^{\prime}):=\underbrace{\mathbbm{1}\Big[S(r_{\text{new}})\neq S(r)\Big]}_{\text{Case 1: Reasoning Causality}}\ \lor\ \underbrace{\mathbbm{1}\Big[S(a^{\prime})\neq S(a)\Big]}_{\text{Case 2: Answer Causality}}\in\{0,1\}.
(3)
Crucially, κ\kappa acts as a necessary condition to verify if the intervention had any effect. The coherence of that effect—e.g., if reasoning changes but the answer remains static without justification—is enforced separately via χ⁡(o′)\chi(o^{\prime}).
Finally, we combine these properties—internal logical coherence χ\chi (Eq. 2) and external procedural causality κ\kappa (Eq. 3)—into our unified definition of reasoning faithfulness.
Definition 2.5 (Reasoning Faithfulness).

RF(o,o′):=𝟙[χ(o)=1∧χ(o′)=1∧κ(o,o′)=1]∈{0,1}.\textsc{RF}(o,o^{\prime}):=\mathbbm{1}\Big[\chi(o)=1\land\chi(o^{\prime})=1\land\kappa(o,o^{\prime})=1\Big]\in\{0,1\}.
(4)

For causal identifiability, we impose a contrast precondition, which we define as
δ(x,r′;ℳ)=𝟙{S(r)≠S(r′)}\delta(x,r^{\prime};\mathcal{M})=\mathbbm{1}\{S(r)\neq S(r^{\prime})\}.
We evaluate faithfulness only on contrastive pairs (δ=1\delta=1), where the injected counterfactual reasoning r′r^{\prime} asserts a stance opposite to the model’s own baseline stance.
This restriction creates a proper counterfactual contrast. When S⁡(r)=S⁡(r′)S(r)=S(r^{\prime}), the intervention is stance-aligned and any “no change” outcome is ambiguous, while any “change” can be driven by unrelated factors.
