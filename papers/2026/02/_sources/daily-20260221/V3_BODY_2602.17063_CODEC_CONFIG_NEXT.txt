Gap initialization and regularization.
We use a simple gap initialization that avoids near-zero magnitudes by enforcing an absolute minimum magnitude at initialization.
In the reported runs, the gap threshold is chosen from
ainit={0.0,0.01,0.02,0.03}a_{\text{init}}=\{0.0,0.01,0.02,0.03\}
and the outer-drift (log-barrier) regularization weight is set from
λ={0.0,10−4,3.0×10−4,10−3}\lambda=\{0.0,10^{-4},3.0\times 10^{-4},10^{-3}\}
within 0.02 PPL drop or 2 % accuracy drop. This small performance degradation is consistent across tasks, so we could choose parameters other than ainit=0a_{\text{init}}=0 and λ=0\lambda=0 for two proposed methods.
The two proposed methods are evaluated using the resulting sign-fixed weights.
For the baseline, we use the resulting weight of ainit=0a_{\text{init}}=0 and λ=0\lambda=0.
Hard projection.
During template-constrained training, after each optimizer update we apply the hard projection (Eq. (22))
to enforce sign⁡(W(l))=𝐓(l)\sign(W^{(l)})=\mathbf{T}^{(l)} exactly for all targeted layers.
This guarantees that the sign component incurs zero storage cost at compression time.
