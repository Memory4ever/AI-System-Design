B.1. Rationale Faithfulness Validation
Our evaluation pipeline analyzes claims extracted from agent rationales to detect temporal leakage. This approach is valid only if agents’ predictions genuinely follow from their rationales—if predictions were made independently of the stated reasoning, analyzing rationale claims would not capture the actual decision process. This experiment validates that predictions are faithful to rationales, ensuring our claim-level leakage detection measures genuine temporal contamination.
Setup.
We test whether the rationale contains sufficient information to reproduce the original prediction. For each agent output, we: (1) clean the rationale by removing explicit prediction statements while retaining the reasoning and evidence; (2) prompt the same backbone model (Claude Sonnet 4) with the task input and cleaned rationale to generate a new prediction. If the re-prediction closely matches the original, the prediction faithfully followed the rationale. We measure consistency using Mean Relative Error (MRE) for legal and salary predictions:

Results and Analysis.
Across all agents and tasks, re-prediction errors are consistently low: MRE remains below 0.3% for both legal probability and salary predictions, and ranking disagreement stays below 0.1 (fewer than 10% of pairwise comparisons differ). These results confirm that all agents’ predictions faithfully follow their stated rationales. This validation establishes the foundation for our evaluation pipeline: since predictions are grounded in rationales, detecting temporal leakage in rationale claims directly measures contamination in the reasoning that drives predictions.

Results and Analysis.
The three tasks exhibit distinct patterns that reflect their underlying information requirements.
Legal Prediction. All agents achieve near-zero Top-K leakage (≤0.01\leq 0.01), with no meaningful differentiation across methods. This result aligns with the task’s structure: legal prediction relies on precedent, statutory interpretation, and procedural reasoning—information that exists before case decisions.
Salary Prediction. This task reveals substantial differences. Superforecasting exhibits Top-1 leakage of 0.257, indicating that in roughly one-quarter of instances, the single most influential claim contains leaked information—likely contract details that post-date the reference cutoff. Temporal Hint reduces Top-1 leakage to 0.224 (13% reduction), suggesting partial mitigation through prompt-based constraints. TimeSPEC achieves Top-1 leakage of 0.073, a 72% reduction relative to Superforecasting. The pattern holds across Top-3 and Top-5, demonstrating that our agent prevents leaked information from dominating decision-critic
