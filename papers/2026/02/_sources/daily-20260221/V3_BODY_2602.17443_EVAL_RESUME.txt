4.2 Evaluation Protocol
We adopt a round-robin tournament design so that each model is evaluated against every other model under controlled role permutations.
AIDG-I (Pragmatic Defense).
We implement a complete block design where each model pair (Mi,Mj)(M_{i},M_{j}) interacts under both role configurations (Mi→MjM_{i}\to M_{j} and Mj→MiM_{j}\to M_{i}) and both strategic modes (Mode A: prior knowledge; Mode B: blind deduction):
Ng​a​m​e​s=Nt​o​u​r​n​a​m​e​n​t​s×(62)×2r​o​l​e​s×2m​o​d​e​sN_{games}=N_{tournaments}\times\binom{6}{2}\times 2_{roles}\times 2_{modes}
(4)
This yields 60 games per tournament. We conduct five independent tournaments (random seeds σ1..5\sigma_{1..5}), resulting in 300 total games. The 20-secret corpus is shuffled each round to mitigate memorization effects.
AIDG-II (Constrained Search).
Due to the 16-turn interaction horizon, we use an ordered pairing design. Each model serves as Seeker against all five distinct Holder models:
Ng​a​m​e​s=Nt​o​u​r​n​a​m​e​n​t​s×(6s​e​e​k​e​r​s×5h​o​l​d​e​r​s)N_{games}=N_{tournaments}\times(6_{seekers}\times 5_{holders})
(5)
This yields 150 games. Within each tournament, target variables are sampled without replacement from the 100-word ontology to ensure semantic coverage.
Inference Configuration.
Agents generate responses at temperature T=0.7T=0.7 to encourage strategic diversity and avoid degenerate questioning loops. The Arbiter operates at T=0.01T=0.01 to minimize adjudication variance. Full dialogue history HtH_{t} is preserved at each turn, ensuring access to accumulated constraints and prior commitments.

6 Discussion
Our findings indicate a systematic asymmetry in how LLMs handle information extraction versus information containment. We analyze potential computational mechanisms underlying this divergence, focusing on state complexity, pragmatic biases, and working memory constraints.
6.1 Asymmetry in State Tracking
A central observation of AIDG is that models perform substantially better in the Holder role than in the Seeker role (d=4.07d=4.07). We hypothesize that this difference reflects distinct computational demands associated with static versus dynamic state tracking.
Static vs. Dynamic Load.
The Holder maintains a fixed latent variable SS throughout the interaction. The task resembles a discriminative mapping: f⁡(q​u​e​r​y,S)→{a​c​c​e​p​t,r​e​j​e​c​t}f(query,S)\to\{accept,reject\}, which can often be resolved locally with limited history integration. In contrast, the Seeker must maintain and update a belief distribution P⁡(S∣Ht)P(S\mid H_{t}) over hypotheses at each turn tt. This requires iterative context integration, uncertainty reduction, and strategy refinement, all of which increase computational burden as dialogue length grows.
Training Distribution Bias.
Instruction-tuned models are extensively optimized for refusal and boundary maintenance behaviors. As a result, defensive trajectories may correspond to high-probability action templates. By contrast, systematic information extraction strategies (e.g., entropy-reducing questioning) are less explicitly represented in typical alignment corpora. This imbalance may contribute to the observed performance gap, with Seekers relying more heavily on on-the-fly reasoning.
6.2 Pragmatics and Cooperative Bias
The difference between Mode A (21.9%) and Mode B (3.5%) success rates suggests that cooperative dialogue priors influence adversarial outcomes.
Confirmation Vulnerability.
In Mode A, the Seeker proposes a hypothesis H≈SH\approx S. The Holder must reconcile the constraint to protect SS with the conversational norm of engaging with user premises. When presuppositions are framed as plausible or partially correct, models may default toward cooperative validation. This tension between helpfulness and containment may explain increased leakage under confirmation attacks.
6.3 Constraint–Reasoning Trade-off
The high disqualification rate in AIDG-II (41.3%) highlights a potential trade-off between strategic reasoning and constraint adherence.
When models allocate computation toward planning entropy-reducing questions, adherence to negative syntactic constraints (e.g., prohibiting direct guesses) may degrade. This suggests that maintaining rule compliance under adversarial cognitive load poses a distinct challenge from unconstrained reasoning.
Scale and Compliance.
We observe no correlation between parameter count and disqualification rate (ρ=0.0\rho=0.0). For example, Llama-4 (∼\sim400B) exhibits higher violation rates than Qwen3-235B (235B). This indicates that adversarial instruction-following may depend more on training objectives and alignment procedures than on scale alone.
6.4 Architectural Priors and Planning Depth
The profile of Granite-3.3-8B strong defensive performance but weak offensive capability suggests a separation between boundary maintenance and strategic planning.
1.
Boundary Maintenance: Identifying sensitive contexts and generating refusal-style responses may rely on surface-level alignment heuristics that are accessible to smaller models.
2.
Strategic Deduction: Multi-step information seeking requires maintaining an explicit hypothesis space and planning across turns. The low Seeker success rate for smaller models suggests that this capability may depend on greater representational depth or more specialized training.
7 Conclusion
We introduced AIDG (Adversarial Information Deduction Game), a game-theoretic framework designed to isolate the asymmetry between information seeking (deduction) and information containment (state maintenance). Across 439 adversarial dialogues involving six frontier LLMs, we identify four recurring patterns:
1.
Maintenance–Deduction Gap: A consistent capability gap (d=4.07d=4.07) favors state maintenance over active deduction. This suggests that current models more reliably support boundary maintenance behaviors than long-horizon strategic inquiry.
2.
Confirmation Vulnerability: Confirmation-based attacks are 7.75×\times more effective than blind extraction, indicating that cooperative dialogue priors can increase leakage risk under presuppositional questioning.
3.
Constraint–Reasoning Tension: In AIDG-II, parameter scale does not correlate with constraint adherence (ρ=0.0\rho=0.0), suggesting that reasoning under adversarial load and negative constraint enforcement are separable capabilities.
4.
Capability Decomposition: Defensive performance exhibits low cross-model variance (σ=1.9\sigma=1.9 ELO), whereas offensive performance varies substantially (σ=53.3\sigma=53.3 ELO), indicating heterogeneous proficiency in multi-step deduction.
Together, these results indicate that robustness in multi-turn dialogue is not a single scalar property but a composition of partially independent capabilities, including pragmatic control, constraint satisfaction, and strategic planning. We release all interaction traces, rating histories, and analysis scripts to facilitate reproducible research on adversarial dialogue dynamics.
