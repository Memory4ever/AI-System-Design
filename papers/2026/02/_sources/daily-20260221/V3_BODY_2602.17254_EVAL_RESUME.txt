6. Evaluation
In order to complement our analytical results derived in Sec. 2.3 and Sec. 5 (in particular latency-optimality), we assess in this section the performance of Trivance and state-of-the-art AllReduce algorithms empirically on different torus topologies. We conducted extensive simulations using the Structural Simulation Toolkit (SST) (Adalsteinsson et al., 2010), an event-driven, packet-level network simulator. Within SST, we implemented Trivance as well as Bruck’s AllReduce algorithm. For the Bucket, Recursive Doubling, and Swing algorithms, we rely on the publicly available implementations by Daniele et al. (Sensi et al., 2024). In our evaluation, we extend Recursive Doubling to fully exploit all 2​D2D network ports in multidimensional torus topologies. To ensure a reasonable comparison with the original Bruck algorithm, we modify its routing strategy to employ shortest-path routing and reorder data exchanges in the AllGather phase to reduce overall end-to-end transmission latency (Jeaugey, 2025).
We simulate networks with link bandwidth of 800 Gb/s, a link latency of 100 ns, and a per-hop packet processing latency of 100 ns (Sensi et al., 2024). Further, we introduce a per step latency of α=1.5​μ​s\alpha=1.5\mu s (Shah et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025). We evaluate message sizes ranging from 32 B to 128 MiB. Each plot reports the relative performance of existing approaches compared to Trivance.
Traditional AllReduce algorithms are optimal on rings or tori with power-of-two network sizes, whereas Trivance and Bruck’s communication patterns are optimal for power-of-three network sizes and remain resilient to suboptimal sizes. However, deviations from these introduce additional data transmission due to routing adjustments, which is negligible for small messages but becomes significant for larger message sizes. Accordingly, we evaluate all algorithms on power-of-two configurations, and separately compare Bucket, Bruck, and Trivance on a 27×2727\times 27 torus network (Swing and Recursive Doubling have no implementation for arbitrary nn in SST).
We organize the evaluation as follows: Section 6.1 presents results for ring networks. Section 6.2 evaluates square two-dimensional tori for powers of two and three with varying bandwidth. Finally, Section 6.3 reports results for three-dimensional tori.
Our results show that Trivance is the best performing latency-optimal algorithm for AllReduce in latency-bound scenarios with small and moderate message sizes. In these regimes across all evaluated topologies, Trivance reduces completion time by 5-30% compared to existing approaches, depending on network size and parameters. This advantage persists for message sizes up to 512 KiB in ring networks and extends to 8 MiB in multidimensional tori while up to 32 MiB in high bandwidth networks. For 3D tori Trivance reduces completion time by 5-15% compared to all state-of-the-art approaches up to 128 MiB.

Due to improved transmission delay optimality, the tradeoff point at which Trivance is outperformed by existing approaches shifts to higher message sizes, reaching 4 MiB in Fig. 7(a). Moreover, in 8×88\times 8 torus networks, Trivance outperforms all existing approaches by up to 25% for message sizes between 32 KiB and 2 MiB. Within this span, message sizes are too large for Bruck to remain effective, yet too small for bandwidth-optimized algorithms such as Swing or Bucket to achieve minimal completion time. For larger tori, the tradeoff point shifts further to the right, reaching 8 MiB in Fig. 7(b).
Notably, the performance of Trivance and Bruck on power-of-two networks strongly depends on how closely the network size matches their optimal topologies. When the network size deviates from these, both algorithms transmit data volumes comparable to those required for the next larger power-of-three topology, resulting in relatively higher communication overhead. While this overhead is negligible for small message sizes, it becomes significant for larger messages, particularly beyond 8 MiB.
Bandwidth Impact on Completion Time:
To analyze the impact of network bandwidth on the performance of Trivance, Fig. 6.2 illustrates the relative completion time of the best-performing existing approach for each message size compared to Trivance on a 32×3232\times 32 torus. The evaluated network bandwidths range from 200 Gb/s to 3.2 Tb/s. We observe that the performance improvement of Trivance for small message sizes is largely independent of bandwidth, as this regime is latency bound. In this setting, Trivance reduces completion time by 6-14% for message sizes up to 2 MiB.

At lower bandwidths, transmission delay constitutes a larger fraction of the overall completion time, causing bandwidth optimized approaches to surpass Trivance at approximately 4 MiB. As bandwidth increases, the relative impact of congestion diminishes, allowing the performance advantage of Trivance to persist for larger message sizes, extending up to 64 MiB for networks operating at 2.4 Tb/s and 3.2 Tb/s. These results indicate that Trivance consistently achieves better performance at high bandwidth, whereas bandwidth-optimized algorithms dominate in low-bandwidth regimes. Regardless of network bandwidth, Trivance remains the best-performing latency-optimal algorithm.
Performance on Power-of-Three Tori
The communication pattern of Trivance supports AllReduce on arbitrary network sizes. However, its optimal performance is achieved on networks with dimensions sizes of powers of three, under which Trivance operates most efficiently. In Fig. 9, we analyze the completion time improvement of Trivance on a 27×2727\times 27 torus network. For small message sizes, the comparison with Bruck matches earlier results of 5% improvement. Notably, for larger message sizes, Trivance significantly outperforms both Bucket and Bruck, achieving a 10% performance gain for messages larger than 32 KiB and exceeding 50% for messages larger than 512 KiB. Even at 32 MiB, Trivance continues to outperform Bucket and Bruck more than 40%, with Bucket only matching the performance of Trivance at 128 MiB. These results highlight the advantage of Trivance over all existing approaches on power-of-three network topologies for messages up to 128 MiB.
