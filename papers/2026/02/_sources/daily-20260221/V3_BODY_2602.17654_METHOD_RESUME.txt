Abstract.
We propose a two-stage “Mine and Refine” contrastive training framework for semantic text embeddings to enhance multi-category e-commerce search retrieval.
Large scale e-commerce search demands embeddings that generalize to long tail, noisy queries while adhering to scalable supervision compatible with product and policy constraints.
A practical challenge is that relevance is often graded: users accept substitutes or complements beyond exact matches, and production systems benefit from clear separation of similarity scores across these relevance strata for stable hybrid blending and thresholding.
To obtain scalable policy consistent supervision, we finetune a lightweight LLM on human annotations under a three-level relevance guideline and further reduce residual noise via engagement driven auditing.
In Stage 1, we train a multilingual Siamese two-tower retriever with a label aware supervised contrastive objective that shapes a robust global semantic space.
In Stage 2, we mine hard samples via ANN and re-annotate them with the policy aligned LLM, and introduce a multi-class extension of circle loss that explicitly sharpens similarity boundaries between relevance levels, to further refine and enrich the embedding space.
Robustness is additionally improved through additive spelling augmentation and synthetic query generation.
Extensive offline evaluations and production A/B tests show that our framework improves retrieval relevance and delivers statistically significant gains in engagement and business impact.

1. Introduction
Semantic retrieval has become a core component of modern e-commerce search, enabling systems to retrieve relevant items even when user queries do not lexically match product text (e.g., synonyms, paraphrases, or implicit intent). In a multi-category marketplace, this problem is compounded by heterogeneous catalog quality (varying titles and attribute coverage), rapidly changing inventory, and a query distribution dominated by long tailed and noisy inputs such as misspellings, abbreviations, and occasional multilingual queries. At the same time, production retrieval operates under strict latency, memory, and indexing constraints: query embeddings must be computed in real time, item embeddings must be precomputed and indexed for approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search, and candidate generation must be stable enough to support downstream ranking and business logic.
Despite strong progress in embedding based retrieval (EBR), we found that deploying a robust semantic retriever in this setting faces two practical challenges.
First, relevance is graded and policy driven.
Unlike many dense retrieval benchmarks that assume binary relevance, shopping queries often admit multiple acceptable outcomes. Beyond an exact match, users may accept substitutes (e.g., different brands or sizes) or complements (items that satisfy a closely related shopping intent), while irrelevant results must be avoided for user trust and downstream conversion. This graded structure matters not only for ranking quality but also for similarity score separability: clearer boundaries between relevance strata simplify hybrid blending, reduce brittle threshold tuning, and make downstream ranking more reliable when candidate sets are noisy.
Second, scalable training requires hard mining, but naive mining amplifies label noise.
Hard negative mining is widely used to improve dense retrievers by exposing confusable candidates. In production e-commerce search, however, top ANN retrieved candidates are frequently near misses that are not truly irrelevant under policy (especially when substitutes/complements are allowed). Treating such candidates as negatives can corrupt training signals, collapse interclass margins, and degrade generalization on long tail queries. Meanwhile, obtaining high quality human relevance judgments on the scale required for continual retriever improvement is operationally expensive.
To address these challenges, we propose a two-stage Mine and Refine training framework for multilingual semantic retrieval under three-level relevance. We first build scalable, policy consistent supervision by finetuning a lightweight LLM on a human annotated dataset to predict three-level query and item relevance, and further reduce noise via an engagement guided audit and selective reannotation. In Stage 1, we train a Siamese two-tower retriever with a label aware supervised contrastive objective that shapes a robust global semantic space. In Stage 2, we perform ANN based mining to surface semantically confusable items, re-annotate mined query and item pairs with the policy aligned LLM to avoid false negatives as well as mining hard positives, and refine the model using a multi-class extension of circle loss that explicitly sharpens similarity boundaries between relevance strata.
We evaluate the proposed approach using both offline relevance metrics and production A/B experiments. Offline, our method improves NDCG, recall, and precision across multiple retrieval depths, and produces better separability of similarity scores in difficult cases of high lexical overlap. Online A/B tests showed statistically significant lifts in add to cart rate, conversion rate, and gross order value when swapping only the embedding retriever while keeping downstream ranking and business logic unchanged.

2. Related Work
Embedding-based Retrieval.
Lexical retrieval methods such as BM25 (Robertson and Zaragoza, 2009) remain strong baselines, but often fail to bridge the semantic gap between user intent and product text in e-commerce search.
Embedding-based retrieval (EBR) addresses this by encoding queries and items into a shared vector space using bi-encoder / two-tower architectures and retrieving candidates via ANN search (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019; Karpukhin et al., 2020).
This family of approaches has been widely adopted in large scale retrieval due to its favorable latency and quality trade off, and has been extended with richer interaction patterns to improve accuracy while preserving offline indexing (Khattab and Zaharia, 2020).
Given that the quality of the embedding model can vary substantially between domains and query distributions, broad benchmarks such as BEIR and MTEB have been used to study the robustness and out-of-distribution generalization of embedding models (Thakur et al., 2021; Muennighoff et al., 2022).
Finally, multilingual support is often achieved by initializing from multilingual sentence encoders or multilingual embedding models that align multiple languages into a shared semantic space (Yang et al., 2019; Feng et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022).
Contrastive Learning.
Contrastive learning objectives are central to learning embedding spaces suitable for retrieval.
Early deep metric learning formulations include triplet loss (Weinberger and Saul, 2009) and N-pair loss (Sohn, 2016), while modern contrastive learning commonly uses softmax based objectives that compare one positive against many negatives (e.g. InfoNCE style objectives).
Contrastive learning has produced strong sentence and dense retrieval representations under supervised and weak/unsupervised signals (Gao et al., 2021; Izacard et al., 2021).
Supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) (Khosla et al., 2020) generalizes batch contrastive training by leveraging label information to pull examples from the same class together while pushing examples from different classes apart, and is often more stable than pairwise or triplet based objectives when training on hard examples.
Separately, margin based objectives from computer vision metric learning (e.g. ArcFace and additive/angular margin softmax) explicitly encourage separation in similarity space (Deng et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2023), and circle loss (Sun et al., 2020) provides a unified similarity optimization framework through adaptive reweighting of pair similarities.
However, these objectives are typically applied to binary similarity learning (positive vs. negative).
In contrast, our setting imposes three-level relevance judgments, where both the ranking and the relative spacing between relevance strata matter.
Our contribution is to operationalize graded relevance directly in the contrastive objective: we use a multi-class supervised contrastive stage to shape a global semantic space, and then refine separability with a circle style loss extended to graded relevance, explicitly targeting sharper and more usable similarity score boundaries.
Hard Sample Mining.
The quality and difficulty of negative samples critically affect embedding based retrieval training.
A common strategy is to use in-batch negatives, which is computationally efficient but can introduce false negatives when semantically related items appear in the same batch, particularly in settings with graded relevance or broad intents.
To obtain stronger and more controllable negatives, dense retrieval systems frequently employ offline or iterative mining, where a trained retriever is used to retrieve the top candidates and then select hard negatives (and sometimes hard positives) for subsequent training rounds.
ANCE (Xiong et al., 2021) popularized ANN-based global hard-negative mining to reduce train–test mismatch by constructing negatives from a corpus-wide index that is updated during training.
However, aggressive mining from top retrieved results can amplify label noise via false negatives.
Distinct from prior approaches that rely on heuristics or implicit assumptions about negatives, our pipeline integrates an explicit re-annotation step for mined pairs under a product policy aligned, finetuned LLM, enabling reliable selection of both hard negatives and hard positives in a three class setting at scale.
This yields a refinement set that is difficult and policy consistent, which is crucial when the goal is not only higher recall but also robust score separability among semantically confusable items.
Iterative Refinement.
In dense retrieval, multi-stage refinement effects arise naturally when training alternates between retrieval with the current model and retraining on newly mined candidates (often from ANN search), and have been shown to substantially improve performance over static training sets (Xiong et al., 2021; Karpukhin et al., 2020).
More broadly, curriculum learning and multi-stage finetuning are commonly used to balance global semantic structure learned from broad supervision with domain specific refinements that target confusable cases and long tail queries (Gao et al., 2021; Izacard et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022).
Our approach differs in what the refinement stage is optimized to achieve and how its data are constructed: rather than treating mining as an iterative efficiency trick, we use mining as a mechanism to expose graded relevance and then apply a geometry aware objective (multi-class circle style refinement) that explicitly sharpens interclass similarity margins.
Coupled with realistic augmentation through spelling variation (Jayanthi et al., 2020), this Mine and Refine design targets robustness and controllable similarity score calibration under graded relevance. This is a practical requirement for downstream serving and thresholding in large scale e-commerce search that is not directly addressed by prior binary relevance dense retrieval pipelines.
3. Labeling Framework
Given a query qq and an item dd, where each item dd has attributes such as item name, hierarchical item taxonomy path, item description, etc., relevance judgment based on our product guidelines categorizes the query and item pair (q,d)(q,d) into three classes 22, 11, and 00 which correspond to relevant, moderately relevant (substitute or complement), and irrelevant respectively.
3.1. Finetuned LLM for Scalable Labeling
Similar to the Amazon shopping data set (Reddy et al., 2022), we have a dataset for relevance judgement with human annotations according to the 3 class labeling principle mentioned above.
However, collecting human feedback for the search products at scale is not trivial, especially when the volume goes beyond millions.
To effectively leverage the human feedback to evaluate and improve search relevance quality, we finetuned a lightweight LLM, gpt-4o-mini (OpenAI, 2024), using the human annotated dataset to adapt the relevance prediction to be aligned with our principles.
This finetuned LLM has achieved approximately 87.6%87.6\% in 3-class accuracy compared to human annotations and 98.8%98.8\% in "within-1" accuracy (i.e. |prediction−human annotation|≤1\lvert\text{prediction}-\text{human annotation}\rvert\leq 1) on an evaluation dataset consisting of 105105k query and item pairs spanning diverse categories and broadness of intent.
This allowed us to scale the relevance labeling for embedding model training.

Engagement signals vary with the broadness of the query.
Broad queries typically have a much lower query–item conversion rate than narrow queries, as user purchases are distributed across a larger number of items.
