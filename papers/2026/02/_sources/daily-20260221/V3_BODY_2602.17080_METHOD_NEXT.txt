2 New Optimization Algorithms: NAMO and NAMO-D
In this section, we describe the proposed algorithm NAMO and its diagonal extension NAMO-D.
We consider optimization problems with matrix-structured parameters of the form:
minΘ∈ℝm×n⁡ℒ⁡(Θ),\min_{\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\mathcal{L}(\Theta),
where ℒ:ℝm×n→ℝ\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} is the (nonconvex) loss function.
We impose the following standard smoothness of loss function and bounded-variance noise assumptions.
Assumption 1.
The gradient of ℒ⁡(Θ)\mathcal{L}(\Theta) is Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for arbitrary Θ,Θ′∈ℝm×n\Theta,\Theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:
‖∇ℒ​(Θ)−∇ℒ​(Θ′)‖∗≤L​‖Θ−Θ′‖2,\left\|\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta)-\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta^{\prime})\right\|_{*}\leq L\left\|\Theta-\Theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2},
(1)
for some constant L>0,L>0, where ‖⋅‖∗\left\|\cdot\right\|_{*} and ‖⋅‖2\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2} denote the nuclear norm and the spectral norm respectively.
Note that gradient of the loss, ∇ℒ​(Θ)\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta), is in ℝm×n\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} and recall that the nuclear norm is the sum of the singular values while the spectral norm is the maximum singular value.
Assumption 2.
At each iteration t,t, the stochastic gradient GtG_{t} is an unbiased estimate of the true gradient, i.e.,
𝔼⁡[Gt]=∇ℒ​(Θt−1),\mathbb{E}[G_{t}]=\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{t-1}),
with a uniformly bounded variance:
𝔼⁡[‖Gt−∇ℒ​(Θt−1)‖F2]≤σ2b,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|G_{t}-\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{t-1})\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle F}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{b},
where b≥1b\geq 1 is the batch size and ‖⋅‖F\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle F} denotes the Frobenius norm.
Assumption 1 is equivalent to a more commonly seen smooth assumption:
‖∇ℒ​(Θ)−∇ℒ​(Θ′)‖F≤L′​‖Θ−Θ′‖F\left\|\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta)-\nabla\!\mathcal{L}(\Theta^{\prime})\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle F}\leq L^{\prime}\left\|\Theta-\Theta^{\prime}\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle F}
(2)
for a different Lipschitz constant L′>0L^{\prime}>0.
Since orthogonalized gradient descent can be interpreted as steepest descent under the spectral norm (Shen et al., 2025),
we state the smoothness assumption in the equivalent form (1).
Details of NAMO and NAMO-D are summarized in Algorithms 1 and 2, respectively.
For the theoretical analysis in Section 3, we assume exact orthogonalization in both algorithms,
as is standard in existing works (Shen et al., 2025; Sato et al., 2025; Li and Hong, 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Kovalev, 2025; Pethick et al., 2025).
In practice, exact orthogonalization can be expensive to compute,
and for the experiments in Section 4 we use Newton–Schulz iterations to obtain an approximate orthogonalization, as in the Muon optimizer.
NAMO maintains a biased second raw-moment estimate of the squared Frobenius norm:
vt=μ2​vt−1+(1−μ2)​‖Gt‖F2,v_{t}=\mu_{2}v_{t-1}+(1-\mu_{2})\left\|G_{t}\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle F}^{2},
where GtG_{t} is the stochastic gradient matrix at iteration tt, and the momentum matrix MtM_{t} is a biased first moment estimate of the stochastic gradient.
Applying bias correction yields M^t:=Mt/(1−μ1t)\hat{M}_{t}:=M_{t}/(1-\mu_{1}^{t}) and v^t:=vt/(1−μ2t)\hat{v}_{t}:=v_{t}/(1-\mu_{2}^{t}).
The parameters Θ∈ℝm×n\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} are updated by:
Θt=Θt−1−η​αt​Ot,\Theta_{t}=\Theta_{t-1}-\eta\alpha_{t}O_{t},
(3)
where η>0\eta>0 is a prescribed learning rate, and the orthogonalized momentum OtO_{t} is adaptively scaled by a scalar:
αt:=(1−μ2t)121−μ1t​‖Mt‖vt+ϵ=‖M^t‖v^t+ϵt,\alpha_{t}:=\frac{(1-\mu_{2}^{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\mu_{1}^{t}}\frac{\left\|M_{t}\right\|}{\sqrt{v_{t}}+\epsilon}=\frac{\left\|\hat{M}_{t}\right\|}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t}}+\epsilon_{t}},
with ϵt:=ϵ/1−μ2t\epsilon_{t}:=\epsilon/\sqrt{1-\mu_{2}^{t}} for a small ϵ>0\epsilon>0. When stochastic gradients are noisy, or when the iterates approach a stationary point,
αt\alpha_{t} is small, promoting stable convergence.

While NAMO scales the orthogonalized momentum using a single adaptive stepsize, NAMO-D applies a column-wise scaling based on the column norms of the momentum matrix. This design assigns an individual adaptive stepsize to each neuron and is consistent with the near block-diagonal Hessian structure commonly observed in neural networks (Dong et al., 2025; An et al., 2025).
For each j=1,2,⋯,nj=1,2,\cdots,n, NAMO-D maintains a biased second raw-moment estimate of the squared norm of the jj-th colum of the stochastic gradient:
[𝐯t]j=μ2[𝐯t]j−1+(1−μ2)‖[Gt]:j‖2.\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{j}=\mu_{2}\left[\mathbf{v}_{t}\right]_{j-1}+(1-\mu_{2})\left\|\left[G_{t}\right]_{:j}\right\|^{2}.
To write this in an equivalent vector form, define the operator 𝒩c:ℝm×n→ℝn\mathcal{N}_{c}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n} by:
[𝒩c(M)]j:=‖M:j‖,j=1,2,⋯,n,\left[\mathcal{N}_{c}(M)\right]_{j}:=\left\|M_{:j}\right\|,\quad j=1,2,\cdots,n,
where ‖⋅‖\left\|\cdot\right\| denotes the Euclidean norm. Then the iterates can be written as:
𝐯t=μ2​𝐯t−1+(1−μ2)​𝒩c​(Gt)⊙𝒩c​(Gt).\mathbf{v}_{t}=\mu_{2}\mathbf{v}_{t-1}+(1-\mu_{2})\mathcal{N}_{c}(G_{t})\odot\mathcal{N}_{c}(G_{t}).
Applying bias correction yields M^t:=Mt/(1−μ1t)\hat{M}_{t}:=M_{t}/(1-\mu_{1}^{t}) and 𝐯^t:=𝐯t/(1−μ2t)\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}:=\mathbf{v}_{t}/(1-\mu_{2}^{t}). Now let:
𝐝t=𝒩c​(M^t)⊘(𝐯^t+ϵt),\mathbf{d}_{t}=\mathcal{N}_{c}(\hat{M}_{t})\oslash\left(\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}+\epsilon_{t}\right),
where ϵt:=ϵ/1−μ2t\epsilon_{t}:=\epsilon/\sqrt{1-\mu_{2}^{t}} for a small ϵ>0\epsilon>0, and ⊘\oslash denotes entrywise division. By Lemma 7,
the entries of 𝐝t\mathbf{d}_{t} are bounded above by:
‖𝐝t‖∞=maxj⁡[𝐝t]j≤1−μ11−μ2.\left\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{j}\left[\mathbf{d}_{t}\right]_{j}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1-\mu_{1}}{1-\mu_{2}}}.
Compared with NAMO, NAMO-D’s column-wise scaling enables finer-grained noise adaptation, but it no
longer strictly preserves orthogonality of the update direction. Let d¯t:=‖𝐝t‖1/n\bar{d}_{t}:=\left\|\mathbf{d}_{t}\right\|_{1}/n be the average of the entries of 𝐝t\mathbf{d}_{t}.
To ensure that the scaled direction remains well-conditioned, we clamp the adaptive stepsizes toward this average via:
𝐝~t:=min⁡{max⁡{𝐝t,c​d¯t​𝟏},d¯t/c},\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{t}:=\min\left\{\max\left\{\mathbf{d}_{t},c\bar{d}_{t}\mathbf{1}\right\},\bar{d}_{t}/c\right\},
for a prescribed constant c∈(0,1],c\in(0,1], where the maximum and minimum operations are applied entrywise, and 𝟏∈ℝn\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} denotes the vector of all-ones.
Let Dt:=diag⁡(𝐝~t)D_{t}:=\diag\left(\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{t}\right). NAMO-D updates the parameters by:
Θt=Θt−1−η​Ot​Dt.\Theta_{t}=\Theta_{t-1}-\eta O_{t}D_{t}.
This update rule combines column-wise, noise-adaptive scaling with a simple clamping safeguard,
tempering updates when gradients are noisy or the iterates are near a stationary point, while keeping the scaled update direction well-conditioned.
