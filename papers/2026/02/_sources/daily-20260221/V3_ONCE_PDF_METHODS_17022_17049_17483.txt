L130@P2: 2.2 METHODOLOGICAL SIMILARITIES AND DIFFERENCES
L131@P2: Prompt Injection: Prompt injection has been extensively discussed in safety research, focusing
L132@P2: on its malicious exploitation and mitigation through red-teaming (Perez & Ribeiro, 2022; Greshake
L133@P2: et al., 2023; Zhan et al., 2024; 2025; Chen et al., 2025). Such injections can occur during external
L134@P2: interaction stages, such as user messages and tool outputs, where they may influence an agent’s
L135@P2: behavior. To systematically prevent these risks, Wallace et al. (2024) introduced an instruction
L136@P2: hierarchy, enforcing a specific ordering of instructions within an agentic pipeline. While REIN
L137@P2: shares certain characteristics with external prompt injection, we demonstrate that REIN operates
L138@P2: under the constraints of the instruction hierarchy and can be safely deployed when appropriate tools
L139@P2: are assigned, which will be discussed in §4.6.
L140@P2: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): RAG integrates a generator with non-parametric
L141@P2: memory queried at inference time3(Lewis et al., 2020; Guu et al., 2020; Borgeaud et al., 2022).
L142@P2: It mitigates hallucinations and allows rapid knowledge updates via index refresh but assumes that
L143@P2: user requests are already well-formed and information-seeking in nature. Both LLM-based RAG
L144@P2: and REIN operate at inference without parameter updates, yet differ in scope: RAG activates when
L145@P2: external factual knowledge is potentially needed, whereas REIN is designed to intervene when a
L146@P2: dialogue appears to have derailed. In practice, these approaches are complementary–an agent may
L147@P2: employ RAG for factual accuracy and REIN for recovery from conversational errors.
