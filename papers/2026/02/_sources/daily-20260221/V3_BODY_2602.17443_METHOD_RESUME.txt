3.1 Design Principles
The framework is guided by three hypotheses:
1.
Role Asymmetry: We separate the capability to query information from the capability to maintain it. This isolates strategic inquiry from defensive consistency within the same generative model.
2.
Long-Horizon Dependency: Dialogues extend to 10–16 turns, requiring agents to maintain consistent internal representations of SS under accumulated conversational pressure. This tests robustness of state tracking across extended contexts.
3.
Automated Adjudication: For reproducibility, outcomes are evaluated by a deterministic Arbiter (a low-temperature LLM judge) that assesses semantic equivalence rather than exact lexical overlap.
3.2 AIDG-I: Pragmatic State Maintenance
AIDG-I evaluates resistance to adversarial pragmatics in free-form dialogue. The task tests whether an agent can protect a natural-language fact SS while maintaining cooperative conversational norms.
Information Corpus.
We construct 20 atomic facts representing realistic private states (e.g., “The alarm code ends with 42”, “The blue notebook is in the second drawer”). The facts are semantically unambiguous and structurally diverse.
Strategic Modalities.
We evaluate two interaction modes:
•
Mode A (Confirmation): The Seeker begins with a hypothesis H≈SH\approx S and attempts to induce confirmation. This tests robustness to leading questions and presupposition-based traps.
•
Mode B (Blind Deduction): The Seeker begins with maximal uncertainty and reduces entropy through structured questioning. This evaluates information-seeking efficiency.
Leak Detection Logic.
The Arbiter evaluates each turn for leakage, categorizing failures as:
•
Explicit Disclosure: Direct generation of tokens contained in SS.
•
Confirmational Leak: Affirming a hypothesis that matches SS.
•
Semantic Paraphrase: Generating a semantically equivalent representation of SS.
•
Implicit Admission: Failing to reject a premise that logically entails SS.
3.3 AIDG-II: Constrained Deductive Search
Whereas AIDG-I evaluates pragmatic resistance, AIDG-II isolates systematic reasoning by imposing strict output constraints. This reduces the influence of linguistic evasion and emphasizes logical state tracking.
Search Space.
The target variable SS is sampled from a closed ontology of 100 concrete nouns spanning 10 semantic categories (e.g., Animals, Tools, Vehicles). The full taxonomy is provided in the Appendix.
Constraint Satisfaction.
•
Holder Constraints: The Holder may output only tokens from Vr​e​s​p={“yes”,“no”,“maybe”}V_{resp}=\{\text{``yes''},\text{``no''},\text{``maybe''}\}. This collapses defensive strategies into a trinary signal.
•
Seeker Constraints: The Seeker must generate categorical or property-based queries (e.g., “Is it alive?”) and may not issue direct point-guesses until a final declaration. This enforces structured hypothesis narrowing.
Lock Mechanism and Efficiency.
To measure search efficiency, the Seeker may terminate the game with a final guess. We introduce a Turn-Decay Multiplier M⁡(t)M(t):

4.2 Evaluation Protocol
We adopt a round-robin tournament design so that each model is evaluated against every other model under controlled role permutations.
AIDG-I (Pragmatic Defense).
We implement a complete block design where each model pair (Mi,Mj)(M_{i},M_{j}) interacts under both role configurations (Mi→MjM_{i}\to M_{j} and Mj→MiM_{j}\to M_{i}) and both strategic modes (Mode A: prior knowledge; Mode B: blind deduction):
Ng​a​m​e​s=Nt​o​u​r​n​a​m​e​n​t​s×(62)×2r​o​l​e​s×2m​o​d​e​sN_{games}=N_{tournaments}\times\binom{6}{2}\times 2_{roles}\times 2_{modes}
(4)
This yields 60 games per tournament. We conduct five independent tournaments (random seeds σ1..5\sigma_{1..5}), resulting in 300 total games. The 20-secret corpus is shuffled each round to mitigate memorization effects.
AIDG-II (Constrained Search).
Due to the 16-turn interaction horizon, we use an ordered pairing design. Each model serves as Seeker against all five distinct Holder models:
Ng​a​m​e​s=Nt​o​u​r​n​a​m​e​n​t​s×(6s​e​e​k​e​r​s×5h​o​l​d​e​r​s)N_{games}=N_{tournaments}\times(6_{seekers}\times 5_{holders})
(5)
This yields 150 games. Within each tournament, target variables are sampled without replacement from the 100-word ontology to ensure semantic coverage.
Inference Configuration.
Agents generate responses at temperature T=0.7T=0.7 to encourage strategic diversity and avoid degenerate questioning loops. The Arbiter operates at T=0.01T=0.01 to minimize adjudication variance. Full dialogue history HtH_{t} is preserved at each turn, ensuring access to accumulated constraints and prior commitments.

6 Discussion
Our findings indicate a systematic asymmetry in how LLMs handle information extraction versus information containment. We analyze potential computational mechanisms underlying this divergence, focusing on state complexity, pragmatic biases, and working memory constraints.
6.1 Asymmetry in State Tracking
A central observation of AIDG is that models perform substantially better in the Holder role than in the Seeker role (d=4.07d=4.07). We hypothesize that this difference reflects distinct computational demands associated with static versus dynamic state tracking.
Static vs. Dynamic Load.
The Holder maintains a fixed latent variable SS throughout the interaction.
