MDP Planning as Policy Inference
David Tolpin
Affiliation: Offtopia
Email: david.tolpin@gmail.com
Abstract
We cast episodic Markov decision process (MDP) planning as Bayesian
inference over policies. A policy is treated as the latent
variable and is assigned an unnormalized probability of optimality that
is monotone in its expected return, yielding a posterior distribution
whose modes coincide with return-maximizing solutions while posterior
dispersion represents uncertainty over optimal behavior. To approximate
this posterior in discrete domains, we adapt variational sequential Monte
Carlo (VSMC) to inference over deterministic policies under stochastic
dynamics, introducing a sweep that enforces policy consistency across
revisited states and couples transition randomness across particles to
avoid confounding from simulator noise. Acting is performed by posterior
predictive sampling, which induces a stochastic control policy through a
Thompson-sampling interpretation rather than entropy regularization.
Across grid worlds, Blackjack, Triangle Tireworld, and Academic Advising,
we analyze the structure of inferred policy distributions and compare the
resulting behavior to discrete Soft Actor-Critic, highlighting
qualitative and statistical differences that arise from policy-level
uncertainty.
1 Introduction
Casting Markov decision process (MDP) planning as Bayesian policy
inference offers a two-fold benefit.
First, it enables the use of general-purpose Bayesian inference
algorithms for planning, potentially obviating the need for
custom-designed and domain-specific solution methods.
Second, uncertainty about the optimal policy becomes explicit and is
represented as part of the inferred posterior, rather than being
implicitly absorbed into approximation artifacts or heuristic
regularization.
Despite these advantages, formulating MDP planning as Bayesian
inference presents fundamental challenges arising from the special
structure of the planning problem.
Most Bayesian statistical models assume a clear separation between
latent and observable random variables, and treat uncertainty as
purely epistemic—each random variable has a fixed but unknown value.
In contrast, MDP planning involves intrinsic aleatoric uncertainty due
to stochastic environment dynamics and future action realizations.
Moreover, identifying which components of the MDP—states, actions,
trajectories, or policies—should be treated as latent variables, and
which (if any) are observed, is not straightforward.
Several Bayesian formulations of planning and control have been
proposed, including approaches based on control-as-inference,
maximum-entropy reinforcement learning, and active inference
(Dayan and Hinton, 1997; Toussaint and Storkey, 2006; Ziebart, 2010; Levine, 2018).
While these approaches recast planning as probabilistic inference, they
do so by altering the optimization objective to fit standard Bayesian
modeling assumptions. In particular, the expected return is
augmented or replaced by entropy-regularized or evidence-based
objectives, and stochasticity of the resulting policies is introduced
as an intrinsic modeling preference rather than as a consequence of
explicit uncertainty. As a result, the inferred policies are optimal
with respect to surrogate objectives, and uncertainty over policies is
not directly interpretable as posterior uncertainty about the solution
of the original MDP planning problem.

policy inference that preserves the standard MDP objective. The
