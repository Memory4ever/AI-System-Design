Implementation Details.
We used QwenVL-2.5-7B [2] as our baseline due to its strong video understanding ability and open-source availability. We also use this baseline for EVSG generation, including generating the multi-grained dense captions and building the EVSG process. The event number limit for dense caption is set to (5, 10, 15). We set λ1=0.7\lambda_{1}{=}0.7, λ2=0.3\lambda_{2}{=}0.3, λ3=0.6\lambda_{3}{=}0.6 and α=0.3\alpha{=}0.3 for the reward function of GRPO post-training. We also utilise the same baseline for event graph generation and input the generated graph during inference. All experiments are conducted using PyTorch on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, with a maximum prompt length of 4096, response length of 2048, 8 rollouts, a batch size of 16, and 1 training epoch.
Evaluation Metrics.
We adopted task-specific metrics to evaluate reasoning accuracy, grounding precision, and hallucination reduction.
We used Accuracy (Acc), Accuracy@IoU≥\geq0.5 (Acc@0.5) for VQA and R@1,IoU=0.3/0.5 (R1@0.3/0.5) and mIoU for Moment Localization for Rextime. Acc@0.5 measures the fraction of samples for which the model both answers the VQA question correctly and its predicted temporal span overlaps the ground-truth span with IoU ≥\geq0.5.
On VidHalluc-Bench, we compute the Accuracy for the ACH and TSH tests, and Score for the STH test, following the benchmark.
4.2 Comparisons of the state-of-the-arts
RexTime.
Tab. 1 presents the performance of our method compared with state-of-the-art models on the Rextime benchmark. After incorporating with EVSG, GraphThinker achieves significant improvements in mIoU (+11.74%), Accuracy (+10.22%), and Accuracy@IoU≥\geq0.5 (+8.86%), demonstrating that the proposed EVSG effectively captures implicit relationships between events and helps the model understand causal structures more clearly. After further Event Graph-based RL post-training, GraphThinker achieves the best moment localization and competitive VQA accuracy compared to methods [70, 53] that use tools. Notably, VITAL [70] and Timesearch [53] cut long videos into short clips for VQA, which improves accuracy on short videos but results in lower Accuracy@IoU≥\geq0.5, indicating weaker temporal consistent reasoning. In contrast, GraphThinker predicts on full videos, producing higher Accuracy@IoU≥\geq0.5 with grounded in precise temporal spans and achieving more consistent video reasoning. More importantly, GraphThinker surpasses both open- and closed-source state-of-the-art models in Accuracy@IoU≥\geq0.5, outperforming TimeSearch-7B by 1.31% and GPT-4o by 2.08%. These results indicate that, with reinforcement optimization, the model can leverage EVSG to build stronger event-level reasoning, achieving more consistent semantic understanding and temporal localization. Moreover, it helps alleviate reasoning hallucinations caused by the model’s uncertainty in event causal relations.
