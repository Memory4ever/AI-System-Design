6 Discussion
When to use cascades vs. end-to-end models.
Our results suggest a decision framework.
For text-sufficient tasks in clean conditions, cascades are informationally equivalent to current speech LLMs, offering lower latency, cost, and engineering complexity, with independently upgradeable components.
For noisy environments, our Whisper-based cascades prove more robust, losing only 0.5–4.2% at 0 dB SNR vs. 5.9–12.7% for the E2E models tested, though this advantage has been demonstrated with Whisper’s noise-hardened front-end, it should generalize to most modern ASR systems given the widespread adoption of Whisper-adjacent modeling approaches.
The case for E2E architectures rests on tasks with genuine acoustic surplus (emotion, sarcasm, speaker intent), but even there, probing reveals a gap between retaining and exploiting acoustic features.
Breaking cascade equivalence: a path forward.
Our results identify the precise bottleneck: the models have the acoustic information but do not use it.
Pitch and energy are retained through the final layers (R2>0.29R^{2}>0.29), yet erasing these directions barely affects Ultravox on natural-speech tasks (MELD −3.1-3.1%, MUStARD −4.0-4.0%).
The problem is training incentive, not architectural capacity.
Qwen2-Audio shows a larger effect from acoustic erasure (MELD −18.0-18.0%), suggesting its cross-attention architecture partially exploits acoustic information, consistent with its lower cascade equivalence and higher behavioral divergence from its cascade.
Three directions could break this bottleneck.
First, paralinguistic auxiliary losses: a contrastive loss penalizing the model when acoustic features are predictable from text alone could encourage functionally independent acoustic channels.
Architectures like WavLLM [6], which explicitly separate semantic and paralinguistic pathways, provide a structural foundation.
Second, minimal-pair prosodic training: training on audio pairs that differ only in prosody (e.g., sincere vs. sarcastic readings of the same sentence) could force models to attend to paralinguistic cues rather than defaulting to textual shortcuts.
Building on contrastive objectives from self-supervised speech learning [32], a loss that pulls apart representations of prosodically distinct utterances with identical transcripts would directly target the textual bottleneck.
Third, architectural modality separation: cross-attention or dual-stream architectures that maintain a structurally independent acoustic pathway may be necessary for genuine multi-modal reasoning.
Implications for benchmarking.
Our findings show that comparing E2E speech LLMs against cascades requires controls that current evaluation practices lack: backbone matching to isolate architectural effects, per-example behavioral analysis beyond aggregate accuracy, and conditions that actually stress the audio pathway.
A benchmark suite designed to reveal genuine architectural advantages should include: (1) paralinguistic tasks where the label depends on how something is said, not just what is said; (2) noisy and reverberant conditions that stress the audio front-end; and (3) matched-backbone cascade baselines to control for LLM reasoning differences.
Without these controls, aggregate accuracy comparisons risk attributing backbone differences to architectural innovation.
Limitations.
TTS-synthesized speech may underestimate acoustic surplus due to less natural prosody; acoustic erasure results corroborate this, showing larger effects on TTS tasks (Ultravox SST-2 −15.7-15.7%) than on natural-speech tasks (Ultravox MELD −3.1-3.1%).
Both linear probes and LEACE operate in the linear subspace; nonlinearly encoded information would be invisible to both methods.
Mechanistic analysis covers two of the four E2E models; Gemini is API-only, and Phi-4-MM was not probed due to its distinct architecture (Mixture-of-LoRAs with a separate conformer encoder), so the mechanistic conclusions may not generalize to all speech LLM designs.
7 Conclusion
We presented the first systematic test of whether speech LLMs are computationally equivalent to ASR→\rightarrowLLM cascades, combining matched-backbone behavioral testing with mechanistic evidence across two architectures.
Our results reveal a spectrum: Ultravox is near-indistinguishable from its matched cascade on text-sufficient tasks, while Qwen2-Audio genuinely diverges.
LEACE concept erasure confirms text representations are causally necessary in both architectures, collapsing accuracy to near-zero; the pattern of degradation further reveals that connector and cross-attention architectures encode text differently, explaining the behavioral spectrum.
Under noise, our Whisper-based cascades prove substantially more robust than all E2E models tested.
Three takeaways for practitioners.
First, evaluate with matched backbones: mismatched comparisons inflate apparent architectural differences and lead to incorrect conclusions about when end-to-end models add value.
Second, use cascades as the default for text-sufficient tasks: they are informationally equivalent to current speech LLMs in clean conditions, cheaper to serve, and allow independent upgrades to the ASR or LLM component; with Whisper, they are also more noise-robust.
Third, invest in end-to-end architectures only where acoustic surplus exists and can be exploited: current models retain paralinguistic features but fail to leverage them, suggesting that training objectives, not architectures, are the bottleneck.
Until training objectives reward direct exploitation of what audio uniquely provides, speech LLMs will remain implicit cascades, and teams building them should ask whether the modularity, robustness, and cost advantages of an explicit cascade are worth surrendering for architectural elegance that produces the same computation.
