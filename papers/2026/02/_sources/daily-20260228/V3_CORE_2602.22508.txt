Abstract
Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often exhibit structural fragility in complex reasoning tasks, failing to produce correct answers even after successfully deriving valid intermediate steps.
Through systematic analysis, we observe that these failures frequently stem not from a lack of reasoning capacity, but from a deficiency in self-regulatory control, where valid logic is destabilized by uncontrolled exploration or the failure to recognize logical sufficiency.
Motivated by this observation, we propose Metacognitive Behavioral Tuning (MBT), a post-training framework that explicitly injects metacognitive behaviors into the model’s thought process.
MBT implements this via two complementary formulations: (1) MBT-S, which synthesizes rigorous reasoning traces from scratch, and (2) MBT-R, which rewrites the student’s initial traces to stabilize intrinsic exploration patterns.
Experiments across multi-hop QA benchmarks demonstrate that MBT consistently outperforms baselines, achieving notable gains on challenging benchmarks.
By effectively eliminating reasoning collapse, MBT achieves higher accuracy with significantly reduced token consumption, demonstrating that internalizing metacognitive strategies leads to more stable and robust reasoning.

Introduction
Large Language Models (LLMs) have recently evolved into Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by internalizing the chain-of-thought process through large-scale reinforcement learning
(
Xu et al., 2025
;
Zhang et al., 2025
)
.
This paradigm shift unlocks test-time scaling, a new dimension of scaling where performance improves as the model allocates more time to thinking
(
Snell et al., 2024
;
Agarwal et al., 2025a
)
. Modern LRMs demonstrate exceptional proficiency in complex tasks, particularly within STEM domains
(
Jaech et al., 2024
;
Guo et al., 2025
;
Yang et al., 2025
;
Team et al., 2025
)
.

This illustrates a critical deficiency in metacognitive monitoring, where valid logic is overridden by uncontrolled exploration rather than a lack of reasoning capacity.

The substantial proportion of Answer-Inclusive errors reveals that performance gaps frequently stem from a failure to monitor and preserve correct intermediate conclusions, rather than insufficient knowledge or retrieval capacity.
As reasoning becomes a central component of model behavior, the concept of hallucination requires re-evaluation.
Traditionally, hallucination refers to the generation of factually incorrect content
(
Ji et al., 2023
)
.
However, with the emergence of LRMs, explicit internal chains of thought make the underlying logic observable, enabling a process-centric analysis of model failures.
This suggests extending the concept of hallucination from the outcome level to the process level.
Our analysis reveals that failures often arise not from a lack of reasoning capacity, but from a lack of logical control over the problem-solving process.
When models lose this control, they begin to hallucinate invalid logical steps by inventing non-existent constraints or fabricating justifications, which leads to incorrect conclusions.
Our Findings
To quantify the prevalence of these process-level deviations, we analyze reasoning traces from Multi-Hop Question Answering (MHQA) benchmarks.
We begin with a representative example in Figure
1
, generated by Qwen3-4B, which illustrates a recurring failure pattern.
In this retrieval-augmented scenario asking,
“Who is the child of Caroline LeRoy’s spouse?”
, the model successfully retrieves the necessary facts: it identifies the spouse as Daniel Webster and locates his son, Fletcher Webster.
However, despite correctly deriving the answer, the model falls into a self-imposed constraint trap, assuming the child must be from their specific marriage.
The model fails to monitor or verify the validity of this new constraint, leading it to unjustifiably reject the correct candidate.
This example vividly demonstrates that the failure stems not from insufficient capacity, but from a lack of metacognitive monitoring, specifically the failure to critically evaluate its own logical constraints before overriding a valid conclusion.
We demonstrate that this failure pattern occurs at scale across multiple MHQA benchmarks, including MuSiQue
(
Trivedi et al., 2022
)
, HotpotQA
(
Yang et al., 2018
)
, and 2WikiMultiHopQA
(
Ho et al., 2020
)
.
We classify reasoning traces to identify instances where the correct answer is successfully derived during intermediate steps yet discarded in the final output.
We term these cases
answer-inclusive errors
.
To distinguish genuine logical derivation from incidental mentions, such as random guesses or rote enumeration, we employ an LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation.
This approach ensures that the presence of the correct answer is explicitly grounded in relevant retrieved evidence.
Figure
2
reports the prevalence of these verified errors, confirming that a substantial fraction of model failures stem from a critical breakdown in metacognitive monitoring, where the model successfully derives the truth but fails to recognize and preserve it.
Details regarding the specific judge model and evaluation prompts are provided in the Appendix
E.1
.
Motivated by these observations, we characterize two dominant failure modes in LRMs:
overthinking
and
underthinking
.
While prior works in mathematical reasoning defined overthinking as generating an excessive number of solution candidates and underthinking as frequent thought switching that hinders deep exploration
(
Chen et al., 2024
;
Wang et al.,
)
, we refine these definitions to better capture the structural dynamics of reasoning in MHQA.
We define overthinking in correctly answered samples as post-solution stagnation, where the model successfully derives the answer but engages in unproductive redundancy, such as repetitive confirmation or baseless self-doubt that adds no new confidence.
Conversely, we define underthinking as incorrect samples exhibiting pre-solution stagnation, where the reasoning process becomes trapped in a cycle of revisiting previous hypotheses or repeating the same questions, failing to integrate new information or converge on a solution.
Existing post-training paradigms, particularly reinforcement learning methods like RLVR
(
Lambert et al., 2024
;
Shao et al., 2024
;
Yu et al., 2025
;
Liu et al., 2025b
;
Zheng et al., 2025
)
that rely on outcome-based rewards, can inadvertently exacerbate these instability issues.
Given the high prevalence of answer-inclusive errors, assigning rewards solely based on the final output introduces noisy training signals by indiscriminately penalizing the entire trajectory, even when it encompasses valid intermediate steps
(
Deng et al., 2025b
;
Deng et al., 2025a
)
.
Furthermore, because the objective is tied exclusively to the final answer, there is little incentive for the model to optimize the intrinsic quality or stability of the reasoning process, leaving it vulnerable to the uncontrolled deviations described above.
To address these limitations, we propose
M
etacognitive
B
ehavioral
T
uning (
MBT
), a simple and fully automated post-training framework that explicitly injects structured metacognitive behaviors into the reasoning process.
MBT offers flexible data construction strategies, injecting metacognitive behaviors either by synthesizing reasoning traces from scratch or by rewriting existing ones.
By fine-tuning on reasoning traces regenerated to exhibit these behaviors, MBT proactively suppresses both overthinking and underthinking.
Furthermore, MBT serves as a foundational alignment stage that refines the exploration space for subsequent reinforcement learning.
This structural stabilization enables the model to maintain logical stability even when optimized with outcome-based rewards.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that MBT consistently improves accuracy and robustness, effectively closing the gap between the model’s latent capacity and its realized performance.
Our main contributions are as follows:
•
We identify a critical bottleneck in LRMs where valid intermediate reasoning is often destabilized by a deficiency in logic regulation, manifesting as structural failures like overthinking and underthinking. This highlights a significant disparity between the model’s latent reasoning capacity and its ability to effectively monitor and control the progression of its thought process.
•
We propose
Metacognitive Behavioral Tuning (MBT)
, a post-training framework that explicitly injects metacognitive behaviors to stabilize reasoning trajectories. By teaching the model to actively plan, monitor, and verify its logic, MBT refines the exploration space, effectively preventing reasoning collapse and ensuring robust learning during subsequent stages.
•
We introduce two complementary formulations for behavior injection: synthesizing new traces (MBT-S) and rewriting native traces (MBT-R). Our experiments validate that both strategies effectively induce robust metacognitive control, achieving a superior trade-off between accuracy and efficiency across diverse MHQA benchmarks.

Related Works
Process Supervision
Recent research emphasizes supervising intermediate reasoning steps rather than relying solely on sparse outcome rewards.
Process Reward Models (PRMs) have demonstrated that verifying intermediate steps significantly enhances performance in complex reasoning tasks
(
Lightman et al., 2023
;
Uesato et al., 2022
)
.
However, these methods typically depend on training external verifiers or collecting expensive step-level annotations.
In contrast, MBT internalizes this supervision directly into the generation policy, enabling the model to inherently monitor and regulate its trajectory without the computational overhead of external test-time verification.
Cognitive Structures
Integrating cognitive theories into LLMs aims to induce deliberate reasoning.
Approaches like System 2 Attention
(
Weston and Sukhbaatar, 2023
)
and Plan-and-Solve
(
Wang et al., 2023
)
decouple planning from execution, while Self-Refine
(
Madaan et al., 2023
)
, Reflexion
(
Shinn et al., 2023
)
and Chain-of-Verification
(
Dhuliawala et al., 2024
)
introduce iterative self-correction.
Recent works extend this by modeling reasoning as an internal search
(
Xiang et al., 2025
)
or identifying behavioral seeds for RL
(
Gandhi et al., 2025
)
.
However, these methods often focus on discovery or initial seeding rather than stabilizing the entire trajectory, leaving models prone to structural fragility.
In contrast, MBT explicitly targets the post-training regulation of reasoning to ensure stability and efficiency.
Reasoning Distillation
A growing body of work focuses on distilling reasoning rationales rather than just final answers.
Methods such as Distilling Step-by-Step
(
Hsieh et al., 2023
)
and Orca
(
Mukherjee et al., 2023
)
show that learning from rich explanation traces allows smaller models to outperform larger baselines.
Similarly, STaR
(
Zelikman et al., 2022
)
utilizes self-generated rationales to bootstrap reasoning capabilities.
MBT extends this paradigm by distilling not merely the content of reasoning, but the metacognitive structure itself, thereby transferring the ability to regulate the thought process rather than just the logical steps.
Efficient Reasoning
Prior works also address reasoning efficiency.
Inference-time methods like TokenSkip
(
Xia et al., 2025
)
compress traces by skipping low-information tokens, while data-centric approaches like LIMOPro
(
Xiao et al., 2025
)
prune functional steps deemed redundant.
Additionally, recent reinforcement learning strategies such as DLER
(
Liu et al., 2025a
)
and ShorterBetter
(
Yi et al., 2025
)
explicitly optimize reasoning efficiency by incorporating length penalties into the reward structure.
While effective for latency, our experiments suggest that aggressive pruning can compromise stability in complex multi-hop scenarios.

3
Method
In this section, we introduce
M
etacognitive
B
ehavioral
T
uning (MBT), a streamlined post-training framework designed to enhance reasoning stability and efficiency by explicitly injecting metacognitive behaviors into LRMs.
The core objective is to refine how models plan, monitor, and stabilize their reasoning trajectories, preventing the structural failures of overthinking and underthinking.
To accommodate different training needs, MBT provides two complementary data construction strategies, distinguished by their dependency on the student model’s initial reasoning traces.
(1) MBT-S (Synthesis)
constructs reasoning paths from scratch using a teacher model.
This formulation enforces a rigorous metacognitive structure independent of the student’s initial capabilities, effectively serving as a cold start for reasoning behaviors.
(2) MBT-R (Rewriting)
refines the initial reasoning traces generated by the student model.
By identifying and rewriting unstable segments while preserving valid intermediate deductions, this approach aligns the student’s existing knowledge with structured metacognitive control.
The overall MBT pipeline consists of three stages: (1) Acquisition of initial reasoning samples from the student model (for MBT-R), (2) Injection of metacognitive behaviors via teacher-guided synthesis or rewriting, and (3) Internalization through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and subsequent reinforcement learning.

3.2
Metacognitive Reasoning Trace Construction
We now describe how metacognitively grounded reasoning traces are constructed.
Central to both approaches is the injection of a structured reasoning flow comprising five distinct metacognitive phases:
(1) Understanding and Filtering
, where the model explicitly re-states the core goal and filters out irrelevant information to ensure a robust grasp of requirements;
(2) Planning
, where it outlines a high-level strategy and breaks the problem into manageable sub-tasks before deduction;
(3) Execution and Monitoring
, where the model executes steps while actively questioning the logical soundness of each transition;
(4) Self-Correction
, where it simulates doubt upon detecting deviations, identifies logical gaps, and adjusts the path; and
(5) Verification
, where the model rigorously examines the conclusion against edge cases and alternative scenarios to determine whether further exploration is required, ultimately establishing high confidence before deciding to terminate the process.

4.4
Reasoning Efficiency and Stability
We focus our efficiency and stability analysis on MuSiQue, the most challenging benchmark in our evaluation.
Due to its multi-step complexity and high propensity for reasoning collapse, MuSiQue serves as the most rigorous testbed for assessing a model’s ability to control reasoning length and prevent degeneration under stress.
As shown in Table
2
, baseline approaches exhibit significant limitations in balancing efficiency and stability.
Rejection Sampling (RS) tends to amplify redundant reasoning patterns, resulting in bloated average output lengths without proportional accuracy gains.
More critically, efficiency-oriented methods such as TokenSkip and LIMOPro, which rely on artificial pruning or skipping, severely compromise structural stability.
We observe that this heuristic manipulation disrupts reasoning coherence, causing frequent infinite loops (Degeneration Failures).
Paradoxically, this instability inflates the overall inference cost, rendering these methods impractical for complex multi-hop tasks.
Standard GRPO also fails to mitigate this instability; it remains highly vulnerable to reasoning collapse and ineffective at reducing redundancy, particularly at the larger 4B scale.
In contrast, MBT demonstrates superior stability, achieving near-zero degeneration across all model scales.
By internalizing metacognitive regulation rather than applying external constraints, MBT enables the model to recognize logical sufficiency and naturally suppress unnecessary continuation.
MBT thus produces substantially more concise reasoning traces while maintaining high accuracy, resulting in the highest AES among all methods.
These results confirm that MBT offers the most efficient and robust solution for deploying LRMs, effectively eliminating the risk of structural collapse even in the most demanding scenarios.

5
Discussion
In this section, we analyze the mechanisms driving the effectiveness of MBT and address three critical questions regarding its design and impact: (1) Is the performance gain merely a byproduct of distilling a stronger teacher? (2) Does MBT effectively mitigate the structural failures of overthinking and underthinking? (3) Does the framework successfully inject distinct metacognitive behaviors into the model?
Unless otherwise specified, all analytical experiments in this section are conducted using Qwen3-4B on the challenging MuSiQue benchmark.
5.1
Is Metacognitive Behavior Injection Reducible to Reasoning Distillation?
A natural hypothesis is that the improvements achieved by MBT stem solely from distilling the superior reasoning capabilities of the teacher model, regardless of the metacognitive structure.
To test this, we construct a baseline denoted as gpt-oss-distill.
This baseline follows the identical post-training pipeline (SFT followed by GRPO), but utilizes the raw reasoning traces generated by the teacher model without any explicit metacognitive formatting or behavioral injection.
We apply rejection sampling to retain only correct teacher-generated traces.
This setup allows us to isolate the effect of teacher strength from metacognitive regulation.
As shown in Table
3
, gpt-oss-distill achieves high accuracy, in some cases slightly surpassing MBT.
However, this accuracy comes at a significant cost: the model exhibits extreme inefficiency and frequent degeneration, often mimicking the teacher’s verbose exploration without the capacity to terminate effectively.
This results in bloated output lengths and a substantially negative AES, rendering it impractical for real-world deployment.
In contrast, MBT strikes a critical balance.
While maintaining accuracy comparable to the distilled baseline, MBT explicitly injects structural control, enabling the model to reason concisely and stabilize its trajectory.
This results in significantly higher AES and near-zero degeneration.
These findings demonstrate that while raw distillation transfers what to think, MBT is essential for transferring how to regulate that thinking, ensuring that high performance is achieved with the stability and efficiency required for practical application.

This process of reorganizing and refining its own logic creates a richer display of metacognitive dynamics, as the model must actively demonstrate self-correction and integration to align its original exploration with the imposed structure.

6
Conclusion
In this work, we identify a critical limitation in LRMs, where reasoning failures frequently stem not from a lack of capacity, but from inadequate monitoring and control over the logical flow.
Our analysis reveals that correct intermediate reasoning is often discarded or destabilized due to the absence of a regulatory mechanism.
To address this, we propose Metacognitive Behavioral Tuning (MBT), a post-training framework offering two complementary formulations (MBT-S and MBT-R) that inject explicit metacognitive behaviors to stabilize reasoning through rigorous monitoring and logical control.
Comprehensive experiments across various scales of models and diverse MHQA benchmarks demonstrate that MBT consistently enhances reasoning accuracy and efficiency.
Notably, the framework exhibits strong generalization to complex MHQA tasks, effectively tackling problems significantly harder than those seen during training.
Behavioral analyses further confirm that MBT mitigates both underthinking and overthinking, indicating that the model has successfully internalized the capacity to monitor and control its own logic, leading to superior structural stability.
Overall, our results suggest that establishing rigorous metacognitive control is as crucial as scaling raw reasoning capacity, highlighting behavioral supervision as a vital avenue for advancing reasoning models.
Limitations
MBT’s reliance on a large teacher model introduces additional data preparation costs.
While our results show that these traces generalize effectively across model scales, future work is needed to explore lighter-weight trace generation methods and to extend this framework to domains beyond MHQA.
Broadly, we believe that shifting the focus from what to reason (content accumulation) to how to reason (process regulation) offers a promising direction for the next generation of robust reasoning models.
Impact Statements
This work introduces Metacognitive Behavioral Tuning (MBT), a framework designed to transform Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) from capable but fragile systems into stable and efficient reasoners.
The primary societal impact of this work lies in advancing the reliability and interpretability of AI systems.
By enforcing a structured flow of planning, monitoring, and verification, MBT makes the model’s decision-making process more transparent and auditable, which is crucial for deployment in high-stakes domains such as healthcare and legal analysis where “black-box” reasoning is unacceptable.
Furthermore, MBT contributes to sustainable AI deployment.
By effectively eliminating reasoning collapse and redundant loops, our method significantly reduces inference-time token consumption.
This directly translates to lower computational energy requirements and a reduced carbon footprint for large-scale model serving.
However, we acknowledge that enhanced reasoning and planning capabilities carry dual-use risks, as they could potentially be exploited for malicious purposes, such as sophisticated cyber-attacks or social engineering.
While metacognitive regulation improves logical stability, it does not inherently align moral intent.
Therefore, MBT should be viewed as a tool to enhance capability and stability, which must be rigorously complemented by alignment training and human oversight frameworks to ensure safe application.

Appendix A
Answer Inclusion Analysis
Standard evaluation metrics often fail to capture the nuances of the reasoning process of the model.
To address this, we provide a complementary analysis to Figure
2
by quantifying answer inclusion using two distinct methods: strict string-based matching and a semantic LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation.
Tables
A1
–
A3
report results across MuSiQue
(
Trivedi et al., 2022
)
, HotpotQA
(
Yang et al., 2018
)
, and 2WikiMultiHopQA
(
Ho et al., 2020
)
benchmarks, respectively.
We categorize predictions into correct and incorrect cases based on the final output.
For each reasoning trace, we determine whether the gold answer was identified or derived at any intermediate step regardless of the final outcome.
Substring Match
determines whether the gold answer string explicitly appears in the reasoning trace.
While simple and transparent, this method is brittle; it misses paraphrased answers and can yield false positives by matching contextually irrelevant mentions.
To address this limitation, we additionally employ an LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation.
In contrast, the
LLM_judge
follows a more permissive criterion aligned with Figure
2
: the judge outputs “answer inclusive” if the reasoning trace identifies the correct answer (or a semantically equivalent answer) as a valid candidate with any supporting fact, context, or logical connection, even if the model later rejects it.
The judge returns “answer exclusive” only when the gold answer is never mentioned, appears solely as part of an unfocused enumeration, or is explicitly framed as a random guess without supporting context.
Importantly, the judge is configured to detect the presence of the correct answer during reasoning, without evaluating reasoning quality.
The full prompt and decision criteria used by the judge are provided in Figure
A4
.
Our analysis reveals two key insights regarding the reliability of these metrics and the nature of reasoning failures.
First, string matching systematically underestimates reasoning in correct samples.
Even when models correctly predicted the final answer, the gold answer string was missing from the reasoning trace in approximately 3–15% of cases (see Tables
A1
–
A3
).
This result is counter-intuitive: one would expect the correct answer to appear explicitly in the trace.
However, the LLM judge consistently recovers these cases, showing inclusion rates of 98–99%.
This discrepancy indicates that models often rely on internal representations or paraphrasing that strict string matching fails to capture.
Second, incorrect samples often contain the correct answer contextually, but string matching is noisy.
Notably, in incorrect samples, Substring Match often yields higher inclusion rates than the LLM judge (e.g., in Table
A1
, 24.72% vs. 14.78%).
This suggests that simple string matching generates false positives by flagging unrelated mentions of the answer entity.
In contrast, the LLM judge is more discerning; it confirms that while models frequently consider the correct answer (answer inclusion) before discarding it, they do not merely hallucinate it randomly.
Together, these findings reinforce our central motivation for MBT.
The prevalence of verified answer-inclusive errors, detected robustly by the LLM judge, suggests that models often possess the correct knowledge but may struggle to preserve it due to insufficient metacognitive monitoring.

B.1
Reasoning Efficiency Analysis
B.1.1
Extended Degeneration and Length Analysis
Table
A4
provides a granular analysis of reasoning efficiency and structural stability across model scales and benchmarks.
While Table
1
focuses on final-task performance, Table
A4
offers complementary evidence that clarifies how different post-training strategies shape the reasoning process itself.
Reasoning Stability and Degeneration
Across all model sizes (0.6B–4B) and datasets, MBT-S and MBT-R consistently exhibit near-zero degeneration events, standing in sharp contrast to competing baselines.
Efficiency-oriented methods like TokenSkip
(
Xia et al., 2025
)
and LIMOPro
(
Xiao et al., 2025
)
frequently suffer from reasoning collapse, manifesting as infinite loops or repetition, particularly on the complex OOD benchmark, MuSiQue.
This high rate of degeneration paradoxically inflates the average inference cost, rendering these “efficient” methods impractical for complex multi-hop reasoning.
In contrast, MBT prevents such uncontrolled continuation by internalizing metacognitive regulation, ensuring that the model terminates reasoning effectively even under stress.
Efficiency on Correct Samples: The Metacognitive Overhead
A closer inspection of output lengths on correct samples reveals a nuanced trade-off.
On simpler in-distribution benchmarks (HotpotQA), pruning-based baselines often yield shorter traces than MBT.
However, the shortness often comes at the cost of stability.
MBT variants, while occasionally producing slightly longer traces on these easier tasks, do so to execute necessary metacognitive behaviors, such as planning, monitoring, and verification.
We interpret this modest increase not as inefficiency, but as intentional metacognitive overhead required to ensure robustness.
Robustness on OOD and Incorrect Samples
The benefits of this structured approach become decisive on challenging OOD benchmarks.
On MuSiQue dataset, baselines exhibit dramatic length inflation due to reasoning collapse.
For instance, incorrect samples from gpt-oss-distill model often spiral into excessively long, unproductive loops.
MBT substantially mitigates this failure mode; even when the final answer is incorrect, MBT maintains bounded and stable reasoning traces.
This suggests that the injected metacognitive monitoring effectively suppresses runaway exploration, enforcing termination when confidence cannot be established.

The Risk of Unstructured Reinforcement (GRPO-only)
Applying GRPO directly to the base model consistently improves accuracy metrics (EM, F1) across all scales.

This indicates that reinforcement learning without prior structural guidance tends to amplify exploration blindly, leading to unstable and excessive reasoning behaviors.
SFT as a Stabilizing Prior (SFT-only)
In contrast, SFT using MBT-generated traces yields notable accuracy improvements while substantially reducing degeneration and reasoning length compared to GRPO-only baselines.

The most consistent improvements are observed when SFT and GRPO are combined.
In the full MBT configuration, GRPO operates on an already stabilized policy, allowing it to further refine performance without reintroducing instability.
Across all scales, this two-stage approach achieves the optimal balance between accuracy, efficiency, and stability.
These findings highlight a clear division of labor: SFT establishes the metacognitive structure (learning how to reason), while GRPO refines the policy (learning what generates rewards).
The result confirms that naively applying RL leads to structural collapse, whereas MBT ensures robust and efficient generalization.

C.2
Baseline Methods
In the main paper, we compare MBT against representative efficient reasoning baselines, TokenSkip
(
Xia et al., 2025
)
and LIMOPro
(
Xiao et al., 2025
)
.
Both approaches aim to produce compact reasoning traces by selectively retaining important components from model-generated reasoning and then fine-tuning a target model via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) so that it learns to reason more efficiently.
Unlike MBT, these approaches focus on compressing or pruning existing reasoning traces rather than stabilizing intermediate reasoning through metacognitive control.
TokenSkip
TokenSkip first obtains full reasoning traces from a target model. It then estimates token-level importance scores using a bidirectional BERT-based model
(
Xia et al., 2025
;
Pan et al., 2024
)
.
Based on these scores, unimportant tokens are pruned to construct compact reasoning traces, which are subsequently used as SFT data.
Following the TokenSkip, we generate reasoning traces on the HotpotQA training set using the target model Qwen3
(
Yang et al., 2025
)
.
We filter samples based on answer correctness and randomly sample a compression ratio
γ
\gamma
for each training instance from the set {0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9,1.0}.
We prune tokens based on the sampled compression ratio and use the resulting compressed reasoning traces as the SFT dataset for training.
LIMOPro
LIMOPro leverages reasoning traces generated by a model that is more powerful than the target model.
It focuses on pruning functional reasoning components that do not contribute to logical progression, using a perplexity-based importance score.
Specifically, LIMOPro first segments the entire reasoning trace into intermediate steps using an LLM.
It then classifies each step as either logical progression or functional (e.g., validation or error correction).
For steps in the functional group, LIMOPro computes importance scores based on the change in perplexity when removing each step.
Based on these scores, it prunes steps with lower importance and uses the resulting compressed reasoning traces as SFT data.
In our experiments, we generate reasoning traces on the HotpotQA training set using gpt-oss-120B
(
Agarwal et al., 2025b
)
.
We use the same model for step segmentation and step classification.
Following the official LIMOPro implementation, we filter samples based on answer correctness, apply a pruning ratio of 0.5, and use the pruned reasoning traces to construct the SFT dataset for training.

E.1
Answer Inclusion Evaluation Prompt
The answer inclusion prompt (Figure

The evaluation does not assess final-answer correctness or the reasoning quality.
Instead, it applies a lenient criterion that prioritizes detecting the presence of correct intermediate conclusions, even if the model later rejects them.
Conversely, the judge explicitly excludes cases where the correct answer appears only as part of an unfocused enumeration or as a random guess.
The prompt underlines the answer-inclusive error analysis presented in Figure

E.2
Metacognitively Grounded Reasoning Trace Prompt
E.2.1
MBT-S: Metacognitive Trace Synthesis
The synthesis prompt used for MBT-S (Figure
A5
) instructs the teacher model (gpt-oss-120B) to generate reasoning traces from scratch.
Given a query, contextual documents, and the gold answer, the prompt enforces a rigorous five-phase process: goal clarification, planning, execution monitoring, self-correction, and verification.
Although the gold answer is provided as input, the model is instructed to simulate an independent discovery process without referencing the answer key.
The resulting traces serve as high-quality supervision for MBT-S, enabling the student model to internalize explicit metacognitive structures.
E.2.2
MBT-R: Metacognitive Trace Rewriting
The rewriting prompt (Figure
A6
) guides the teacher model (gpt-oss-120B) to refine an existing reasoning trace generated by the student model.
Unlike synthesis, which starts from scratch, this prompt focuses on reorganization.
It explicitly instructs the teacher to preserve valid intermediate deductions while injecting metacognitive behaviors such as monitoring, self-correction, and verification.
When the original trace contains errors, the prompt simulates a realization of error, directing the model to identify the flaw and naturally redirect the trajectory toward the correct solution.
This design ensures that MBT-R stabilizes the initial reasoning traces while retaining informative intermediate steps.
