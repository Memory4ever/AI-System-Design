Affiliation: KAIST Email: hyunwoojkim@kaist.ac.kr\nL53: ###### Abstract\nL54: Despite the remarkable capabilities of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs), they still lack detailed knowledge about specific entities. Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) is a widely adopted solution that enhances LVLMs by providing additional contexts from an external Knowledge Base. However, we observe that previous decoding methods for RAG are sub-optimal as they fail to sufficiently leverage multiple relevant contexts and suppress the negative effects of irrelevant contexts.\nL55: To this end, we propose R elevance-aware M ulti-context C ontrastive D ecoding (RMCD), a novel decoding method for RAG. RMCD outputs a final prediction by combining outputs predicted with each context, where each output is weighted based on its relevance to the question. By doing so, RMCD effectively aggregates useful information from multiple relevant contexts while also counteracting the negative effects of irrelevant ones.\nL56: Experiments show that RMCD consistently outperforms other decoding methods across multiple LVLMs, achieving the best performance on three knowledge-intensive visual question-answering benchmarks. Also, RMCD can be simply applied by replacing the decoding method of LVLMs without additional training. Analyses also show that RMCD is robust to the retrieval results, consistently performing the best across the weakest to the strongest retrieval results.\nL57: Code is available at cite38†https://github.com/mlvlab/RMCD†github.com .\nL58: ## 1 Introduction\nL59: Despite the remarkable performance of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) [cite39†33 , cite40†12 , cite41†41 , cite42†40 ] in various vision-language tasks, including image captioning [cite43†35 , cite44†1 ], visual question answering [cite45†17 , cite46†43 , cite47†22 ] and more, these models often lack detailed knowledge about specific objects.\nL60: Pointing out the problem, knowledge-intensive visual question answering (VQA) benchmarks [cite46†43 , cite48†51 , cite49†23 , cite50†7 , cite51†45 , cite52†20 ] have been proposed, requiring detailed knowledge about specific images. For example, a question might be, “When was this tower built?” when the Eiffel Tower is present in an image.\nL61: cite53†Image: Refer to caption Figure 1: Motivation. RMCD generates better responses without additional training by contrasting outputs based on relevance of contexts, thereby effectively leveraging multiple relevant contexts and counteracting irrelevant contexts.\nL62: Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) [cite54†31 ] is a widely adopted solution to the problem, enhancing LVLMs by providing additional contexts retrieved from an external Knowledge Base (KB). A typical RAG pipeline consists of two stages: a retrieval stage and a generation stage. In the retrieval stage, multiple contexts with the highest retrieval scores are retrieved. In the generation stage, the final answer is generated through the decoding process of LVLMs, based on retrieved contexts.\nL63: We observe that current decoding methods adopted by LVLMs [cite55†36 , cite56†37 , cite57†38 , cite58†52 , cite59†39 ] for generation are sub-optimal since they fail to sufficiently leverage relevant contexts, and are disturbed by irrelevant contexts. In detail, a decoding method taking only a single context as input significantly degrades as retrieval quality gets worse, as it cannot leverage information from multiple relevant contexts.\nL64: On the other hand, while decoding methods taking multiple contexts as input aggregate information from multiple contexts, they are negatively influenced by irrelevant contexts. Thus, we emphasize the importance of a decoding method that effectively leverages multiple relevant contexts while also successfully handling irrelevant contexts.\nL65: In this paper, we propose a novel decoding method for RAG, based on the idea of Contrastive Decoding (CD) [cite60†34 , cite61†46 ], which was initially proposed to enhance the generation results of Large Language Models. CD is an idea of contrasting two different outputs. It can amplify or counteract the effect of a specific output, which we refer to as ‘reflection’ and ‘deflection’.\nL66: Motivated by the idea of CD, we present a simple baseline named Single CD (SCD) for the RAG framework, which amplifies the effect of a context with the highest retrieval score by contrasting the outputs obtained with and without the context. SCD improves RAG by amplifying the context’s effect, showing the potential of a reflection in improving RAG with promising retrieval results.\nL67: Still, it suffers from significant performance loss when retrieval results get worse, as it cannot leverage multiple relevant contexts.\nL68: To this end, we extend the idea of SCD and propose R elevance-aware M ulti-context C ontrastive D ecoding (RMCD), an effective decoding method for RAG that properly reflects and deflects multiple retrieved contexts depending on their relevance to a question. RMCD outputs a final prediction by combining the predictions obtained with each context. The weight of each context for combining outputs is adaptively determined based on its retrieval score.\nL69: Higher weights are assigned to contexts with high scores, while lower or even negative weights are assigned to contexts with low scores. Consequently, RMCD outputs the final prediction by reflecting relevant contexts and deflecting irrelevant contexts with high and low retrieval scores. Compared to previous decoding methods, RMCD better leverages multiple relevant contexts while also counteracting irrelevant context, thereby generating better responses (Fig. cite62†1 ).\nL70: Results on three knowledge-intensive VQA benchmarks, InfoSeek [cite50†7 ], Encyclopedic VQA [cite51†45 ], and OK-VQA [cite46†43 ] show the effectiveness of RMCD as a decoding method for RAG, consistently outperforming other decoding methods applied to seven different LVLMs. RMCD integrates seamlessly into existing LVLMs by simply replacing their decoding methods, without additional training.\nL71: Moreover, it has the lowest time complexity among methods processing the same number of contexts, achieving a 33% throughput gain alongside performance improvements (see Table cite63†3 ), when applied to the state-of-the-art method on OK-VQA.\nL72: Our analyses also demonstrate that RMCD is robust to the results from the retrieval stage. It performs the best regardless of the retrieval quality, across the weakest to the strongest retrieval results, even including the case where the oracle context is always provided. Such results show the importance of RMCD, which enhances LVLMs robust to the results from the retrieval stage.\nL73: \nL74: In sum, our contributions are three-fold:\nL75: \nL76:   * •\nL77: We observe that current decoding methods for Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) are insufficiently leveraging multiple relevant contexts and are impacted by irrelevant contexts.\nL78: \nL79:   * •\nL80: \nL81: We propose Relevance-aware Multi-context Contrastive Decoding (RMCD), a training-free decoding method improving RAG by contrasting multiple contexts based on their relevance to a question, thereby effectively reflecting relevant contexts and deflecting irrelevant contexts.\nL82: \nL83:   * •\nL84: RMCD outperforms other decoding methods on three knowledge-intensive visual question-answering benchmarks when applied to various models, and is also robust to the quality of retrieval results.\nL85: ## 2 Related Works\nL86: ### 2.1 LVLMs and Contrastive Decoding (CD)\nL87: Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) [cite64†8 , cite39†33 , cite40†12 , cite41†41 , cite65†57 , cite66†61 , cite67†6 , cite68†26 ] have shown superior performance in various multimodal tasks [cite44†1 , cite45†17 , cite47†22 , cite69†27 , cite70†29 , cite71†24 ]. Given a set of input tokens that consist of image features, instructions, and generation results from previous timesteps, LVLMs generate output sequences via autoregressive decoding, which is an iterative process of predicting the next token.\nL88: Contrastive Decoding [cite60†34 , cite61†46 ] is an improved decoding scheme further enhancing the original prediction result by contrasting it with a prediction from a model with a different size [cite60†34 ], from an intermediate layer within the same model [cite72†10 ], or from a model conditioned with modulated inputs [cite73†25 , cite74†30 , cite75†60 ] (e.g. perturbed instruction or image).\nL89: We extend CD to contrast outputs across multiple contexts, with contrast strength and direction guided by each context’s relevance to the question.\nL90: ### 2.2 Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG)\nL91: Recently, multiple knowledge-intensive visual question answering benchmarks [cite46†43 , cite48†51 , cite49†23 , cite50†7 , cite51†45 , cite52†20 ] have been proposed to test models’ capability of answering detailed knowledge. As a solution, retrieval-augmented visual question answering have been proposed [cite76†18 , cite59†39 , cite58†52 , cite77†21 , cite55†36 , cite56†37 , cite57†38 ], based on the idea of retrieval-augmented generation [cite54†31 , cite78†47 ], which provides externally retrieved knowledge to models.\nL92: However, effectively utilizing retrieved contexts while preventing the negative effects of those contexts is a crucial challenge as providing irrelevant contexts may even degrade generation quality [cite79†2 , cite80†53 , cite81†58 ]. Previous works address this issue with training-based strategies [cite76†18 , cite56†37 , cite77†21 , cite82†5 , cite81†58 ], which require a significant computational cost.\nL93: In this paper, we propose a novel decoding strategy named Relevance-aware Multi-context Contrastive Decoding (RMCD), enabling better utilization of contexts by contrasting multiple contexts without additional training.\nL94: ## 3 Method\nL95: ### 3.1 Preliminaries\nL96: In this paper, we denote a Large Vision Language Model (LVLM) parameterized by $\\theta$ as $\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}$. Decoding of an LVLM is an iterative process of generating the most probable sequence through multiple timesteps. At each timestep $i$, an LVLM takes image features $I$, instruction $x$ (including a question), and generated sequences from previous timesteps $y_{<i}$ as inputs.\nL97: Given inputs, the LVLM predicts a logit distribution $\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i})$, which is converted into a probability distribution by softmax as follows:\nL98:  | $$p(y_{i})=\\text{Softmax}(\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i})),$$  |  | (1)\nL99: \nL100: where $p(y_{i})\\in\\mathbb{R}^{|\\mathcal{V}|}$ is a vector with a dimension of $\\mathcal{|V|}$, where $\\mathcal{V}$ is a vocabulary set. In the following sections, we refer to this decoding process as ‘unconditional’ decoding as it takes no retrieved contexts as inputs.\nL101: Retrieval-augmented Generation. To address knowledge-intensive visual question answering tasks, Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) [cite54†31 ] is widely adopted. RAG complements the model by providing contexts retrieved from an external Knowledge Base (KB) that are relevant to the question. In the retrieval stage, textual contexts (e.g. Wikipedia article) from the KB that are relevant to the question are retrieved with off-the-shelf (e.g. CLIP [cite83†48 ]) or fine-tuned retrievers [cite55†36 , cite57†38 ].\nL102: Given an input image $I$ and an instruction $x$ including a question, a query $q$ is set as an image $I$ [cite83†48 ] or an image with a question as $q=(I,x)$ [cite55†36 , cite57†38 ], depending on the retriever. Then, a retriever measures the relevance between a query $q$ and every context $c_{j}$ in KB as:\nL103:  | $$s_{c_{j}}=\\text{Retriever}(q;c_{j}).$$  |  | (2)\nL104: The retrieved context set $\\mathcal{C}$ is then defined, which consists of $n$ contexts with the highest retrieval scores, where elements in the set are sorted in descending order by their scores. After the retrieval stage, the generation stage follows, where an LVLM generates a response considering retrieved contexts. The decoding process is the process of generating the final probability distribution with retrieved contexts $\\mathcal{C}$, in which those are fed into an LVLM as additional inputs as:\nL105:  | $$p(y_{i})=\\text{Softmax}(\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i},\\mathcal{C})),$$  |  | (3)\nL106: \nL107: where information from a part or whole context in $\\mathcal{C}$ is aggregated, depending on the decoding method.\nL108: cite84†Image: Refer to caption Figure 2: Relevance-aware Multi-context Contrastive Decoding (RMCD). An LVLM $\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}$ outputs multiple predictions $q(y_{i}|c_{j})$ provided contexts $c_{j}\\in\\mathcal{C}^{r}$ retrieved from a knowledge base. Using the retrieval score $s_{c_{j}}$ of a context, a context weight $\\alpha_{j}$ is obtained. Then, RMCD combines multiple predictions by contrasting multiple contexts, where each prediction is weighted by $\\alpha_{j}$.\nL109: Thereby, outputs with high $\\alpha_{j}$ are effectively reflected while outputs with low $\\alpha_{j}$ are deflected as illustrated in the figure.\nL110: ### 3.2 Baseline: Single-context Contrastive Decoding\nL111: \nL112: Here, we present a simple adoption of CD into the decoding process of RAG. We define a simple decoding strategy that contrasts the difference between an unconditional logit and a logit conditioned on a context $c_{1}$, as follows:\nL113: \nL114:  | $$\\begin{split}p(y_{i})=\\text{Softmax}(&\\alpha_{1}\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i},c_{1})\\\\\nL115: &-\\alpha_{2}\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i})),\\end{split}$$  |  | (4)\nL116: where $\\alpha_{1}$ and $\\alpha_{2}$ are hyperparameters, and $c_{1}\\in\\mathcal{C}$ is a retrieved context with the highest retrieval score. We refer to the decoding strategy as Single-Context Contrastive Decoding (SCD), as it leverages a difference between an unconditional logit and a single logit with a context.\nL117: Through experiments, we observe that SCD outperforms most of the previous decoding methods when retrieval results are promising, as actively reflecting contexts is largely helpful in such cases (Tab. cite85†1 ). However, we also observe that SCD significantly degrades as the retrieval quality gets worse (Fig. cite86†5 ). This limitation shows that SCD cannot effectively handle noisy retrieval, as it relies on a fixed contrast strength with only one context rather than dynamically leveraging multiple contexts.\nL118: ### 3.3 Relevance-aware Multi-context Contrastive Decoding (RMCD)\nL119: To this end, we propose a R elevance-aware M ulti-context C ontrastive D ecoding (RMCD), a training-free method effectively leveraging multiple contexts by adaptively contrasting multiple logits obtained for each context based on their relevance to a question. To do so, context weights, which determine the degree of reflection and deflection of each context, are firstly obtained based on their retrieval scores (Eq. (cite87†7 )).\nL120: Then, the final prediction is defined as a combination of logits, where each logit is weighted by its corresponding context weight (Eq. (cite88†8 )). Consequently, RMCD more effectively reflects the influence of relevant contexts, while deflecting the effect of irrelevant contexts. The overall pipeline of RMCD is illustrated in Fig. cite89†2 .\nL121: Relevance-aware multi-context contrastive decoding. To predict a probability distribution at timestep $i$, RMCD utilizes contexts from the retrieved context set $\\mathcal{C}$, where contexts $c_{j}\\in\\mathcal{C}$ are sorted in descending order by the retrieval score $s_{c_{j}}$. Note that RMCD includes an empty context $c_{n+1}$ with $s_{c_{n+1}}$ set to $-\\infty$ in $\\mathcal{C}$, which corresponds to an unconditional generation.\nL122: In the supplementary material, we report results with other design choices in determining $s_{c_{n+1}}$, while we empirically found $-\\infty$ worked the best. For every context $c_{j}\\in\\mathcal{C}$, a predicted logit is first obtained as:\nL123:  | $$q(y_{i}|c_{j})=\\mathcal{M}_{\\theta}(y_{i}|I,x,y_{<i},c_{j}),$$  |  | (5)\nL124: where $q(y_{i}|c_{j})\\in\\mathbb{R}^{|\\mathcal{V}|}$ is a vector of a predicted logit distribution provided a context $c_{j}$. Note that inputs other than the context $c_{j}$ in $q(\\cdot)$ are omitted for conciseness in the rest of the paper. Then, the final prediction logit in RMCD is defined as a weighted sum of predicted logits $q(y_{i}|c_{j})$, with each logit weighted by a context weight $\\alpha_{j}$.\nL125: Here, we introduce the process of determining $\\alpha_{j}$ of each context $c_{j}$ using retrieval score $s_{c_{j}}$. To calculate $\\alpha_{j}$, we first calculate relative context scores $w_{c_{j}}$ using $s_{c_{j}}$ as below:\nL126:  | $$w_{c_{j}}=\\frac{\\exp(s_{c_{j}}/\\tau_{1})}{\\sum_{k=1}^{|\\mathcal{C}|}\\exp(s_{c_{k}}/\\tau_{1})},$$  |  | (6)\nL127: where $\\tau_{1}$ is a hyperparameter determining the softmax temperature. Calculated relative context score $w_{c_{j}}$ implies the relative relevance of a context compared to other retrieved contexts in $\\mathcal{C}$. Then, based on $w_{c_{j}}$, the context weight $\\alpha_{j}$ is obtained with the mapping process below. Note that the transformation below allows $\\alpha_{j}$ to have a negative value, thus enabling deflection of information from contexts with low scores. The process is defined as:\nL128:  | $$\\alpha_{j}=M-\\delta\\left(\\frac{w_{c_{1}}-w_{c_{j}}}{w_{c_{1}}}\\right),$$  |  | (7)\nL129: where $\\delta=(M-m)$ is a gap between two hyperparameters, $M$ and $m$, which determine the maximum and minimum context weights, respectively. Note that $m$ is set as a negative value to assign negative weights to $\\alpha_{j}$. Based on the formula above, $\\alpha_{j}$ is defined with a ratio between $w_{c_{j}}$ and $w_{c_{1}}$, which are relative scores of the context $c_{j}$ and the most likely context $c_{1}$. If $w_{c_{j}}$ is close to $w_{c_{1}}$, $\\alpha_{j}$ is set close to $M$.\nL130: On the contrary, if $w_{c_{j}}$ is significantly smaller than $w_{c_{1}}$, $\\alpha_{j}$ is set close to $m$. Then, softmax is applied to the weighted sum of logits to form a predicted probability distribution at the timestep $i$. The decoding process of RMCD is formally defined as:\nL131:  | $$p(y_{i})=\\text{Softmax}(\\sum_{j=1}^{|\\mathcal{C}|}\\alpha_{j}q(y_{i}|c_{j})).$$  |  | (8)\nL132: \nL133: With RMCD, a larger weight is applied to a prediction logit with relatively higher retrieval scores. In comparison, a smaller or even negative weight is applied to a logit with lower retrieval scores, actively reflecting relevant contexts and deflecting irrelevant contexts’ influence.\nL134: Ensembled plausibility constraint. Although RMCD helps effective utilization of contexts by adjusting the probabilities of vocabularies to be sampled, sampling from the entire vocabulary $v\\in\\mathcal{V}$ may be sub-optimal, as shown in [cite60†34 ], since some vocabularies are highly unlikely to be generated.\nL135: Thus, we develop an ensembled plausibility constraint restricting the plausible vocabulary set to sample from, based on ensembled predictions of constraint contexts, expanding the idea of a plausibility constraint [cite60†34 ]. To apply the ensembled plausibility constraint, we first obtain constrained logits with $c_{j}\\in\\mathcal{C}$ as below:\nL136:  | $$\\tilde{q}(y_{i}|c_{j})_{v}=\\begin{cases}q(y_{i}|c_{j})_{v}&\\text{if }v\\in\\mathcal{S}_{i},\\\\\nL137: -\\infty&\\text{if }v\\notin\\mathcal{S}_{i},\\end{cases}$$  |  | (9)\nL138: where $\\mathcal{S}_{i}\\in\\mathcal{V}$ is a subset of the whole vocabulary set $\\mathcal{V}$ consisting only of plausible vocabularies at $i$-th timestep, in which the detailed process to obtain $\\mathcal{S}_{i}$ will be elaborated shortly after. Given an original logit $q(y_{i}|c_{j})$, a constrained logit $\\tilde{q}(y_{i}|c_{j})$ is obtained by replacing the $v$-th entry of the logit with $-\\infty$ if the corresponding vocabulary is not in the plausible set $\\mathcal{S}_{i}$.\nL139: Then, the final decoding process of RMCD with constraints is defined as:\nL140:  | $$p(y_{i})=\\text{Softmax}(\\sum_{j=1}^{|\\mathcal{C}|}\\alpha_{j}\\tilde{q}(y_{i}|c_{j})),$$  |  | (10)\nL141: \nL142: where the decoding process is identical to Eq. (cite88†8 ), except that the original logit $q(y_{i}|c_{j})$ is replaced with the constrained logit $\\tilde{q}(y_{i}|c_{j})$. With constraints, only the vocabularies in $\\mathcal{S}_{i}$ are allowed to be sampled, since the probability is set to zero otherwise.\nL143: From now on, we elaborate on the detailed process to obtain $\\mathcal{S}_{i}$. First, we define a constraint context set $\\mathcal{C}^{c}:=\\{c_{k}\\in\\mathcal{C}\\ |\\ w_{c_{k}}\\geq\\gamma\\}$, which is a subset of $\\mathcal{C}$ consisting of contexts with a relative context score $w_{c_{k}}$ larger than the score threshold $\\gamma$. Then, the relative importance of each constraint context is calculated as:\nL144:  | $$\\tilde{w}_{c_{k}}=\\frac{\\exp(s_{c_{k}}/\\tau_{2})}{\\sum_{l=1}^{|\\mathcal{C}^{c}|}\\exp(s_{c_{l}}/\\tau_{2})},$$  |  | (11)\nL145: where $\\tau_{2}$ is a hyperparameter for the softmax temperature. Note that the softmax is applied only to contexts in the constraint context set $\\mathcal{C}^{c}$ rather than the whole retrieved context set $\\mathcal{C}$.
