<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">People are integrating AI tools into their daily routines at a pace that would have been difficult to predict even a year ago. But adoption alone doesn&#x27;t tell us much about the impact of these tools. <strong>A further, equally important question is: as AI becomes part of everyday life, are individuals developing the skills to use it well?</strong></p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">Previous Anthropic Education Reports have studied how <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_k2nmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">university students<span id="_R_k2nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a> and <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_142nmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-educators-use-claude" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">educators<span id="_R_142nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a> use Claude. We found that students use it to create reports and analyze lab results; educators use it to build lesson materials and automate routine work. But we know that <em>any</em> person who uses AI is likely to improve at what they do. We wanted to explore this further, and to understand how people using AI develop &quot;fluency&quot; with this technology over time.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">In this report, we begin answering that question. We track the presence or absence of a taxonomy of behaviors that we take to represent AI fluency across a large sample of anonymized conversations.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">In line with our recent <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_k4nmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-primitives" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">Economic Index<span id="_R_k4nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>, we find that the most common expression of AI fluency is <em>augmentative</em>—treating AI as a thought partner, rather than delegating work entirely. In fact, these conversations exhibit more than double the number of AI fluency behaviors than quick, back-and-forth chats.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">But we also find that when AI produces artifacts—including apps, code, documents, or interactive tools—users are <em>less</em> likely to question its reasoning (-3.1 percentage points) or identify missing context (-5.2pp). This aligns with related patterns we observed in our <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_145nmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">recent study on coding skills<span id="_R_145nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">These initial findings present us with a baseline that we can use to study the development of AI fluency over time.</p>
<h2 id="measuring-ai-fluency" class="group/heading mt-12 font-heading text-[1.375rem] font-medium tracking-tight first:mt-0 scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Measuring AI fluency<a href="#measuring-ai-fluency" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">To quantify AI fluency, we use the <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_k8nmd35kq_" href="/collections/collaborating-with-ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">4D AI Fluency Framework<span id="_R_k8nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>, developed by Professors Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller in collaboration with Anthropic. This framework helps us define 24 specific behaviors that we take to exemplify safe and effective human-AI collaboration.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">Of these 24 behaviors, 11 (listed in the graph below) are directly observable when humans interact with Claude on Claude.ai or Claude Code. The other 13 (including things like being honest about AI&#x27;s role in work, or considering the consequences of sharing AI-generated output), happen outside Claude.ai&#x27;s chat interface, so they&#x27;re much harder for us to track. These unobservable behaviors are arguably some of the most consequential dimensions of AI fluency, so in future work we plan to use qualitative methods to assess them.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">For this study, we focused on the 11 directly observable behaviors. We used our <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_kanmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">privacy-preserving analysis tool<span id="_R_kanmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a> to study 9,830 conversations that included several back-and-forths with Claude on Claude.ai during a 7-day window in January 2026.<sup>1</sup> We then measured the presence or absence of the 11 behaviors; each conversation could display evidence of multiple behaviors. We assessed the reliability of our sample by checking whether our results were consistent across each day of the week, and across the different languages in our sample (we found that they were).<sup>2</sup> This, finally, gave us the AI Fluency Index: a baseline measurement of how people collaborate with AI today, and a foundation for tracking how those behaviors evolve over time as models change.</p>

<h2 id="results" class="group/heading mt-12 font-heading text-[1.375rem] font-medium tracking-tight first:mt-0 scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Results<a href="#results" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">With our first study, we&#x27;ve found two main patterns in Claude use: a strong relationship between AI fluency and iteration and refinement through longer conversations with Claude, and changes in users&#x27; fluency behaviors when coding or building other outputs.</p>
<h3 id="fluency-is-strongly-associated-with-conversations-that-exhibit-iteration-and-refinement" class="group/heading pt-2 font-heading text-lg font-semibold scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Fluency is strongly associated with conversations that exhibit iteration and refinement<a href="#fluency-is-strongly-associated-with-conversations-that-exhibit-iteration-and-refinement" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h3>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">One of the strongest patterns in the data is the relationship between iteration and refinement and every other AI fluency behavior. 85.7% of the conversations in our sample exhibited iteration and refinement: building on previous exchanges to refine the user&#x27;s work, rather than accepting the first response and moving to a new task. These conversations showed substantially higher rates of other fluency behaviors, as the chart below shows:</p>
<figure class="my-6 flex flex-col gap-2"><button type="button" aria-haspopup="dialog" aria-label="Enlarge image" aria-describedby="_R_2gnmd35kq_" class="group/zoom relative block max-w-full cursor-zoom-in rounded-lg transition-shadow duration-fast focus-visible:shadow-focus focus-visible:outline-hidden w-full"><img loading="lazy" id="_R_2gnmd35kq_" src="/assets/media/0bd12db879f7b56eabf487d504d83d3c88586384ff1cecd5442978edc467cc50.png" alt="Grouped bar chart titled &#x27;Iteration and refinement&#x27; comparing behavior rates in conversations with iteration versus without iteration: clarifies goal 54.5% vs 30.9% (+23.6pp); provides examples 44.4% vs 21.8% (+22.6pp); identifies missing context 22.8% vs 5.7% (+17.1pp); specifies format 32.4% vs 16.1% (+16.3pp); questions reasoning 17.9% vs 3.2% (+14.7pp)." class="block h-auto w-full rounded-[inherit]"/><span aria-hidden="true" class="pointer-events-none absolute right-2 top-2 inline-flex size-8 items-center justify-center rounded-md bg-surface-1 text-primary shadow-lg transition-opacity duration-fast md:opacity-0 md:group-hover/zoom:opacity-100 md:group-focus-visible/zoom:opacity-100 pointer-coarse:opacity-100"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></span></button><figcaption class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-muted"><p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><em>Behavioral indicator prevalence in conversations where the user iterates and refines (n=8,424) versus conversations without iteration and refinement (n=1,406). All behaviors are substantially more prevalent in conversations with iteration and refinement.</em></p></figcaption></figure>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">On average, conversations with iteration and refinement exhibit 2.67 additional fluency behaviors—roughly double the non-iterative rate of 1.33. This is especially pronounced for fluency behaviors related to evaluating Claude&#x27;s outputs. Conversations with iteration and refinement are 5.6x more likely to involve users questioning Claude&#x27;s reasoning, and 4x more likely to see them identify missing context.</p>
<h3 id="when-creating-outputs-users-become-more-directive-but-less-evaluative" class="group/heading pt-2 font-heading text-lg font-semibold scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">When creating outputs, users become more directive but less evaluative<a href="#when-creating-outputs-users-become-more-directive-but-less-evaluative" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h3>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">12.3% of conversations in our sample involved artifacts, including code, documents, interactive tools, and other outputs. In these conversations, people collaborated with AI quite differently.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">Specifically, we found substantially higher rates of behaviors that fall within the broader themes of &quot;description&quot; and &quot;delegation.&quot; For instance, these conversations are more likely to see users clarify their goal (+14.7pp), specify a format (+14.5pp), provide examples (+13.4pp), and iterate (+9.7pp) compared to non-artifact conversations. In other words, they&#x27;re doing more to direct AI at the outset of their work.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">But this directiveness doesn&#x27;t correspond with greater levels of evaluation or discernment. <strong>In fact, it&#x27;s the opposite: in conversations where artifacts are created, users are <em>less</em> likely to identify missing context (-5.2pp), check facts (-3.7pp), or question the model&#x27;s reasoning by asking it to explain its rationale (-3.1pp).</strong> Our <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_14lnmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">Economic Index<span id="_R_14lnmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a> finds that—unsurprisingly—the most complex tasks are where Claude struggles the most, so this seems particularly noteworthy.</p>
<figure class="my-6 flex flex-col gap-2"><button type="button" aria-haspopup="dialog" aria-label="Enlarge image" aria-describedby="_R_2mnmd35kq_" class="group/zoom relative block max-w-full cursor-zoom-in rounded-lg transition-shadow duration-fast focus-visible:shadow-focus focus-visible:outline-hidden w-full"><img loading="lazy" id="_R_2mnmd35kq_" src="/assets/media/f0fab654d7d2116a512dd642554ba480e27840466cefaf56dba6536922df1915.png" alt="Grouped bar chart titled &#x27;The artifact effect&#x27; comparing behavior rates in conversations with artifacts versus without artifacts: refines and iterates 94.1% vs 84.5% (+9.7pp); clarifies goal 64% vs 49.3% (+14.7pp); provides examples 52.9% vs 39.5% (+13.4pp); specifies format 42.8% vs 28.3% (+14.5pp); checks facts 5.5% vs 9.2% (-3.7pp); questions reasoning 13% vs 16.2% (-3.1pp); identifies missing context 15.8% vs 21% (-5.2pp)." class="block h-auto w-full rounded-[inherit]"/><span aria-hidden="true" class="pointer-events-none absolute right-2 top-2 inline-flex size-8 items-center justify-center rounded-md bg-surface-1 text-primary shadow-lg transition-opacity duration-fast md:opacity-0 md:group-hover/zoom:opacity-100 md:group-focus-visible/zoom:opacity-100 pointer-coarse:opacity-100"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></span></button><figcaption class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-muted"><p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><em>Behavioral indicator prevalence in conversations with artifacts (n=1,209) versus without artifacts (n=8,621). Description and delegation behaviors increase in artifact conversations, while all three discernment behaviors decrease.</em></p></figcaption></figure>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">There are several possible explanations for this pattern. It might be that Claude is creating polished, functional-looking outputs, for which it doesn&#x27;t seem necessary to question things further: if the work <em>looks</em> finished, users might treat it as such. But it&#x27;s also possible that artifact conversations involve tasks where factual precision matters less than aesthetics or functionality (designing a UI, for instance, versus writing a legal analysis). Or users might be evaluating artifacts through channels we can&#x27;t observe—running code, testing an app elsewhere, sharing a draft with a colleague—rather than expressing their evaluation within that same initial conversation.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">Whatever the explanation, the pattern is worth paying attention to. As AI models become increasingly capable of producing polished-looking outputs, the ability to critically evaluate those outputs, whether in direct conversation or through other means, will become more valuable rather than less.</p>
<h2 id="developing-your-own-ai-fluency" class="group/heading mt-12 font-heading text-[1.375rem] font-medium tracking-tight first:mt-0 scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Developing your own AI fluency<a href="#developing-your-own-ai-fluency" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">As with all skills, AI fluency is a matter of degree—for most of us, it&#x27;s possible to develop our techniques much further. Based on the patterns in our data, there are three areas where we&#x27;ve found many users could improve their skills:</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Staying in the conversation.</strong> Iteration and refinement is the single strongest correlate of all other fluency behaviors in our data. So, when you get an initial response, it&#x27;s worth treating it as only a starting point: ask follow-up questions, push back on any parts that don&#x27;t feel right, and refine what you&#x27;re looking for.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Questioning polished outputs.</strong> When AI models produce something that <em>looks</em> good, it&#x27;s the perfect moment to pause and ask: is this accurate? Is anything missing? Does this reasoning hold up? As we discussed above, our data show that polished outputs coincide with lower rates of critical evaluation, even though users go to greater lengths to direct Claude&#x27;s work at the outset.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Setting the terms of the collaboration.</strong> In only 30% of conversations do users tell Claude how they&#x27;d like it to interact with them. Try being explicit by adding instructions like, &quot;Push back if my assumptions are wrong,&quot; &quot;Walk me through your reasoning before giving me the answer,&quot; or, &quot;Tell me what you&#x27;re uncertain about.&quot; Establishing these expectations up front can change the dynamic of the rest of the conversation.</p>
<h2 id="limitations" class="group/heading mt-12 font-heading text-[1.375rem] font-medium tracking-tight first:mt-0 scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Limitations<a href="#limitations" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">This research comes with important caveats:</p>
<ul class="flex list-disc flex-col gap-xs pl-lg">
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Sample limitations:</strong> Our sample reflects Claude.ai users who engaged in multi-turn conversations during a single week in January 2026. Since we think this is still relatively early on in the diffusion of AI tools, these users likely skew towards early adopters who are already comfortable with AI—i.e., who may not represent the broader population. Our sample should be understood as providing a baseline for <em>this</em> population, not as a universal benchmark. Because the data comes from a single week, it is also unable to capture any seasonal or longitudinal effects. And because it&#x27;s focused on <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_kl0nmd35kq_" href="https://claude.ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">Claude.ai<span id="_R_kl0nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>, we don&#x27;t capture how users interact with other AI platforms.</li>
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Partial framework coverage:</strong> In this study, we only assessed the 11 of the 24 behavioral indicators that are directly observable in conversations on Claude.ai. All behaviors related to the responsible and ethical use of AI outputs occur outside of these conversations, and are not captured.</li>
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Binary classification:</strong> For each conversation in our sample, we classify each behavior as either present or absent. But this likely misses significant nuance—like arguable or partial demonstrations of behaviors, or overlapping signals between them.</li>
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Implicit behaviors:</strong> Users might demonstrate fluency behaviors mentally (such as fact-checking Claude&#x27;s claims against their own knowledge) without expressing these behaviors in conversation. This seems especially relevant for our data on artifacts—users might be evaluating Claude&#x27;s outputs through testing and practical use, rather than through conversation-visible behaviors.</li>
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary"><strong>Correlational findings:</strong> The relationships we identify are correlational. We don&#x27;t know whether one behavior <em>causes</em> another, or whether they both reflect some common underlying factor, like task complexity or user preferences.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="looking-ahead" class="group/heading mt-12 font-heading text-[1.375rem] font-medium tracking-tight first:mt-0 scroll-mt-[calc(var(--academy-header-h,3.5rem)+1.5rem)]">Looking ahead<a href="#looking-ahead" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" class="pointer-events-none -mr-6 inline-flex select-none pl-2 align-middle text-muted opacity-0 transition-opacity duration-fast max-lg:hidden pointer-coarse:hidden group-hover/heading:pointer-events-auto group-hover/heading:opacity-100 hover:text-primary"><span data-cds="Icon" style="font-size:calc(1rem * var(--cds-rem-scale, 1));font-weight:533.3" aria-hidden="true"></span></a></h2>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">This study offers us a baseline that we can use to assess how AI fluency is changing over time. As AI capabilities evolve and adoption increases, we&#x27;re aiming to learn whether users are developing more sophisticated behaviors, which skills are emerging naturally with experience, and which will require more intentional development.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">In future work, we plan to extend our analysis in several directions. First, we plan to conduct &quot;cohort analyses,&quot; comparing new users to experienced ones in order to understand how familiarity with AI is correlated with fluency development. Second, we plan to use qualitative research methods to assess the behaviors that aren&#x27;t directly observable in Claude.ai conversations. And third, we aim to explore the <em>causal</em> questions that this work raises—like whether encouraging iterative conversations leads to greater critical evaluation, or whether there are other interventions that could encourage this more effectively.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">In addition, we&#x27;d like to explore AI fluency behaviors in Claude Code, a platform mostly used by software developers. In preparation for this study, we conducted some initial analysis that found consistency between Claude Code conversations and ones in <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_l4nmd35kq_" href="https://claude.ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">Claude.ai<span id="_R_l4nmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>. But this is still preliminary, and Claude Code&#x27;s very different user base and functionality implies that more substantial research is necessary.</p>
<p class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">We expect that the nature of AI fluency will develop and evolve substantially over time. With this and future research, we&#x27;re aiming to make that development visible, measurable, and actionable.</p>

<ol class="flex list-decimal flex-col gap-xs pl-lg">
<li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">When researching how people use AI models, protecting user privacy is paramount. For this project, we used our <a data-cds="TextLink" aria-describedby="_R_4lcnmd35kq_" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="cds-reset cds-text-link cds-text-link-underline inline cursor-pointer text-primary">privacy-preserving analysis tool<span id="_R_4lcnmd35kq_" hidden="" class="sr-only">(opens in new tab)</span></a>, which enables bottom-up discovery of AI usage patterns by distilling user conversations into high-level usage summaries, such as &quot;troubleshoot code&quot; or &quot;explain economic concepts.&quot; For this analysis, we ran 11 separate binary classifiers (one per behavioral indicator) using Claude Sonnet 4 for behavioral classification and Claude Haiku 3.5 for language detection. <li class="text-base leading-relaxed text-secondary">Behavioral indicators were calculated across a one-week sample (January 20–26, 2026) and held stable day-to-day, with most behaviors varying by only 1–5 percentage points. Rates were also consistent across six languages (English, French, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese, and German), with most behaviors varying by 3 percentage points or fewer across language groups. Together, these findings suggest that the behavioral patterns captured here reflect consistent habits in how people engage with AI, rather than being artifacts of timing, day of week, or linguistic and cultural context.</li>
