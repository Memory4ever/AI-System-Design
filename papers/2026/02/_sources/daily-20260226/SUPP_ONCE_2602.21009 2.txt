Abstract.
Modern recommender systems leverage ultra-long user behavior sequences to capture dynamic preferences, but end-to-end modeling is infeasible in production due to latency and memory constraints. While summarizing history via interest centers offers a practical alternative, existing methods struggle to (1) identify user-specific centers at appropriate granularity and (2) accurately assign behaviors, leading to quantization errors and loss of long-tail preferences. To alleviate these issues, we propose
Hierarchical Sparse Activation Compression
(HiSAC), an efficient framework for personalized sequence modeling. HiSAC encodes interactions into multi-level semantic IDs and constructs a global hierarchical codebook. A hierarchical voting mechanism sparsely activates personalized
interest-agents
as fine-grained preference centers. Guided by these agents, Soft-Routing Attention aggregates historical signals in semantic space, weighting by similarity to minimize quantization error and retain long-tail behaviors. Deployed on Taobao’s “Guess What You Like” homepage, HiSAC achieves significant compression and cost reduction, with online A/B tests showing a consistent
1.65%
CTR uplift—demonstrating its scalability and real-world effectiveness.
Keywords:
Long Sequence Modeling, Recommender System.
1.
Introduction
Modern recommender systems
(
Zhang et al., 2019
)
, widely deployed in domains such as e-commerce and video streaming, rely on user behavior sequences to capture evolving preferences
(
Zhou et al., 2018
;
Pi et al., 2020
;
Chang et al., 2023
)
.
For highly active users, these histories can contain thousands of interactions, reflecting both short-term and long-term dynamics.
While such ultra-long sequences provide rich signals for personalization, directly modeling them in production environments is computationally expensive and memory-intensive, often driving inference latency beyond acceptable limits in real-time serving.
Although many strategies have been explored for compressing long behavior sequences, substantial limitations persist in practical recommendation scenarios.
For instance, ELASTIC
(
Deng et al., 2024
)
and PolyEncoder
(
Humeau et al., 2019
)
transform each user’s behaviors into a fixed set of
global
interest embeddings, overlooking that users vary widely in both the number and granularity of their interest centers.
Other grouping approaches—such as temporal segmentation, locality-sensitive hashing (LSH)
(
Kitaev et al., 2020
)
, and K-Means clustering—are often unstable and sensitive to hyperparameters: temporal splitting ignores semantic proximity, while hash- and cluster-based techniques can misassign semantically related behaviors to different groups.
These issues highlight two fundamental challenges:
(1) accurately identifying user-specific interest centers, whose number and granularity differ across individuals; and
(2) reliably assigning historical behaviors to the most compatible interest center to reduce quantization error and preserve semantic consistency.
Deficiencies in the second aspect naturally lead to loss of rare yet valuable long-tail preferences, diminishing recommendation diversity and overall quality.
To address these challenges, we propose
Hierarchical Sparse Activation Compression
(HiSAC),
a framework for ultra-long sequence modeling that suppresses noise and attention dilution while maintaining stable, semantically consistent interest grouping.
Firstly
, HiSAC tokenizes user histories into discrete multi-level semantic identifiers via a large-scale multimodal encoder
(integrating product text and images) and a Residual Quantized Variational Autoencoder (RQ-VAE)
(
Lee et al., 2022
)
.
Secondly
, a global hierarchical semantic tree is built and a
hierarchical voting mechanism
then aligns each user’s tokenized sequence to this hierarchy,
pruning and retaining the most representative branches to produce personalized
interest-agents
capturing both coarse- and fine-grained preference centers.
Finally
, guided by these agents,
Soft-Routing Attention
aggregates signals from past interactions in the learned semantic space,
weighting contributions by similarity to reduce quantization error
(
Van Den Oord et al., 2017
)
and preserve infrequent yet valuable long-tail behaviors.
The resulting compact, expressive user representation accelerates retrieval and ranking, lowers serving costs,
and—in industrial-scale deployment—yields a consistent
1.65%
CTR uplift over the strongest prior compression method.
Our main contributions are:
•
We design a hierarchical voting mechanism for sparse activation of user interests, built upon a multimodal RQ-VAE. This mechanism generates user-specific interest-agents that represent the primary centers of user interest.
•
We develop a Soft-Routing Attention mechanism to reliably assign behaviors, minimize quantization error, and inherently preserve long-tail preferences.
•
We validate HiSAC in industrial-scale deployment, demonstrating reduced computational and serving costs alongside a 1.65% CTR uplift.
2.
Related Work
Sequence Modeling in Recommendation.
Modeling user interaction sequences
(
Xie et al., 2022
;
Zhou et al., 2019
;
Sun et al., 2019
;
Liu et al., 2024
)
is central to modern recommender systems, enabling fine-grained tracking of dynamic preferences.
Early attention-based models like DIN
(
Zhou et al., 2018
)
leveraged recent behaviors to capture short-term interests, achieving notable gains.
Later work recognized that longer histories offer a richer view of user preferences, yet processing ultra-long sequences incurs high computational, memory, and latency costs.
To mitigate this, two-stage methods such as SIM
(
Pi et al., 2020
)
and TWIN
(
Chang et al., 2023
;
Si et al., 2024
)
first retrieve a subset of behaviors before ranking, effectively shortening the sequence.
While efficient, these approaches risk retrieval–ranking inconsistency and discard potentially valuable behavioral signals.
Sequence Compression for Ultra-long Histories.
To improve efficiency, various sequence compression strategies have been explored. ELASTIC
(
Deng et al., 2024
)
and PolyEncoder
(
Humeau et al., 2019
)
compress sequences into a fixed set of global interest embeddings, offering computational savings but ignoring diversity in
user-specific interest granularity
. Locality-sensitive hashing (LSH)
(
Kitaev et al., 2020
)
, K-Means clustering, and temporal segmentation
(
Chai et al., 2025
;
Liao et al., 2025
)
have also been used for grouping behaviors, but temporal splitting disregards semantic proximity, and clustering- or hash-based methods can suffer from instability and misassignment, undermining personalization and discarding valuable long-tail signals.
Quantized Representation Learning.
Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAE)
(
Van Den Oord et al., 2017
)
introduce discrete codebooks to learn compact latent representations, defining quantization error as the
ℓ
2
\ell_{2}
distance between encoder outputs and selected codebook vectors. Extensions such as VQ-VAE-2
(
Razavi et al., 2019
)
employ multi-level quantization, while Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE)
(
Lee et al., 2022
)
progressively quantizes residuals to reduce total quantization error. These approaches have proved effective in domains like vision and speech, and their principles can be adapted to recommender systems for scalable sequence modeling.
Our method combines multimodal RQ-VAE tokenization, hierarchical voting, and soft-routing attention to identify user-specific interest centers. Unlike global fixed embeddings
(
Deng et al., 2024
;
Humeau et al., 2019
)
, HiSAC learns personalized interest agents, reduces quantization error, preserves long-tail preferences, and scales to industrial deployment.

(b) sparse activation by hierarchical voting for interest-agents selection;
(c) soft-routing attention to produce the compressed representation.

3.2.
Overview of Method
The proposed method is designed to model ultra-long user behavior sequences by mitigating noise and attention dilution, whose structure is shown in Figure
1

It compresses sequences in three stages: (a) tokenizing historical behaviors, (b) hierarchical voting for sparse activating coarse- and fine-grained interest agents, and (c) soft-routing attention to produce a compact, expressive representation.
Beyond these core components, we also provide details on its industrial-scale deployment, demonstrating HiSAC’s practicality in real-world recommendation systems.
3.3.
Tokenization of User Historical Behaviors
In modern recommender systems, item representations are typically learned end-to-end, effectively capturing task-specific patterns but often inheriting biases from item popularity and exposure frequency—compromising fairness and generalization. To mitigate this, we employ a large-scale multimodal encoder to generate embeddings for all items in the corpus

These multimodal signals are processed by an encoder that aligns and fuses them via modality-specific encoders and shared fusion layers. The resulting embedding unifies visual and textual semantics in a common space, enabling effective retrieval and ranking. By decoupling item representation learning from the recommendation model’s optimization, our approach mitigates exposure bias and improves embedding generalization.
Although multimodal-based embeddings offer rich semantic information, they reside in a continuous feature space where clustering behavior can be ambiguous and direct grouping of items may incur substantial quantization errors.

3.6.
Online Deployment
We deploy HiSAC with multi-head attention (MHA)
(
Vaswani et al., 2017
)
for cross modeling between compressed user sequences and candidate items, achieving an optimal balance between accuracy and latency in large-scale production.
As shown in Figure

(1) Offline Interest-Agent Construction.
For ultra-long sequences, a user’s primary interests typically remain stable over short time horizons. Leveraging this property, we construct each user’s

Specifically, a multimodal encoder with Residual RQ-VAE initializes the item corpus, which is updated daily for newly added items.
Historical behaviors are tokenized into semantic IDs, followed by personalized interest-tree building and interest-agent extraction.
These are stored and directly retrieved during online inference, effectively reducing latency and improving runtime efficiency.

4.1.
Setup
4.1.1.
Dataset
.
We evaluate our method on two datasets.
Industrial Dataset
: We constructed a large-scale industrial dataset using authentic, high-quality user interaction logs collected from Taobao, one of the world’s largest e-commerce platforms. The logs were continuously recorded over a 14-day period, with several billion interaction events generated daily. In total, the dataset contains approximately 200 million users, 1 billion items, and about 30 billion interaction records. The maximum sequence length per user is capped at 10,000. Data from the first 13 days is used for training, while the final day serves as the validation set.
Taobao-MM

4.1.2.
Metrics
.
In offline experiments, we adopt three standard metrics widely used in recommendation systems. We adopt the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) and Group AUC (GAUC). GAUC is calculated as the average of per-user AUCs, which mitigates the influence of highly active users and better reflects the typical user experience.
In online experiments, we evaluate two categories of metrics:
utility and user engagement. Utility metrics quantify the recommendation system’s ability to drive user actions and downstream revenue. We primarily track Item Page View (IPV), Click-Through Rate (CTR), Click-Through Conversion Rate (CTCVR) and Orders.
4.1.3.
Compared Methods & Implementation Details
.
We begin by presenting several representative and widely adopted sequence compression techniques. These methods are combined with multi-head attention (MHA) to facilitate user sequence modeling:
•
K-Means
(
Chong et al., 2021
)
: As adopted in the Routing Transformer
(
Roy et al., 2021
)
, this approach clusters tokens into dynamic groups based on feature similarity, allowing intra-cluster attention and providing a stable, task-adaptive, similarity-driven compression mechanism.
•
LSH
: The locality-sensitive hashing
(
Datar et al., 2004
)
mechanism used in Reformer
(
Kitaev et al., 2020
)
buckets similar tokens together, enabling attention computation within buckets and thereby reducing complexity while maintaining semantic proximity in long user histories.
•
Patching
: Groups historical items according to their timestamps, preserving temporal ordering and enabling time-aware compression of behavioral sequences.
•
Aggregator
: The method leverages a set of learnable tokens to compress user behavior sequences, following a similar design philosophy to ELASTIC
(
Deng et al., 2024
)
and PolyEncoder
(
Humeau et al., 2019
)
.
We further integrate our proposed method with several state-of-the-art (SOTA) approaches widely adopted in the industry to validate its effectiveness.
These approaches include:
•
PatchRec
(
Liao et al., 2025
)
: The method divides the user’s behavioral history into sequential blocks according to temporal order, performs pooling within each block to produce its representation, and employs different block sizes to separately model long-term and short-term behaviors.
•
ELASTIC
(
Deng et al., 2024
)
: In this approach, a fixed-size set of representation vectors is learned. The user’s behavior sequence is then compressed by applying cross-attention that aligns each behavior with the most relevant representation in the set.
•
Longer
(
Chai et al., 2025
)
: This method partitions the user’s behaviors into evenly spaced short sequences according to chronological order. Each short sequence is then compressed into a final representation vector using Linear Attention
(
Shen et al., 2021

, thereby reducing the overall sequence length.

Overall Performance Comparison
In this section, we compare the performance of HiSAC against all baseline methods on both datasets, the user sequence is compressed into 200 groups. The results are illustrated in Table
1
.
Compared with Different Compression Methods.
We adopt a simplified MHA baseline, where the candidate item acts as the query and the compressed historical sequence serves as both key and value. Results show that Patching compression performs worst, as it fails to preserve semantic relationships among items. K-Means clustering improves performance via explicit modeling of inter-item similarity, but its iterative updates incur high computational cost and leave notable quantization artifacts. LSH alleviates complexity while maintaining reasonable clustering quality, though inherent randomness in hashing introduces performance variance. The Aggregator mechanism yields additional gains by learning shared user-interest representations, but sacrifices personalization. In contrast, our HiSAC method, introducing interest-agents and Soft-Routing Attention, captures fine-grained personalized interests while mitigating quantization error, achieving the best overall performance.
Robustness and Generalizability Across SOTA Models.
To rigorously assess the robustness and generalizability of our framework, we integrate HiSAC into several representative state-of-the-art (SOTA) sequence modeling architectures that employ sequence compression. Specifically, in each architecture, the original compression or grouping module is replaced with HiSAC, while all other components remain unchanged. For fair comparison, the number of compressed groups is fixed at 200—consistent with HiSAC+MHA. Results show that these SOTA baselines already outperform the plain MHA model; however, their compression mechanisms exhibit suboptimal inductive bias, limiting their ability to fully capture sequential information. Replacing them with HiSAC yields more semantically coherent groupings that better align with personalized user interests, thereby unlocking latent modeling capacity and consistently improving both AUC and GAUC across all methods.

Sequence Length Scaling Analysis.
We evaluate six methods across varying sequence lengths to assess their ability to leverage long-range user interaction histories (Figure

).
Plain Multi-Head Attention (MHA) shows moderate gains with increasing length but declines beyond 10k, indicating that attention dilution and noise accumulation
(
Zeng et al., 2023

impair performance.
In contrast, compression-based methods consistently improve as sequences grow, as reduced input length and noise suppression alleviate attention dilution.
HiSAC achieves the largest gain, effectively exploiting long-range context without saturation, whereas Patching plateaus with longer sequences.
Aggregator also benefits but is limited by insufficient representation capacity and poor preservation of fine-grained temporal patterns.
Overall, sequence compression mitigates the drawbacks of long histories, with HiSAC maximizing information retention and delivering the best performance.

5.
Conclusion
We proposed HiSAC, a hierarchical semantic-agent compression framework for modeling multi-granular user interests in large-scale recommendation.
Combining Residual RQ-VAE encoding, hierarchical voting, and soft-routing, HiSAC captures diverse interests while mitigating quantization errors from hard assignment.
The framework has been deployed on a large-scale e-commerce platform, where it achieved a 1.65% improvement in online CTR over strong baselines, demonstrating its practical value and effectiveness.

Appendix A
Case Study of Soft-Routing Attention
Figure 6.
Soft-routing in action. Behavior_0 (main interest) and Behavior_1 (long-tail) are assigned to different hierarchical interest-agents based on semantic similarity, demonstrating HiSAC’s ability to retain sparse signals.
In Figure
6
, we present a case study demonstrating HiSAC’s ability to model heterogeneous user interests. The user’s dominant behavior (Behavior_0: T-shirt) exhibits strong semantic alignment with a dedicated interest-agent (e.g., Agent_0 representing “topwear”). Consequently, it is assigned a large routing weight to this agent, ensuring accurate and efficient encoding of the core preference. In contrast, the long-tail behavior (Behavior_1: cotton socks) does not have a perfectly matching agent in the codebook—no agent is explicitly dedicated to “socks.” However, HiSAC leverages the frozen multimodal semantic space to identify agents with related semantics, such as Agent_4 (“footwear”), and distributes the behavior across these agents with weights proportional to their semantic similarity. This soft-routing mechanism ensures that even sparse or niche interactions are meaningfully preserved in the final user representation, avoiding the quantization loss inherent in hard-assignment approaches.

Appendix D
Comparison with Two-Stage Methods
Modern recommendation systems increasingly adopt a two-stage architecture to handle long user behavior sequences efficiently.
The first stage, termed the
General Search Unit
(GSU), retrieves a short and relevant subsequence from the full historical behaviors by applying lightweight matching or retrieval mechanisms.
This step acts as a coarse filter, focusing subsequent computation on potentially informative interactions while suppressing noise and reducing sequence length.
The second stage, the
Exact Search Unit
(ESU), performs fine-grained user interest modeling on the retrieved subsequence, typically employing attention-based or Transformer architectures.
ESU refines the representation by capturing intricate temporal dependencies
and high-resolution feature interactions most pertinent to the target item.
Together, GSU and ESU form a coarse-to-fine pipeline: GSU efficiently narrows the search space, and ESU leverages the reduced yet relevant history to generate precise representations for final prediction.
Representative implementations of the two-stage paradigm include:
•
SIM
(
Pi et al., 2020
)
: A two-stage long-sequence modeling framework based on the GSU–ESU paradigm. It includes two variants:
SIM-Hard
and
SIM-Soft
. In
SIM-Hard
, the GSU stage performs retrieval using the item category as the matching key, whereas
SIM-Soft
relies on the cosine similarity between item embeddings. In the ESU stage, multi-head attention (MHA) is applied to perform cross-attention between the retrieved short sequence and the target item, enabling target-aware interaction modeling.
•
TWIN
(
Chang et al., 2023
)
: In SIM, the retrieval objectives of the GSU and ESU stages are not fully aligned. TWIN addresses this by employing the same retrieval strategy (MHA-based cross-attention) and parameter settings in both stages, thereby enhancing the consistency and coherence of the overall model pipeline.
