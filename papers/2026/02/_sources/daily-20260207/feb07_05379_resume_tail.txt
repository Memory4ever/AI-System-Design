185: 5 Variance Reduction Replay for Policy Optimization
186: Effective experience replay requires selectively reusing historical samples to balance computational cost, stability, and gradient variance reduction. To address this, we propose a selection criterion in Section 5.1 that targets variance minimization for LR/CLR estimators, supported by the variance estimation techniques in Section 5.2. To scale with growing history sizes, we derive a computationally efficient approximation of this rule in Section 5.3. These components culminate in the Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER) framework and the generic PG-VRER algorithm presented in Section 5.4, designed to enhance state-of-the-art policy optimization methods.

500: 8 Conclusion
501: To improve the sample efficiency of policy optimization algorithms, we introduce a novel experience replay approach known as Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER) by integrating the classical experience replay method with a sample selection rule designed to reduce gradient variance.
502: By selectively reusing the most relevant historical samples and automatically allocating more weights to those samples that are more likely generated from the target distribution, VRER guarantees to reduce the policy gradient estimation variance. This integration represents a significant advancement in improving the efficiency of policy optimization algorithms according to both theoretical and empirical studies.
503: Despite their popularity, experience replay methods in RL lack theoretical investigations into their finite-time convergence and limiting properties, highlighting a gap in the literature. Our proposed theoretical framework addresses this gap by considering sample dependence due to Markovian noise and behavior policy interdependence. It elucidates the effectiveness of experience replay methods and explains (1) why reusing too old samples could reduce the policy optimization performance; and (2) how the performance is affected by the buffer size through a bias-variance trade-off in policy gradient estimation. By applying this new theory, we further study the finite-time convergence of the PG-VRER approach, providing a theoretical grounding for practical applications.
504: The proposed VRER is general and it can be seamlessly integrated with SOTA policy gradient approaches to improve their performance. In practice, it is simple to apply VRER as it does not require any structural change of the original algorithms (e.g., PPO, TRPO, and A2C); only
506: Extensive empirical studies have shown that our approach can substantially increase the convergence rate of SOTA policy gradient methods and enhance their performance.
