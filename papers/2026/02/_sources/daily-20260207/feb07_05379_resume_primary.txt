60: Abstract
61: Effective reinforcement learning (RL) for complex stochastic systems requires leveraging historical data collected in previous iterations to accelerate policy optimization. Classical experience replay treats all past observations uniformly and fails to account for their varying contributions to learning.
62: To overcome this limitation, we propose Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER), a principled framework that selectively reuses informative samples to reduce variance in policy gradient estimation. VRER is algorithm-agnostic and integrates seamlessly with existing policy optimization methods, forming the basis of our sample-efficient off-policy algorithm, Policy Gradient with VRER (PG-VRER).
63: Motivated by the lack of rigorous theoretical analysis of experience replay, we develop a novel framework that explicitly captures dependencies introduced by Markovian dynamics and behavior-policy interactions. Using this framework, we establish finite-time convergence guarantees for PG-VRER and reveal a fundamental bias–variance trade-off: reusing older experience increases bias but simultaneously reduces gradient variance. Extensive empirical experiments demonstrate that VRER consistently accelerates policy learning and improves performance over state-of-the-art policy optimization algorithms.
64: Keywords Reinforcement Learning, Policy Optimization, Importance Sampling, Variance Reduction, Experience Replay
65: 1 Introduction
66: In recent years, various policy optimization approaches are developed to solve challenging control problems in healthcare (Yu et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2021b), continuous control tasks (Lillicrap et al., 2016; Schulman et al., 2015; Schulman et al., 2017), and biomanufacturing (Zheng et al., 2023).
67: These approaches often consider parametric policies and search for optimal solution through policy gradient approach (Sutton and Barto, 2018), whose performance and convergence crucially depends on the accuracy of gradient estimation.
68: Reusing historical samples is one way to improve gradient estimation, especially when historical data is scarce.
69: In this paper, we address an important question in policy optimization methods: How to intelligently select and reuse historical samples to accelerate the learning of the optimal policy for complex stochastic systems?
70: According to the different basic unit of historical samples to reuse, policy gradient (PG)
71: algorithms can be classified into episode-based and step-based approaches (Metelli et al., 2020).
72: Episode-based approaches reuse historical trajectories through importance sampling (IS) strategy accounting for the distributional difference induced by the target and behavior policies.
73: In this case, the importance sampling weight is built on the product of likelihood ratios (LR) of state-action transitions occurring within each process trajectory. As a result, the likelihood-ratio-weighted observations can have large or even infinite variance, especially for problems with long planning horizons (Schlegel et al., 2019).
74: On the other hand, the step-based approaches take individual state-action transitions as the basic reuse units.
75: This overcomes the limitation of episode-based approaches, provides a more flexible reuse strategy, and supports process online control.
76: In this paper, we will develop a variance-reduction based experience replay framework applicable to step-based approaches.
77: A noticeable limitation of current reinforcement learning (RL) techniques is the low sample efficiency (Botvinick et al., 2019). This challenge becomes especially pronounced in complex stochastic systems, such as healthcare (Hall and others, 2012; Zheng et al., 2021a) and biopharmaceutical manufacturing (Zheng et al., 2023), where each real or simulated experiment can be financially or computationally expensive. Another growing application of RL is RLHF, short for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (Christiano et al., 2017), used extensively in large language model-based chat models (Workshop et al., 2022) to align with human preferences. However, it’s crucial to underscore that RLHF heavily depends on accumulating significant volumes of expensive human feedback data.
78: Traditional on-policy methods only utilize newly generated samples to estimate the policy gradient for each policy update.
79: Ignoring the relevant information carried with historical samples can lead to low sample efficiency and high uncertainty in policy gradient estimation. In light of these considerations, it becomes important to fully utilize all available information when addressing RL optimization problems.
80: Fortunately, this information loss can be reduced through a combination of experience replay (ER) (Lin, 1992; Mnih et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2017) and off-policy optimization methods, which can store and “replay" past relevant experiences to accelerate the search for optimal policy. Off-policy methods typically employ importance sampling (IS) to address distribution mismatches (Owen, 2013; Hesterberg, 1988), yet this often inflates variance when behavior and target policies diverge (Owen, 2013; Zheng et al., 2020). Furthermore, the theoretical mechanisms governing experience replay (ER)—specifically the impact of buffer size, policy age, and mixing rates—are lack of discussion. To address this, we present a novel framework that investigates a fundamental bias-variance trade-off: reusing older samples introduces bias but reduces gradient estimation variance. To address these challenges, we introduce Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER). By selectively reusing historical samples, VRER reduces gradient variance and speeds up convergence; meanwhile, it employs a downsampling strategy and controlled replay capacity to mitigate the bias typically caused by policy lag and correlated data.
81: The key contributions of this study are summarized as follows.
82: •
83: We introduce a Variance Reduction Experience Replay (VRER) framework for policy gradient optimization that accelerates step-based policy learning by selectively reusing the most informative historical samples. VRER improves sample efficiency and reduces gradient variance by intelligently prioritizing relevant past experience.
84: •
85: We develop a novel finite-time convergence analysis for experience replay that explicitly accounts for sample dependencies arising from Markovian noise and behavior-policy interdependence. This framework characterizes how behavior-policy age, state-transition structure, and replay-buffer size jointly influence RL performance through a fundamental bias–variance trade-off in policy gradient estimation. Applying this theory to PG-VRER enables a rigorous finite-time convergence guarantee for the proposed algorithm.
86: •
87: Extensive empirical studies demonstrate that the proposed VRER framework can efficiently utilize past samples, reduce the policy gradient estimation variance, and accelerate the learning of optimal policy for complex stochastic systems.
88: The organization of this paper is as follows.
89: We review the most related literature studies in Section 2, and
90: present the problem description and assumptions of policy gradient optimization for infinite horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs)
91: in Section 3. We propose the IS-based policy gradient estimators for step-based algorithms in Section 4. Then, we develop the computationally efficient selection rules and propose a generic VRER based policy gradient optimization algorithm in Section 5. We further provide a finite-time convergence analysis of PG-VRER and show the asymptotic properties of experience replay in Section 6.
92: We conclude this paper with a comprehensive empirical study on the proposed framework in Section 7. The implementation of VRER can be found at GitHub.
93: 2 Related Works
94: The goal of RL is to learn the optimal policy through dynamic interactions with the systems of interest to achieve the best reward
95: (Sutton and Barto, 2018). Stochastic gradient
96: approaches are often used to solve RL problems (Sutton et al., 1999b).
97: The study of policy optimization can be traced back to REINFORCE, also known as vanilla policy gradient (VPG) (Williams, 1992). Later advancements, such as the introduction of value function approximations to policy optimization, gave rise to actor-critic methods (Konda and Tsitsiklis, 1999; Sutton et al., 1999a). In recent years, numerous approaches have emerged to enhance sample efficiency, scalability, and convergence speed, including Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) (Mnih et al., 2016), Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) (Haarnoja et al., 2018), trust region policy optimization (TRPO) (Schulman et al., 2015), and the proximal policy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017). An important perspective of policy gradient methods is to prevent dramatic updates in policy parametric space. Driven by this principle, TRPO considers a surrogate objective function subject to the trust region constraint which enforces the distance between old policy and new update measured by KL-divergence to be small enough. Following the similar idea, PPO truncates the likelihood ratio of new and old candidates
98: to discourage excessively large policy updates.
99: Importance sampling (IS) is a staple for off-policy evaluation and correction (Jiang and Li, 2016; Thomas et al., 2015b; Precup, 2000; Degris et al., 2012), though it traditionally suffers from high variance. To mitigate this, various reduction techniques have been proposed, including weight truncation (e.g., V-trace) (Espeholt et al., 2018; Munos et al., 2016), concentration bounds for safe policy improvement (Thomas et al., 2015b; Thomas et al., 2015a), and surrogate objectives that explicitly balance performance against variance inflation (POIS) (Metelli et al., 2018; Metelli et al., 2020). Other advancements include robust multiple importance sampling (MIS) to handle heavy-tailed estimators (Papini et al., 2019) and "green simulation" metamodels for reusing experimental outputs (Feng and Staum, 2017; Dong et al., 2018). However, applying IS to step-based algorithms faces two primary challenges: Markovian noise, which induces sample correlation, and behavior policy interdependencies, which introduce bias across optimization iterations (Eckman and Henderson, 2018; Liu and Zhou, 2020). While Lin and Zhou (2023) utilized ODE methods to analyze asymptotic convergence under policy reuse, their framework addresses policy interdependencies but explicitly neglects Markovian noise by assuming independent sampling from the occupancy measure.
100: Experience replay (ER) and its extension, such as prioritized experience replay (Schaul et al., 2016), are often used in policy gradient optimization and RL algorithms to reduce data correlation and improve sample efficiency. Zhang and Sutton (2017) examined the relationship between replay buffer size and agent performance. They found that too small or too large replay buffers negatively affected performance in three specific tasks. Fu et al. (2019) highlighted the sensitivity of agent performance to the ratio of environment steps to gradient steps. van Hasselt et al. (2019) experimented with this ratio and batch sizes for a more efficient Rainbow version. Fedus et al. (2020) studied the effect of replay capacity, buffer size, and policy age. Furthermore, the refinement and deeper understanding of experience replay algorithms remains active with contributions from various other studies (Sun et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2019; Novati and Koumoutsakos, 2019; Pan et al., 2022).
101: The proposed VRER method stands out from existing approaches by tackling the problems of biased policy gradient estimation and inflated variance, supported by our novel and rigorous theoretical analysis on bias-variance trade-off. This approach incorporates a selection rule and a managed buffer capacity that collectively reduce the variance induced by reusing correlated historical samples and mitigate bias due to the use of outdated samples. Furthermore, this selection process imposes a control on the magnitude of parameter updates, ensuring that the discrepancy between successive policies remains constrained.
