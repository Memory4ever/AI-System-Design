1076: between nℓn_{\ell} target features and nm′n^{\prime}_{m} source features using Sinkhorn iterations.

1091: In practice, IinI_{\mathrm{in}} and IoutI_{\mathrm{out}} denote the numbers of Sinkhorn iterations for the

1221: Appendix D More Discussion
1222: Q1: Does the use of translated benchmarks (or mixed-language evaluation sets) induce shortcut learning and exaggerate transfer gains?
1223: We acknowledge that translated or automatically curated benchmarks may differ from native usage (e.g., translationese, missing cultural nuances), which can bias absolute scores and sometimes change the difficulty distribution.
1224: However, our findings focus on relative comparisons under the same evaluation protocol.
1225: To reduce potential artifacts, we (i) report results on multiple benchmarks per language/domain where possible, (ii) include both domain-specific and general-capability evaluations to check for inconsistent improvements, and (iii) validate that gains are not driven by a single dataset.
1226: A more robust solution is to expand evaluation with human-verified native benchmarks; we leave this as future work.
1227: Q2: Is our method sensitive to the choice/size of the calibration set 𝒟\mathcal{D} used for activation alignment?
1228: Because transport plans {Qℓ​m}\{Q^{\ell m}\} and PP are estimated from activation statistics, a poorly matched or too-small 𝒟\mathcal{D} can lead to noisy correspondences and suboptimal fusion.
1229: We partially mitigate this by (i) using a moderately sized calibration set, (ii) using entropic regularization in OT for stability, and (iii) restricting fusion to top-kk highly-activated neurons to reduce interference.
1230: In practice, when 𝒟\mathcal{D} is extremely limited, a simple workaround is to reuse lightweight, task-relevant unlabeled text from the target domain/language; when such data is unavailable, distillation or adapter-based training may be more appropriate.
1231: Q3: When can cross-architecture fusion fail even if OT finds seemingly sharp correspondences?
1232: Sharp transport maps do not necessarily imply functionally correct alignment.
1233: Failure can happen when (i) the models use substantially different tokenization or pretraining distributions, (ii) architectural components are not semantically comparable (e.g., different attention implementations or MLP structures), or (iii) the target capacity is too small to absorb injected knowledge without harming existing representations.
1234: Our current mitigation is conservative fusion (top-kk replacement + small α\alpha) and optional residual-frozen adaptation to recalibrate the fused model.
1235: A more principled solution would incorporate structure-aware constraints (e.g., head-level or block-level priors) into the transport objective.
1236: Q4: Does fusion propagate harmful behaviors/biases from the source model, and how should this be handled?
1237: Yes.
1238: Because our method directly transports parameters, it can transfer not only helpful capabilities but also biases or unsafe tendencies present in the source model.
1239: In deployment, we recommend applying standard safety mitigation pipelines (data filtering, safety evaluation, and targeted alignment) on the final fused model.
1240: Studying safety-aware transport objectives is a promising future direction.
