L397: ### B.1 Importance of assigning non-zero weights to zero-success groups
L398: 
L399: The asymmetric weightings we consider in this paper assign non-zero weight to prompts with $\hat{\rho}=0$. As mentioned this is important for performance. To demonstrate this, we performed further TinyZero experiments for Plateau-R, for which we assign zero weight to zero-success prompts (i.e., groups with $\hat{\rho}=0$).
L400: See Figure cite125†9 for the results. It can be seen that with the same stepsize, training is less stable, and leads to worse performance. A smaller stepsize fixes the instability, but results in worse performance.
L401: We also considered the weight $\omega_{x}=\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\rho}$, which is another example of an asymmetric weight that assigns zero weight to zero-success groups. We call this kimi-weighting since the prioritized sampling strategy used by [cite49†25 ] where samples are reweighted by $1-\hat{\rho}$, can be interpreted through surrogate reward maximization [cite50†26 ] (cf. Section cite11†2.1.2 ) yielding that weight.
L402: 
L403: As seen in Figure cite125†9 , kimi-weighting performs worse than the other asymmetric weightings.

L409: ### B.3 Importance of gradient direction
L410: In this work we vary the gradient weights $\omega_{x}(\rho)$ and choose the gradient direction $\hat{d}_{x}$ as the direction used by RLOO and GRPO. Choosing the gradient direction as the REINFORCE direction (see Section cite9†2.1 , which effectively does not leverage gradients corresponding to zero-rewards) yields significantly worse performance: See Figure cite127†11 for a comparison of Linear-R vs the Linear-R weight with the REINFORCE direction (rejection sampling).

L413: ## Appendix C Experimental details
L414: 
L415: Here we briefly describe the experimental details. As mentioned, for our RL experiments, we used the SkyRL codebase [cite67†2 ], and we have implemented our advantage estimators withing that framework; the code is in the supplementary material.
L416: The hyperparameters used in the experiments are in Table cite128†2 . We used the same hyperparameters (such as batchsize, etc) within each of the four experimental setups for different advantage estimators, appart from the stepsize, where we considered and used different ones. Specifically we considered (2e-6,1e-6,5e-7) for some experiments for the different advantage estimators.
L417: For the TinyZero experiment the stepsizes for the runs shown are 1e-6 for each advantage estimator but for RLOO, where it is 2e-6. Looking at effective weights in Figure cite62†2 it makes sense that a stepsize that is good for RLOO should be twice of that of GRPO, given that the effective weights are very similar if we multiply the RLOO effective weight with 2.
L418: Applying the same thinking to Plateau-R, it might seem reasonable to decrese the stepsize of Plateau-R too relative to the other advantage estimators but here the situation is different: the effective batchsize (corresponding to completions with non-zero weight) for the Plateau-R is larger than for RLOO, and for larger batch sizes the stepsize can often be larger too.

L435: ## Appendix D Proofs
L436: ### D.1 The continuous limit of success-rate dynamics: Proof of Equation (cite81†8 )
L437: 
L438: The optimization problem in (cite80†7 ) is concave. Thus, rewriting its first-order optimality condition gives the following recursion for the optimal policies (see [cite76†15 ]):
L439:  |  | $\displaystyle\pi_{t}(y|x)=\frac{\pi_{t-1}(y|x)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\cdot A_{y}(\rho_{t-1})\right)}{\mathbb{E}_{y^{\prime}\sim\pi_{t-1}(\cdot|x)}\pi_{t-1}(y^{\prime}|x)\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\cdot A_{y^{\prime}}(\rho_{t-1})\right)}$  |  | (13)
L440:  |  | $\displaystyle\,\,=\frac{\pi_{t-1}(y|x)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\cdot A_{y}(\rho_{t-1})\right)}{\rho_{t-1}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\cdot A_{1}(\rho_{t-1})\right)+(1-\rho_{t-1})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\cdot A_{0}(\rho_{t-1})\right)}\,.$  |
L441: In the second equality, we used that rewards are binary, we recalled the definition
L442: 
L443:  | $$\rho_{t-1}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{t-1}(\cdot|x)}\mathbb{I}\left[r(y)=1\right]\,,$$  |
L444: and let $A_{0/1}$ be the advantage scores of wrong/correct responses. This expression makes explicit how the next policy re-weights correct and incorrect responses relative to the previous policy. Importantly, because the update depends on the policy only through its current success rate, the induced dynamics can be described entirely in terms of how this success rate evolves over time.
L445: Concretely, using $\rho_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|x)}\mathbb{I}\left[r(y)=1\right]=\int\mathrm{d}\pi_{t}(\cdot|x)\mathbb{I}\left[r(y)=1\right]$ yields the following recursion over success rates:
L446:  | $\displaystyle\rho_{t}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1-\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t-1}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\left(A_{0}(\rho_{t-1})-A_{1}(\rho_{t-1})\right)\right)}\,.$  |
L447: 
L448: Substituting the general advantage expressions from Eq. (cite63†5 ) for weight function $\omega(\rho)$ we get the following recursion for the population (over model responses) success rate:
L449: 
L450:  | $\displaystyle\rho_{t}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1-\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t-1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\beta}\omega(\rho_{t-1})\right)}\,.$  |
L451: To further simplify the dynamics, it is convenient to work with the log-odds of the success probability,
L452: 
L453:  | $$L_{t}:=\log\frac{\rho_{t}}{1-\rho_{t}}.$$  |
L454: 
L455: This change of variables linearizes multiplicative updates in probability space and reveals that learning proceeds additively in log-odds space.
L456: 
L457:  | $$\frac{1-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{t}}=\frac{1-\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t-1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\beta}\omega(\rho_{t-1})\right)\implies L_{t}=L_{t-1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\omega(\rho_{t-1})\,.$$  |
L458: The log-odds of the success rate increases by a step size proportional to the gradient of the surrogate reward. The derivation up to this point follows [cite76†15 , Thm. 2], extended here to general weight functions $\omega(\rho)$.
L459: 
L460: To gain further intuition about these dynamics and the role played by the weight, we consider a continuous-time limit of the log-odds update. Concretely, let us consider a continuous-time limit of the form
L461:  | $$\frac{{\mathrm{d}}L_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\beta}\omega(\rho_{t})\;\implies\;\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\beta}\rho_{t}(1-\rho_{t})\omega(\rho_{t})\,,$$  |
L462: 
L463: where for the latter implication we used the chain rule.
L464: ### D.2 Dynamics for regular time for RLOO, Linear-R, Sqrt-R, and GRPO
L465: ###### Proposition 3.
L466: 
L467: Solving the ODE (cite81†8 ), i.e.,
L468: 
L469:  | $\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\rho_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\beta}\rho_{t}(1-\rho_{t})\cdot\omega(\rho_{t})\,.$  |
L470: 
L471: for various weights $\omega$ and for initialization $\rho_{0}\in(0,1)$ gives:
L472: 
L473:   * •
L474: 
L475: RLOO, $\omega(\rho)=1$: $\rho_{t}=\left({1+\left(\frac{1-\rho_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\right)e^{-t/\beta}}\right)^{-1}$
L476: 
L477:   * •
L478: 
L479: Linear-R, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho}$: $\rho_{t}=1-(1-\rho_{0})e^{-t/\beta}$
L480: 
L481:   * •
L482: Sqrt-R, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt{1-\rho}}$: $\rho_{t}=1-\left(\max\left\{\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}-\frac{t}{2\beta},0\right\}\right)^{2}$
L483: 
L484:   * •
L485: 
L486: GRPO, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}}$: $\rho_{t}=\sin^{2}\left(\min\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{t}{2\beta}+\arcsin(\sqrt{\rho_{0}})\right)\right)$
L487: ###### Proof.
L488: 
L489: We prove each case separately.
L490: 
L491: RLOO: For RLOO, the ODE becomes
L492: 
L493:  | $\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\beta}\rho(1-\rho),$  |
L494: 
L495: and integration yields
L496: 
L497:  | $\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\rho(1-\rho)}d\rho=\int\frac{1}{\beta}dt.$  |
L498: 
L499: Solving this gives
L500: 
L501:  | $\displaystyle\log\rho-\log(1-\rho)-(\log\rho_{0}-\log(1-\rho_{0}))=\frac{t}{\beta},$  |
L502: 
L503: which after some algebra gives the stated solution for RLOO above.
L504: Linear-R: Next consider Linear-R, for which the ODE becomes
L505: 
L506:  | $\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\beta}(1-\rho).$  |
L507: 
L508: This is a linear ODE
L509: 
L510:  | $\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(1-\rho)=-\frac{1}{\beta}(1-\rho),$  |
L511: 
L512: with solution
L513: 
L514:  | $\displaystyle(1-\rho)=(1-\rho_{0})e^{-t/\beta}\,.$  |
L515: 
L516: which after re-arranging yields the expression for Linear-R.
L517: 
L518: Sqrt-R: For Sqrt-R, the ODE becomes
L519: 
L520:  | $\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{1-\rho}$  |
L521: Let $u_{t}:=1-\rho_{t}$. Then, while $u_{t}>0$:
L522: 
L523:  | $$\frac{du_{t}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{u_{t}}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,2\sqrt{u_{t}}=2\sqrt{u_{0}}-\frac{1}{\beta}t\,.$$  |
L524: 
L525: Therefore ${u_{t}}=\left(\max\{\sqrt{u_{0}}-\frac{t}{2\beta},0\}\right)^{2}$ which finishes the proof.
L526: 
L527: GRPO: For GRPO, the ODE becomes
L528: 
L529:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}.$$  |
L530: 
L531: Let $\theta_{t}=\arcsin(\sqrt{\rho_{t}})$. Then $\sin^{2}(\theta)=\rho$, and by the chain rule,
L532:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\sin^{2}\theta\big)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}=2\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}$$  |
L533: 
L534: Equating this with the ODE gives
L535: 
L536:  | $$2\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;=\;\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}.$$  |
L537: 
L538: Thus, for $\rho_{t}\in(0,1)$ we obtain the linear ODE
L539: 
L540:  | $$\frac{d\theta}{dt}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\beta}.$$  |
L541: 
L542: Integrating and using $\theta_{t=0}=\arcsin(\sqrt{\rho_{0}})$ yields
L543:  | $$\theta(t)\;=\;\frac{t}{2\beta}+\arcsin(\sqrt{\rho_{0}}).$$  |
L544: 
L545: This derivation is valid as long as $\rho_{t}<1$, which corresponds to $\theta_{t}<\pi/2$. Once $\theta_{t}$ reaches $\pi/2$, $\rho_{t}$ reaches $1$. Since $\rho=1$ is an equilibrium point, the solution stays at $1$ thereafter. Therefore, the general solution is
L546: 
L547:  | $$\rho_{t}\;=\;\sin^{2}\left(\min\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{t}{2\beta}+\arcsin(\sqrt{\rho_{0}})\right)\right)\,,$$  |
L548: 
L549: which concludes the proof for GRPO. ∎
L550: ### D.3 Effective time dynamics for Linear-R, Sqrt-R, RLOO, and GRPO
L551: ###### Proposition 4.
L552: 
L553: Solving the effective-time ODE (cite129†10 ), i.e.,
L554: 
L555:  | $\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{\tau}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\rho_{\tau}^{2}(1-\rho_{\tau})\cdot\omega(\rho_{\tau})$  |
L556: 
L557: for various weights $\omega$ and for initialization $\rho_{\tau=0}=\rho_{0}\in(0,1)$ gives:
L558: 
L559:   * •
L560: 
L561: Linear-R, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho}$: $\rho_{\tau}=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{1-\rho_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\right)e^{-\tau/\beta}}$
L562: 
L563:   * •
L564: Sqrt-R, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt{1-\rho}}$: Define $\tau_{*}:=2\beta\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}\big)$. Then,
L565: 
L566:  | $\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{sech}^{2}\left(\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}\big)-\frac{\tau}{2\beta}\right),&\tau\leq\tau_{\star},\\[5.16663pt]
L567: 1,&\tau>\tau_{\star}.\end{cases}$  |
L568: 
L569:   * •
L570: 
L571: GRPO, $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}}$: Define $z_{0}:=\sqrt{\frac{1-\rho_{0}}{\rho_{0}}}$ and $\tau_{*}:=2\beta z_{0}$. Then,
L572:  | $\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}=\frac{1}{1+\left(\max\left\{z_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2\beta},\,0\right\}\right)^{2}}$  |
L573: 
L574: In particular, $\rho_{\tau}=1$ for all $\tau\geq\tau_{*}$.
L575: 
L576:   * •
L577: 
L578: RLOO, $\omega(\rho)=1$: Define $s_{\tau}:=\frac{\tau}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\log\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{1-\rho_{0}}\right).$ Then $\rho_{\tau}$ is uniquely determined by the implicit relation
L579: 
L580:  | $\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho_{\tau}}+\log\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{1-\rho_{\tau}}\right)\;=\;s_{\tau},$  |
L581: and admits an explicit form in terms of the principal branch of the Lambert-$W$ function:
L582: 
L583:  | $\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}=\frac{1}{1+W\left(\exp\left(-s_{\tau}-1\right)\right)}\,.$  |
L584: ###### Proof.
L585: 
L586: We prove each case separately.
L587: 
L588: Linear-R: $\omega(\rho)=1/\rho$. The ODE becomes
L589: 
L590:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{\beta}\rho(1-\rho).$$  |
L591: 
L592: Separate variables:
L593: 
L594:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{\rho(1-\rho)}=\frac{d\tau}{\beta}.$$  |
L595: 
L596: Using $\frac{1}{\rho(1-\rho)}=\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{1-\rho}$, integrate to get
L597: 
L598:  | $$\log\Big(\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\Big)=\frac{\tau}{\beta}+C.$$  |
L599: 
L600: Apply $\rho(0)=\rho_{0}$ to obtain $C=\log\big(\frac{\rho_{0}}{1-\rho_{0}}\big)$. Exponentiating,
L601:  | $$\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}=\frac{\rho_{0}}{1-\rho_{0}}e^{\tau/\beta}.$$  |
L602: 
L603: Solving this for $\rho$ gives the result.
L604: 
L605: Sqrt-R: $\omega(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho\sqrt{(1-\rho)}}$. We solve the ODE
L606: 
L607:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\rho\sqrt{1-\rho}.$$  |
L608: 
L609: Separate variables:
L610: 
L611:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{\rho\sqrt{1-\rho}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\,d\tau.$$  |
L612: 
L613: Let $u=\sqrt{1-\rho}$, so $\rho=1-u^{2}$ and $d\rho=-2u\,du$. Then
L614:  | $$\int\frac{d\rho}{\rho\sqrt{1-\rho}}=\int\frac{-2u\,du}{(1-u^{2})u}=-2\int\frac{du}{1-u^{2}}=-2\,\operatorname{arctanh}(u).$$  |
L615: 
L616: Hence
L617: 
L618:  | $$\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}\big)=C-\frac{\tau}{2\beta},$$  |
L619: 
L620: as long as $C\geq\frac{\tau}{2\beta}$. Applying $\tanh$ and squaring yields
L621: 
L622:  | $$\sqrt{1-\rho_{\tau}}=\tanh\left(C-\frac{\tau}{2\beta}\right)\,\,\implies\,\,\rho_{\tau}=1-\tanh^{2}\left(C-\frac{\tau}{2\beta}\right)=\operatorname{sech}^{2}\left(C-\frac{\tau}{2\beta}\right).$$  |
L623: Since $\rho_{\tau=0}=\rho_{0}\in(0,1)$, then $C=\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}\big),$ so the solution is
L624: 
L625:  | $$\rho_{\tau}=\operatorname{sech}^{2}\left(\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}\big)-\frac{\tau}{2\beta}\right)\,,$$  |
L626: as long as $\tau\leq\tau_{*}=2\beta\operatorname{arctanh}\big(\sqrt{1-\rho_{0}}\big)$. At $\tau=\tau_{*}$ we have $\rho(\tau_{*})=1$. Since the right-hand side of the original ODE vanishes at $\rho=1$ (because $1-\rho=0$), the constant function $\rho_{\tau}=1$ is a solution for all $\tau\geq\tau_{*}$.
L627: 
L628: GRPO: $\omega(\rho)=1/\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}$. The ODE becomes
L629: 
L630:  | $\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\rho^{3/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}.$  |  | (14)
L631: 
L632: Define
L633:  | $$z:=\sqrt{\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\rho=\frac{1}{1+z^{2}}.$$  |
L634: 
L635: Differentiating the latter gives
L636: 
L637:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{d\tau}=-\frac{2z}{(1+z^{2})^{2}}\frac{d{z}}{d\tau}.$$  |
L638: 
L639: Using Eq. (cite130†14 ) and the identity $\rho^{3/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}=\frac{z}{(1+z^{2})^{2}}$, we get for $z>0$ the following linear ODE:
L640: 
L641:  | $$\frac{dz}{d\tau}=-\frac{1}{2\beta}.$$  |
L642: 
L643: Hence
L644: 
L645:  | $$z(\tau)=z_{0}-\frac{\tau}{2\beta},\qquad\text{where }z_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\rho_{0}}{\rho_{0}}}.$$  |
L646: This formula is valid as long as $z(\tau)\geq 0$, i.e. for $\tau\leq\tau_{*}:=2\beta z_{0}$. At $\tau=\tau_{*}$ we have $z(\tau_{*})=0$, hence $\rho(\tau_{*})=1$. Since the right-hand side of the original ODE vanishes at $\rho=1$ (because $1-\rho=0$), the constant function $\rho_{\tau}\equiv 1$ is a solution for all $\tau\geq\tau_{*}$.
L647: By uniqueness of solutions for the $z$-equation on the region $z>0$ and the fact that $\rho=1$ is an equilibrium of the $\rho$-equation, the global solution can be written as stated in the lemma.
L648: RLOO: $\omega(\rho)=1$. The ODE becomes
L649: 
L650:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{\beta}\rho^{2}(1-\rho).$$  |
L651: 
L652: Separate variables:
L653: 
L654:  | $$\frac{d\rho}{\rho^{2}(1-\rho)}=\frac{d\tau}{\beta}.$$  |
L655: 
L656: Using $\frac{1}{\rho^{2}(1-\rho)}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{1-\rho}$ and integrating gives
L657: 
L658:  | $$-\frac{1}{\rho}+\log\Big(\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\Big)=\frac{\tau}{\beta}+C,$$  |
L659: 
L660: where, by applying initialization $\rho_{\tau=0}=\rho_{0}$,
L661:  | $$C=-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}+\log\Big(\frac{\rho_{0}}{1-\rho_{0}}\Big).$$  |
L662: 
L663: Thus, $\rho_{\tau}$ is determined implicitly by solving
L664:
