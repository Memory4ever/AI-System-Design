L84: In contrast to these works, which formulate unlearning as a parameter recovery problem, we treat unlearning as a distribution correction problem and operate directly on the learned data distribution. This avoids reliance on the original parameter space, which is prohibitively large for modern generative models such as LLMs.
L85: Other works proposed data partitioning approaches which achieve exact unlearning through model checkpointing [cite78†BCCJTZLP21 , cite79†GKKMSZ23 ]. While effective in their intended settings, they rely on specialized training pipelines and are not applicable to arbitrarily trained models.
L86: A separate line of work addresses unlearning in LLMs through supervised fine-tuning objectives that explicitly degrade performance on the forget set while preserving performance on the retain set [cite72†MFSLK24 , cite80†WZZSHW25 , cite81†YWHLZH25 , cite82†DLBHV25 , cite83†LPGYBGLDGM+24 , cite84†ZLBM24 , cite85†FLLJZML25 ]. These schemes require fine-tuning the base model, which is computationally costly and can induce unintended degradation on behavior unrelated to the unlearning task.
L87: In contrast, our method freezes the base model and instead learns a distributional reweighting via a lightweight parameterization, yielding substantially lower computational cost. Further, unlike fine-tuning-based methods that suppress forget-set generations, our method admits theoretical guarantees for minimizing specific Retain and Forget Errors measuring key notions of discrepancy to the ground-truth retain distribution.
L88: Recent work, closer in spirit to our framework, has proposed an alternative unlearning paradigm for LLMs where the base model is kept frozen and unlearning is performed by learning a perturbation of the model’s output distribution [cite86†ER23 , cite87†JLZLKLC24 , cite88†SSW25 ].
L89: For a pretrained model $p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}(y\mid\bm{x})$ parameterized by $\bm{\theta}^{\ast}\in\bm{\Theta}$, these methods introduce a tilt function $f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},y)$, parameterized separately by $\bm{\phi}$, and define the updated next-token distribution as $\hat{p}(y\mid\bm{x})\propto p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}(y\mid\bm{x})\cdot f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},y)$.
L90: This formulation preserves the original model $p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}$ and decouples unlearning from $\bm{\Theta}$, enabling updates over a lower-dimensional auxiliary parameter space. However, these approaches parameterize $f_{\bm{\phi}}$ using an auxiliary LLM or a LoRA-style adaptation [cite89†HWALWWC+22 ], requiring forward and backward passes through deep networks and large amounts of data to train a highly expressive tilting function.
L91: While these methods aim to up-weight retain set sequences and suppress forget set sequences, they incur substantial computational cost and lack theoretical guarantees for recovering the target retain distribution. In contrast, building on the same tilting scheme, we propose a computationally efficient unlearning method with provable finite-sample guarantees for recovering the target distribution $p_{r}$.
L92: Notation. We abuse notation by using the symbols $p$, $p_{r}$, and $p_{f}$ interchangeably to denote a measure over samples (e.g., $\bm{z}\sim p$), its corresponding density, or probability mass (e.g., $p(\bm{z})$ or $p(y\mid\bm{x})$). $H(p)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbin{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{$-$}}}\ln p\right]$ denotes entropy, and $\operatorname{Std}_{p}[\,\cdot\,]$ denotes standard deviation under $p$.
L93: For a function $g$ and $k\geq 1$, $\left\|g\right\|_{k,p}:=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim p}\left[|g(\bm{z})|^{k}\right]^{1/k}$.
L94: ## 2 Proposed Method: T3-Unlearning
L95: We formalize the generative model unlearning task. We consider a generative model with query access $p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{z})$ parameterized by $\bm{\theta}$. For LLMs, we write $\bm{z}=(\bm{x},y)$, where $\bm{x}$ denotes the context and $y$ the next token, with the autoregressive factorization $p_{\bm{\theta}}(y\mid\bm{x})$. For generality, we present the formulation in terms of a generic variable $\bm{z}\sim p(\bm{z})$ where $p$ is either a density or probability mass, and specialize as needed.
L96: As mentioned earlier, we are given the original pretrained model $p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}$, which we assume approximates the data distribution $p$ over the full set $\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{r}\sqcup\mathcal{D}_{f}$, together with sample access to both $\mathcal{D}_{r}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{f}$. The goal is to produce a model that generates samples from the ground-truth retain distribution $p_{r}$, corresponding solely to the information contained in $\mathcal{D}_{r}$.
L97: ### 2.1 Probabilistic Formulation
L98: 
L99: Under the distribution tilting framework, where the updated distribution takes the form $\hat{p}_{r}\propto p\cdot\hat{f}$, the optimal tilt $f^{\ast}$ that exactly recovers $p_{r}$ is given by the density ratio of the target $p_{r}$ and original distribution $p$. For autoregressive models with $\bm{z}=(\bm{x},y)$, this reduces to $f^{\ast}(\bm{x},y)=p_{r}(y\mid\bm{x})/p(y\mid\bm{x})$, and more generally to $f^{\ast}(\bm{z})=p_{r}(\bm{z})/p(\bm{z})$.
L100: We aim to recover the unknown target distribution $p_{r}$ by estimating this ratio. Formally, we label each sample in the full dataset $\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{r}\,\sqcup\,\mathcal{D}_{f}$ by its membership in the retain set, yielding the labeled dataset $\mathcal{S}=\{(\bm{z},s)\mid\bm{z}\in\mathcal{D}\}$ with $s=\mathds{1}\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{r}\}$. We assume $(\bm{z},s)$ are drawn from a measure $\mathbb{P}$ over $\Omega=\mathcal{Z}\times\{0,1\}$.
L101: Let $\gamma\in(0,1)$ denote the population proportion of the forget set. Then $s\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1-\gamma)$ indicates whether $\bm{z}$ is drawn from the retain or forget component, with $p_{r}(\bm{z})\coloneq\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{z}\mid s=1\right)$ and $p_{f}(\bm{z})\coloneq\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{z}\mid s=0\right)$. The marginal $p$ is thus the $\gamma$-weighted mixture
L102:  | $$p(\bm{z})=(1-\gamma)p_{r}(\bm{z})+\gamma p_{f}(\bm{z}).$$  |  | (1)
L103: By Bayes’ rule, the retain distribution is a tilt of the original model $p$, i.e., $p_{r}(\bm{z})\propto p(\bm{z})\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{z}\right)$, which under the auto-regressive factorization of LLMs simplifies to $p_{r}(y\mid\bm{x})\propto p(y\mid\bm{x})\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{x},y\right)$. This recovers the standard classifier-guidance formulation: the density ratio $f^{\ast}$ can be interpreted as the scaled posterior probability
L104:  | $$f^{\ast}(\bm{z})\propto\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{z}\right).$$  |  | (2)
L105: 
L106: Thus, we can estimate $f^{\ast}$ in practice by training a probabilistic classifier to infer $s$ from $\bm{z}$. This reframes the unlearning problem, which is often characterized by ill-posed objectives, into a well-defined supervised learning problem.
L107: ### 2.2 T3-Unlearning Procedure
L108: Standard classifier guidance implicitly assumes access to an accurate estimate of the optimal classifier $f^{*}$ which yields an accurate estimate of the desired posterior. However, as detailed in Section cite12†3 , this assumption breaks down in the finite-sample regime when the forget distribution $p_{f}$ is highly concentrated. In such settings, even small classification errors can be amplified by sharp modes in $p_{f}$, leading to information leakage.
L109: To address this, we propose the T3-Unlearning algorithm which applies a two-stage process:
L110: Classifier Training. Given a dataset $\mathcal{S}_{n}=\{(\bm{z}_{i},s_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}$ drawn from the data model in Section cite9†2.1 , we learn a classifier $\hat{f}(\bm{z})\approx\mathbb{P}(s=1\mid\bm{z})$. For a specific parameterization $f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{z})$, we minimize the regularized cross-entropy loss
L111: 
L112:  | $$L_{n}^{\lambda}(\bm{\phi})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{z}_{i}),s_{i})+\lambda\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{2}^{2},$$  |  | (3)
L113: where $\ell(f(\bm{z}),s)=-s\ln f(\bm{z})-(1-s)\ln(1-f(\bm{z}))$ and $\lambda\geq 0$ is the regularization coefficient.
L114: 
L115: Tempered Inference. We apply base model tempering, first smoothing the base distribution $p$ via a temperature $T\geq 1$ and then tilting using the learned classifier $\hat{f}$, yielding:
L116: 
L117:  | $$\hat{p}^{(T)}_{r}(\bm{z})\propto p(\bm{z})^{1/T}\hat{f}(\bm{z}).$$  |  | (4)
L118: For LLMs, where $\hat{\bm{\phi}}$ are the learned parameters which minimize (cite90†3 ) and $p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}$ is the frozen original model, this translates to the autoregressive update rule:
L119: 
L120:  | $$\hat{p}^{(T)}_{r}(y\mid\bm{x})\propto p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}(y\mid\bm{x})^{1/T}f_{\hat{\bm{\phi}}}(\bm{x},y).$$  |  | (5)
L121: While standard classifier guidance sharpens the classifier to control guidance strength [cite52†DN21 ], we instead temper the base model, reducing the magnitude of the correction needed by the classifier. See Remark cite91†3.6 for the limitations of classifier sharpening for unlearning.
L122: cite92†Image: Refer to caption Figure 1: T3-Unlearning for LLMs. We freeze the base model and train a linear head (shaded) on pooled hidden states to predict the vector $\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})$ of class posteriors for all possible next tokens.
L123: In training, we apply the loss to the entry $[\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})]_{y}$ corresponding to the estimator of the class posterior $\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{x},y\right)$, while the entire vector $\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})$ tilts the base model’s tempered logits for inference.
L124: ### 2.3 LLM Implementation
L125: We propose the following procedure for applying T3-Unlearning to LLMs (Figure cite71†1 ). We define the classifier $f_{\bm{\phi}}$ as a low-rank linear head over pre-computed features from the frozen base model $\bm{\Psi}$. For vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ and an $L$-length context $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{V}^{L}$, we obtain a fixed-size representation by mean-pooling over the sequence dimension of the final hidden states $(\bm{H}_{1},\dots,\bm{H}_{L})=\bm{\Psi}(\bm{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times L}$.
L126: This is passed through the low-rank classifier factorized by $\bm{A}_{\bm{\phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times d}$, $\bm{B}_{\bm{\phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|\times h}$ ($h\ll d$) to produce per-token probabilities:
L127:  | $$\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})=\sigma\big(\bm{B}_{\bm{\phi}}\bm{A}_{\bm{\phi}}\operatorname{MeanPool}(\bm{\Psi}(\bm{x}))\big)\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal{V}|}.$$  |
L128: 
L129: We then define $f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},y)$ as the $y^{\text{th}}$ entry of $\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})$:
L130: 
L131:  | $$f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},y)=\left[\bm{g}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x})\right]_{y}\approx\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{x},y\right).$$  |
L132: During training (Figure cite71†1 , Top), we compute the loss $\ell(f_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},y),s)$ for each pair $(\bm{x},y)$ and label $s$ using only the classifier output. For inference (Figure cite71†1 , Bottom), we apply the classifier guidance to the tempered base model distribution, sampling from $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}(y\mid\bm{x})$:
L133: 
L134:  | $$\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}(y\mid\bm{x})=\operatornamewithlimits{softmax}_{y}\Biggl(\frac{\ln p_{\bm{\theta}^{\ast}}(y\mid\bm{x})}{T}+\ln[\bm{g}_{\phi}(\bm{x})]_{y}\Biggr).$$  |
L135: Our design offers two key advantages: hidden states in training can be cached, eliminating repeated forward passes through the base model, and the number of trainable parameters is minimal, enabling fast, memory-efficient unlearning.
L136: ## 3 Theoretical Guarantees
L137: ### 3.1 Unlearning Metrics
L138: Before establishing our theoretical guarantees, we first formalize the criteria used to evaluate an unlearning procedure. Recall from (cite93†1 ) that we observe samples from the mixture $p(\bm{z})=(1-\gamma)\,p_{r}(\bm{z})+\gamma\,p_{f}(\bm{z})$, and our goal is to generate samples according to the retain distribution $p_{r}(\bm{z})$.
L139: For any candidate estimate $\hat{p}_{r}$ of $p_{r}$, we evaluate unlearning along two axes: Retain Error, which measures how accurately the estimate recovers the target retain distribution, and Forget Error, which measures how effectively it avoids leaking information in regions associated with the forget distribution.
L140: The first notion of unlearning error, termed the Retain Error, evaluates the accuracy of $\hat{p}_{r}$ in regions where the target distribution itself places probability mass. We formally quantify this via the forward KL divergence
L141: 
L142:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{r}(\hat{p}_{r}):=\ {\mathrm{KL}}\left(p_{r}\,\|\,\hat{p}_{r}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim p_{r}}\left[\ln\frac{p_{r}(\bm{z})}{\hat{p}_{r}(\bm{z})}\right]$$  |  | (6)
L143: As this is an expectation under $p_{r}$, it is insensitive to discrepancies on sets where $p_{r}$ assigns negligible probability. While such insensitivity is often benign in standard estimation settings, it is problematic in unlearning: an estimate can achieve small ${\mathrm{KL}}\left(p_{r}\,\|\,\hat{p}_{r}\right)$ while assigning nontrivial probability to regions where the forget distribution $p_{f}$ concentrates, thereby leaking information about the forget set.
L144: To capture this failure mode, we define a complementary notion of error, the Forget Error, that focuses explicitly on the behavior of $\hat{p}_{r}$ over regions emphasized by $p_{f}$. Concretely, we measure this error as the $p_{f}$-weighted $\ell_{1}$-norm
L145: 
L146:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}):=\|p_{r}-\hat{p}_{r}\|_{1,p_{f}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim p_{f}}\left[\left|p_{r}(\bm{z})\mathbin{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{$-$}}}\hat{p}_{r}(\bm{z})\right|\right].$$  |  | (7)
L147: Taken together, the Retain and Forget Errors provide complementary control: the former ensures faithful recovery of $p_{r}$ on its typical set, while the latter detects residual mismatch in regions where $p_{f}$ places weight, which is precisely where unlearning failures manifest.
L148: ###### Remark 3.1.
L149: While direct control of distributional discrepancies ($\mathcal{E}_{r}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{f}$) gives a definitive notion of unlearning, these quantities are intractable to compute for LLMs, as they require expectations over a combinatorial input space. Consequently, empirical evaluations rely on hypothesis tests [cite72†MFSLK24 ] and membership inference attacks [cite94†SLHMZHLZSZ25 ] to assess distinguishability from a retrained reference.
L150: Our theory targets these fundamental discrepancies directly, since minimizing them implies indistinguishability under any test function, and in Section cite17†4 we show strong performance under the empirical proxies used in practice.
L151: ### 3.2 Surrogate Analysis Framework
L152: 
L153: We work in a general nonparametric setting where the retain and forget distributions admit densities $p_{r}$ and $p_{f}$ over a continuous domain $\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}$. We approximate $p_{r}$ via the T3-Unlearning estimator $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ defined as
L154: 
L155:  | $$\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}(\bm{z})=\frac{p(\bm{z})^{1/T}\hat{f}(\bm{z})}{\int_{\bm{u}}p(\bm{u})^{1/T}\hat{f}(\bm{u})},$$  |  | (8)
L156: where $\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}$ is a learned surrogate classifier intended to approximate the Bayes posterior $f^{\ast}(\bm{z})\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{z}\right)$.
L157: ###### Remark 3.2.
L158: 
L159: We assume $T$ is chosen so that $\int p^{1/\tau}<\infty$ for all $1\leq\tau\leq T$, ensuring $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ is well-defined. This always holds for sub-exponential distributions; for power-law tails $p\propto\left\|\bm{z}\right\|_{2}^{-\alpha}$, it requires $\alpha>dT$, for ambient dimension $d$.
L160: Our analysis directly relates the unlearning error of $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ to the statistical quality of $\hat{f}$. Let $L(f)\coloneq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},s}\left[\ell(f(\bm{z}),s)\right]$ denote the population risk under the cross-entropy loss $\ell$. We assume $\hat{f}$ achieves excess risk at most $\delta$, i.e., $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{\ast})\leq\delta$, and derive bounds on the Retain and Forget Errors of $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ as functions of $\delta$ and the mixture parameters.
L161: While particular function classes admit standard excess risk rates when the true posterior lies in the hypothesis space (e.g., logistic regression achieving $\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})$ rates; see Appendix cite30†C ), our theory is stated in terms of the surrogate excess risk $\delta$. This abstraction allows it to apply across learning settings with favorable risk guarantees, agnostic to any function class.
L162: Consequently, regardless of how the surrogate classifier $\hat{f}$ is obtained, we characterize unlearning performance solely through its surrogate risk.
L163: ### 3.3 Unlearning Guarantees: Untempered Estimator
L164: 
L165: To motivate the role of tempering, we first analyze the baseline that directly tilts the observed mixture using standard classifier-guidance. Concretely, for a classifier $\hat{f}$, we define the untempered estimator as $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)}$, the special case of the T3-Unlearning estimator (cite95†8 ) with fixed temperature $T=1$.
L166: We first establish in Theorem cite60†3.3 that this standard approach strictly controls Retain Error, and then subsequently show that it can fail to bound Forget Error when the forget distribution $p_{f}$ is sharply concentrated.
L167: ###### Theorem 3.3.
L168: 
L169: Let $\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}$ satisfy the excess risk bound $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{\ast})\leq\delta$. Then the Retain Error (cite96†6 ) of the untempered estimate $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)}$ in (cite95†8 ) satisfies
L170: 
L171:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{r}(\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)})\leq\frac{\delta}{1-\gamma}.$$  |
L172: Theorem cite60†3.3 shows that the Retain Error of the untempered estimator is controlled by the classifier’s excess risk, up to the factor $(1-\gamma)^{-1}$. The bound scales linearly in $\delta$, as improved classification performance leads to a more accurate reconstruction of the retain distribution. The factor $(1-\gamma)^{-1}$ reflects the inverse proportion of the retain component in the original mixture. As $\gamma\to 1$, the signal from $p_{r}$ vanishes, rendering recovery ill-conditioned.
L173: However, in standard unlearning scenarios where the forget set is small ($\gamma\ll 1$), this factor is negligible. In this regime, the bound in Theorem cite60†3.3 remains sharp, effectively equating the Retain Error with the classifier’s excess risk. While Theorem cite60†3.3 shows the untempered estimator attains small Retain Error when the classifier has low excess risk (and $\gamma$ is not too large), this alone does not ensure effective unlearning.
L174: As discussed in Section cite13†3.1 , controlling the Forget Error (cite97†7 ) is essential to prevent information leakage from the forget set. The next theorem makes this explicit by upper-bounding the Forget Error of the untempered estimator, showing that it may degrade even when the Retain Error is small.
L175: ###### Theorem 3.4.
L176: 
L177: Let $\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}$ satisfy the excess risk bound $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{\ast})\leq\delta$. Then the Forget Error (cite97†7 ) of the untempered estimate $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)}$ in (cite95†8 ) satisfies
L178: 
L179:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)})\leq\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{1-\gamma}}.$$  |
L180: The bound in Theorem cite61†3.4 depends on the surrogate excess risk $\delta$ and the mixture weight $\gamma$ in the expected way: it improves as $\delta$ decreases and deteriorates as $\gamma$ increases, with the same $(1-\gamma)^{-1}$ amplification as in Theorem cite60†3.3 . Unlike the Retain Error guarantee, this bound scales with $\sqrt{\delta}$ and $\sqrt{(1-\gamma)^{-1}}$.
L181: This reflects that Retain Error and excess risk are both KL-based metrics, while Forget Error is $\ell_{1}$-based; applying Pinsker’s inequality relates the two quantities, degrading the bound from linear to square-root.
L182: Crucially, the bound also carries a multiplicative dependence on $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$. This captures the sharpness of the forget distribution and can be very large when $p_{f}$ is highly concentrated on a small region, in which case the bound may become uninformative even for small $\delta$.
L183: In the next theorem, we show that this sensitivity to the concentration of $p_{f}$ is not an artifact of the analysis by constructing an instance where the Forget Error is lower bounded in terms of $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$.
L184: ###### Theorem 3.5.
L185: 
L186: Let $p_{r}$ and $p_{f}$ have disjoint supports, with $p_{f}$ uniform on its support. Then for any mixture weight $\gamma\in(0,1)$ and any $\delta>0$, there exists a classifier $\hat{f}$ achieving excess risk $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{*})\leq\delta$ such that the Forget Error (cite97†7 ) of the untempered estimate $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)}$ in (cite95†8 ) satisfies
L187:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)})\geq\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\cdot\frac{\gamma\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)\right)}{1-\gamma\exp\left(-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)}.$$  |
L188: 
L189: In particular, as $\delta\to 0$, we have $\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(1)})=\Omega\left(\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\!\!\cdot\delta\right)$.
L190: Theorem cite62†3.5 shows that for a broad class of component distributions $p_{r}$ and $p_{f}$, the Forget Error of the untempered estimator necessarily scales with the peak density. This confirms that the dependence on $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ in Theorem cite61†3.4 is intrinsic rather than an artifact of the analysis. The proof constructs a worst-case classifier $\hat{f}$ which incurs error only on the forget set.
L191: In this case, the Forget Error is lower bounded by the sharpness of $p_{f}$, quantified by its squared $\ell_{2}$-norm $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{2}^{2}$. When $p_{f}$ is uniform, this coincides with $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ yielding the stated bound. While the lower bound scales linearly in $\delta$, compared to the $\sqrt{\delta}$ rate in the upper bound in Theorem cite61†3.4 , it establishes the unavoidable dependence on the peak density of the forget distribution.
L192: ###### Remark 3.6 (Limitations of Classifier Sharpening).
L193: 
L194: Prior work [cite52†DN21 , cite87†JLZLKLC24 ] controls guidance strength by sharpening the classifier $\hat{f}^{\,w}$ for $w>0$. While this can be interpreted as a strategy for improving the surrogate task excess risk to some $\delta_{w}$, Theorems cite61†3.4 and cite62†3.5 show that such sharpening cannot eliminate the fundamental dependence of the Forget Error on the sharpness of $p_{f}$.
L195: ### 3.4 Unlearning Guarantees: The Tempered Estimator
L196: Theorems cite61†3.4 and cite62†3.5 show that the untempered estimator is highly sensitive to the sharpness of $p_{f}$: its Forget Error scales linearly with $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$, and the corresponding lower bound confirms this dependence is intrinsic in general. We show that tempering overcomes this limitation.
L197: Under a mild integrability condition on the mixture density $p$, the $T$-tempered estimator $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ admits a Forget Error bound which depends on $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ sublinearly as $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{1/T}$, substantially improving robustness when $p_{f}$ is sharply concentrated.
L198: ###### Theorem 3.7.
L199: 
L200: Let $\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}$ satisfy the excess risk bound $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{\ast})\leq\delta$. Define the $\tau$-tempered oracle estimate $p_{r}^{(\tau)}\propto p^{1/\tau}\cdot f^{\ast}$ for $\tau\in[1,T]$. Consider $k\geq T$ such that $\int p^{\frac{k-T}{T(k-1)}}<\infty$. Then for some $\tau\in[1,T]$, the Forget Error (cite97†7 ) of the $T$-tempered estimate $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ in (cite95†8 ) satisfies
L201:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)})\leq{\left(1-\tfrac{1}{T}\right)\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{2,p_{r}^{(\tau)}}\!\cdot\operatorname{Std}_{p_{r}^{(\tau)}}[\ln p]}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{1/T}\cdot\,\delta^{1/2k}).$$  |
L202: Theorem cite69†3.7 bounds the Forget Error of the $T$-tempered estimator by a sum of two terms: a tempering bias term and a $\delta$-dependent estimation error term. The second term exhibits the key benefit of tempering: the dependence on the sharpness of the forget distribution is sublinear, scaling as $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{1/T}$ rather than $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ as in the untempered bound of Theorem cite61†3.4 .
L203: While the dependence on the classifier excess risk worsens from $\delta^{1/2}$ to $\delta^{1/2k}$, for fixed $\delta$ and large $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ tempering can greatly improve the Forget Error guarantee.
L204: The first term quantifies the price of tempering. It depends on the intermediate “oracle” tempered distribution $p_{r}^{(\tau)}$, for some $\tau\in[1,T]$, and measures the distortion caused by raising $p$ to the power $1/T$. When $p_{r}$ and $p_{f}$ are well separated, this bias is small, and it vanishes entirely in the idealized case of disjoint supports, since then $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{2,p_{r}^{(\tau)}}=0$.
L205: In this regime, the overall error is dominated by the estimation term, directly addressing the failure mode of the untempered estimator, whose Forget Error provably exhibits linear dependence on $\left\|p_{f}\right\|_{\infty}$ in the disjoint-support setting (cf.
