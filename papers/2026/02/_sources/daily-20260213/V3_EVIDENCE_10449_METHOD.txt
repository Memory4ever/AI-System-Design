L173: ###### Remark 3.
L174: 
L175: The core technical challenge in the proof of cite83†Theorem 2 is to bound $\lVert F(F+\lambda I)^{-1}-G(G+\lambda I)^{-1}\rVert_{2}$. A natural alternative is to invoke an oblivious subspace embedding (OSE) (cite90†Woodruff, 2014 ). For a fixed matrix $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$, $P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ is an $\varepsilon$-OSE for $\range(A)$ if
L176: 
L177:  | $$-\varepsilon A^{\top}A\preceq A^{\top}(P^{\top}P-I)A\preceq\varepsilon A^{\top}A.$$  |
L178: Instantiating $A=F^{1/2}$ yields a sandwich $(1-\varepsilon)F\preceq G\preceq(1+\varepsilon)F$, which implies $\|F(F+\lambda I)^{-1}-G(G+\lambda I)^{-1}\|_{2}=O(\varepsilon)$ by operator monotonicity of $t\mapsto t/(t+\lambda)$ (see cite25†Section B.2 ).
L179: However, OSE enforces uniform multiplicative accuracy over $\range(F^{1/2})$, so even directions with $\lambda_{j}(F)\ll\lambda$ must be preserved up to a $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ factor, leading to $m=\Omega(r/\varepsilon^{2})$ (cite90†Woodruff, 2014 , Theorems 2.3 and 6.10).
L180: Our proof instead exploits the weaker, $\lambda$-dependent requirement: it suffices for $P$ to be an approximate isometry on the $\lambda$-whitened influence subspace $B=F^{1/2}(F+\lambda I)^{-1/2}$, i.e., $\lVert B^{\top}(P^{\top}P-I)B\rVert_{2}\leq O(\varepsilon)$. This yields the ridge-regularized sandwich $(1-\varepsilon)(F+\lambda I)\preceq G+\lambda I\preceq(1+\varepsilon)(F+\lambda I)$.
L181: Importantly, this condition controls $F+\lambda I$ rather than $F$ itself: in directions where $\lambda_{j}(F)\ll\lambda$, both $F+\lambda I$ and $G+\lambda I$ are dominated by $\lambda$, so even large relative errors in $F$ have negligible impact on the inverse. Consequently, such low-eigenvalue directions need not be preserved multiplicatively, and the required sketch size is governed by the effective dimension at scale $\lambda$, yielding the sharper bound $m=\Omega(d_{\lambda}(F)/\varepsilon^{2})$.
L182: We now complement cite83†Theorem 2 with a worst-case matching lower bound, showing that the effective dimension $d_{\lambda}(F)$ characterizes the tight dependence of $m$ for oblivious sketching in regularized influence. Concretely, we show that for Gaussian oblivious sketches, if the sketch size is smaller than $\Theta(d_{\lambda}(F)/\varepsilon^{2})$, then there exist problem instances on which the sketched influence incurs $\Omega(\varepsilon)$ error with constant probability.
L183: ###### Theorem 4 (Lower bound for regularized projection).
L184: 
L185: Let $P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ be a Gaussian oblivious sketch with rows i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})$. There exists a family of $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ such that if $m=o(d_{\lambda}(F)/\varepsilon^{2})$, then there exists $g\in\range(F)$ with $\lvert\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g)-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g)\rvert=\Omega(\varepsilon)\tau_{0}(g,g)$ with constant probability.
L186: Full details are in cite23†Appendix B . We see that cite91†Theorem 4 formalizes a worst-case limitation for this class of sketches: with Gaussian oblivious projections, one cannot uniformly beat the $d_{\lambda}(F)/\varepsilon^{2}$ scaling. Combined with the instance-adaptive upper bound in cite83†Theorem 2 , this identifies $d_{\lambda}(F)$ as the fundamental complexity parameter governing regularized projection.
L187: ### 2.3 Factorized Influence
L188: In many large-scale settings, explicitly forming or inverting the empirical Fisher/Hessian $F$ is infeasible, and second-order methods instead rely on structured approximations.
L189: A common choice is a Kronecker factorization (e.g., K-FAC (cite74†Martens and Grosse, 2015 ; cite77†Grosse et al., 2023 )), which models each layerwise block as $F\approx A\otimes E$ for smaller PSD factors $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{A}\times d_{A}}$ and $E\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{E}\times d_{E}}$, which are forward activation and backprop-gradient covariances, respectively.
L190: This structure suggests a natural computational counterpart on the sketching side: use a factorized sketch $P=P_{A}\otimes P_{E}$, where $P_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{A}\times d_{A}}$ and $P_{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{E}\times d_{E}}$ are respectively the standard oblivious sketching considered in cite83†Theorem 2 .^{4}^{4} 4 Concretely, rows of $P_{A}$ and $P_{E}$ are i.i.d. isotropic sub-Gaussian random vectors with scaling $1/\sqrt{m_{A}}$ or $1/\sqrt{m_{E}}$.
L191: The resulting sketch has ambient dimension $d\coloneqq d_{A}d_{E}$ and sketch dimension $m\coloneqq m_{A}m_{E}$, i.e., $P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$. Moreover, write a per-example layer gradient as a matrix $G\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{E}\times d_{A}}$ with $g=\operatorname{vec}(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$. Then the projection can be computed without materializing the full $m\times d$ sketching matrix as $Pg=(P_{A}\otimes P_{E})\operatorname{vec}(G)=\operatorname{vec}(P_{E}GP_{A}^{\top})$.
L192: Consequently, the per-example cost reduces to two smaller multiplies $P_{E}G$ and $(P_{E}G)P_{A}^{\top}$, plus solving the resulting regularized system in sketch dimension $m$. Similarly, we can also form the sketched curvature efficiently: using the mixed-product identity of Kronecker products, $PFP^{\top}=(P_{A}\otimes P_{E})(A\otimes E)(P_{A}\otimes P_{E})^{\top}=(P_{A}AP_{A}^{\top})\otimes(P_{E}EP_{E}^{\top})$.
L193: In the unregularized case ($\lambda=0$), the exact invariance barrier becomes strictly more stringent under a Kronecker sketch: exact preservation on $\range(F)$ holds if and only if both factor sketches are injective on their respective ranges.
L194: ###### Theorem 5 (Barrier of unregularized projection for factorized influence).
L195: 
L196: Let $F=A\otimes E\succeq 0$ and $P=P_{A}\otimes P_{E}$ as above. Then $\widetilde{\tau}_{0}(g,g^{\prime})=\tau_{0}(g,g^{\prime})$ for all $g,g^{\prime}\in\range(F)$ if and only if $P_{A}$ is injective on $\range(A)$ and $P_{E}$ is injective on $\range(E)$. In particular, this necessitates $m_{A}\geq\rank(A)$ and $m_{E}\geq\rank(E)$, hence $m=m_{A}m_{E}\geq\rank(A)\rank(E)=\rank(F)$.
L197: See cite36†Section C.1 for a proof. This motivates integrating regularization and consider
L198: 
L199:  | $$\begin{split}\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})&=(Pg)^{\top}(PFP^{\top}+\lambda I_{m})^{-1}(Pg^{\prime})\\
L200: &=\operatorname{vec}(P_{E}GP_{A}^{\top})^{\top}\left((P_{A}AP_{A}^{\top})\otimes(P_{E}EP_{E}^{\top})+\lambda I_{m}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(P_{E}G^{\prime}P_{A}^{\top}).\end{split}$$  |

The key technical step is a factorized covariance deviation bound (cite92†Theorem 15 ), proved in cite35†Appendix C .

L215: ###### Remark 7.
L216: cite93†Theorem 6 highlights a fundamental computational–statistical trade-off. While factorized sketches offer substantial computational and memory advantages over unfactorized ones, they incur a higher statistical cost in terms of the required sketch size.
L217: In particular, since the total sketch size is $m=m_{A}m_{E}$, achieving an $\varepsilon$-approximation error requires $m=m_{A}m_{E}=\widetilde{\Omega}\big(\varepsilon^{-4}(d_{\lambda_{E}}(A)d_{\lambda_{A}}(E))\big)$, which exhibits a worse dependence on $\varepsilon$ (from $\varepsilon^{-2}$ to $\varepsilon^{-4}$) compared to the unfactorized sketch guarantee in cite83†Theorem 2 .
L218: Importantly, this gap is not an artifact of loose analysis, but follows from the separable nature of the factorized sketch: $P_{A}$ and $P_{E}$ must independently satisfy an $\varepsilon$-level concentration bound at its own regularization scale. Consequently, the total sketch size reflects the product of the factor-level requirements. As a result, factorized sketches are most effective in regimes where the computational and memory savings from separability outweigh the increased statistical overhead.
L219: ## 3 Influence with Out-of-Range Test Gradients
L220: The analysis in cite10†Section 2 assumes that both arguments of the (regularized) influence bilinear form lie in $\range(F)$. This assumption is natural for training gradients: when $F$ is instantiated as the (empirical) Fisher information matrix, $F=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}g_{i}^{\top}$, every training gradient lies in $\range(F)$ by construction.
L221: In practice, however, we are often interested in the influence of an unseen test point $z^{\prime}$ with respect to a training point $z$, for which the corresponding test gradients $g^{\prime}$ need not lie in $\range(F)$.
L222: We extend the above guarantees to this setting by explicitly characterizing the additional sketch-induced error arising from the component of $g^{\prime}$ orthogonal to $\range(F)$.
L223: ### 3.1 Leakage of Projection
L224: To make the source of this additional term explicit, we decompose $g^{\prime}=g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}+g^{\prime}_{\perp}$, where $g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\in\range(F)$ and $g^{\prime}_{\perp}\in\ker(F)$, such that the decomposition is orthogonal in the Euclidean inner product.
L225: Using linearity of $\tau_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)$ and $\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)$ in their second argument, we have $\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})=\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})+\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})$ and $\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})=\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})+\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})$. Consequently,
L226:  | $$\lvert\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})\rvert=\left\lvert\big(\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})\big)+\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})\right\rvert.$$  |
L227: Observe that the true (regularized) influence does not couple $\range(F)$ and $\ker(F)$, i.e., $\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})=g^{\top}(F+\lambda I)^{-1}g^{\prime}_{\perp}=0$ for all $\lambda\geq 0$: indeed, $F$ and $(F+\lambda I)^{-1}$ share the same eigenbasis, and since $g\in\range(F)$ and $g^{\prime}_{\perp}\in\ker(F)$, hence $(F+\lambda I)^{-1}g$ and $g^{\prime}_{\perp}$ lie in orthogonal subspaces. Hence,
L228:  | $$\lvert\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime})\rvert\leq\lvert\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})-\tau_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}})\rvert+\lvert\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})\rvert.$$  |
L229: The first term can be bounded via cite83†Theorem 2 ; on the other hand, the remaining term is a purely sketch-induced artifact: the sketch can introduce a nonzero leakage term $\widetilde{\tau}_{\lambda}(g,g^{\prime}_{\perp})$ due to mixing between $\range(F)$ and $\ker(F)$ under $P^{\top}P$. We now present a general bound on the leakage:

L243: ###### Proof sketch.
L244: The proof is organized around a deterministic reduction: cite94†Lemma 16 (in cite43†Appendix D ) shows that both the unregularized and regularized leakage bounds follow as soon as two concentration conditions hold for the sketch $P$: (i) an operator-norm bound on $\range(F)$, $\lVert U^{\top}(P^{\top}P-I)U\rVert_{2}\leq\varepsilon$ for an orthonormal basis $U$ of $\range(F)$, and (ii) a cross-term bound between $\range(F)$ and the kernel direction(s), $\lVert U^{\top}(P^{\top}P-I)g^{\prime}\rVert_{2}\leq\varepsilon\lVert g^{\prime}\rVert_{2}$.
L245: For a single test gradient $g_{\perp}^{\prime}$, both conditions follow from applying cite88†Lemma 12 to the $(r+1)$-dimensional subspace $\Span(\range(F)\cup\{g^{\prime}\})$, which yields the claimed $m=\Omega((r+\log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^{2})$ scaling.
L246: To obtain uniform control over multiple test gradients, we use two complementary arguments: a subspace argument, which applies the same concentration bound to $\Span(\range(F)\cup\{g^{\prime}_{j,\perp}\}_{j=1}^{k})$ and yields the dependence on $k^{\prime}=\dim\Span(\{g^{\prime}_{j,\perp}\})$ (cite95†Proposition 18 ); or a union-bound argument, which establishes a fixed-$g^{\prime}$ tail bound and unions over $k$, yielding the $O(\log k)$ dependence (cite96†Proposition 19 ). ∎

L265: The factorized theorem is proved by following the same high-level template as cite97†Theorem 8 : we first reduce the leakage bound to the two concentration conditions in cite94†Lemma 16 (stability on $\range(F)$ and a cross-term bound between $\range(F)$ and $\ker(F)$).
L266: For a Kronecker sketch $P=P_{A}\otimes P_{E}$, the stability condition on $\range(F)=\range(A)\otimes\range(E)$ is obtained by controlling the factor-level subspace deviations $\lVert U_{A}^{\top}(P_{A}^{\top}P_{A}-I)U_{A}\rVert_{2}$ and $\lVert U_{E}^{\top}(P_{E}^{\top}P_{E}-I)U_{E}\rVert_{2}$ (with $U_{A},U_{E}$ bases of $\range(A),\range(E)$).
L267: For the cross-term condition, we expand $P^{\top}P-I$ into factor deviations and use cite98†Lemma 21 (in cite53†Appendix E ) to reduce $\lVert U^{\top}(P^{\top}P-I)g^{\prime}_{\perp}\rVert_{2}$ to a small collection of factor-level “primitive” quantities such as $\lVert U_{A}^{\top}(P_{A}^{\top}P_{A}-I)(\cdot)\rVert_{2}$ and $\lVert U_{E}^{\top}(P_{E}^{\top}P_{E}-I)(\cdot)\rVert_{2}$.
L268: Finally, as in the proof of cite97†Theorem 8 , these primitives are controlled via a union-bound argument (yielding the $O(\log k_{\cdot})$ terms) or a subspace argument (yielding the $k_{\cdot}^{\prime}$ terms).
