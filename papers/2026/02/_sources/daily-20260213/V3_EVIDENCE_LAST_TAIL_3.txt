L233: ##### Our framework improves the performance of full fine-tuning with fewer training samples.
L234: For the Llama3.2-1B-instruct backbone, NeSyS achieves an average accuracy of 68.3% using only 45% of the training data, surpassing the standard SFT baseline (64.4%) which utilizes the full dataset. Similarly, for the larger Qwen3-4B model, NeSyS (71.0%) also outperforms the SFT baseline (68.3%). This validates our hypothesis that a significant portion of interactive trajectory data is redundant once the underlying deterministic rules are extracted.
L235: In other words, a large part of training data can be replaced by symbolic rules.
L236: ##### Each WM benefits from the reciprocal refinement phase.
L237: Comparing the results from Phase 1 to Phase 2 validates the effectiveness of our iterative training pipeline. For both backbone models, the performance of each single WM significantly improves during the reciprocal refinement phase. While the improvement of Neural WM is expected, the improvement of Symbolic WM is not trivial.^{3}^{3} 3 We find marginal improvement when iterating the rule set over the same error clusters again and again.
L238: It indicates that as Neural WM improves, it exposes new, more subtle failure modes that Symbolic WM effectively captures in the second iteration. This simultaneous growth demonstrates that our reciprocal refinement prevents the modules from becoming redundant, instead pushing each to address the other’s remaining blind spots.
L239: ##### Each part of our framework contributes to the final performance.
L240: Even after the individual components are refined, their combination yields performance superior to either in isolation. For both backbones, the final performance of NeSyS surpasses the one of each single WM in each phase. This additive gain confirms that the two modules remain fundamentally complementary: Symbolic WM secures the deterministic constraints while Neural WM captures the semantic information, and their integration covers the intersection more robustly than any single modality.

L446: 
L447: As discussed in cite22†Section 5.1 , we create the benchmarks based on the official expert trajectories provided by the environment. Here we enclose the details for each environment.
L448: 
L449: For ScienceWorld, we sample at most 50 dev/test variations from each pre-defined task (there are 30 predefined tasks in the environment), and at most 5 positions from each variation. We create one question at each position.
L450: For Webshop, we do a random 90:5:5 train:dev:test split on the provided trajectories. We use every time step in the dev/test trajectories to create a question. Since all the trajectories end up successfully buying the desired item, we randomly select middle positions of each trajectory and insert a “click[buy now]” action, which will result in a ”Fail” state. Similar operation is performed on the training set.
L451: For Plancraft, we sample at most 6 steps from each provided dev/test trajectories. We enriched the training trajectory by

L62: ###### Abstract
L63: Large language models (LLMs) exhibit strong general-purpose reasoning capabilities, yet they frequently hallucinate when used as world models (WMs), where strict compliance with deterministic transition rules—particularly in corner cases—is essential. In contrast, Symbolic WMs provide logical consistency but lack semantic expressivity.
