L1146@P24: C More Details Of Bitrate Controllable Speech Generation
L1147@P24: C.1 Architecture
L1148@P24: The Temporal Transformer is initialized from Qwen3-1.7B [7]. The Depth Transformer is randomly initialized
L1149@P24: and consists of 4 Transformer blocks with a hidden size of 1536 and an FFN dimension of 8960.
L1150@P24: C.2 Training Details
L1151@P24: We adopt a global batch size of 1.35M tokens, including both text tokens and speech tokens, where speech
L1152@P24: tokens are counted at a frame rate of 12.5 Hz. During training, text tokens and speech tokens are concatenated
L1153@P24: along the temporal dimension to form the input sequence for the TTS model. FlashAttention-2 [90] is used to
L1154@P24: accelerate training. All models are trained using the AdamW optimizer with a peak learning rate of 2×10−4.
L1155@P24: For ablation studies, models are trained for 50k steps, while the final models are trained for 200k steps.
L1156@P24: C.3 Inference Details
L1157@P24: At inference time, to maintain consistency with the training procedure, we synthesize speech in a continuationbased manner. Given a prompt audio with its transcription and a target text to be synthesized, we concatenate the prompt transcription tokens xprompt, the target text tokens xsyn, and the prompt audio tokens sprompt

L1161@P25: D More Details on Baseline Text-to-Speech Systems
L1162@P25: We compare our CAT-TTS system with a wide range of open-source text-to-speech (TTS) models. These
L1163@P25: models can be broadly categorized into three groups.
L1164@P25: The first group consists of cascaded TTS systems that employ multiple generative models, such as AR+NAR
L1165@P25: or NAR+NAR architectures. Representative examples include MaskGCT [41], FireRedTTS [34], CosyVoice2 [40],
L1166@P25: Qwen2.5-Omni [48], CosyVoice3 [91], IndexTTS2 [43], FlexiCodec-TTS [92], and GLM-TTS [44].
L1167@P25: The second group includes purely non-autoregressive TTS systems, such as F5-TTS [36].
L1168@P25: The third group comprises prior fully autoregressive TTS models based on either discrete or continuous representations, including Llasa [32], SparkTTS [33], OpenAudio-s1 [93], HiggsAudio-v2 [60], FireRedTTS2 [34],
L1169@P25: DiTAR [72], and VoxCPM [73].
L1170@P25: CAT-TTS adopts a purely autoregressive architecture based on discrete tokens to perform zero-shot TTS in an
L1171@P25: end-to-end manner, directly generating speech from text without relying on predefined intermediate representations, such as semantic tokens [17, 40]. Moreover, CAT-TTS supports variable-bitrate speech generation
L1172@P25: through Progressive Sequence Dropout.

L1185@P25: We explore the capability of CAT for speech understanding tasks by developing CAT-ASR. Specifically, we
L1186@P25: investigate whether CAT tokens can be directly used as inputs to a large language model (LLM) for automatic
L1187@P25: speech recognition (ASR), in order to evaluate the alignment between CAT and text as well as the information
L1188@P25: preservation of the discrete speech representation.
L1189@P25: We adopt Qwen3-1.7B [7] as the backbone LLM. To enable speech understanding, we initialize a set of 32
L1190@P25: speech tokens in the vocabulary and directly feed the discretized CAT speech tokens into the LLM. For each
L1191@P25: speech frame, the tokens along the RVQ dimensions are summed and treated as a single input embedding
L1192@P25: to the LLM. The model is then trained in a fully autoregressive manner to predict the corresponding text
L1193@P25: sequence given the speech token inputs.
L1194@P25: The model is trained on an internal dataset consisting of approximately 2 million hours of paired (audio, text)
L1195@P25: data. We use a global batch size of 1M tokens and train the model for 200k steps with a warmup of 4k steps.
L1196@P25: The Adam optimizer is adopted with a peak learning rate of 5×10−5. All experiments are conducted without
L1197@P25: any additional alignment or auxiliary supervision beyond the standard ASR objective.
L1198@P25: We evaluate the trained CAT-based ASR model on both English and Chinese benchmarks. For English, we
L1199@P25: report word error rate (WER) on the LibriSpeech test-clean set [83]. For Chinese, we report character error
L1200@P25-26: 26rate (CER) on the AIShell-2 iOS subset [84]. We compare our model with a range of previous open-source
L1201@P26: ASR systems and speech-language models [16, 19, 45–48, 79, 94], as summarized in Table 4.
