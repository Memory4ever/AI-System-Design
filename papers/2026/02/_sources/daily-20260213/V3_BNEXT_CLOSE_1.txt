L220: 
L221: ###### Definition 1 (Time-bounded success / provability).
L222: 
L223: Fix a horizon $B\in\mathbb{N}$. For a policy $\pi$ and initial state $\mu\in\mathcal{X}$, define the time-bounded success probability
L224: 
L225:  | $$V_{B}^{\pi}(\mu):=\Pr^{\pi}_{\mu}\left(\exists t\in\{0,1,\dots,B\}\ \text{such that}\ \mu_{t}\in G\right).$$  |
L226: 
L227: The optimal provability is
L228: 
L229:  | $$V_{B}^{*}(\mu):=\sup_{\pi}V_{B}^{\pi}(\mu).$$  |
L230: ###### Definition 2 (Finite-horizon value functions).
L231: 
L232: For $b=0,1,\dots,B$ define
L233: 
L234:  | $$V_{b}^{*}(\mu):=\sup_{\pi}\Pr^{\pi}_{\mu}\left(\exists t\in\{0,1,\dots,b\}\ \text{s.t.}\ \mu_{t}\in G\right).$$  |
L235: 
L236: Then $V_{0}^{*}(\mu)=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]$.
L237: ###### Definition 3 (One-step optimal action-value).
L238: 
L239: For $b\geq 1$ define
L240: 
L241:  | $$Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}V_{b-1}^{*}(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a).$$  |
L242: 
L243: We have Bellman recursion (finite-horizon reachability). For $b\geq 0$:
L244: 
L245:  | $$V_{b+1}^{*}(\mu)=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{b+1}^{*}(\mu,a).$$  |
L246: 
L247: ###### Lemma 1 (Bounds and monotonicity).
L248: 
L249: For all $b$ and $\mu$, $0\leq V_{b}^{*}(\mu)\leq 1$ and $V_{b}^{*}(\mu)\leq V_{b+1}^{*}(\mu)$.
L250: ###### Proof.
L251: 
L252: Immediate from the definition of reachability probabilities. ∎

L254: ## 4 Existence of optimal value functions and optimal Markov policies
L255: 
L256: We state mild continuity assumptions ensuring that the supremum over actions is attained.
L257: ###### Assumption 1 (Weak continuity / Feller-type condition).
L258: 
L259: For every bounded continuous $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$, the map
L260: 
L261:  | $$(\mu,a)\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a)$$  |
L262: 
L263: is continuous on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}$.
L264: 
L265: This implies that $(\mu_{n},a_{n})\to(\mu,a)$ entails $P(\cdot\mid\mu_{n},a_{n})\Rightarrow P(\cdot\mid\mu,a)$ weakly.
L266: ###### Theorem 2 (Existence of maximizers and optimal Markov policies).
L267: 
L268: Assume: (1) $\mathcal{X}=M_{\leq W}(K)$ is compact under $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$ (cite66†Theorem 1 ), (2) $\mathcal{A}$ is compact, (3) the kernel $P$ satisfies cite67†Assumption 1 , (4) $G=\{0\}$ is closed (hence $\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]$ is upper semicontinuous).
L269: Then for each $b=1,\dots,B$: (a) The Bellman recursion defines a Borel-measurable $V_{b}^{*}$, (b) For each $\mu$, the supremum $\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)$ is attained by some $a_{b}^{*}(\mu)\in\mathcal{A}$, (c) hence there exists an optimal deterministic Markov policy $\pi^{*}=(a_{b}^{*})_{b=1}^{B}$ achieving $V_{B}^{*}$.
L270: 
L271: The proof is based on backward induction. See cite50†Appendix C for the full proof.
L272: ## 5 Bellman inequalities and provability certificates
L273: 
L274: The following monotonicity principle is central: it yields upper/lower bound certificates without solving the full MDP.
L275: 
L276: ###### Definition 4 (Bellman operator).
L277: 
L278: Define $T$ on bounded measurable $V:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]$ by
L279: 
L280:  | $$(TV)(\mu):=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}V(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a).$$  |
L281: 
L282: Then $V_{b+1}^{*}=TV_{b}^{*}$ with $V_{0}^{*}=\mathbf{1}[\cdot\in G]$.
L283: ###### Lemma 2 (Monotonicity).
L284: 
L285: If $V\leq W$ pointwise then $TV\leq TW$ pointwise.
L286: 
L287: ###### Proof.
L288: 
L289: Supremum and expectation preserve order. ∎
L290: ###### Theorem 3 (Sub-/super-solution certificates).
L291: 
L292: Let $(U_{b})_{b=0}^{B}$ and $(L_{b})_{b=0}^{B}$ be sequences of functions $\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]$.
L293: 
L294: If $U_{0}\geq\mathbf{1}[\cdot\in G]$ and
L295: 
L296:  | $$U_{b+1}\geq TU_{b}\quad\text{for all }b=0,\dots,B-1,$$  |
L297: 
L298: then $U_{b}\geq V_{b}^{*}$ for all $b$.
L299: 
L300: If $L_{0}\leq\mathbf{1}[\cdot\in G]$ and
L301: 
L302:  | $$L_{b+1}\leq TL_{b}\quad\text{for all }b=0,\dots,B-1,$$  |
L303: 
L304: then $L_{b}\leq V_{b}^{*}$ for all $b$.
L305: 
L306: ###### Proof.
L307: 
L308: Induction using cite68†Lemma 2 . ∎
L309: ###### Definition 5 (provability certificate and certificate gap).
L310: 
L311: For an initial state $\mu_{0}$, any pair $(L_{B}(\mu_{0}),U_{B}(\mu_{0}))$ such that
L312: 
L313:  | $$L_{B}(\mu_{0})\leq V_{B}^{*}(\mu_{0})\leq U_{B}(\mu_{0})$$  |
L314: 
L315: is a provability certificate, and
L316: 
L317:  | $$\mathrm{Gap}(\mu_{0}):=U_{B}(\mu_{0})-L_{B}(\mu_{0})$$  |
L318: 
L319: is the certificate gap.

L325: 
L326: Let $(h_{b})_{b=1}^{B}$ be score functions
L327: 
L328:  | $$h_{b}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1].$$  |
L329: 
L330: Define the greedy policy
L331: 
L332:  | $$\pi_{h}(\mu,b)\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}h_{b}(\mu,a).$$  |
L333: 
L334: Similarly, define top-$k$ candidate sets $\mathrm{Top}\text{-}k(h_{b}(\mu,\cdot))$ and beam-search variants (beam introduces a tree policy, but for analysis we track how often near-optimal actions remain in the candidate set).
L335: ### 6.2 Uniform score approximation and worst-case regret
L336: 
L337: ###### Assumption 2 (Uniform approximation of $Q_{b}^{*}$ on a domain).
L338: 
L339: Let $\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}$ be a compact “relevant” domain (e.g., reachable pairs). Assume that for each $b$,
L340: 
L341:  | $$\sup_{(\mu,a)\in\mathcal{D}}\big|h_{b}(\mu,a)-Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)\big|\leq\varepsilon_{b}.$$  |
L342: 
L343: (How to obtain such bounds with high probability is addressed in cite24†Section 7 )
L344: ###### Lemma 3 (One-step suboptimality bound).
L345: 
L346: Under cite69†Assumption 2 , for every $\mu$ such that $(\mu,a)\in\mathcal{D}$ for all relevant $a$,
L347: 
L348:  | $$\sup_{a}Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)-Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,\pi_{h}(\mu,b))\leq 2\varepsilon_{b}.$$  |
L349: 
L350: ###### Proof.
L351: 
L352: Let $a^{*}\in\arg\max_{a}Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)$ and $\hat{a}\in\arg\max_{a}h_{b}(\mu,a)$. Then $Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a^{*})\leq h_{b}(\mu,a^{*})+\varepsilon_{b}\leq h_{b}(\mu,\hat{a})+\varepsilon_{b}\leq Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,\hat{a})+2\varepsilon_{b}$. Rearrange. ∎
L353: ###### Theorem 4 (Worst-case provability regret bound).
L354: 
L355: Under cite69†Assumption 2 , for any initial $\mu_{0}$ whose relevant trajectories remain inside $\mathcal{D}$,
L356: 
L357:  | $$0\leq V_{B}^{*}(\mu_{0})-V_{B}^{\pi_{h}}(\mu_{0})\leq 2\sum_{b=1}^{B}\varepsilon_{b}.$$  |
L358: ###### Proof.
L359: 
L360: At each step $b$, the greedy action loses at most $2\varepsilon_{b}$ in the optimal one-step value (cite70†Lemma 3 ). Since finite-horizon reachability accumulates these losses additively under dynamic programming, summing yields the bound. ∎

L362: ### 6.3 Certificate construction from score bounds
L363: ###### Corollary 1 (Score-based certificate).
L364: 
L365: Under cite69†Assumption 2 , define for $b\geq 1$:
L366: 
L367:  | $$U_{b}(\mu):=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \mathrm{clip}\Big(\sup_{a}h_{b}(\mu,a)+\varepsilon_{b}\Big),$$  |
L368:  | $$L_{b}(\mu):=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \mathrm{clip}\Big(\sup_{a}h_{b}(\mu,a)-\varepsilon_{b}\Big),$$  |
L369: 
L370: with $\mathrm{clip}(t)=\min\{1,\max\{0,t\}\}$. Then
L371: 
L372:  | $$L_{b}(\mu)\leq V_{b}^{*}(\mu)\leq U_{b}(\mu),\qquad U_{b}(\mu)-L_{b}(\mu)\leq 2\varepsilon_{b}.$$  |
L373: ###### Proof.
L374: 
L375: For each $\mu$, $\sup_{a}Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)\in[\sup_{a}h_{b}(\mu,a)-\varepsilon_{b},\ \sup_{a}h_{b}(\mu,a)+\varepsilon_{b}]$, then apply cite71†Theorem 3 . ∎

L377: ### 6.4 Top-$k$/beam and a margin condition (fast rates)
L378: 
L379: Define the $(k+1)$-gap
L380: 
L381:  | $$\Delta_{b}^{(k)}(\mu):=Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a_{1}^{*}(\mu))-Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a_{k+1}^{*}(\mu)),$$  |
L382: 
L383: where $a_{i}^{*}(\mu)$ are actions sorted by $Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,\cdot)$.
L384: ###### Assumption 3 (Small-margin tail under occupancy).
L385: 
L386: Let $\nu_{b}$ be an occupancy distribution over states at depth $b$ (induced by the algorithm or a reference policy). Assume there exist $C_{\Delta},\beta>0$ such that for small $t$,
L387: 
L388:  | $$\nu_{b}(\Delta_{b}^{(k)}(\mu)\leq t)\leq C_{\Delta}t^{\beta}.$$  |
L389: ###### Theorem 5 (Expected fast-rate regret under margin).
L390: 
L391: If cite69†Assumptions 2 and cite72†3 hold, then for a top-$k$ policy (and similarly for a beam that preserves the top-$k$ set at each node),
L392: 
L393:  | $$\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}\sim Q}\big[V_{B}^{*}(\mu_{0})-V_{B}^{\pi_{h,k}}(\mu_{0})\big]\ \leq\ C\sum_{b=1}^{B}\varepsilon_{b}^{\beta+1}$$  |
L394: 
L395: for a constant $C$ depending on $C_{\Delta},\beta$.
L396: ###### Proof.
L397: 
L398: If $\Delta_{b}^{(k)}(\mu)>2\varepsilon_{b}$, uniform perturbation of size $\varepsilon_{b}$ cannot push the best action below rank $k$, so top-$k$ contains an optimal action. Errors only occur on ${\Delta_{b}^{(k)}\leq 2\varepsilon_{b}}$, which has probability $\leq C_{\Delta}(2\varepsilon_{b})^{\beta}$, and the loss there is $O(\varepsilon_{b})$. Multiply and sum over $b$. ∎
L399: We refer to cite51†Appendix D for more details on occupancy distribution, margin condition, fast rates, and what if margin fails.

L422: ### 7.2 Statistical estimation error (high probability)
L423: 
L424: We now state a generic “adaptive uniform deviation” lemma sufficient for non-i.i.d. data produced by exploration.
L425: ###### Lemma 4 (Adaptive uniform deviation via covering numbers).
L426: 
L427: Let $(Z_{t})_{t=1}^{n}$ be a process adapted to a filtration $(\mathcal{F}_{t})$ taking values in $\mathcal{D}$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a class of functions $f:\mathcal{D}\to[-1,1]$. Then for any $\varepsilon>0$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
L428:  |  | $\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\Big(f(Z_{t})-\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\Big)\right|$  |
L429:  |  | $\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\varepsilon+\sqrt{\frac{2\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon;\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})+2\log(2/\delta)}{n}}.$  |
L430: 
L431: The proof is given in cite56†Appendix E .
L432: ###### Lemma 5 (Metric entropy of Lipschitz score classes on doubling domains).
L433: 
L434: There exists a constant $C$ depending on $C_{\mathcal{D}},d_{\mathcal{D}}$ such that for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$,
L435: 
L436:  | $$\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon;\mathcal{H}_{b},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C\left(\frac{L_{H}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d_{\mathcal{D}}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right).$$  |
L437: ###### Proof.
L438: 
L439: Discretize $\mathcal{D}$ by an $\eta$-net and quantize function values; Lipschitzness controls interpolation error. Standard. ∎
L440: ###### Theorem 6 (A high-probability uniform approximation bound; sufficient condition).
L441: 
L442: Suppose that for each $(\mu,a)\in\mathcal{D}$ we can produce bounded targets $Y\in[0,1]$ such that
L443: 
L444:  | $$\mathbb{E}[Y\mid\mu,a]=Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a),$$  |
L445: 
L446: and we fit $\hat{h}_{b}\in\mathcal{H}_{b}$ using empirical risk minimization for squared loss (or any method achieving the uniform deviation guarantees of cite73†Lemma 4 ). Then with probability at least $1-\delta$,
L447:  | $$\sup_{(\mu,a)\in\mathcal{D}}|\hat{h}_{b}(\mu,a)-Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)|\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{app},b}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta),$$  |
L448: 
L449: where (hiding polylog factors)
L450: 
L451:  | $$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n,\delta)=O\left(L_{H}^{\frac{d_{\mathcal{D}}}{d_{\mathcal{D}}+2}}\left(\frac{\log(n/\delta)}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{d_{\mathcal{D}}+2}}\right).$$  |
L452: 
L453: The proof is given in cite57†Appendix F .
L454: ###### Remark 1 (What is $Y$).
L455: 
L456: Here $Y$ is a training dataset for hypothesis $\hat{h}$. With verifiers such as Lean and Rocq, a canonical construction is the following Monte Carlo rollout labeling: (1) Fix a horizon $b-1$. (2) Start from proof state $x$. (3) Apply action $a$ once (tactic invocation / lemma call / tool query). (4) Then follow some continuation procedure for at most $b-1$ further steps. (5) Output $Y=1$ if the proof is solved within the remaining steps, else $Y=0$.
L457: ## 8 Variable number of goals: truncation and tail control (compactness-preserving treatment)
L458: 
L459: The measure encoding naturally supports variable goal counts via $\mu(K)=|\text{goals}|$. However, compactness requires a mass bound.
L460: ### 8.1 Truncated state space and overflow event
L461: 
L462: Let $W>0$ and define $\mathcal{X}:=M_{\leq W}(K)$. Consider the overflow event
L463: 
L464:  | $$\mathsf{Overflow}:=\left\{\max_{t\leq B}\mu_{t}(K)>W\right\}.$$  |
L465: 
L466: In practice, $W$ can correspond to: (1) a hard cap induced by the prover (goal management), (2) a cap on maintained subgoals due to beam limits, (3) or a high-probability cap (quantile) justified empirically.
L467: ###### Proposition 1 (High-probability truncation).
L468: 
L469: Let $\pi$ be any policy. If
L470: 
L471:  | $$\Pr^{\pi}_{\mu_{0}}(\mathsf{Overflow})\leq\delta_{W},$$  |
L472: 
L473: then the true time-bounded success probability satisfies
L474: 
L475:  | $$V_{B}^{\pi}(\mu_{0})\geq V_{B,\leq W}^{\pi}(\mu_{0})-\delta_{W},$$  |
L476: 
L477: where $V_{B,\leq W}^{\pi}$ is the success probability in the truncated MDP that sends all overflow states to an absorbing failure state.
L478: ###### Proof.
L479: 
L480: Couple the true and truncated processes until the first overflow time. They agree on the complement of $\mathsf{Overflow}$. On $\mathsf{Overflow}$, truncated success is $\leq$ true success (since truncation forces failure). Hence the difference is at most $\Pr(\mathsf{Overflow})$. ∎
L481: Consequence. All bounds proved on the compact space $M_{\leq W}(K)$ extend to the original system with an additive $-\delta_{W}$ (for lower bounds) or $+\delta_{W}$ (for upper bounds), depending on the direction.
L482: ## 9 Representation learning via certificate gap minimization
L483: 
L484: This section formalizes an objective for learning a continuous embedding/representation that makes provability certificates tight.
L485: ### 9.1 Embedding as a decision variable
L486: 
L487: Let $\Phi$ be a class of embeddings $\phi:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to K$ (or more generally, a class of metrics/feature maps inducing $K$ and $d_{K}$). Each $\phi$ induces: (1) a state representation $\mu_{\phi}(x)=\sum_{i}\delta_{\phi(g_{i})}\in M_{\leq W}(K)$, (2) a domain $\mathcal{D}_{\phi}\subseteq M_{\leq W}(K)\times\mathcal{A}$, (3) hypothesis classes $\mathcal{H}_{\phi,b}\subseteq\mathrm{Lip}_{L_{H}}(\mathcal{D}_{\phi},[0,1])$.
L488: ### 9.2 The “best certificate” within a restricted function family
L489: 
L490: Fix a function family $\mathcal{F}_{\phi}$ (e.g., all $L$-Lipschitz functions on $\mathcal{X}_{\phi}$) from which we construct sub-/super-solutions. Define the tightest certificate bounds within $\mathcal{F}_{\phi}$:
L491:  |  | $\displaystyle U_{B,\phi}^{*}(\mu_{0})$  |
L492:  |  | $\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{U_{B}(\mu_{0}):(U_{b})_{b=0}^{B}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\phi},\ U_{b+1}\geq TU_{b},\ U_{0}\geq\mathbf{1}_{G}\right\},$  |
L493:  |  | $\displaystyle L_{B,\phi}^{*}(\mu_{0})$  |
L494:  |  | $\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{L_{B}(\mu_{0}):(L_{b})_{b=0}^{B}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\phi},\ L_{b+1}\leq TL_{b},\ L_{0}\leq\mathbf{1}_{G}\right\}.$  |
L495: These are well-defined as optimization problems over a convex cone of inequalities (when $\mathcal{F}_{\phi}$ is convex).
L496: ### 9.3 Certificate-gap minimization objective
L497: 
L498: A natural representation-learning objective is:
L499: 
L500:  | $$\min_{\phi\in\Phi}\ \mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}\sim Q}\Big[U_{B,\phi}^{*}(\mu_{0})-L_{B,\phi}^{*}(\mu_{0})\Big]\ +\ \lambda\mathsf{Comp}(\phi),$$  |
L501: where $\mathsf{Comp}(\phi)$ penalizes representations that are statistically difficult, e.g. via: (1) doubling dimension / covering numbers of $\mathcal{D}_{\phi}$, (2) Lipschitz constants required to represent $Q^{*}$, (3) distortion/bi-Lipschitz constraints to prevent degenerate embeddings.
L502: Interpretation. This objective makes explicit the tradeoff: better representations reduce approximation error and increase margins (tight certificates), but overly complex geometries increase statistical error (loose certificates).
L503: ## 10 Discussion: Easy vs hard instances through the lens of provability bounds
L504: The bounds in cite19†Sections 6 and cite24†7 show that the achievable success probability is governed by a small set of parameters: (1) effective horizon / proof length ($B$ or an effective $L_{\mathrm{eff}}$), (2) approximation error $\epsilon_{\mathrm{app}}$, (3) statistical error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stat}}(n)$ driven by geometry (doubling dimension, Lipschitz constants), (4) margin parameters $(C_{\Delta},\beta)$ for top-$k$/beam stability, (5) overflow tail $\delta_{W}$.
