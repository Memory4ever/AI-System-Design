L42: # Rank-Accuracy Trade-off for LoRA: A Gradient-Flow Analysis
L43: 
L44: Michael Rushka Affiliation: Department of Engineering Sciences and Applied Mathematics, Northwestern University, Evanston, USA Correspondence to: michaelrushka2028@u.northwestern.edu    Diego Klabjan Affiliation: Department of Industrial Engineering and Management Sciences, Northwestern University, Evanston, USA
L45: ###### Abstract
L46: Previous empirical studies have shown that LoRA achieves accuracy comparable to full-parameter methods on downstream fine-tuning tasks, even for rank-1 updates. By contrast, the theoretical underpinnings of the dependence of LoRA’s accuracy on update rank remain relatively unexplored. In this work, we compare the accuracy of rank-$r$ LoRA updates against full-parameter updates for fine-tuning tasks from a dynamical systems perspective.
L47: We perform gradient flow analysis in both full-rank and low-rank regimes to establish explicit relationships between rank and accuracy for two loss functions under LoRA. While gradient flow equations for LoRA are presented in prior work, we rigorously derive their form and show that they are identical for simultaneous and sequential LoRA parameter updates.
L48: We then use the resulting dynamical system equations to obtain closed-form relationships between LoRA rank and accuracy for trace-squared and Frobenius-norm low-rank approximation loss functions.

L54: ## 1 Introduction
L55: Parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) has become the norm for training deep learning models on downstream tasks. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (cite30†Hu et al., 2022 ) in particular has gained wide popularity among such methods. Consider $f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\to\mathbb{R}$ which has been optimized by parameters $W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$.
L56: Keeping $W_{0}$ frozen, finetuning consists of finding an update $\Delta W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ which approximates $\min\limits_{\Delta W}f(W_{0}+\Delta W)$ for smaller downstream datasets introduced post-training.
L57: While full-parameter finetuning (FPFT) methods search all of $\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ for optimal $\Delta W$, LoRA splits the classical gradient descent (GD) algorithm into separate updates which solve $\min_{A,B}\limits f(W_{0}+BA)$, where $B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ and $A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}$ for $r<<\min(n,m)$. By lowering the number of finetuning parameters from $nm$ to $r(n+m)$, LoRA enables parameter efficiency crucial for models with hundreds of billions of trainable parameters.
L58: While several empirical studies have verified the effectiveness of LoRA for finetuning models in both natural language processing (cite31†Li et al., 2024 ; cite32†Lazauskas, 2025 ; cite33†Hanindhito et al., 2025 ; cite34†Mao et al., 2025 ) and computer vision tasks (cite35†Zanella & Ayed, 2024 ; cite36†Agiza et al., 2024 ), the breadth of theoretical literature on LoRA remains sparse.
L59: Existing work in this area focuses on convergence (cite37†Malinovsky et al., 2024 ; cite38†Mu & Klabjan, 2025 ), expressiveness bounds (cite39†Zeng & Lee, 2024 ), and initialization (cite40†Hayou et al., 2024a ). Moreover, several such studies (cite41†Malladi et al., 2023 ; cite42†Jang et al., 2024 ) are conducted entirely within the neural-network setting, relying on specific architectural assumptions which do not directly generalize to the loss functions studied in our analysis.
L60: In particular, we know of only one prior work (cite43†Xu et al., 2025 ) which studies the learning dynamics of LoRA using gradient flow (GF), an area we seek to build upon. While prior studies (cite43†Xu et al., 2025 ; cite39†Zeng & Lee, 2024 ) have derived theoretical bounds on the loss and approximation error attainable by LoRA for certain rank thresholds, to our knowledge none have studied explicit, closed-form relationships between either loss or approximation error and rank parameter $r$.
L61: By approximation error, we mean the Frobenius-norm discrepancy between the full-rank fine-tuning update and its rank-$r$ approximation. We establish here these explicit rank-accuracy tradeoffs for LoRA via a gradient-flow analysis.
L62: ### 1.1 Related Work
L63: #### 1.1.1 Expressive Power of LoRA
L64: Previous work by Zeng and Lee (cite39†Zeng & Lee, 2024 ) shows that, in the context of Fully Connected Neural Networks (FNNs) and Transformer Networks (TFNs), there exists a low-rank update which perfectly adapts a prefrozen network to a smaller target network. Let $W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ represent a prefrozen model and $\bar{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ a smaller target model.
L65: Zeng and Lee demonstrate, under mild assumptions on network architecture, that there exists a rank-$R$ (where $R<\min(n,m)$) adapter $\Delta W$ such that $W_{0}+\Delta W$ exactly represents the target model, i.e. $W_{0}+\Delta W=\bar{W}$. They note that $R$ must exceed an explicit threshold which they derive. For FNNs where $\rank(\Delta W)$ is lower than the required threshold for perfect adaptation, they provide an upper bound on the resulting approximation error.
L66: However, they only demonstrate a rank-independent error bound for any $\rank(\Delta W)$ below the exact-adaptation threshold without analyzing any particular finetuning algorithm. In contrast, our analysis operates outside any specific neural network architecture and provides a rank-dependent final loss and approximation error from an optimization perspective, demonstrating the accuracy achieved by LoRA-adapted GD for any given rank $r<\min(n,m)$.
L67: #### 1.1.2 LoRA Gradient Flow
L68: To our knowledge, only Xu et al. have previously analyzed LoRA from a GF perspective (cite43†Xu et al., 2025 ). While their work studies the convergence of LoRA within a matrix factorization (MF) setting, they focus on how convergence rates and behavior depend on initialization schema. In particular, Xu et al. consider fine-tuning a perfectly factorized matrix $Y_{\text{pre}}=W_{2}W_{1}$ toward an updated target matrix $Y_{\text{ft}}$ via low-rank updates $B_{2}A_{2}$ and $B_{1}A_{1}$.
L69: They find that the low-rank updates converge to the target matrix with arbitrary precision, i.e.
L70:  | $\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert Y_{\text{ft}}-\Big(W_{2}+B_{2}(t)A_{2}(t)\Big)\Big(W_{1}+B_{1}(t)A_{1}(t)\Big)\right\rVert^{2}=0.$  |
L71: By contrast, our work focuses on the distinct classical low-rank approximation problem. In particular, we analyze how well LoRA can approximate any given matrix $W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ as a function of rank. In this sense, our work connects more closely to foundational MF work by Eckhart, Young, and Mirsky (cite44†Eckart & Young, 1936 ; cite45†Mirsky, 1960 ), providing a continuous-time extension of their theoretical findings from the perspective of LoRA optimization dynamics.
L72: Finally, while Xu et al. state the gradient-flow equations governing LoRA, we provide their rigorous derivation and show that these equations are invariant to whether LoRA updates are performed sequentially or simultaneously, resolving an ambiguity between theoretical formulations and common implementation practice.
L73: #### 1.1.3 Other Related Theoretical Work
L74: Further theoretical work on LoRA focuses on convergence rates (cite37†Malinovsky et al., 2024 ; cite38†Mu & Klabjan, 2025 ), initialization (cite40†Hayou et al., 2024a ), and learning-rate selection (cite46†Hayou et al., 2024b ). Related work on MF (cite47†Ward & Kolda, 2023 ) studies convergence behavior of factorized optimization problems but does not consider low-rank approximation.
L75: Other studies propose spectral initialization methods for LoRA (cite48†Bałazy et al., 2024 ; cite49†Meng et al., 2024 ; cite50†Wang et al., 2025 ; cite51†Mu et al., 2025 ) which we adopt but do not novelly contribute to in our analysis.
L76: ### 1.2 Contributions
L77: 
L78: We build on prior theoretical studies of LoRA by applying GF to analyze the loss and approximation error attained by LoRA as a function of rank. Our contributions are:
L79: 
L80:   1. 1.
L81: While the ODEs governing LoRA are presented in previous work (cite43†Xu et al., 2025 ), we provide the first (to our knowledge) rigorous derivation of these GF dynamics as the continuous-time limit of deterministic gradient descent with fixed step size under a low-rank parameterization.
L82: In particular, for updates of the form $BA$, we show that the resulting GF equations are identical under simultaneous updates of $A$ and $B$ (using data from the previous iterate) and sequential updates (with $A$ updated after $B$ using data from the current iterate).
L83:   2. 2.
L84: 
L85: We use the resulting GF equations to analyze the learning dynamics for the trace-squared objective under LoRA:
L86: 
L87:  | $\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\\
L88: A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}\end{subarray}}{\min}\,\frac{1}{2}\Tr^{2}(W_{0}-BA).$  |  | (1)
L89: where $r<\min(n,m)$. The trace-squared loss acts as a smooth spectral regularizer closely related to nuclear-norm minimization, arising in applications such as kernel alignment (cite52†Cristianini et al., 2006 ) and covariance shrinkage (cite53†Lancewicki, 2019 ). We show that LoRA GF dynamics admit a closed-form solution to (cite54†1 ) for near-arbitrary initial conditions.
L90: We solve for this solution and compute the resulting asymptotic loss and approximation error relative to the full-rank optimum as explicit functions of $r$.
L91:   3. 3.
L92: 
L93: We use GF to analyze the learning dynamics of the low-rank approximation problem under LoRA, namely
L94: 
L95:  | $\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\\
L96: A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}\end{subarray}}{\min}\,\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert W_{0}-BA\right\rVert^{2}.$  |  | (2)
L97: where $r<\min(n,m)$. Low-rank approximation is ubiquitous in applied mathematics and ML, with applications in areas such as recommender systems (cite55†Chen et al., 2020 ) and image processing (cite56†Liu et al., 2019 ). We show that LoRA GF dynamics for (cite57†2 ) admit a closed-form solution under a spectral initialization scheme described in previous works (cite49†Meng et al., 2024 ; cite48†Bałazy et al., 2024 ; cite43†Xu et al., 2025 ; cite50†Wang et al., 2025 ; cite51†Mu et al., 2025 ).
L98: Using spectral initialization, we show that the nonzero singular values of the rank-$r$ finetuning matrix $BA$ converge to the top $r$ singular values of $W_{0}$, demonstrating that LoRA achieves the optimal low-rank solution characterized by the Eckart–Young–Mirsky (EYM) theorem (cite44†Eckart & Young, 1936 ).
L99: ### 1.3 Notation
L100: Throughout this paper, capital letters denote matrices, and lowercase letters denote scalars. Boldface letters denote vectors. For a matrix $A$, we write $A^{T}$ for its transpose and $a_{ij}$ for its $(i,j)$-th entry. We use $\left\lVert A\right\rVert$ to denote the Frobenius norm of $A$ and $\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{2}$ to denote the Euclidean norm of a vector $\mathbf{a}$. We use $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ to denote the integer floor function. We denote $\Tr^{2}(W):=\Big(\Tr(W)\Big)^{2}$.
L101: For a time-dependent function $f(t)$ we write
L102: $f^{\prime}(t):=\frac{d}{dt}f(t)$ for its time derivative. Uppercase $A(t)$ refers to a matrix-valued function of time. When emphasizing time dependence, we write $a_{ij}(t)$ for the $(i,j)$-th entry of $A(t)$, with $a_{ij}(0)$ denoting its value at initialization.
L103: 
L104: We refer to the set of all natural numbers using $\mathbb{N}$ and to the set of non-negative real numbers using $\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}$.
L105: Let $B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$. For a scalar-valued function $g(B,A)$ of two matrices, we define the partial gradient $\nabla_{B}g(B,A):=\frac{\partial g}{\partial B}$ to be the matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ whose $(i,j)$-th entry is $\frac{\partial g}{\partial b_{ij}}$.
L106: Similarly, let $W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$. For a scalar-valued function $f(W)$ of a single matrix, we define the gradient ${\nabla f(W):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial W}}$ to be the matrix in $\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ whose $(i,j)$-th entry is $\frac{\partial f}{\partial w_{ij}}$.
L107: 
L108: The $n\times n$ identity matrix is denoted by $I_{n}$. The $n\times m$ matrix of all zeroes is denoted by $\mathbf{0}_{n\times m}$.
L109: ## 2 Results
L110: ### 2.1 LoRA Gradient Flow Equations
L111: 
L112: Denote the Cartesian product space $\Theta:=\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}$. Consider a function $g:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}$ which we seek to minimize over $\Theta$:
L113: 
L114:  | $\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\\
L115: A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}\end{subarray}}{\min}\,g\left(B,A\right)$  |  | (3)
L116: In the context of LoRA, we will have ${g(B,A)=f(W_{0}+BA)}$ for $f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\to\mathbb{R}$, where $W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ gives the weights from a pre-trained task. We solve (cite58†3 ) via LoRA-adapted GD given in Algorithm cite59†1 of Appendix cite19†A . Algorithm cite59†1 applies alternating updates to $A$ and $B$ and is agnostic to how these updates are performed.
L117: For $\lambda=1$, the updates of $A$ and $B$ are performed simultaneously, with both parameters evaluated using data from the previous iteration. In contrast, $\lambda=0$ corresponds to sequential updates, where $B$ is first updated using its prior iterate, after which $A$ is updated using the newly computed value of $B$ from the current iteration. For $0<\lambda<1$, the update of $A$ uses a convex combination of the values of $B$ from the current and previous iterations.
L118: Algorithm cite59†1 further generalizes the update scheme by allowing $A$ and $B$ to be updated a total of $k$ times within each iteration. While $\lambda=1$ is most efficient in practice for parallelism, we allow arbitrary $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}$ to show that the resulting GF equations for LoRA are invariant to the choice of update scheme.
L119: The update equations at any given iteration $i$ of Algorithm cite59†1 are given by
L120: 
L121:  | $\displaystyle B_{ik+j+1}^{(\alpha)}$  | $\displaystyle=B_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)}-\alpha\nabla_{B}g\left(B_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)},A_{ik}^{(\alpha)}\right)$  |  | (4)
L122:  | $\displaystyle A_{ik+j+1}^{(\alpha)}$  | $\displaystyle=A_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)}-\alpha\nabla_{A}g\left(\left[\lambda B_{ik}^{(\alpha)}+(1-\lambda)B_{(i+1)k}^{(\alpha)}\right],A_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)}\right).$  |  | (5)
L123: The discrete sets of updates for $A$ and $B$ motivate us to define their continuous affine interpolations:
L124:  | $\displaystyle Y_{\alpha}(t)$  | $\displaystyle=B_{\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\rfloor}^{(\alpha)}-\left(t-\left\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\right\rfloor\alpha\right)\nabla_{B}g\left(B_{\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\rfloor}^{(\alpha)},A_{h(t)}^{(\alpha)}\right)$  |  | (6)
L125:  | $\displaystyle X_{\alpha}(t)$  | $\displaystyle=A_{\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\rfloor}^{(\alpha)}-\left(t-\left\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\right\rfloor\alpha\right)\nabla_{A}g\left(\left[\lambda B_{h(t)}^{(\alpha)}+(1-\lambda)B_{h(t)+k}^{(\alpha)}\right],A_{\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\rfloor}^{(\alpha)}\right),$  |  | (7)
L126: where
L127: 
L128:  | $\displaystyle h(t)$  | $\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\right\rfloor-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{t}{\alpha}\right\rfloor\mod k\right).$  |  | (8)
L129: 
L130: We proceed by defining the candidate GF dynamics a priori. Specifically, let $X:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}$ and $Y:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ be matrix-valued functions satisfying the initial-value problem (IVP)
L131:  | $\displaystyle\frac{dY(t)}{dt}$  | $\displaystyle=-\nabla_{Y}g\Big(Y(t),X(t)\Big)$  |  | (9)
L132:  | $\displaystyle\frac{dX(t)}{dt}$  | $\displaystyle=-\nabla_{X}g\Big(Y(t),X(t)\Big)$  |  | (10)
L133:  | $\displaystyle Y(0)$  | $\displaystyle=B_{0}$  |  | (11)
L134:  | $\displaystyle X(0)$  | $\displaystyle=A_{0}.$  |  | (12)
L135: 
L136: We justify the existence of solutions $Y(t)$ and $X(t)$ under the following assumptions:
L137: ###### Assumption 2.1 (Uniform Boundedness of Iterates)
L138: 
L139: We assume that all iterates $\left\lVert B_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)}\right\rVert$ and $\left\lVert A_{ik+j}^{(\alpha)}\right\rVert$ remain uniformly bounded during finetuning.
L140: ###### Assumption 2.2 (Uniform Boundedness of Gradient)
L141: 
L142: We assume that the gradient $\left\lVert\nabla g(B,A)\right\rVert$ is uniformly bounded on bounded subsets of $\Theta$. That is, for every bounded set $S\subset\Theta$, there exists a constant $M_{S}<\infty$ such that
L143: 
L144:  | $$\|\nabla g(B,A)\|\leq M_{S}\quad\text{for all }(B,A)\in S.$$  |
L145: 
L146: Definitions of norms and gradients on $\Theta$ are provided in Remark cite60†A.1 of Appendix cite19†A .
L147: In subsequent portions of our derivation, we assume that $g$ is Lipschitz smooth:
L148: ###### Assumption 2.3 (Lipschitz Smoothness of $g$)
L149: 
L150: The function $g$ is Lipschitz smooth on bounded subsets of $\Theta$. That is, for any $(B_{1},A_{1}),(B_{2},A_{2})\in\Theta$ satisfying
L151: 
L152:  | $\displaystyle\max\!\Big(\left\lVert B_{1}\right\rVert,\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert,\left\lVert B_{2}\right\rVert,\left\lVert A_{2}\right\rVert\Big)\leq R,$  |  | (13)
L153: 
L154: there exists a constant $L_{R}>0$ such that
L155:  | $\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla g(B_{2},A_{2})-\nabla g(B_{1},A_{1})\right\rVert\leq L_{R}\left\lVert(B_{2},A_{2})-(B_{1},A_{1})\right\rVert.$  |  | (14)
L156: 
L157: Under these assumptions, we show that
L158: 
L159:  | $\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0}||Y_{\alpha}(t)-Y(t)||$  | $\displaystyle=0$  |  | (15)
L160:  | $\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0}||X_{\alpha}(t)-X(t)||$  | $\displaystyle=0$  |  | (16)
L161: for all $t\geq 0$. The uniform convergence in (cite61†15 –cite62†16 ) implies that the continuous affine interpolations defined in (cite63†6 –cite64†7 ), and thus the underlying discrete iterates themselves, evolve according to the dynamical system in (cite65†9 –cite66†10 ) in the limit where $\alpha\to 0$. This brings us to our final result:
L162: ###### Theorem 2.4
L163: 
L164: Consider an objective function $g:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying Assumptions cite67†2.1 –cite68†2.3 which is minimized via Algorithm cite59†1 . During finetuning, the iterates produced by Algorithm cite59†1 evolve according to the dynamical system in (cite65†9 –cite66†10 ) for arbitrary $\lambda\in[0,1]$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}$.
L165: The full proof of Theorem cite69†2.4 is provided in Appendix cite19†A . We now present two problems to demonstrate use of this result to analyze how the accuracy of LoRA depends on update rank.
L166: ### 2.2 Trace-Squared Objective
L167: 
L168: Consider the finetuning problem
L169: 
L170:  | $\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\end{subarray}}{\min}\,\frac{1}{2}\Tr^{2}(W_{0}-W),$  |  | (17)
L171: 
L172: as well as its rank-$r$ constrained equivalent:
L173: 
L174:  | $\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\\
L175: A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}\end{subarray}}{\min}\,\frac{1}{2}\Tr^{2}(W_{0}-BA),$  |  | (18)
L176: where $r<n$. We seek to compare the final losses obtained by full-rank GD on (cite70†17 ) and by LoRA-adapted GD on (cite71†18 ) as a function of $r$. Additionally, we quantify the rank-dependent approximation error between the respective minimizers of (cite70†17 ) and (cite71†18 ).
L177: 
L178: From the GF equation for full-rank GD, we know that the learning dynamics of (cite70†17 ) during finetuning are governed by
L179:  | $\displaystyle\frac{dU(t)}{dt}$  | $\displaystyle=-\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\Tr^{2}\Big(W_{0}-U(t)\Big)\right)=\Tr\big(W_{0}-U(t)\big)\,I_{n}.$  |  | (19)
L180: 
L181: Here, $U:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ denotes the full-rank iterate produced by full-rank GF applied to (cite70†17 ) at time $t\geq 0$. We show that the unique closed-form solution to (cite72†19 ) is given by
L182: 
L183:  | $\displaystyle U(t)$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{1-e^{-nt}}{n}\Tr\left(W_{0}-Y_{0}X_{0}\right)I_{n}+Y_{0}X_{0},$  |  | (20)
L184: where we initialize $U(0)=Y_{0}X_{0}$. The full-rank iterates converge to the minimizer of (cite70†17 ):
L185: 
L186:  | $\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}U(t)=\frac{1}{n}\Tr\!\left(W_{0}-Y_{0}X_{0}\right)I_{n}+Y_{0}X_{0}.$  |  | (21)
L187: For consistency in our comparison of the full-rank and low-rank minimizers, we employ the same initialization $Y_{0}X_{0}$ in the LoRA setting. Following LoRA initialization (cite30†Hu et al., 2022 ), we initialize $Y_{0}=\mathbf{0}_{n\times r}$ and draw the entries of $X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}$ i.i.d. from a centered Gaussian distribution:
