L434: ### 5.3 Hierarchical (cut-aware) learning
L435: ##### Setting.
L436: 
L437: Data are generated from the cut-structured distribution $Q_{\mathrm{DAG}}$ with latent proof DAG $Z$. We learn a decomposition policy $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{dec}}\in H_{\mathrm{dec}}$ and a solver policy $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}\in H_{\mathrm{sol}}$ via EM/VAE-style alternation: variational inference $r_{\phi}(z\mid x,y)$ and posterior-weighted ERM. Test-time search uses top-$k$ at both levels with completeness and finite candidate sets.
L438: ###### Theorem 2 (informal; Statistical provability lowerbound of Hierarchical (cut-aware) learning).
L439: 
L440: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 , cite158†5 and cite159†6 , and the margin conditions for both decision types, there exist $\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dec}},\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{sol}}$ such that
L441:  | $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[V^{\mathrm{hier}\text{-}\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}(T,X)\big]\ \geq\ 1$  | $\displaystyle-K_{\mathrm{dec}}\Big(\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\mathrm{dec}}(S)]+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dec}}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{dec}})^{p_{\mathrm{dec}}}\Big)$  |
L442:  |  | $\displaystyle-K_{\mathrm{sol}}\Big(\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k_{\mathrm{sol}}}^{\mathrm{sol}}(S)]+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{sol}}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{sol}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{sol}})^{p_{\mathrm{sol}}}\Big),$  |
L443: where $K_{\mathrm{dec}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{sol}}$ are the expected numbers of high-level and low-level decisions along a successful hierarchical proof, and $\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}$ denotes the one-step mixture KL under the true success-filtered posterior decision distribution.
L444: 
L445: Furthermore, with probability at least $1-\delta$, each one-step KL admits the three-term decomposition
L446:  | $$\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{typ}})\ \leq\ \underbrace{\inf_{\pi\in H_{\mathrm{typ}}}\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}(\pi)}_{\text{approximation}}+\underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}(n,T,\delta;H_{\mathrm{typ}})}_{\text{sequential estimation}}+\underbrace{O\!\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{PostKL}(\phi)}\right)}_{\text{inference (ELBO gap)}},\qquad\mathrm{typ}\in\{\mathrm{dec},\mathrm{sol}\}.$$  |
L447: ##### Interpretation.
L448: The hierarchical guarantee decomposes additively into a high-level and a low-level term, reflecting the need to balance decomposition quality with local solving quality. The bound makes explicit how latent inference (ELBO tightness), sequential generalization, and margins jointly control success. Structural parameters such as $(D,\alpha,b_{\mathrm{eff}})$ influence success through the induced counts $K_{\mathrm{dec}},K_{\mathrm{sol}}$ (e.g. contraction $\alpha$ can reduce low-level workload).

L467: ###### Theorem 3 (informal; Exponential separation (cut-aware vs cut-free on cut-structured problems)).
L468: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 , cite158†5 , cite159†6 and cite162†7 , (and matching margin/learning-curve assumptions), to achieve the same target success level $1-\delta$, a cut-free flat learner requires exponentially more data/resources than a cut-aware hierarchical learner. Concretely, if one-step learning follows $\bar{\mathrm{KL}}\lesssim N^{-\gamma}$ and the margin exponent is $p\in(0,1)$, then sufficient sample sizes obey
L469:  | $$N_{\mathrm{flat}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{flat}}}{\delta}\right)^{1/(p\gamma)},\qquad N_{\mathrm{hier}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{hier}}}{\delta}\right)^{1/(p\gamma)},$$  |
L470: 
L471: hence
L472: 
L473:  | $$\frac{N_{\mathrm{flat}}}{N_{\mathrm{hier}}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{D/(p\gamma)}.$$  |
L474: ##### Interpretation.
L475: When proofs have reusable cut/lemma/sketch structure, explicitly modeling and searching over this structure (subgoal decomposition + memoization) reduces the effective number of required decisions from $\Omega(\Lambda^{D})$ to $O(\lambda^{D})$, yielding an exponential improvement in the learning burden required to reach a fixed success probability.
L476: This provides theoretical support for hierarchical subgoal decomposition mechanisms used in modern agentic theorem provers, without contradicting worst-case hardness: the gain is distributional and hinges on the presence of reusable structure in the problem distribution.
L477: ## 6 Conclusion
L478: We presented a theoretical framework for LLM-guided formal theorem proving as a finite-horizon deterministic MDP over compact metric state/action spaces, together with distributional assumptions that capture “proof-friendly” structure.
L479: Modeling cut/lemma/sketch reuse via a latent proof DAG yields success guarantees for hierarchical top-$k$ search in which (i) search and learning contributions separate cleanly and (ii) learning is controlled by sequential complexity measures even in the presence of latent structure.
L480: Most importantly, when cut elimination provably expands reusable DAG structure into exponentially larger cut-free trees, we obtain an exponential separation: cut-aware hierarchical learning and search can achieve a given success level with exponentially fewer samples than any flat cut-free approach under the same margin-driven analysis.
L481: This provides a principled explanation for why subgoal decomposition, despite being a difficult auxiliary task, can be a statistically and computationally rational design choice in modern agentic theorem provers.

L1802: ### H.3 Lower bounding cut-elimination blow-up from latent DAG statistics
L1803: 
L1804: cite276†Assumption 17 postulates blow-up directly at the level of decision counts. Here we give a general way to justify such blow-up from statistics of the latent proof DAG.
L1805: 
L1806: Fix a DAG instance $z\in\mathcal{Z}$ with a depth partition $U_{d}(z)\subseteq U(z)$ of unique subgoals (canonicalized nodes). Let $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ be the unfolded cut-free tree.
L1807: ##### Path-multiplicity statistic.
L1808: 
L1809: For each unique node $u\in U(z)$, define its path multiplicity
L1810: 
L1811:  | $$M(u;z):=\#\{\text{directed root-to-$u$ paths in }z\}.$$  |  | (96)
L1812: 
L1813: Intuitively, $M(u;z)$ counts how many times $u$ must be duplicated when shared structure is unfolded into a tree.
L1814: ###### Lemma 11 (Unfolding multiplicity equals path multiplicity).
L1815: 
L1816: For any DAG $z$ and any node $u\in U(z)$, the number of occurrences of the subproof rooted at $u$ in the unfolded tree $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ is at least $M(u;z)$. Consequently, the number of goal-occurrences at depth $d$ in $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ satisfies
L1817: 
L1818:  | $$\mathrm{Occ}_{d}(\mathrm{CE}(z))\ \geq\ \sum_{u\in U_{d}(z)}M(u;z).$$  |  | (97)
L1819: ###### Proof sketch.
L1820: 
L1821: Each distinct root-to-$u$ path in $z$ corresponds to a distinct context in which $u$ is used. Unfolding removes sharing by duplicating each shared subproof for each distinct usage context, hence at least one copy per path. Summing over nodes at depth $d$ yields (cite284†97 ). ∎
L1822: 
L1823: cite285†Lemma 11 implies that blow-up is controlled by how rapidly path multiplicities grow with depth. A particularly transparent sufficient condition uses a reuse factor.
L1824: ###### Assumption 20 (Layered reuse lower bound).
L1825: 
L1826: There exists $r>1$ such that for every depth $d\in\{1,\dots,D\}$ and every node $u\in U_{d}(Z)$, the in-degree of $u$ from depth $d-1$ is at least $r$ almost surely:
L1827: 
L1828:  | $$\mathrm{indeg}_{d-1\to d}(u)\ \geq\ r.$$  |  | (98)
L1829: ###### Proposition 3 (Exponential blow-up from reuse).
L1830: 
L1831: Under cite286†Assumption 20 , for every $z$,
L1832: 
L1833:  | $$\sum_{u\in U_{d}(z)}M(u;z)\ \geq\ r^{d},\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad\mathrm{Occ}_{d}(\mathrm{CE}(z))\ \geq\ r^{d}.$$  |  | (99)
L1834: 
L1835: In particular, the total number of tree decision points (and thus $N_{\mathrm{flat}}(\mathrm{CE}(z))$ in any reasonable encoding) is $\Omega(r^{D})$.
L1836: ###### Proof.
L1837: 
L1838: Let $P_{d}(z):=\sum_{u\in U_{d}(z)}M(u;z)$ be the total path multiplicity mass at depth $d$. By definition $P_{0}(z)=1$. If each node at depth $d$ has at least $r$ parents at depth $d-1$, then each path to depth $d-1$ can be extended in at least $r$ ways to depth $d$, implying $P_{d}(z)\geq rP_{d-1}(z)$. Induction gives $P_{d}(z)\geq r^{d}$. Finally apply cite285†Lemma 11 . ∎
L1839: ##### Connection to $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (unique branching).
L1840: 
L1841: In many hierarchical generators one has both: (i) unique-node growth $\mathbb{E}[|U_{d}(Z)|]\asymp b_{\mathrm{eff}}^{d}$, and (ii) reuse $r>1$ as above. Then the unfolded tree depth-$d$ occurrences scale as $(rb_{\mathrm{eff}})^{d}$, while the DAG unique nodes scale as $b_{\mathrm{eff}}^{d}$, producing an exponential ratio $r^{d}$.
L1842: ### H.4 Concrete example: a layered reuse generator with $(D,\alpha,b_{\mathrm{eff}},r)$
L1843: 
L1844: We give a simple stylized generator where the blow-up and the hierarchical advantage can be computed explicitly and connected to the parameters $(D,\alpha,b_{\mathrm{eff}})$.
L1845: ##### Example model.
L1846: 
L1847: Fix integers $D\geq 1$ and $b_{\mathrm{eff}}\geq 1$, and a reuse factor $r\geq 2$. Construct a layered DAG with unique nodes
L1848: 
L1849:  | $$|U_{d}|=b_{\mathrm{eff}}^{d},\qquad d=0,\dots,D.$$  |  | (100)
L1850: 
L1851: Each unique node at depth $d-1$ produces exactly
L1852: 
L1853:  | $$b:=rb_{\mathrm{eff}}$$  |  | (101)
L1854: child occurrences at depth $d$ in the (conceptual) tree expansion, but canonicalization merges them so that only $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$ unique children remain.
