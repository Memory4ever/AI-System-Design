L521: This violates the requirement in Definition cite76†3.1 , which mandates that the transition operators $\mathbf{A}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{t}$ depend solely on the input $\mathbf{x}_{\leq t}$ and not on any intermediate state.
L522: Standard linear attention approximates the gain term in (cite116†25 ) as $\mathbf{1}$ and omit the non-linearity $\sigma(\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\mathbf{k}_{t})$ in residual. While this simplification ensures state-independent operators and thus satisfies the parallelism condition in Definition cite76†3.1 , it comes at the cost of reduced expressivity—effectively discarding the very mechanisms that enable dynamic, input-aware memory updates.
L523: Crucially, however, Definition cite76†3.1 is satisfied as long as $\mathbf{A}_{t}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{t}$ are state-independent, regardless of how expressive they are. This observation forms the foundation of PRISM: rather than computing the ideal update directly, we design a state-independent predictor for $\mathbf{B}_{t}$ that approximates the residual and gain terms of the ideal solver using only local input context.
L524: By anchoring the simulation to the current token and decoupling write and forget dynamics, PRISM recovers the essential structure of the ideal rule while strictly adhering to the parallelism constraint.
L525: ## Appendix D Sensitivity Analysis of Write-Forget Dynamics via Spectral Perturbation
L526: 
L527: In this section, we rigorously analyze the error propagation properties of the Write-Forget Decoupling strategy. We establish the theoretical foundation by bridging HiPPO theory with discrete sequence modeling via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) discretization, and then perform a worst-case spectral perturbation analysis to bound the total accumulated error.
L528: ### D.1 Theoretical Foundation: HiPPO, ODEs, and ZOH
L529: 
L530: We posit that the optimal strategy for sequence modeling is to approximate a continuous-time measure update process.
L531: 
L532: The HiPPO Formalism. As established in HiPPO (cite64†Gu et al., 2020 ), the problem of online function approximation over a history window can be cast as an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE):
L533: 
L534:  | $$\dot{\mathbf{h}}(t)=\mathcal{A}(t)\mathbf{h}(t)+\mathcal{B}(t)\mathbf{x}(t)$$  |  | (28)
L535: where the state $\mathbf{h}(t)$ represents the coefficients of the input history projected onto a basis of orthogonal polynomials (e.g., Legendre or Laguerre). The operator $\mathcal{A}(t)$ governs the ”forgetting” or compression of history.
L536: Justification for Zero-Order Hold (ZOH). In language modeling, the input $\mathbf{x}(t)$ is not a continuous signal but a sequence of discrete tokens arriving at times $t_{k}$. The standard method to discretize Eq. (cite117†28 ) while preserving the underlying continuous memory characteristics is the Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) assumption, which posits that the input $\mathbf{x}(t)$ is constant during the interval $[t_{k-1},t_{k})$. This yields the discrete recurrence:
L537:  | $$\mathbf{h}_{k}=\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{h}_{k-1}+\mathbf{B}_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}$$  |  | (29)
L538: 
L539: where $\mathbf{A}_{k}=\exp(\Delta\mathcal{A})$ and $\mathbf{B}_{k}=(\mathbf{A}_{k}-\mathbf{I})\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{B}$. Consequently, the discrete matrix $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ inherits the spectral stability properties of the continuous operator $\mathcal{A}$. PRISM approximates this discrete $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ directly.
L540: ### D.2 Spectral Bounds via Gated DeltaNet
L541: 
L542: A critical requirement for stability is that the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ must lie within the unit circle. PRISM adopts the decay parameterization from Gated DeltaNet.
L543: ###### Lemma D.1 (Bounded Spectrum of Gated DeltaNet).
L544: 
L545: The forgetting operator in PRISM is parameterized as $\mathbf{A}_{t}=\mathbf{I}-\beta_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}^{\top}$, where $\beta_{t}\in[0,1]$ and $\left\lVert\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\rVert\leq 1$. The eigenvalues of this matrix satisfy:
L546: 
L547:  | $$\lambda(\mathbf{A}_{t})\in[-1,1]$$  |  | (30)
L548: ###### Proof.
L549: 
L550: We analyze the spectrum of $\mathbf{A}_{t}$ by considering the eigenspace aligned with $\mathbf{k}_{t}$ and its orthogonal complement:
L551: 
L552:   1. 1.
L553: 
L554: Orthogonal Subspace: For any vector $\mathbf{v}$ such that $\mathbf{v}\perp\mathbf{k}_{t}$ (spanning $d-1$ dimensions), we have:
L555: 
L556:  | $$\mathbf{A}_{t}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}-\beta_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}(\mathbf{k}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v}$$  |  | (31)
L557: 
L558: Thus, there are $d-1$ eigenvalues equal to $1$.
L559: 
L560:   2. 2.
L561: Principal Subspace: For the vector $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{k}_{t}$, we have:
L562: 
L563:  | $$\mathbf{A}_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}=\mathbf{k}_{t}-\beta_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}(\mathbf{k}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{t})=(1-\beta_{t}\left\lVert\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2})\mathbf{k}_{t}$$  |  | (32)
L564: 
L565: The remaining eigenvalue is $\lambda_{min}=1-\beta_{t}\left\lVert\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}$.
L566: Given the constraints $0\leq\beta_{t}\leq 1$ and $0\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 1$, the term $\beta_{t}\left\lVert\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}$ lies in $[0,1]$. Consequently, $\lambda_{min}\in[0,1]\subset[-1,1]$. This structural guarantee ($\rho(\mathbf{A}_{t})\leq 1$) ensures the system is non-expansive and prevents exponential divergence. ∎
L567: ### D.3 Decomposition of Cumulative Error Terms
L568: 
L569: We now analyze the impact of using a Low-Rank approximation for $\mathbf{A}$. Let the ideal operator be $\mathbf{A}^{*}$ and the PRISM approximation be $\hat{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}^{*}(I+\Delta)$. The total state error $\mathbf{e}_{T}$ at step $T$ is the sum of error contributions from all previous time steps $k$. We decompose this into Transmission Error (from $\Delta\mathbf{A}$) and Injection Error (from $\Delta\mathbf{B}$).
L570:  | $$\mathbf{e}_{T}=\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{path}(T,k)+\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{inj}(T,k)$$  |  | (33)
L571: 
L572: For the Transmission Error, we focus on a single invariant subspace with eigenvalue $\lambda$. The variance of the error contribution from lag $\tau=T-k$ is derived using the unbiased log-normal perturbation model:
L573: 
L574:  | $$V(\tau,\lambda)\coloneqq\text{Var}(\mathcal{E}_{path}(\tau))\approx\tau|\lambda|^{2\tau}\sigma^{2}f(\lambda)$$  |  | (34)
L575: where $\sigma^{2}f(\lambda)$ is the variance of the relative perturbation in the eigenvalue.
L576: ### D.4 Robust Error Bound via Max-Sum Analysis
L577: 
L578: We seek a robust upper bound for the total error. Assumption (Quadratic Scaling of Floating Point Error). We assume the numerical precision of the decay rate is bounded by relative error. Since $\lambda=1-\beta$, a relative error in $\beta$ implies the noise variance scales quadratically with the decay magnitude $\gamma=1-|\lambda|$ (or $\gamma\approx-\ln|\lambda|$):
L579: 
L580:  | $$\sigma^{2}f(\lambda)\leq K\gamma^{2}$$  |  | (35)
L581: Substituting this into the variance equation:
L582: 
L583:  | $$V(\tau,\gamma)\leq\tau e^{-\gamma\tau}(K\gamma^{2})$$  |  | (36)
L584: ###### Theorem D.2 (Logarithmic Worst-Case Stability).
L585: 
L586: The worst-case accumulated error of the Forgetting operator at step $T$, maximizing over possible decay rates, grows at most logarithmically.
L587: ###### Proof.
L588: 
L589: The proof proceeds in two steps:
L590: 
L591: 1. Define Worst-Case Envelope $g(\tau)$. We aim to find the decay rate $\gamma^{*}$ that maximizes the error at a specific lag $\tau$. Let $h(\gamma)=K\tau\gamma^{2}e^{-\gamma\tau}$. Differentiating with respect to $\gamma$ and setting the derivative to zero yields the critical point $\gamma^{*}=2/\tau$. Substituting $\gamma^{*}$ back into the variance function $V$:
L592:  | $$g(\tau)=\max_{\gamma}V(\tau,\gamma)=K\tau\left(\frac{2}{\tau}\right)^{2}e^{-2}=\frac{4Ke^{-2}}{\tau}$$  |  | (37)
L593: 
L594: 2. Summation Bound. The total error variance at step $T$ is bounded by the sum of these worst-case envelopes:
L595: 
L596:  | $$\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}g(\tau)\propto\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\tau}\approx\ln T$$  |  | (38)
L597: 
L598: Thus, we conclude that $\text{Var}(\mathbf{e}_{T})\leq O(\ln T)$. ∎
L599: While the worst-case error grows logarithmically, the average behavior is even more stable. We now show that the total error energy (sum of variances over time) is linear in $T$, implying the average error per step is constant.
L600: ###### Theorem D.3 (Constant Average-Case Error).
L601: 
L602: For any fixed mode $\lambda$ (and thus fixed decay rate $\gamma$), the total accumulated error energy over $T$ steps is $O(T)$, implying that the average error variance per step is $O(1)$.
L603: ###### Proof.
L604: 
L605: Let $\mathcal{V}_{total}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{Var}(\mathbf{e}_{t})$ denote the total error energy accumulated over the entire sequence. We exchange the order of summation to count the total contribution of each individual injection $\mathbf{B}_{k}$ throughout its lifetime:
L606: 
L607:  | $$\mathcal{V}_{total}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{k=1}^{t}V(t-k,\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{T}\underbrace{\sum_{\tau=0}^{T-k}V(\tau,\gamma)}_{\text{Lifetime Error of }\mathbf{B}_{k}}$$  |  | (39)
L608: Consider the inner sum for a fixed $\gamma$. Recall that $V(\tau,\gamma)\leq\tau e^{-\gamma\tau}(K\gamma^{2})$. We upper bound the finite sum by the infinite series. Using the identity $\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\tau r^{\tau}=\frac{r}{(1-r)^{2}}$ with $r=e^{-\gamma}$:
L609: 
L610:  | $$\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}V(\tau,\gamma)\leq K\gamma^{2}\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\tau(e^{-\gamma})^{\tau}=K\gamma^{2}\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{(1-e^{-\gamma})^{2}}$$  |  | (40)
L611: For small $\gamma$ (long context), we use the Taylor approximation $1-e^{-\gamma}\approx\gamma$ and $e^{-\gamma}\approx 1$:
L612: 
L613:  | $$\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}V(\tau,\gamma)\approx K\gamma^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}=K$$  |  | (41)
L614: 
L615: This cancellation is crucial. It demonstrates that the total error contributed by any single injection $\mathbf{B}_{k}$ over its entire lifetime is bounded by a constant $K$, independent of the decay rate $\gamma$. Finally, summing over all $T$ injections:
L616:  | $$\mathcal{V}_{total}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}K=O(T)\implies\text{Average}(\text{Var}(\mathbf{e}_{t}))=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{total}}{T}=O(1)$$  |  | (42)
L617: 
L618: This concludes that while the worst-case error at a specific adversarial step may scale with $\ln T$, the average error remains constant for any stable dynamics. ∎
L619: ### D.5 The Necessity of Rank-L Injection (B-Perturbation)
L620: 
L621: In contrast, consider the Injection Error (Term B). The error variance at lag $\tau$ is simply $e^{-\gamma\tau}\sigma_{B}^{2}$. The worst case is trivial to achieve: simply set $\gamma\to 0$ (i.e., $\lambda\to 1$), which corresponds to persistent memory.
L622: 
L623:  | $$g_{B}(\tau)=\max_{\gamma}e^{-\gamma\tau}\sigma_{B}^{2}=\sigma_{B}^{2}$$  |  | (43)
L624: 
L625: Summing this over the sequence yields:
L626:  | $$\text{Var}(\mathbf{e}_{T}^{(B)})=\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\sigma_{B}^{2}=T\cdot\sigma_{B}^{2}$$  |  | (44)
L627: 
L628: Final Comparison:
L629: 
L630:   * •
L631: 
L632: A-Error (Forgetting): Worst-case $O(\ln T)$, Average-case $O(1)$.
L633: 
L634:   * •
L635: 
L636: B-Error (Writing): Worst-case $O(T)$ (Linear, simply set $\lambda\to 1$).
L637: This provides a decisive theoretical justification for PRISM: The system is exponentially more robust to approximations in $A$ than in $B$. Therefore, computational budget (Rank) must be prioritized for $B$ (via Iterative Residual Fitting) rather than $A$.

L638: ## Appendix E Simulating State-Dependence via Structural Alignment
L639: 
L640: The ideal update is driven by two terms derived from the pre-activation vector $\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\mathbf{k}_{t}$. We now analyze the error incurred by replacing the unavailable global interaction $\mathbf{z}_{t}$ with a local proxy $\mathbf{u}_{t}=\text{ShortConv}(\mathbf{x}_{\leq t})$ in PRISM.
L641: 
L642: Assume the linear recurrence follows a stable decay pattern (Fading Memory). The true pre-activation vector can be expanded as:
L643:  | $$\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{S}_{t-1}\mathbf{k}_{t}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\gamma^{t-i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{k}_{t},$$  |  | (45)
L644: 
L645: where $\gamma\in(0,1)$ is the effective decay rate. Assume the ShortConv proxy effectively captures the most recent terms through a local window of size $w$:
L646: 
L647:  | $$\mathbf{u}_{t}=\left(\sum_{i=t-w}^{t-1}\gamma^{t-i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{k}_{t}$$  |  | (46)
L648: and $\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{t}\|_{2}\leq 1.$ Then, the approximation error is dominated by the “tail” of the sequence:
L649: 
L650:  | $$\|\mathbf{z}_{t}-\mathbf{u}_{t}\|_{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{t-w-1}\gamma^{t-i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{k}_{t}\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{t-w-1}\gamma^{t-i}\leq\frac{\gamma^{w+1}}{1-\gamma}.$$  |  | (47)
L651: 
L652: Thus, the error between the global state projection and the local proxy decays exponentially with the kernel size $w$.
L653: ## Appendix F Rank Expansion via Multi-Component Injection
L654: 
L655: We analyze the rank of the total injection matrix $\mathbf{B}_{t}$ in PRISM. The update at time $t$ is constructed as a sum of $L$ rank-$1$ components:
L656: 
L657:  | $$\mathbf{B}_{t}=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\beta^{(l)}_{t}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(l)}_{t}\mathbf{k}^{(l)\top}_{t},$$  |  | (48)
L658: 
L659: where each term is an outer product scaled by a scalar gain $\beta^{(l)}_{t}$. By the sub-additivity of matrix rank,
L660:  | $$\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}_{t})\leq\sum_{l=1}^{L}\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(l)}_{t}\mathbf{k}^{(l)\top}_{t}\bigr)=L.$$  |  | (49)
L661: 
L662: This bound is tight when the vectors $\{\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(l)}_{t}\}_{l=1}^{L}$ (or $\{\mathbf{k}^{(l)}_{t}\}_{l=1}^{L}$ ) are linearly independent.
L663: In contrast, standard linear attention uses a single outer product $\mathbf{v}_{t}\mathbf{k}_{t}^{\top}$ , yielding $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}_{t})=1$. PRISM expands the reachable update directions to a structured low-dimensional cone spanned by $\{\mathbf{k}_{t}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{k}_{t}^{(L)}\}$. This allows the model to perform multi-modal updates—simultaneously modifying orthogonal semantic subspaces (e.g., updating ‘Gender’ and ‘Tense’ attributes independently) within a single timestep.
L664: ## Appendix G Stability of Nested Non-Linear Refinement
L665: 
L666: Stacking multiple non-linear layers ($\boldsymbol{\delta}=\text{GELU}({p}\odot r)$) inside a recurrent loop may leads to gradient explosion or instability. We provide a stability bound via perturbation analysis.
L667: Lemma F.1 (Stability under Perturbation). Consider a single refinement step. Let $r^{(l)}$ be the nominal residual vector, and $\tilde{r}^{(l)}=r^{(l)}+\mathbf{\epsilon}$ be a perturbed residual vector (e.g., due to numerical precision or upstream approximation errors). The update function is $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(l)}=\text{GELU}(\boldsymbol{p}^{(l)}\odot\boldsymbol{r}^{(l)})$.
L668: Assume the activation function (GELU) is $L_{\phi}$-Lipschitz continuous (where $L_{\phi}\approx 1$) and the predictor outputs are bounded by $\|\boldsymbol{p}^{(l)}\|_{\infty}\leq M$ (ensured by normalization).
L669: We analyze the divergence of the update vectors $\delta$ and $\tilde{\delta}$:
L670: 
L671:  | $\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(l)}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{(l)}\|$  | $\displaystyle=\|\text{GELU}({p}^{(l)}\odot\mathbf{r}^{(l)})-\text{GELU}({p}^{(l)}\odot\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(l)})\|$  |  | (50)
L672:  |  | $\displaystyle\leq L_{\phi}\|{p}^{(l)}\odot(\mathbf{r}^{(l)}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}}^{(l)})\|$  |  | (51)
L673:  |  | $\displaystyle\leq L_{\phi}M\|\mathbf{\epsilon}\|$  |  | (52)
L674: Conclusion: The output perturbation is linearly bounded by the input perturbation scaled by $M$. Since $M\approx 1$ in practice (due to LayerNorm) and the loop depth $L$ is small (typically $2\sim 4$), the iterative refinement remains numerically stable. It does not suffer from the chaotic divergence typical of deep unrolled optimizers. The greedy subtraction ($r\leftarrow r-\delta$) further acts as a negative feedback mechanism, naturally bounding the residual energy.
