L315@P6: 4 Experiments
L316@P6: 4.1 Implementation Details
L317@P6: Building upon the CAT architecture, we develop MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, a large-scale audio tokenizer
L318@P6: featuring 1.6 billion parameters. The model utilizes a causal Transformer-based encoder–decoder paired
L319@P6: with hierarchical patching, which facilitates efficient streaming audio modeling. Discrete representations
L320@P6: are learned using a 32-layer residual vector quantizer with quantizer dropout to support variable-bitrate
L321@P6: tokenization. To encourage semantic alignment, we attach a decoder-only causal language model for audioto-text supervision. Training is performed on approximately 3M hours of diverse speech, sound, and music data, using a combination of reconstruction, semantic, and adversarial objectives. All components of
L322@P6: MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, including the encoder, quantizer, decoder, discriminator, decoder-only LLM are
L323@P6: optimized jointly in an end-to-end manner. All architectural details, optimization hyperparameters, and
L324@P6: training schedules are provided in Appendix A.
L325@P6: 4.2 Reconstruction Evaluation
L326@P6: We compare MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer with open-source audio tokenizers using both objective and subjective evaluation metrics across low (750–1500 bps), medium (1500–2500 bps), and high (2500–6000 bps) bitrate
L327@P6: regimes. Table 2 summarizes the objective reconstruction results on speech, general audio, and music benchmarks.
L328@P6: Across all evaluated bitrate regimes, MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer achieves strong performance on speech reconstruction, outperforming prior methods at low bitrates and achieving state-of-the-art results at medium and
L329@P6: high bitrates. On audio and music benchmarks, MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer maintains competitive performance
L330@P6: across all evaluated bitrates, with reconstruction quality improving as bitrate increases, indicating that the
L331@P6: model effectively benefits from increased bitrate and model capacity through joint end-to-end optimization.
L332@P6: Additional details on the compared open-source audio tokenizers, as well as subjective evaluation results
L333@P6: are provided in Appendix B.

L607@P10: 5.3 Reconstruction Fidelity Benefits Consistently from Increased Training Scale
L608@P10: Beyond model parameters and bitrate, a key indicator of a tokenizer’s scalability is its capacity to effectively
L609@P10: translate increased training computation into higher fidelity. We investigate this by varying the global training batch size from a baseline factor of 2
L610@P10: 0 up to 28
L611@P10: , while keeping the total number of training steps and other
L612@P10: hyperparameters constant.
L613@P10: As illustrated in Figure 6, CAT exhibits a clear and positive correlation between training scale and reconstruction quality across all evaluated speech metrics (SIM, STOI, and PESQ). At any given point in the training
L614@P10: process, increasing the batch size yields strictly better performance. Notably, the performance curves for
L615@P10: larger batch sizes maintain a strong upward trajectory even at 250k steps, achieving substantially higher
L616@P10: quality within the same step budget compared to smaller scales.
L617@P10: These results suggest that CAT exhibits stable and predictable scaling behavior with respect to training batch
L618@P10: size. Increasing data throughput leads to systematically higher-fidelity representations, highlighting the
