L760: ###### Theorem 5 (0–1 trivialization and characterization by reachability).
L761: 
L762: Assume deterministic transition. Then for any $T$ and $x_{0}$,
L763: 
L764:  | $$V^{*}(T,x_{0})=\mathbf{1}\{K(x_{0})\leq T\}.$$  |
L765: 
L766: Moreover, if $K(x_{0})\leq T$ then there exists a deterministic Markov policy $\pi^{\dagger}$ such that $V^{\pi^{\dagger}}(T,x_{0})=1$.
L767: ###### Proof.
L768: 
L769:   1. (1)
L770: 
L771: If $K(x_{0})>T$, then there is no action sequence of length at most $T$ that reaches $G$, hence for every (possibly randomized) policy $\pi$, the trajectory is a deterministic function of the sampled actions and cannot reach $G$ within $T$; thus $V^{\pi}(T,x_{0})=0$ and $V^{*}(T,x_{0})=0$.
L772: 
L773:   2. (2)
L774: If $K(x_{0})\leq T$, choose a successful sequence $(a_{0}^{\ast},\dots,a_{K-1}^{\ast})$ and define the corresponding deterministic state sequence $x_{t+1}^{\ast}=f(x_{t}^{\ast},a_{t}^{\ast})$ with $x_{0}^{\ast}=x_{0}$. Define a deterministic Markov policy $\pi^{\dagger}$ by
L775: 
L776:  | $$\pi^{\dagger}(\cdot\mid x_{t}^{\ast})=\delta_{a_{t}^{\ast}},\qquad t=0,\dots,K-1,$$  |
L777: and define $\pi^{\dagger}(\cdot\mid x)$ arbitrarily elsewhere. Under $\pi^{\dagger}$, the induced trajectory follows $(x_{t}^{\ast})$ with probability $1$ (because actions are deterministic), hence reaches $G$ by time $K\leq T$, so $V^{\pi^{\dagger}}(T,x_{0})=1$ and $V^{*}(T,x_{0})=1$.

L806: ### D.1 Conditioning on success: Doob $h$-transform
L807: 
L808: We want to sample successful length-$L$ proof traces without heavy rejection. The canonical construction is a Doob $h$-transform (a change of measure on path space) for Markov processes (cite197†Chung and Walsh, 2005 ; cite198†Rogers and Williams, 2000 )
L809: ###### Definition 1 (Doob-transformed, success-conditioned policy).
L810:

L812: 
L813:  | $$q_{r}^{\star}(a\mid x):=\mathbb{P}^{q}(A_{0}=a\mid X_{0}=x,\ X_{r}\in G)=\frac{q(a\mid x)h_{r-1}(f(x,a))}{h_{r}(x)}.$$  |
L814: ###### Theorem 6 (Exact conditional sampling of successful traces).
L815: 
L816: Fix $L$ and an initial state $x_{0}$ with $h_{L}(x_{0})>0$. If we generate a trajectory by
L817: 
L818:  | $$A_{t}\sim q_{L-t}^{\star}(\cdot\mid X_{t}),\qquad X_{t+1}=f(X_{t},A_{t}),\qquad t=0,\dots,L-1,$$  |

L415: ### 5.2 Flat (cut-free) learning
L416: ##### Setting.
L417: 
L418: Data are generated from the cut-free distribution $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$ induced by a reference policy $q$ (success-filtered). L419: ###### Theorem 1 (informal; Statistical provability lowerbound of flat (cut-free) learning).
L420: 
L421: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 and cite158†5 , there exists $\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(C,\beta)$ such that
L422:  | $$\mathbb{E}\big[V^{\mathrm{flat}\text{-}\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}(T,X)\big]\ \geq\ 1-K_{\mathrm{flat}}\Big(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k}(S)]}_{\text{intrinsic top-$k$ mass loss}}+\underbrace{\tilde{C}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}})^{p}}_{\text{learned misranking}}\Big),$$  |
L423: 
L424: where $K_{\mathrm{flat}}$ is the expected number of decision points along a successful proof under $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$, $m_{k}(S)$ is the generator top-$k$ mass at state $S$, and $p=\beta/(\beta+2)$.
L425: Moreover, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
L426: 
L427:  | $$\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}})\ \leq\ \inf_{\pi\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}}\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\pi)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}(n,T,\delta;H_{\mathrm{flat}}),$$  |
L428: 
L429: where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}$ is controlled by sequential Rademacher/covering complexity (cite158†Assumption 5 ).
L430: ##### Interpretation.
L431: 
L432: Success probability degrades linearly with effective proof length $K_{\mathrm{flat}}$ and improves with (i) larger intrinsic top-$k$ mass, (ii) stronger margins (larger $\beta$), and (iii) smaller one-step imitation error (more data / simpler class). Thus, even as a lower bound, the result highlights that reducing effective proof length is a principled route to higher success.

L1802: ### H.3 Lower bounding cut-elimination blow-up from latent DAG statistics
L1803: 
L1804: cite276†Assumption 17 postulates blow-up directly at the level of decision counts. Here we give a general way to justify such blow-up from statistics of the latent proof DAG.
L1805: 
L1806: Fix a DAG instance $z\in\mathcal{Z}$ with a depth partition $U_{d}(z)\subseteq U(z)$ of unique subgoals (canonicalized nodes). Let $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ be the unfolded cut-free tree.
L1807: ##### Path-multiplicity statistic.
L1808: 
L1809: For each unique node $u\in U(z)$, define its path multiplicity
L1810: 
L1811:  | $$M(u;z):=\#\{\text{directed root-to-$u$ paths in }z\}.$$  |  | (96)
L1812: 
L1813: Intuitively, $M(u;z)$ counts how many times $u$ must be duplicated when shared structure is unfolded into a tree.
L1814: ###### Lemma 11 (Unfolding multiplicity equals path multiplicity).
L1815: 
L1816: For any DAG $z$ and any node $u\in U(z)$, the number of occurrences of the subproof rooted at $u$ in the unfolded tree $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ is at least $M(u;z)$. Consequently, the number of goal-occurrences at depth $d$ in $\mathrm{CE}(z)$ satisfies
L1817: 
L1818:  | $$\mathrm{Occ}_{d}(\mathrm{CE}(z))\ \geq\ \sum_{u\in U_{d}(z)}M(u;z).$$  |  | (97)
L1819: ###### Proof sketch.
L1820: 
L1821: Each distinct root-to-$u$ path in $z$ corresponds to a distinct context in which $u$ is used. Unfolding removes sharing by duplicating each shared subproof for each distinct usage context, hence at least one copy per path. Summing over nodes at depth $d$ yields (cite284†97 ). ∎
L1822: 
L1823: cite285†Lemma 11 implies that blow-up is controlled by how rapidly path multiplicities grow with depth. A particularly transparent sufficient condition uses a reuse factor.
