L171: 
L172: We define an atomic evidence unit $e\in\mathcal{E}$ as the minimal identifiable unit of external information that can be acquired and reused by the agent. For each completed trajectory $\tau^{(n)}$, let $\mathcal{E}^{(n)}\subseteq\mathcal{E}$ denote the set of atomic evidence units acquired at least once along the trajectory, and define the acquisition indicator
L173: 
L174:  | $$I_{e}^{(n)}\;=\;\mathbb{I}\!\left[e\in\mathcal{E}^{(n)}\right]\in\{0,1\}.$$  |  | (7)
L175: Each trajectory is associated with a binary outcome $R^{(n)}\in\{0,1\}$ indicating task success.
L176: 
L177: We estimate the empirical success probability conditioned on acquiring $e$ as
L178: 
L179:  | $$\widehat{P}(R=1\mid I_{e}=1)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{e}^{(n)}R^{(n)}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{e}^{(n)}},$$  |  | (8)
L180: 
L181: and similarly, the success probability conditioned on not acquiring $e$ as
L182:  | $$\widehat{P}(R=1\mid I_{e}=0)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(1-I_{e}^{(n)}\right)R^{(n)}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(1-I_{e}^{(n)}\right)}.$$  |  | (9)
L183: 
L184: When the denominator of either estimator is zero, we fall back to the batch-level success rate $\widehat{P}(R=1)\;=\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}R^{(n)}$ to ensure numerical stability.
L185: 
L186: The atomic-evidence counterfactual contribution of $e$ is then defined as the difference between these two conditional success probabilities:
L187:  | $$\Delta_{e}\;=\;\widehat{P}(R=1\mid I_{e}=1)\;-\;\widehat{P}(R=1\mid I_{e}=0).$$  |  | (10)

L66: ###### Abstract
L67: Despite the strong performance achieved by reinforcement learning–trained information-seeking agents, learning in open-ended web environments remains severely constrained by low signal-to-noise feedback. Text-based parsers often discard layout semantics and introduce unstructured noise, while long-horizon training typically relies on sparse outcome rewards that obscure which retrieval actions actually matter.
L68: We propose a visual-native search framework that represents webpages as visual snapshots, allowing agents to leverage layout cues to quickly localize salient evidence and suppress distractors. To learn effectively from these high-dimensional observations, we introduce Information-Aware Credit Assignment (ICA), a post-hoc method that estimates each retrieved snapshot’s contribution to the final outcome via posterior analysis and propagates dense learning signals back to key search turns.
L101: Despite this progress, existing agents still struggle to balance information adequacy with reasoning efficiency during tool–environment interaction. GLM (cite71†Lu et al., 2025 ) retrieves raw webpage content via search/click/open, but the resulting unfiltered text is noisy and can trigger context overflow.
L102: Tongyi DeepSearch (cite65†Li et al., 2025a ) instead adopts a search-and-summary pipeline with a visit tool; however, summarizing raw HTML with heavy formatting noise often requires specialized models and adds latency. Search-R1 (cite72†Jin et al., 2025 ) improves efficiency by chunking results and retrieving segments with a reranker, but performance becomes sensitive to chunking choices, potentially missing key evidence.
L103: Moreover, multimodal IS agents face cross-modal gaps: in MMSearch-R1 (cite73†Wu et al., 2025c ) and DeepMMSearch-R1 (cite74†Narayan et al., 2025 ), visual evidence is frequently compressed into text and weakened in the reasoning chain, leading to the loss of critical multimodal cues.
L104: ### 2.2 Information Seeking Benchmark
L105: The landscape of information-seeking tasks has evolved from basic single-hop queries to complex, multi-hop reasoning, and eventually to highly nonlinear, deep-search challenges. Early single-hop benchmarks, such as NQ (cite59†Kwiatkowski et al., 2019 ), TriviaQA (cite75†Joshi et al., 2017 ), and SimpleQA-Verified (cite76†Haas et al., 2025 ), primarily consist of questions resolvable through structured queries, simple search operations, or even the model’s internal parametric knowledge.
L106: Subsequent multi-hop datasets, including Bamboogle (cite77†Press et al., 2022 ), Xbench-DS (cite78†Xbench-Team, 2025 ), and GAIA (cite61†Mialon et al., 2023 ), have significantly elevated the difficulty by introducing multi-step retrieval requirements and domain-specific annotations. These benchmarks rigorously test an agent’s capability in deep search and multi-turn tool utilization.
L107: At the frontier of this complexity are benchmarks like BrowseComp (cite62†Wei et al., 2025a ) and SealQA (cite64†Pham et al., 2025 ), which represent the most formidable challenges for information-seeking agents. These tasks are characterized by highly nonlinear coupled sub-problems and deliberate information masking, introducing substantial uncertainty and search depth that push the boundaries of current agentic reasoning.
L108: ### 2.3 Reinforcement Learning in IS Agents
L109: Reinforcement Learning has reshaped Large Language Models via RLHF (cite79†Ziegler et al., 2019 ; cite80†Stiennon et al., 2020 ; cite81†Ouyang et al., 2022 ; cite82†Rafailov et al., 2023 ), improving alignment with human preferences. L224: ### 4.2 External Observations
L225: Existing approaches commonly obtain web content in text-only form, either by summarizing webpages or retrieving a subset of passages via RAG-style pipelines (cite72†Jin et al., 2025 ). Such representations introduce irreversible information loss at acquisition time, break cross-trajectory consistency for the same information source, and inject additional model bias through learned summarization or retrieval components, as illustrated in Figure cite93†2 .
L226: These properties are fundamentally misaligned with ICA, which estimates the utility of external information units based on their aggregate contribution to successful outcomes across trajectories.
L227: To address this issue, we model external observations as webpage snapshots rather than summarized or retrieved text. A snapshot preserves the full visible content of a webpage, including textual elements, layout structure, tables, and images, and is treated as a persistent external information unit in the agent’s memory, as illustrated in Figure cite94†3 .
L228: Snapshots are returned directly by tool invocations and consumed by the agent without intermediate filtering or compression, allowing the agent to determine which information becomes relevant during downstream reasoning. This representation provides a stable and consistent basis for identifying recurring information units across trajectories, which is critical for estimating posterior success rates and applying repetition-aware discounting in ICA.

L200: ##### Batch-level centering.
L201: 
L202: Since posterior success rates are estimated across trajectories, their scales may vary across roll-outs, leading to batch-dependent bias in turn-level information credits. To reduce variance, ICA defines a unified normalized advantage $\hat{A}_{t}^{(n)}$ for all turns, using different normalization statistics depending on the turn type.

L235: ##### Benchmarks & Metrics
L236: We evaluate our method on a suite of long-horizon information-seeking benchmarks that require iterative search, evidence acquisition, and multi-step reasoning in dynamic web environments. Specifically, we adopt BrowseComp (cite62†Wei et al., 2025a ), GAIA (cite61†Mialon et al., 2023 ), Xbench-DS (cite78†Xbench-Team, 2025 ), and Seal-0 (cite64†Pham et al., 2025 ) to assess agentic information-seeking capabilities under varying task complexities and environmental dynamics.
L237: For GAIA, we evaluate on the 103 examples from the text-only validation subset (cite98†Li et al., 2025c ), following standard evaluation protocols. For Xbench-DS, we utilize the more mature 2505 version release for evaluation. Across all benchmarks, we report pass@1 as the primary metric, obtained via LLM-as-a-judge evaluation.
L238: ##### Baselines
L239: We compare against a diverse set of strong baselines spanning both proprietary and open-source information-seeking agents.
L240: These include proprietary systems such as Claude-4-Sonnet  (cite99†Anthropic, 2025 ), OpenAI-o3  (cite100†OpenAI, 2025b ), and DeepResearch  (cite67†OpenAI, 2025a ), as well as leading open-source methods including ASearcher  (cite101†Gao et al., 2025 ), DeepDive  (cite71†Lu et al., 2025 ), DeepDiver-V2  (cite102†Ascend Tribe, 2025 ), MiroThinker  (cite103†Team and others, 2025 ), InfoAgent  (cite104†Zhang et al., 2025 ), Kimi-K2  (cite105†Team et al., 2025 ), WebExplorer  (cite106†Liu et al., 2025a ), WebDancer  (cite56†Wu et al., 2025a ), WebSailor  (cite107†Li et al., 2025b ), WebShaper  (cite108†Tao et al., 2025b ), and C-GRPO  (cite58†Zhang et al., 2026 ).
L311: ##### ICA vs. GRPO (RL).
L312: 
L313: With snapshot retrieval fixed as the fetch policy during RL, ICA consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO on all benchmarks and both model scales. The improvements are particularly salient on long-horizon, high-noise tasks, supporting the claim that information-aware post-hoc credit assignment provides a denser and more targeted training signal by attributing outcome rewards to the retrieval turns that introduce high-utility evidence.
L314: ## 6 Conclusion
L315: This work targets long-horizon open-web information seeking, where learning is limited by sparse outcome rewards and noisy text-centric observations that lose layout semantics. We propose a visual-native pipeline that represents fetched pages as rendered snapshots, preserving structural and multimodal cues to support more reliable evidence grounding.
L316: Building on this, we introduce Information-Aware Credit Assignment (ICA), a post-hoc method that estimates the utility of each atomic evidence unit via posterior success association, then converts it into dense turn-level rewards for GRPO-style optimization. Experiments across multiple benchmarks show consistent gains from snapshot grounding and further improvements from ICA over standard policy optimization.
L317: ## Impact Statement
L318: This paper advances methods for training information-seeking agents by introducing Information-Aware Credit Assignment (ICA), which attributes outcome feedback to retrieved atomic evidence and propagates learning signals to the turns that introduced useful information.
L319: The primary intended impact is to improve the reliability and data-efficiency of agent learning in open-web settings, potentially benefiting applications such as question answering, research assistance, and domain-specific information discovery.
