L194: ## 5 Local Optimality of $\tau=1/\alpha$ and a Rényi-Entropy View
L195: 
L196: This section has two goals. First, we establish precisely what temperature $\tau=1/\alpha$ does and does not guarantee when used as the proposal inside Power-SMC. Second, we provide a Rényi-entropy interpretation of the remaining weight variability, motivating the exponent-bridging schedule introduced at the end of this section.
L197: ### 5.1 Locally variance-minimizing proposal
L198: 
L199: Fix time $t$ and a prefix $y_{<t}$. Write the model’s next-token distribution as $p_{t}(v):=p_{\theta}(v\mid x,y_{<t})$ for $v\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\text{EOS}\}$. For any prefix-only proposal $q_{t}(v)$, the incremental weight for drawing token $v$ is
L200: 
L201:  | $$\omega_{t}(v;\,y_{<t})=\frac{p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}}{q_{t}(v)}.$$  |  | (9)
L202: ###### Theorem 1 (Locally variance-minimizing proposal for Power-SMC).
L203: 
L204: Fix $t$ and a prefix $y_{<t}$. Among all proposals $q_{t}$ satisfying $q_{t}(v)>0$ whenever $p_{t}(v)>0$, the unique minimizer of the conditional second moment $\mathbb{E}_{v\sim q_{t}}\!\bigl[\omega_{t}(v;\,y_{<t})^{2}\bigr]$ (and hence of the conditional variance) is
L205: 
L206:  | $$\boxed{q_{t}^{\star}(v\mid x,y_{<t})=\frac{p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}}{\sum_{u}p_{t}(u)^{\alpha}}.}$$  |  | (10)
L207: Under $q_{t}^{\star}$, the incremental weight is deterministic given the prefix: $\omega_{t}(v;\,y_{<t})\equiv\sum_{u}p_{t}(u)^{\alpha}$ for all $v$, so $\mathrm{Var}_{q_{t}^{\star}}\!\bigl(\omega_{t}(\cdot;\,y_{<t})\bigr)=0$.
L208: ###### Corollary 1 (Temperature form).
L209: 
L210: If $p_{t}=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_{t})$ for logits $\ell_{t}$, then $q_{t}^{\star}(v)\propto\exp(\alpha\,\ell_{t}(v))=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_{t}/\tau)$ with $\tau=1/\alpha$.
L211: #### Interpretation.
L212: Corollary cite57†1 does not claim that temperature sampling at $\tau=1/\alpha$ alone produces exact samples from the sequence-level target equation cite47†1 . What it does say is: if the proposal $q_{t}$ is restricted to depend only on the current prefix (no lookahead), then $\tau=1/\alpha$ is the unique way to make the incremental correction equation cite58†9 as stable as possible at each prefix.
L213: Specifically, it eliminates variance due to which token was sampled; remaining variance comes entirely from which prefix path a particle happens to be on.
L214: ### 5.2 Rényi-entropy interpretation of residual weight dispersion
L215: 
L216: Define the prefix-dependent $\alpha$-power normalizer
L217: 
L218:  | $$Z_{t}(\alpha;\,y_{<t}):=\sum_{v}p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}.$$  |  | (11)
L219: 
L220: Under $q_{t}^{\star}$, the incremental weight equation cite58†9 equals $\omega_{t}\equiv Z_{t}(\alpha;\,y_{<t})$, which depends on the prefix but not on the sampled token.
L221: 
L222: To interpret $Z_{t}$, recall the Rényi entropy of order $\alpha$ for a discrete distribution $p$:
L223:  | $$H_{\alpha}(p):=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(\sum_{v}p(v)^{\alpha}\right),\qquad\alpha>0,\;\alpha\neq 1.$$  |  | (12)
L224: 
L225: Applying this to $p_{t}(\cdot)=p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})$ yields
L226: 
L227:  | $$\log Z_{t}(\alpha;\,y_{<t})\;=\;(1-\alpha)\,H_{\alpha}\!\bigl(p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\bigr).$$  |  | (13)
L228: 
L229: Since $\sum_{v}p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}\leq 1$ for $\alpha\geq 1$, we have $\log Z_{t}(\alpha;\,y_{<t})\leq 0$.
L230: #### Path-wise weight accumulation (SIS view).
L231: 
L232: Consider the underlying sequential importance sampling (SIS) weights before any resampling resets. For particle $i$, the accumulated log-weight at time $T_{\max}$ under the locally optimal proposal is
L233: 
L234:  | $$\log\tilde{W}_{T_{\max}}^{(i)}\;=\;\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\max}}\log Z_{t}(\alpha;\,y_{<t}^{(i)})\;=\;(1-\alpha)\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\max}}H_{\alpha}\!\bigl(p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t}^{(i)})\bigr),$$  |  | (14)
L235: which is non-positive. Particles traversing prefixes with higher next-token uncertainty (larger Rényi entropy) accumulate lower weights. Conversely, particles on more “confident” prefix paths receive higher weight, consistent with the sharpening intent of the power distribution.
L236: Equation equation cite59†14 makes the remaining source of degeneracy concrete: even with the locally optimal proposal, weights diverge when different particles encounter prefixes whose next-token uncertainty differs substantially. This motivates the exponent-bridging schedule below.
L237: ### 5.3 Exact exponent-bridging ($\alpha$-ramping)
L238: 
L239: To mitigate path-level weight divergence without changing the final target $\pi_{\alpha}$, we introduce an exponent-bridging schedule
L240: 
L241:  | $$1=\alpha^{(0)}<\alpha^{(1)}<\cdots<\alpha^{(L)}=\alpha,$$  |
L242: and define intermediate targets $\gamma_{t}^{(\ell)}(y_{1:t}\mid x)\propto p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}}$. Within stage $\ell$, the correct incremental weight is obtained by replacing $\alpha$ with $\alpha^{(\ell)}$ in equation cite55†8 . At stage boundaries (after a chosen token index $t$), we apply the standard SMC-samplers reweighting update (cite49†Del Moral et al., 2006 ):
L243:  | $$\log\tilde{W}\;\leftarrow\;\log\tilde{W}+(\alpha^{(\ell)}-\alpha^{(\ell-1)})\cdot\log p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x),$$  |
L244: where $\log p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x)=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\log p_{\theta}(y_{s}\mid x,y_{<s})$ is available from the autoregressive factors. This transitions the target from $\gamma^{(\ell-1)}$ to $\gamma^{(\ell)}$ while preserving correctness of the final target. Within stage $\ell$, the locally optimal proposal is $q_{t,\ell}^{\star}(\cdot)\propto p_{t}(\cdot)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}}$, i.e., temperature $\tau_{\ell}=1/\alpha^{(\ell)}$.
L245: In our experiments, we use a simple linear schedule $\alpha^{(\ell)}=1+(\alpha-1)\cdot\ell/L$ over the first $T_{\mathrm{ramp}}$ tokens (details in Appendix cite40†B ).
L246: ## 6 Compute and Latency Cost Analysis: MH vs. SMC/SIR
L247: 
L248: We now formalize the latency advantage of Power-SMC over MH under an engine-independent cost model. Both methods use KV caching, so the dominant cost is the number of incremental decode steps.
L249: ### 6.1 Setup, cost model, and notation
L250: Let $p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)$ be an autoregressive LM and consider sampling from $\pi_{\alpha}(y_{1:T}\mid x)\propto p_{\theta}(y_{1:T}\mid x)^{\alpha}$. We define one token-eval as a single cached forward step producing next-token logits for one sequence, so a decode step at batch size $b$ costs $b$ token-evals.
L251: To translate token-evals into wall-clock time, let $s(b)\geq 1$ be the batch throughput multiplier at batch size $b$ relative to batch $1$; a batch-$b$ step then takes time proportional to $b/s(b)$, capturing the sub-linear scaling of hardware utilization. Throughout, $T$ is the number of generated tokens, $B$ the block length ($K:=T/B$ blocks, $B\mid T$), $M$ the number of MH moves per block, and $N$ the number of SMC particles.
L252: ### 6.2 Cost of Power-SMC / SIR
L253: 
L254: We begin with Power-SMC because its cost is straightforward and serves as the baseline for comparison.
L255: 
L256: ###### Lemma 1 (Power-SMC cost).
L257: 
L258: Under KV caching, Power-SMC with $N$ particles and horizon $T$ performs $C_{\mathrm{SMC}}=N\cdot T$ token-evals, with wall-clock time proportional to $\mathrm{Time}_{\mathrm{SMC}}\propto T\cdot N/s(N)$.
L259: ###### Proof.
L260: 
L261: Each of $T$ decode steps advances all $N$ particles (one batch-$N$ forward pass: $N$ token-evals), yielding $NT$ total. Weight updates and resampling are $O(N)$ per step and do not change the leading term. Wall-clock time follows from $T$ steps at cost $N/s(N)$ each. ∎
L262: ### 6.3 Cost of MH power sampling
L263: 
L264: We next analyze the MH construction of cite45†Karan and Du (2025) , where each move selects an edit point and regenerates the suffix autoregressively. We consider two edit-index regimes to separate algorithmic structure from implementation choices.
L265: #### General decomposition.
L266: 
L267: Let $L_{k,m}$ be the regenerated suffix length in MH move $m\in\{1,\dots,M\}$ within block $k\in\{1,\dots,K\}$. Block extension costs $B$ token-evals and each move costs $L_{k,m}$, so
L268: 
L269:  | $$C_{\mathrm{MH}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Bigl(B+\sum_{m=1}^{M}L_{k,m}\Bigr)=T+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{m=1}^{M}L_{k,m},$$  |  | (15)
L270: 
L271: with expectation $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]=T+M\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}[L_{k}]$, where $L_{k}$ is the suffix length in a representative move within block $k$.
L272: ###### Lemma 2 (Global-edit MH cost).
L273: 
L274: If each move in block $k$ edits uniformly over the full prefix of length $t_{k}=kB$, then $\mathbb{E}[L_{k}]\approx kB/2$ and $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]\approx T\!\bigl(1+M(K{+}1)/4\bigr)$, which is $\Theta(T^{2}/B)$ for fixed $B$.
L275: 
L276: ###### Proof.
L277: 
L278: Uniform edit on $\{0,\dots,t_{k}-1\}$ gives $\mathbb{E}[L_{k}]=(t_{k}+1)/2\approx kB/2$. Summing: $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]\approx T+(MB/2)\cdot K(K{+}1)/2=T+MBK(K{+}1)/4$; substituting $T=KB$ yields the result. ∎
L279: ###### Lemma 3 (Last-block edit MH cost).
L280: 
L281: If each move edits uniformly within the most recent block only, then $\mathbb{E}[L_{k}]\approx B/2$ for all $k$ and $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]\approx T(1+M/2)$.
L282: 
L283: ###### Proof.
L284: 
L285: The offset to block end is uniform on $\{1,\dots,B\}$, so $\mathbb{E}[L_{k}]=(B{+}1)/2\approx B/2$. Substituting: $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]\approx T+MK(B/2)=T(1+M/2)$. ∎
L286: 
L287: ### 6.4 Compute and latency ratios
L288: 
L289: Combining the results above directly yields the following comparisons.
L290: ###### Corollary 2 (Compute ratio: global-edit MH vs. Power-SMC).
L291: 
L292: $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]/C_{\mathrm{SMC}}\approx\bigl(1+M(K{+}1)/4\bigr)/N.$
L293: 
L294: ###### Corollary 3 (Wall-clock ratio).
L295: 
L296: Assuming MH moves execute at batch $1$ while Power-SMC uses batch $N$,
L297: 
L298:  | $$\mathrm{Time}_{\mathrm{MH}}\big/\mathrm{Time}_{\mathrm{SMC}}\;\approx\;\bigl(1+M(K{+}1)/4\bigr)\cdot s(N)/N\quad\text{(global-edit)}.$$  |
L299: ###### Proof.
L300: 
L301: $\mathrm{Time}_{\mathrm{MH}}\propto\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]$ (batch $1$) and $\mathrm{Time}_{\mathrm{SMC}}\propto T\cdot N/s(N)$ (Lemma cite60†1 ). ∎
L302: ###### Corollary 4 (MH overhead floor under last-block edit).
L303: 
L304: The expected MH overhead relative to baseline decoding satisfies $\rho_{\mathrm{MH}}\gtrsim 1+M/2$; for $M=10$ this gives $\rho_{\mathrm{MH}}\gtrsim 6$ even under a perfect inference engine.
L305: As a concrete example, if $N=48$, $M=10$, and $K=16$, the global-edit compute factor is $1+M(K{+}1)/4=43.5$ and $\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathrm{MH}}]/C_{\mathrm{SMC}}\approx 0.91$. Even here, Power-SMC can be wall-clock favorable when $s(48)$ is large, because its additional compute is batch-parallel rather than serial. We next describe the main systems consideration needed to realize this parallelism in practice.
L306: ## 7 Experiments
L307: 
L308: We evaluate on MATH500 and compare: (i) baseline decoding, (ii) low-temperature decoding at $\tau=1/\alpha$, (iii) MH power sampling (cite45†Karan and Du, 2025 ), (iv) Scalable Power Sampling (cite50†Ji et al., 2026 ), and (v) Power-SMC. We measure end-to-end wall-clock latency on identical hardware using Hugging Face (no specialized inference engine) and report accuracy–latency trade-offs.
L309: #### Implementation details.
L310: Unless otherwise noted, we use $N=64$ particles, exponent $\alpha=4$, and a maximum generation length of $T_{\max}=2048$ tokens. Resampling is triggered when $\mathrm{ESS}_{t}<\kappa N$ with $\kappa=0.5$. We optionally apply $\alpha$-ramping with a linear schedule over the first $T_{\mathrm{ramp}}=100$ tokens.
L311: When resampling fires, we perform systematic resampling: given normalized weights $w_{1:N}$, a single offset $u_{0}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$ defines evenly spaced positions $p_{i}=(u_{0}+i-1)/N$; ancestor indices are $A_{i}=\min\{j:\sum_{k\leq j}w_{k}\geq p_{i}\}$. After resampling, particle prefixes are copied, the Transformer KV cache is reordered (Appendix cite41†C ), and weights are reset to uniform.

L331: ## 8 Conclusion
L332: We introduced Power-SMC, a low-latency particle sampler for the sequence-level power distribution $\pi_{\alpha}(y\mid x)\propto p_{\theta}(y\mid x)^{\alpha}$. Power-SMC avoids MH’s serial accept/reject structure and instead leverages batch-parallel decoding. On the theoretical side, we proved that temperature $\tau=1/\alpha$ is the unique locally variance-minimizing prefix-only proposal and gave a Rényi-entropy interpretation of the residual weight dispersion.
L333: On the practical side, we described exact $\alpha$-ramping schedules, cache-safe resampling for Transformers inference, and formalized engine-independent compute/latency comparisons yielding MH overhead floors. Empirically, Power-SMC matches or exceeds MH power sampling on MATH500 while reducing inference latency from order-of-magnitude overheads to modest increases.

L343: ## Appendix A Proof of Theorem cite82†1 and Corollary cite57†1 L344: ###### Proof of Theorem cite82†1 .
L345: 
L346: Fix a prefix $y_{<t}$ and abbreviate $p(v):=p_{t}(v)$ and $q(v):=q_{t}(v)$ over $v\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\text{EOS}\}$. The incremental weight for sampling $v\sim q$ is $\omega(v)=p(v)^{\alpha}/q(v)$.
L347: 
L348: Step 1: the conditional mean is invariant to $q$.
L349: 
L350:  | $$\mathbb{E}_{v\sim q}[\omega(v)]=\sum_{v}q(v)\frac{p(v)^{\alpha}}{q(v)}=\sum_{v}p(v)^{\alpha}=:Z(\alpha;\,y_{<t}).$$  |
L351: 
L352: Step 2: minimize the second moment.
L353:  | $$\mathbb{E}_{v\sim q}[\omega(v)^{2}]=\sum_{v}q(v)\left(\frac{p(v)^{\alpha}}{q(v)}\right)^{\!2}=\sum_{v}\frac{p(v)^{2\alpha}}{q(v)}.$$  |
L354: 
L355: We minimize $\sum_{v}p(v)^{2\alpha}/q(v)$ subject to $\sum_{v}q(v)=1$ and $q(v)>0$ whenever $p(v)>0$. The Lagrangian is $\mathcal{L}(q,\lambda)=\sum_{v}{p(v)^{2\alpha}}/{q(v)}+\lambda\bigl(\sum_{v}q(v)-1\bigr).$ Stationarity for each $v$ yields
L356:  | $$\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q(v)}=-\frac{p(v)^{2\alpha}}{q(v)^{2}}+\lambda=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad q(v)=\frac{p(v)^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.$$  |
L357: 
L358: Normalization $\sum_{v}q(v)=1$ gives $\sqrt{\lambda}=\sum_{v}p(v)^{\alpha}=Z(\alpha;\,y_{<t})$, hence $q^{\star}(v)=p(v)^{\alpha}\big/Z(\alpha;\,y_{<t})$, which is unique.
L359: Step 3: verify zero conditional variance. Substituting into $\omega(v)=p(v)^{\alpha}/q^{\star}(v)$ yields $\omega(v)\equiv Z(\alpha;\,y_{<t})$ for all $v$, so the conditional variance is $0$. ∎
L360: ###### Proof of Corollary cite57†1 .
L361: 
L362: If $p_{t}=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_{t})$, then $p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}\propto\exp(\alpha\,\ell_{t}(v))$. Thus $q_{t}^{\star}(v)\propto p_{t}(v)^{\alpha}$ equals $\mathrm{softmax}(\alpha\,\ell_{t})$, which is temperature sampling with $\tau=1/\alpha$ (i.e., logits divided by $\tau$). ∎
L363: ## Appendix B Exact Exponent-Bridging ($\alpha$-Ramping)
L364: 
L365: Let $1=\alpha^{(0)}<\alpha^{(1)}<\cdots<\alpha^{(L)}=\alpha$ be a schedule and define intermediate unnormalized prefix targets
L366: 
L367:  | $$\gamma_{t}^{(\ell)}(y_{1:t}\mid x)\propto p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}}.$$  |
L368: 
L369: Within stage $\ell$, the incremental importance weight is obtained by replacing $\alpha$ with $\alpha^{(\ell)}$ in Eq. equation cite55†8 . At chosen boundaries (after a token index $t$), the log-weight update
L370:  | $$\log\tilde{W}\;\leftarrow\;\log\tilde{W}+(\alpha^{(\ell)}-\alpha^{(\ell-1)})\cdot\log p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x),\quad\log p_{\theta}(y_{1:t}\mid x)=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\log p_{\theta}(y_{s}\mid x,y_{<s}),$$  |
L371: transitions the target from $\gamma^{(\ell-1)}$ to $\gamma^{(\ell)}$. Since $\prod_{\ell}p^{\alpha^{(\ell)}-\alpha^{(\ell-1)}}=p^{\alpha^{(L)}-\alpha^{(0)}}=p^{\alpha-1}$, the cumulative reweighting is identical to directly targeting the final exponent $\alpha$, preserving correctness. Within stage $\ell$, the locally optimal prefix-only proposal is $q_{t,\ell}^{\star}(\cdot)\propto p_{t}(\cdot)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}}$, i.e., temperature $\tau_{\ell}=1/\alpha^{(\ell)}$.
L372: ## Appendix C Systems: Cache-Safe Resampling for Transformer Decoding
L373: Power-SMC’s resampling step requires reindexing particle ancestry: when a high-weight particle is duplicated, its model state must be copied as well. For autoregressive Transformers, the dominant state is the KV cache—stored attention keys and values for each particle’s prefix.
L374: Because KV cache layouts differ across architectures and library versions, we implement cache reordering with a three-tier strategy: (i) use model-provided cache-reordering hooks when available (e.g., _reorder_cache); (ii) fall back to cache-object reorder methods exposed by the runtime; and (iii) otherwise apply a recursive tensor reindexer that treats caches as nested containers and reindexes along the batch/particle dimension without assuming a specific internal structure.
L375: This makes resampling correct and efficient across common Hugging Face backends.
L376: ## Appendix D EOS and Variable-Length Decoding
L377: 
L378: We treat EOS as an ordinary token in $\mathcal{V}\cup\{\text{EOS}\}$. Once a particle emits EOS, it transitions to an absorbing state: subsequent steps apply a no-op transition with incremental weight $1$. In implementation, this is achieved by masking proposals to force EOS for terminated particles and skipping cache updates for those particles.

L380: 
L381: Our implementation uses systematic resampling (Algorithm cite54†1 ), which is unbiased and typically lower-variance than multinomial resampling (cite52†Johansen, 2009 ). More broadly, any standard unbiased resampling scheme (multinomial, stratified, residual, systematic) preserves the target distribution; these choices primarily affect variance and particle diversity.
