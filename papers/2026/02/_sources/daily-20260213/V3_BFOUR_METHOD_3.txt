L200: ## 5 Experiment
L201: ### 5.1 Experiment Setup
L202: Training and Evaluation Datasets. Following cite90†Yang et al. (2024b) , our BNRM was trained on the Unified-feedback (UF) preference dataset that contains diverse human preference annotations that cover a wide range of dialogue scenarios and reward signals. Specifically, we randomly sampled 40K and 400K samples from the UF dataset for model training, and assessed the performance on a held-out 8K validation split.
L203: In addition, we further evaluated the models on several out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets, including RM-Bench (cite91†Liu et al., 2024b ), RewardBench (cite92†Lambert et al., 2024 ), HHH-Alignment (cite93†Askell et al., 2021 ), and MT-Bench (cite94†Zheng et al., 2023 ), which better simulate real-world scenarios. Besides, we also consider Skywork-Preference-v0.2 (SP) training dataset (cite95†Liu et al., 2025 ) and compare it with advanced reward modeling approaches.
L204: Base Models and Training Details. In reward modeling experiments, we firstly used gemma-2b-it (cite96†Gemma, 2024b ) and gemma-2-2b-it (cite97†Gemma, 2024a ) as base models, accelerated by Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (cite98†Hu et al., 2021 ) for 2 epochs. In addition, we fully fine-tune the larger Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B (cite40†Liu et al., 2024a ) reward model on the Skywork-Preference-v0.2 (SP) dataset within 1 epoch.
L205: Best-of-N (BoN) sampling test was performed exclusively with the two Gemma models, and our BNRM obtained via LoRA as proxy reward models. In the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (cite99†Schulman et al., 2017 ) of real LLM fine-tuning, we applied LoRA to fine-tune Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (cite100†Meta, 2024 ) and OpenRLHF-Llama3-8B-SFT (cite101†Dong et al., 2024 ) for 1 epoch. More detailed training configurations are provided in Appendix cite30†C .
L206: Baselines. As detailed in Appendix cite33†C.3 , we consider the following baselines: (1) BT and its variants, including BT (cite78†Bradley and Terry, 1952 ), BT-Margin (cite102†Touvron et al., 2023b ), BT-Frozen, BT-Ensemble (cite103†Coste et al., 2024 ), and BT-Label Smoothing (cite50†Wang et al., 2024 ); (2) GRM (cite90†Yang et al., 2024b ) ; (3) InfoRM (cite44†Miao et al., 2024 ).

L220: ### 5.2 Evaluation on Reward Modeling
L221: ID and OOD Evaluation. Table cite104†1 clearly shows that BNRM consistently boosts the corresponding base reward models and further outperforms advanced baselines across most ID and OOD evaluation tasks, regardless of training data scales. For example, with 40K training examples, the BT-based BNRM achieves accuracies of 74.2%, 83.6%, and 75.2% on Unified-Feedback, HHH Alignment, and MT-Bench, respectively, corresponding to improvements of 5.4%, 13.3%, and 6.1% points over the corresponding BT baseline.
L222: Likewise, the GRM-based BNRM attains 74.1%, 82.4%, and 75.1% on the same benchmarks, improving over GRM-SFT by 2.6%, 3.7%, and 2.1% points. In RewardBench, both BT-BNRM and GRM-BNRM achieve significant improvement over strong baselines under both 40/400K training splits, respectively. Table cite104†1 indicates that our Bayesian non-negative framework effectively helps reward model suppresses reliance on spurious features, thereby improving generalization.
L223: Additional experiments on label noise setting, influence analysis of $\eta$, and convergence curve can be found in Appendix cite28†A.3 .

L236: Full Parameter Training Results On the SP. Skywork-Preference-v0.2 (SP) provides higher-quality preference data compared with UF dataset. L243: Advantages in Low-Resource and Noisy Settings. A robust reward model should generalize well despite limited annotations and label noise—both common in practice. We evaluate BT-BNRM using a Gemma-2B-it backbone under two challenging settings: (1) Low-resource: training on 1K to 20K UF samples; (2) Label noise: training on 40K samples with noise rates from 0.1 to 0.4. Figure cite106†4(a) shows that BNRM consistently outperforms BT with the performance gap widening as data volume decreases.
L244: Remarkably, BNRM trained on only 1K examples matches the performance of BT trained on 20K on RewardBench, with similar trends across other datasets. Under label noise (Figure cite107†4(b) ), BNRM demonstrates even greater resilience. At a 40% rate, BNRM improves BT by up to 16.7% and rivals the performance of BT trained with only 10%-20% noise.
L245: These results show that our BNRM is both data-efficient and noise-tolerant, making it highly suitable for real-world scenarios with scarce high-quality preference data.
L246: ### 5.3 RLHF Evaluation
L247: To further investigate whether BNRM can effectively mitigate reward hacking in real RLHF, we fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and OpenRLHF-Llama3-8B-SFT based on LoRA using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (cite99†Schulman et al., 2017 ) on 20K samples from the alpaca-gpt4-data-en dataset (cite108†Peng et al., 2023 ). We use BNBT-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B fully fine-tuned on the SP dataset, and optimized policies are evaluated on widely used benchmarks, where we report Accuracy (%).
L248: As shown in Table cite109†3 , the two BNRM-fine-tuned policies achieve higher performance than their respective baselines, reaching 74.98% and 62.25%, indicating that BNRM can effectively simulate human preferences to guide LLM fine-tuning toward better performance. Additional training and evaluation details can be found in Appendix cite30†C .
