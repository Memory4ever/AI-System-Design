L140: The first notion of unlearning error, termed the Retain Error, evaluates the accuracy of $\hat{p}_{r}$ in regions where the target distribution itself places probability mass. We formally quantify this via the forward KL divergence
L141: 
L142:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{r}(\hat{p}_{r}):=\ {\mathrm{KL}}\left(p_{r}\,\|\,\hat{p}_{r}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim p_{r}}\left[\ln\frac{p_{r}(\bm{z})}{\hat{p}_{r}(\bm{z})}\right]$$  |  | (6)
L143: As this is an expectation under $p_{r}$, it is insensitive to discrepancies on sets where $p_{r}$ assigns negligible probability. While such insensitivity is often benign in standard estimation settings, it is problematic in unlearning: an estimate can achieve small ${\mathrm{KL}}\left(p_{r}\,\|\,\hat{p}_{r}\right)$ while assigning nontrivial probability to regions where the forget distribution $p_{f}$ concentrates, thereby leaking information about the forget set.
L144: To capture this failure mode, we define a complementary notion of error, the Forget Error, that focuses explicitly on the behavior of $\hat{p}_{r}$ over regions emphasized by $p_{f}$. Concretely, we measure this error as the $p_{f}$-weighted $\ell_{1}$-norm
L145: 
L146:  | $$\mathcal{E}_{f}(\hat{p}_{r}):=\|p_{r}-\hat{p}_{r}\|_{1,p_{f}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim p_{f}}\left[\left|p_{r}(\bm{z})\mathbin{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.6}{$-$}}}\hat{p}_{r}(\bm{z})\right|\right].$$  |  | (7)
L147: Taken together, the Retain and Forget Errors provide complementary control: the former ensures faithful recovery of $p_{r}$ on its typical set, while the latter detects residual mismatch in regions where $p_{f}$ places weight, which is precisely where unlearning failures manifest.
L148: ###### Remark 3.1.
L149: While direct control of distributional discrepancies ($\mathcal{E}_{r}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{f}$) gives a definitive notion of unlearning, these quantities are intractable to compute for LLMs, as they require expectations over a combinatorial input space. Consequently, empirical evaluations rely on hypothesis tests [cite72†MFSLK24 ] and membership inference attacks [cite94†SLHMZHLZSZ25 ] to assess distinguishability from a retrained reference.
L150: Our theory targets these fundamental discrepancies directly, since minimizing them implies indistinguishability under any test function, and in Section cite17†4 we show strong performance under the empirical proxies used in practice.
L151: ### 3.2 Surrogate Analysis Framework
L152: 
L153: We work in a general nonparametric setting where the retain and forget distributions admit densities $p_{r}$ and $p_{f}$ over a continuous domain $\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}$. We approximate $p_{r}$ via the T3-Unlearning estimator $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ defined as
L154: 
L155:  | $$\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}(\bm{z})=\frac{p(\bm{z})^{1/T}\hat{f}(\bm{z})}{\int_{\bm{u}}p(\bm{u})^{1/T}\hat{f}(\bm{u})},$$  |  | (8)
L156: where $\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}$ is a learned surrogate classifier intended to approximate the Bayes posterior $f^{\ast}(\bm{z})\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left(s=1\mid\bm{z}\right)$.
L157: ###### Remark 3.2.
L158: 
L159: We assume $T$ is chosen so that $\int p^{1/\tau}<\infty$ for all $1\leq\tau\leq T$, ensuring $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ is well-defined. This always holds for sub-exponential distributions; for power-law tails $p\propto\left\|\bm{z}\right\|_{2}^{-\alpha}$, it requires $\alpha>dT$, for ambient dimension $d$.
L160: Our analysis directly relates the unlearning error of $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ to the statistical quality of $\hat{f}$. Let $L(f)\coloneq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},s}\left[\ell(f(\bm{z}),s)\right]$ denote the population risk under the cross-entropy loss $\ell$. We assume $\hat{f}$ achieves excess risk at most $\delta$, i.e., $L(\hat{f})-L(f^{\ast})\leq\delta$, and derive bounds on the Retain and Forget Errors of $\hat{p}_{r}^{\,(T)}$ as functions of $\delta$ and the mixture parameters.
L161: While particular function classes admit standard excess risk rates when the true posterior lies in the hypothesis space (e.g., logistic regression achieving $\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})$ rates; see Appendix cite30†C ), our theory is stated in terms of the surrogate excess risk $\delta$. This abstraction allows it to apply across learning settings with favorable risk guarantees, agnostic to any function class.
L162: Consequently, regardless of how the surrogate classifier $\hat{f}$ is obtained, we characterize unlearning performance solely through its surrogate risk.

L172: Theorem cite60†3.3 shows that the Retain Error of the untempered estimator is controlled by the classifier’s excess risk, up to the factor $(1-\gamma)^{-1}$. While Theorem cite60†3.3 shows the untempered estimator attains small Retain Error when the classifier has low excess risk (and $\gamma$ is not too large), this alone does not ensure effective unlearning.

L165: ###### 4.3 Positive–negative conditional independence.
L166: 
L167: A distribution $P$ is said to satisfy positive–negative conditional independence if there exist a marginal $P_{X}$ on $\mathcal{X}$ and conditional distributions $p_{+}(\cdot\mid x)$ and $p_{-}(\cdot\mid x)$ on $\mathcal{Y}$ such that

L171: ###### 4.4 CPRD of conditionally independent distribution and BT model.

L173: Let $P$ be a distribution over $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}$, then the following holds;

L182: In addition, the suitable score function $r$ for a positive–negative conditional independence distribution $P$ is given by

L187: This generative perspective yields a clean sufficient route to BT: if $P$ is generated by independently sampling $y^{+}\sim p_{+}(\cdot\mid x)$ and $y^{-}\sim p_{-}(\cdot\mid x)$ given $x$, then $\omega_{P}$ is automatically representable by a BT model, with score $r(x,y)=\log\frac{p_{+}(y\mid x)}{p_{-}(y\mid x)}$. L188: Importantly, this assumption is not merely descriptive, in many modern settings we can enforce it by design, e.g., when we control data collection by synthesizing comparisons via separate “good” and “bad” generators. Nevertheless, our goal is not only to verify if the CPRD is representable with a BT model but to learn the CPRD. This motivates the next section where we analyze the learning objectives.
L189: ## 5 Learning CPRD
L190: 
L191: We now turn to a question of more immediate practical relevance: given i.i.d. triplets $(x,y^{+},y^{-})$ drawn from $P$, what objective should we optimize, and what does that objective actually recover and when it matches the ground-truth CPRD? We study two canonical approaches, generative modeling via MLE of the triplet distribution, and discriminative BT training via pairwise log-likelihood.
L192: First, we will introduce the generative and discriminative learning objectives for learning the CPRD.
L193: 
L194: The generative learning objective. For a class of parametric distribution $P_{\phi}$ over triplets $(x,y^{+},y^{-})$, the generative learning objective is
L195: 
L196:  | $$\min_{\phi}\mathcal{L}_{\text{gen}}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[-\log P_{\phi}(x,y^{+},y^{-})\right].$$  |  | (10)
L197: where $P_{\phi}(x,y^{+},y^{-})$ is the probability of the triplet $(x,y^{+},y^{-})$ under the distribution $P_{\phi}$. Here, we are trying to fit the full generative model for the triplet distribution $P$. Once we have $\hat{P}_{\phi}$, the learned preference distribution for any unordered pair $\{y,y^{\prime}\}$ is given by
L198: 
L199:  | $$\hat{\omega}_{\phi}(y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x)=\frac{\hat{P}_{\phi}(x,y,y^{\prime})}{\hat{P}_{\phi}(x,y,y^{\prime})+\hat{P}_{\phi}(x,y^{\prime},y)}.$$  |  | (11)
L200: Since minimizing the generative learning objective is equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between the true distribution $P$ and the generative model $P_{\phi}$ (cite108†White, 1982 ), the generative learning objective have a CPRD recovery guarantee; the learned preference distribution would converge to the true CPRD when the true CPRD belongs to the model space (realizable).
L201: On the other hand, the discriminative objective works directly at the preference level without explicitly modelling the triplet distribution $P$.
L202: The discriminative BT learning objective. For a class of parametric score functions $r_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$, the discriminative BT learning objective is
L203: 
L204:  | $$\min_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{BT}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[-\log P_{r_{\theta}}(y^{+}\succ y^{-}\mid x)\right].$$  |  | (12)
L205: where $P_{r_{\theta}}(y^{+}\succ y^{-}\mid x)=\sigma(r_{\theta}(x,y^{+})-r_{\theta}(x,y^{-}))$ is the probability of preferring $y^{+}$ over $y^{-}$ given $x$ under the BT model with score function $r_{\theta}$. Once we have $\hat{r}_{\theta}$, the learned preference distribution for any unordered pair $\{y,y^{\prime}\}$ is given by
L206: 
L207:  | $$\hat{\omega}_{\theta}(y\succ y^{\prime}\mid x)=\sigma(\hat{r}_{\theta}(x,y)-\hat{r}_{\theta}(x,y^{\prime})).$$  |  | (13)
L208: In contrast with the generative objective, it is not straightforward to see how this objective would recover the true CPRD. In the next result, we show that this objective is also equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between the true distribution and the preference distribution induced by the BT model but at the preference level instead of the triplet level.
L209: ###### 5.1 Comparison distribution.
L210: 
L211: For any distribution $P$ over $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}$, the comparison distribution $\widetilde{P}$ is a distribution over $x$ and an unordered pair $\{y,y^{\prime}\}$ with $y\neq y^{\prime}$ with density
L212: 
L213:  | $$\widetilde{P}(x,\{y,y^{\prime}\})\propto P(x,y,y^{\prime})+P(x,y^{\prime},y).$$  |  | (14)
L214: This distribution essentially captures how often a pair of outcomes $y$ and $y^{\prime}$ are compared together given the context $x$ in the data, regardless of the outcome.
