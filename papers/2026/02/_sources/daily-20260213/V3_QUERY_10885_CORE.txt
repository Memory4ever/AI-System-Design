L85: ## 2 Related Works
L86: ### 2.1 LLM Reasoning with RL
L87: Reinforcement learning has become an essential technique for incentivizing chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs [RL4LRM, Survey-LLMs]. A milestone paradigm is reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) [DeepSeekMath, O1, Deepseek-R1], where LLMs are trained on verifiable tasks such as math problems [AIME], puzzle solving [ARC-AGI-2], and code execution [LiveCodeBench], by rewarding final-answer correctness using task-specific verifiers.
L88: Since such tasks are intrinsically difficult to reward-hack, RLVR tends to encourage LLMs to produce longer CoTs before answering, thereby improving reasoning performance [Deepseek-R1, O1]. However, despite its effectiveness in incentivizing reasoning, most RLVR training remains outcome-centric and provides no direct supervision over the CoT itself, lacking guidance on how the model should reason.
L89: While several efforts have tried to mitigate this by assigning process rewards using additional reward models (RM) [PRM8K], training such RMs requires labor-intensive training data collection [PRM8K, PRIME, ProcessBench], and a static RM often fails to keep pace with the evolving CoT distribution during training [Deepseek-R1]. Therefore, such challenges hinder the combination of RLVR training with CoT supervision criteria for rewarding in practice.
L90: ### 2.2 Self-Evolving in LLMs
L91: A critical step toward general artificial intelligence lies in the ability to self-evolve with little or no human intervention [self-evolving-survey]. Recent work has made progress via self-training and self-play, where a single policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ can self-generate training data [AbsoluteZero, SSP, SPICE], or self-provide reward signals during learning [SPIN, SeRL, SRLM].
L92: The self-evolving capabilities can further be strengthened with techniques such as multi-role reinforcement learning, where a single policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ is prompted to play different roles (e.g., reasoner, data generator, or reward provider) and trained with role-specific rewards [AbsoluteZero, SPICE, Alignmen-Waltz]. Therefore, these advances in such self-evolving methods suggest the possibilities of self-proposing criteria for CoT supervision, while this direction remains largely under-explored.
L93: ### 2.3 Reinforcement Learning with Rubrics
L94: Recently, reinforcement learning with rubrics has emerged as a promising way to provide fine-grained and interpretable reward signals [Rubicon, Rubrics-as-Rewards, HealthBench, Chasing-the-Tail].
L95: Here, rubrics are structured, checkable evaluation criteria (i.e., explicit requirements for assessing outputs) [Rubrics-as-Rewards] that decompose high-level objectives into multi-facet supervision signals [DR-Tulu], enabling RL to extend towards non-verifiable and open-ended domains [RuscaRL, Rubrics-as-Rewards]. However, existing work largely applies rubrics to score response quality, leaving their effectiveness for CoT supervision underexplored.
L96: Moreover, most approaches rely on predefined or static rubrics, which may become mismatched as model behaviors evolve during training [DR-Tulu]. To address these gaps, we introduce a framework that self-proposes rubrics as CoT supervision criteria and continually evolves them throughout training, integrating rubric-based RL for CoT supervision with self-evolving training.

L98: 
L99: In this section, we briefly review the necessary background that forms the foundation of our work, including incentivizing LLM reasoning with RL in Section cite12†3.1 and multi-role RL under a single policy in Section cite13†3.2 .
L100: ### 3.1 Incentivizing LLM Reasoning with RL
L101: 
L102: In this work, we focus on incentivizing LLMs to reason with RL, where the policy model is optimized by rewarding the high-quality rollouts.
L103: 
L104: Rollout. Given a question $\mathcal{Q}$, the policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ attempts to solve it by generating a response that contains a CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ followed by an answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$, which can be formulated as:
L105: 
L106:  | $$\hat{\mathcal{C}},\hat{\mathcal{A}}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{Q}).$$  |  | (1)
L107: Rewarding. After rollouts generation, rewards are assigned based on response quality, which can depend on: (i) the CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ and (ii) the answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$. In the widely adopted RLVR paradigm, rewards typically depend solely on the correctness of the predicted answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$:
L108: 
L109:  | $$r=\psi(\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{A},\hat{\mathcal{A}})),$$  |  | (2)
L110: where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is an answer correctness indication function which returns $1$ if two answers $\mathcal{A}$ and $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$ are equivalent and 0 otherwise, and a reward assignment function $\psi$ maps this correctness signal $\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{A},\hat{\mathcal{A}})$ to a binary reward $r$. Through such RLVR training, the policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ gradually learns to answer questions, which is often associated with producing longer CoTs [Deepseek-R1].
L111: However, under the RLVR paradigm, the CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ itself is rarely rewarded directly due to practical implementation challenges [Deepseek-R1], while the lack of such supervision may induce sub-optimal reasoning strategies [PRM8K].
L112: ### 3.2 Multi-Role RL Under a Single Policy Model
L113: 
L114: Recently, studies have shown that a single policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ can effectively learn multiple roles by assigning role-specific rewards [AbsoluteZero, SPIRAL]:
L115: 
L116:  | $$r^{\text{role}_{i}}\rightarrow\pi^{\text{role}_{i}}_{\theta},$$  |  | (3)
L117: where $r^{\text{role}_{i}}$ denotes the reward assigned to $\text{role}_{i}$, and $\pi^{\text{role}_{i}}_{\theta}$ represents the same underlying policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ instantiated under the prompt $\mathcal{P}^{\text{role}_{i}}$ corresponding to that role.
L118: A typical application under this framework is to let the model simultaneously act as a reasoner and a data generator, achieving RL training starting from no human-labeled data [AbsoluteZero, AlphaZero, SSP]. More broadly, this multi-role, single-policy setup provides a natural substrate for self-evolving training: different roles instantiated from the same model can compete [AbsoluteZero, SPICE] or collaborate [Alignmen-Waltz, SPIRAL], continuously improving the learning signals and behaviors [MAS-Survey].
L119: Building on this view, we investigate whether the same policy can simultaneously serve as a reasoner and a CoT rubrics generator (i.e., rubricator). With role-specific rewards, the rubricator is encouraged to produce useful CoT supervision criteria, which in turn enables the self-evolution of CoT rewarding. In this case, the reasoner and rubricator collaborate together for better reasoning.
L120: ## 4 Methodology
L121: In this section, we introduce RLCER (R einforcement L earning with C oT Supervision via Self-E volving R ubrics) for bootstrapping chain-of-thought reasoning. In the remainder of this section, we first briefly introduce the key idea of RLCER in Section cite15†4.1 to facilitate a high-level understanding. Then, we discuss the multiple roles under the same policy model in Section cite16†4.2 . After that, we discuss how to incentivize reasoning with self-proposed and rubrics in Section cite17†4.3 .
L122: We then discuss how to self-evolve such rubrics in Section cite18†4.4 . Finally, we present how to optimize the policy $\pi_{\theta}$ under the two roles based on role-specific rewards in Section cite19†4.5 .
L123: ### 4.1 Key Idea
L124: 
L125: cite40†Image: Refer to caption Figure 3: The RLCER loop. Format reward is ignored for brevity. One single policy model self-proposes rubrics for rewarding CoTs, and self-evolves the rubrics via rewarding generation capabilities.
L126: The key idea of RLCER is to go beyond rewarding what LLMs answer (i.e., final-answer correctness) to explicitly and autonomously reward how LLMs think (i.e., CoT quality) using self-proposed, self-evolving rubrics. The rubrics are evolved by tracking how well their satisfaction correlates with the final answer correctness.
L127: To achieve this goal, we introduce two key roles in the multi-role RL framework for optimization: the reasoner $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ for answering the question and the rubricator $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$ for generating rubrics supervising CoTs.
L128: Here, the reasoner $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}(\cdot)=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{P}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}})$ and the rubricator $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}(\cdot)=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{P}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}})$ share the same policy parameter $\theta$, while their roles are distinguished by different prompts $\mathcal{P}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ and $\mathcal{P}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$ respectively.
L129: The prompts can be found in Appendix cite30†9 . Additionally, a verifier $\pi_{\phi}$ is used for judging the satisfaction of rubrics, which is another fine-tuned and frozen model parameterized by $\phi$.
L130: As shown in Figure cite41†3 , we explicitly refine how the reasoner $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ thinks, by rewarding the CoT quality beyond the answer correctness, with the CoT quality evaluated by the verifier $\pi_{\phi}$ based on rubrics generated by the rubricator.
L131: We further enable rubric generation to self-evolve for more autonomous and effective CoT supervision by rewarding the quality of generated rubrics, guided by the correlation between rubric satisfaction degree and answer correctness. We jointly optimize the policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ under two roles by assigning different rewards for each role.
L132: After computing role-specific rewards, we optimize the shared policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ for both roles, with advantages computed separately for each role [SPICE].
L133: ### 4.2 Two Roles in One Policy: Reasoner and Rubricator
L134: 
L135: In this section, we take a closer look at the different roles of the reasoner $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ and the rubricator $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$, within the RLCER framework.
L136: Reasoner $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$. The reasoner answers the question by generating a CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ and a final answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$. Given the question $\mathcal{Q}$, the rollout sampling process of the reasoner can be formulated as:
L137:  | $$\hat{\mathcal{C}},\hat{\mathcal{A}}\sim\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}\!\big(\cdot\mid\mathcal{Q}\big),$$  |  | (4)
L138: 
L139: where $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ denotes the CoT process, and $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$ denotes the predicted final answer.
L140: Rubricator $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$. The rubricator generates a set of $K$ rubrics for evaluating the CoT qualities based on the question $\mathcal{Q}$ and one CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$. Given the question $\mathcal{Q}$ and a generated CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$, the rollout sampling process of the rubricator can be formulated as:
L141:  | $$\hat{\mathcal{R}}\sim\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}\big(\cdot\mid\mathcal{Q},\hat{\mathcal{C}}\big),\qquad\hat{\mathcal{R}}\triangleq\{\hat{\tau}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K},\ \hat{\tau}_{k}\triangleq(\hat{c}_{k},\hat{s}_{k}).$$  |  | (5)
L142: The generated textual output $\hat{\mathcal{R}}$ contains $K$ specific rubrics, each denoted as $\hat{\tau}_{k}$. Here, $\hat{c}_{k}$ is the textual criterion (e.g., “avoids tangential explorations post-interval validation (3-$\infty$) by focusing on count aggregation”) of rubric $\hat{\tau}_{k}$ and $\hat{s}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}$ is the score of rubric $\hat{\tau}_{k}$ reflecting its importance, which can be both positive or negative.
L143: The rubrics generated under the rubricator role will then be used for judging the CoT quality through an external verifier $\pi_{\phi}$.
L144: ### 4.3 Rewarding How to Think via Self-Proposed Rubrics
L145: 
L146: In this section, we discuss how to autonomously reward how LLMs think via self-proposed rubrics.
L147: Rewarding CoT with self-proposed rubrics. Specifically, we reward a CoT based on how well it satisfies the valid rubrics proposed by the rubricator $\pi_{\theta}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$. The CoT reward is computed by aggregating the scores of the satisfied rubrics:
L148:  | $$r_{cot}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}=\texttt{norm}\left(\sum_{\hat{\tau}_{k}\in\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{valid}}\pi_{\phi}\left(\hat{c}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{C}}\right)\cdot\hat{s}_{k}\right),$$  |  | (6)
L149: where $\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{valid}$ denotes the set of valid rubrics from the rubricator, $\pi_{\phi}(\hat{c}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{C}})\in\{0,1\}$ is a binary value produced by the verifier $\pi_{\phi}$ indicating whether the CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ satisfies rubric $\hat{c}_{k}$, and $\texttt{norm}(\cdot)$ is a normalization function that maps the aggregated score to the interval $[0,1]$.
L150: The function $\texttt{norm}(\cdot)$ conducts the min–max normalization as $\texttt{norm}(x)=(x-\text{MinValue})/(\text{MaxValue}-\text{MinValue})$, where MinValue and MaxValue denote the minimum and maximum aggregated rubric scores attainable under the valid rubrics $\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{valid}$, respectively.
L151: Valid rubrics. Here, a rubric $\hat{\tau}_{k}$ is considered valid if it is informative as a rewarding criterion: (i) its satisfaction indicator $\mathbf{v}_{k}$ is positively correlated with final-answer correctness $\mathbf{z}$ across $N$ sampled rollouts of the same question $\mathcal{Q}$ (i.e., $\mathtt{corr}(\mathbf{v}_{k},\mathbf{z})>\alpha$), and (ii) it is discriminative across different CoTs and would not yield consistent satisfaction (i.e., $\mathtt{std}(\mathbf{v}_{k})>0$).
L152: We define $\mathbf{v}_{k}$ and $\mathbf{z}$ over these $N$ rollouts as:
L153:  | $\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{k}$  | $\displaystyle\triangleq\Big[\pi_{\phi}(\hat{c}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}),\ldots,\pi_{\phi}(\hat{c}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{N})\Big],$  |  | (7)
L154:  | $\displaystyle\mathbf{z}$  | $\displaystyle\triangleq\Big[\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{A},\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{0}),\ldots,\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{A},\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{N})\Big].$  |
L155: The intuition is that higher correlation implies that satisfying the rubric is consistently associated with correct final answers and can produce informative rewarding signals. Here $\alpha$ is set as 0.2 by default in our paper.
L156: Outcome reward. The CoT reward $r_{cot}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ can be used as an auxiliary reward alongside the outcome reward $r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{outcome}$, which is calculated as follows:
L157:  | $$r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{outcome}=\begin{cases}1,&\texttt{is\_equiv}(\mathcal{A},\hat{\mathcal{A}})\\
L158: -1,&\text{Otherwise.}\end{cases}$$  |  | (8)
L159: Following DAPO [DAPO], we assign a binary outcome reward based on final answer correctness: $r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{outcome}$=1 if the predicted answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}$ is equivalent to the ground truth answer $\mathcal{A}$, and $r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{outcome}$=-1 otherwise.
L160: Total reward for reasoner. Finally, the overall reward for the reasoner is the combination of the outcome reward and the CoT reward:
L161:  | $$r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}=r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{outcome}+r_{cot}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}.$$  |  | (9)
L162: Such a reward enables autonomously optimizing what LLMs answer and how LLMs think simultaneously.
L163: ### 4.4 Rubrics Self-Evolving for Better Supervision
L164: While the two-role design enables a single policy model to self-propose rubrics for CoT supervision, we argue that such rubrics may get saturated: once consistently satisfied by all rollouts, they lose discriminative power over CoT quality and no longer provide reward signals, as shown in Section cite17†4.3 . Therefore, we further propose to self-evolve the rubric generation capability, so as to progressively produce more informative and challenging rubrics that mitigate saturation.
L165: Self-evolving with validity reward. To achieve rubricator self-evolving, we reward the fraction of the number of valid rubrics among all generated rubrics as follows:
L166: 
L167:  | $$r_{evolving}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}=\frac{K_{\text{valid}}}{K},$$  |  | (10)
L168: where $K$ and $K_{\text{valid}}\triangleq|\{\hat{\tau}^{valid}_{k}\}|$ are the numbers of all generated rubrics and valid rubrics, respectively. Such rewards encourage the rubricator to generate more informative rubrics that are positively correlated with answer correctness (i.e., $\mathtt{corr}(\mathbf{v}_{k},\mathbf{z})>\alpha$) and discriminative (i.e., $\mathtt{std}(\mathbf{v}_{k})>0$), thereby preventing rubric saturation.
L169: This reward provides a simple way for evolving the quality of generated rubrics, by connecting the satisfaction of the rubrics with the final answer correctness.
L170: Format reward. Additionally, to ensure the proposed rubrics can be stably parsed, we reward the rubricator with a format reward, rewarding the correct format as follows:
L171: 
L172:  | $$r_{format}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{The format is correct.}\\
L173: 0,&\text{Otherwise.}\end{cases}$$  |  | (11)
L174: Total reward for rubricator. Finally, the total reward for the rubricator is calculated as the combination of the quality reward and the format reward:
L175:  | $$r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}=r_{evolving}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}+r_{format}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}.$$  |  | (12)
L176: Such a reward design encourages the rubricator to continuously refine its proposed rubrics, consistently providing informative rewarding signals for CoT supervision.
L177: 
L178: cite42†Image: Refer to caption L179: Figure 4: Illustration of the reward calculation process in RLCER. For question $\mathcal{Q}$, the reasoner generates $N$ responses each with CoT $\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}$ and the final answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{k}$. After that, the rubricator generates $K_{n}$ specific rubrics (i.e., $\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}\triangleq\{\hat{\tau}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{n}}$). The outcome reward is applied first by matching the generated answer $\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{k}$ with the ground-truth answer $\mathcal{A}$.
L180: All the valid rubrics (i.e., $\mathtt{corr}(\mathbf{v}_{k},\mathbf{z})>\alpha$ and $\mathtt{std}(\mathbf{v}_{k})>0$) are collected for rewarding CoTs. And the fraction of valid rubrics for the $k$-th rubricator generation (i.e., $\frac{|\{\hat{\tau}^{valid}_{n,k}\}|}{K_{n}}$) is used for rewarding the rubricator to self-evolve.
L181: ### 4.5 Two-Role Optimization under a Single Policy
L182: After computing the rewards for the reasoner and the rubricator, we jointly optimize the shared policy model $\pi_{\theta}$ under both roles in an end-to-end manner.
L183: Specifically, we compute role-specific advantages $\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{t}$ and $\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}_{t}$ from their respective rewards $r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}$ and $r^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}$, and use them to update the policy via a standard policy-gradient objective.
L184: Gradients from both roles are aggregated to update the same set of parameters $\theta$, allowing the model to learn from role-specific feedback while maintaining a unified policy. The overall optimization procedure is illustrated in Algorithm cite43†1 .
L185: With role-specific advantages $\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{t}$ and $\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.86328125,0.41015625,0.48046875}$Rub$}}}_{t}$, the optimization objective can be formulated as follows:
L186:  | $\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\theta)$  | $\displaystyle=\ \mathbb{E}_{(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{A})\sim\mathcal{D}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}},\ \boldsymbol{o}\sim\pi^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{Q})}\Big[\min\Big(\rho_{t}(\theta)\,\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{t},\;\text{clip}\big(\rho_{t}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\big)\,\hat{A}^{\text{{\color[rgb]{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}\definecolor[named]{pgfstrokecolor}{rgb}{0.0859375,0.3046875,0.64453125}$Rea$}}}_{t}\Big)\Big],$  |  | (13)
