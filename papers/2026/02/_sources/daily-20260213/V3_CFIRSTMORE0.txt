L138: ##### SGD algorithm for inner problem:
L139: 
L140: Given the outer variable $v_{k}$, at each iteration $t$ of the inner-loop, we sample data $\xi_{k}^{t}\sim q(\cdot)$ and apply projected SGD:
L141: 
L142:  | $\displaystyle\theta_{k}^{t+1}=\Pi_{\Theta}\left(\theta_{k}^{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\right),$  |  | (3.1)
L143: where the stochastic gradient $\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})$ is the unbiased estimator of the true gradient $\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t})$. Under standard assumptions, $\theta_{k}^{t}$ converges to the inner solution $\theta^{*}(v_{k})$. A detailed convergence analysis is provided in Lemma cite92†4.8 .
L144: ##### Iterative approximation of the Hessian inverse:
L145: A key challenge in hypergradient descent lies in evaluating the Hessian inverse. When the inner problem corresponds to KL-divergence minimization, the FIM coincides with the Hessian at the optimum, i.e., $H(\theta^{*}(v))=I(\theta^{*}(v))$ (see Proposition cite90†B.2 ). This Hessian–FIM equivalence motivates our approximation strategy: the FIM inverse serves as an efficient surrogate for the Hessian inverse.
L146: We further approximate the FIM using the EFIM computed along the inner optimization trajectory $\{\theta_{k}^{0},\ldots,\theta_{k}^{t}\}$:
L147:  | $\displaystyle I_{k}^{t+1}$  | $\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{t+1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{i},\xi_{k}^{i})\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{i},\xi_{k}^{i})^{\top}$  |
L148:  |  | $\displaystyle=\frac{t}{t+1}I_{k}^{t}+\frac{1}{t+1}\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})^{\top}.$  |
L149: Intuitively, by the convergence of the inner-loop SGD and the Law of Large Numbers, the EFIM $I_{k}^{t}$ converges to the FIM $I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))$, which in turn equals the Hessian $H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))$ at the inner optimum.
L150: Although the update of $I_{k}^{t}$ reuses stochastic gradients from the inner-loop SGD at negligible cost, explicitly inverting $I_{k}^{t}$ remains computationally expensive. A key observation is that each update of $I_{k}^{t}$ is a rank-one outer product, which allows its inverse to be updated efficiently via the Sherman–Morrison formula. Specifically, letting $A_{k}^{t}:=(I_{k}^{t})^{-1}$, the inverse can be updated as follows:
L151:  | $\displaystyle A_{k}^{t+1}=\frac{t+1}{t}A_{k}^{t}-\frac{\frac{t+1}{{t}^{2}}A_{k}^{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\left(A_{k}^{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\right)^{\top}}{1+\frac{1}{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})^{\top}A_{k}^{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})}.$  |  | (3.2)
L152: As $I_{k}^{t}$ converges to $H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))$, its inverse $A_{k}^{t}$ provides an efficient approximation of $H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}$. A non-asymptotic justification is established in Theorem cite76†4.7 .
L153: ###### Remark 3.1.
L154: 
L155: The update of $A_{k}^{t}$ reuses stochastic gradients from the inner-loop SGD, making the procedure efficient and highly parallelizable. Specifically, stochastic gradients from inner-loop can be sent to a separate device for synchronous hypergradient updates, requiring only one-way communication during the inner-loop, as shown in Figure cite74†1 .
L156: ##### Iterative approximation of the cross-partial derivative:
L157: 
L158: We approximate the cross-partial derivative $\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}\ell(v,\theta^{*}(v))$ using the sample average along the inner optimization trajectory $\{\theta_{k}^{0},\cdots,\theta_{k}^{t}\}$:
L159:  | $\displaystyle L_{k}^{t+1}$  | $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t+1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{i},\xi_{k}^{i})$  |
L160:  |  | $\displaystyle=\frac{t}{t+1}L^{t}_{k}+\frac{1}{t+1}\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t}).$  |  | (3.3)
L161: Similarly, by the convergence of inner-loop SGD and the Law of Large Numbers, the $L_{k}^{t}$ is expected to converge to the $\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}\ell(v,\theta^{*}(v))$. A non-asymptotic justification of this convergence is given in Proposition cite93†4.12 .
L162: 
L163: In practice, we can also approximate the cross-partial derivative using the sample average at the end of inner loop: $\hat{L}_{k}^{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{T},\xi_{k}^{i})$.
L164: Both estimators $L_{k}^{t}$ and $\hat{L}_{k}^{m}$ involve trade-offs. The trajectory-based estimator $L_{k}^{t}$ reuses inner-loop SGD samples and computational graphs to compute Jacobian–vector products, but requires storing the full inner-loop graph, which can be memory intensive. In contrast, $\hat{L}_{k}^{m}$ avoids retaining the inner-loop graph and is more memory efficient, at the cost of additional samples.
L165: Lemma cite94†C.3 shows that with $m=\mathcal{O}(T)$, $\hat{L}_{k}^{m}$ achieves the same non-asymptotic convergence rate as $L_{k}^{T}$.
L166: Finally, with Eq. (cite95†3.2 ) and Eq. (cite96†3.3 ), the hypergradient can be approximated as:
L167: 
L168:  | $\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k})=\nabla_{v}f(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{T})-(L_{k}^{T})^{\top}A_{k}^{T}\ \nabla_{\theta}f(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{T}),$  |
L169: 
L170: and the outer-level variable is updated as: $v_{k+1}=v_{k}-\alpha\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k})$.

L195: ##### Practical Considerations
L196: (1) Synchronous and parallel hypergradient computation: The EFIM inverse update (cite95†3.2 ) and the cross-partial derivative update (cite96†3.3 ) can both run in parallel with the inner SGD (see Figure cite74†1 ). As a result, the hypergradient is approximated synchronously with the inner optimization, allowing immediate outer updates without extra runtime overhead. The formal parallel implementation of NHGD (Algorithm cite98†2 ) can be found in Appendix cite37†A .
L197: (2) Memory-Efficient Approximation via K-FAC [cite75†26 ]: K-FAC exploits the layer-wise structure of neural networks to construct a Kronecker-factored approximation of the FIM, enabling the EFIM inverse update to be performed in a block-wise manner. Incorporating K-FAC greatly reduces memory and computational cost, enabling NHGD to scale to larger models.
L198: ## 4  Convergence Analysis
L199: 
L200: In this section, we present the convergence analysis of the proposed NHGD algorithm. The analysis is built upon two main theoretical results. Theorem cite76†4.7 establishes the accuracy of the proposed Hessian inverse approximation, which serves as the key technical foundation of our method. Building on this result, Theorem cite77†4.10 characterizes the overall convergence rate of NHGD.
L201: Before presenting these two main theorems, we first introduce a set of standard assumptions commonly adopted in the analysis of bilevel optimization problems [cite60†11 , cite65†16 , cite69†2 , cite81†6 , cite99†5 , cite82†15 ].
L202: ###### Assumption 4.1.
L203: 
L204:   1. (i)
L205: 
L206: $l(v,\theta,\xi)$ is $\mu$-strongly convex in $\theta$, twice continuous differentiable in $(v,\theta)$ and jointly $L$-smooth in $(v,\theta)$, uniformly for all $\xi$.
L207: 
L208:   2. (ii)
L209: For any $v$, $\nabla^{2}_{\theta,v}\ell(v,\cdot)$, $\nabla^{2}_{\theta,\theta}\ell(v,\cdot)$ are $L_{\ell_{\theta,v}}$, $L_{\ell_{\theta,\theta}}$-Lipschitz continuous. For any $\theta$, $\nabla^{2}_{\theta,v}\ell(\cdot,\theta)$, $\nabla^{2}_{\theta,\theta}\ell(\cdot,\theta)$ are $\bar{L}_{\ell_{\theta,v}}$, $\bar{L}_{\ell_{\theta,\theta}}$-Lipschitz continuous.
L210: ###### Assumption 4.2.
L211: 
L212:   1. (i)
L213: 
L214: For any $v$, $\nabla_{v}f(v,\cdot)$, $\nabla_{\theta}f(v,\cdot)$ is $L_{f_{v}}$, $L_{f_{\theta}}$-Lipschitz continuous. For any $\theta$, $\nabla_{v}f(\cdot,\theta)$, $\nabla_{\theta}f(\cdot,\theta)$ is $\bar{L}_{f_{v}}$, $\bar{L}_{f_{\theta}}$-Lipschitz continuous.
L215: 
L216:   2. (ii)
L217: 
L218: For any $v$ and $\theta$, there exists constant $D_{1}>0$ such that $\|\nabla_{\theta}f(v,\theta)\|\leq D_{1}$.
L219: ###### Assumption 4.3.
L220: 
L221: For any $v$ and $\theta$, the stochastic derivative $\nabla_{v}l(v,\theta,\xi)$, $\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)$, $\nabla_{v,\theta}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ and $\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ are unbiased estimator of $\nabla_{v}\ell(v,\theta)$, $\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)$, $\nabla_{v,\theta}^{2}\ell(v,\theta)$ and $\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\ell(v,\theta)$.
L222: Assumptions cite100†4.1 and cite101†4.2 imply the Lipschitz continuity of the solution mapping $\theta^{*}(v)$ and the smoothness of the composite outer objective $\Phi(v)$, as formalized in Lemma 2.2 of cite60†Ghadimi and Wang [11] . Since the outer objective $\Phi(v)$ is generally nonconvex, such smoothness properties are essential for establishing convergence guarantees toward stationary points.
L223: Assumption cite102†4.3 ensures unbiasedness of the stochastic first and second-order derivative estimators used in the proposed algorithm.
L224: ###### Assumption 4.4.
L225: 
L226: There exist constant $C_{1}>0$ such that $\sup\limits_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},\theta\in\Theta,\xi}\|\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)-\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)\|\leq C_{1}.$
L227: Assumption cite103†4.4 is widely used in existing studies on general stochastic approximation algorithms, including, but not limited to, stochastic gradient descent [cite104†17 ] and other data-driven machine learning algorithms [cite105†4 ]. An interesting direction for future work is to investigate whether Assumption cite103†4.4 can be relaxed to allow unbounded but light-tailed (e.g., sub-Gaussian) noise.
L228: Finally, we introduce a structural assumption that is crucial for enabling an efficient Hessian inverse approximation.
L229: ###### Assumption 4.5.
L230: 
L231: For any $v$, the inner minimizer $\theta^{*}(v)$ yields a model distribution that matches the true data distribution $q(\cdot)$, i.e., $p(\cdot;v,\theta^{*}(v))=q(\cdot)$.
L232: 
L233: This assumption guarantees the equivalence between the Hessian of the inner objective and the FIM evaluated at the inner-level optimum, which is critical for our Hessian inverse approximation strategy.
L234: ###### Remark 4.6.
L235: 
L236: In practice, exact model specification may not hold. Our analysis naturally extends to the misspecified setting in which $\mathrm{TV}\!\left(p(\cdot;v,\theta^{*}(v)),\,q(\cdot)\right)\leq\epsilon_{D}$ for all $v$, where $\mathrm{TV}(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the total variation distance.
L237: ### 4.1  Analysis for Hessian Inverse Approximation Bound
L238: 
L239: In this section, we establish a high-probability sample complexity bound showing that the EFIM inverse converges to the true Hessian inverse at the inner optimum. This result is a key component of the convergence analysis of NHGD. We first state the result and then outline the proof sketch.
L240: 
L241: #### 4.1.1  Hessian Inverse Approximation Bound
L242: ###### Theorem 4.7.
L243: 
L244: Suppose Assumptions cite100†4.1 , cite102†4.3 , cite103†4.4 and cite106†4.5 hold. For any outer iteration $k$, given the outer variable $v_{k}$ and set the inner stepsize as $\eta_{t}=4/(\mu(t+\frac{8L^{2}}{\mu^{2}}))$. For any $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $T\geq T_{0}(\delta)$, the following holds with probability at least $1-\delta$:
L245: 
L246:  | $\displaystyle\quad\big\|A_{k}^{T}-H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\big\|=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sqrt{1+\log\left(\frac{1+T}{\delta}\right)}\right),$  |
L247: where $T_{0}(\delta)$ is defined in (cite107†C.15 ).
L248: Our analysis establishes convergence guarantee in a high-probability sense. In contrast, prior works such as [cite60†11 , cite65†16 , cite69†2 , cite81†6 , cite99†5 , cite82†15 ] analyze convergence using expectation-based metrics. Consequently, our result is stronger in the sense that a high-probability bound with a light tail can be directly translated into a mean bound via the identity $\mathbb{E}[X]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X>x)\,dx$ for any non-negative random variable $X$.
L249: By contrast, starting from a mean bound, standard tools such as Markov or Chebyshev inequalities typically yield only high-probability bounds with polynomial (power-law) tails.
L250: #### 4.1.2  Proof Sketch of Theorem cite76†4.7 L251: ##### Step 1: Reduction to EFIM error.
L252: 
L253: We aim to bound the error $\|A_{k}^{T}-H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\|$, or equivalently $\|(I_{k}^{T})^{-1}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\|$, where the equivalence follows from $I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))=H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))$ (stated in Proposition cite90†B.2 ).
L254: 
L255: Under the $\mu$-strong convexity of the inner obejctive $\ell$ and by Weyl’s eigenvalue inequality, we further have
L256:  | $\displaystyle\bigl\|(I_{k}^{T})^{-1}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\bigr\|\leq\frac{\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|}{\bigl(\mu-\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|\bigr)\mu}.$  |
L257: 
L258: Therefore, controlling the Hessian inverse approximation error $\|A_{k}^{T}-H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\|$ reduces to bounding the error $\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|$.
L259: ##### Step 2: Decomposition of the EFIM error.
L260: 
L261: The main challenge of bounding $\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|$ lies in the fact that $I_{k}^{T}$ is computed along the trajectory $\{\theta_{k}^{0},\ldots,\theta_{k}^{T-1}\}$, whereas $I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))$ is defined at the inner-level optimum. Let $G_{k}^{t}:=\nabla_{\theta}l(\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\nabla_{\theta}l(\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})^{\top}$, the EFIM error can be decomposed as:
L262:  | $\displaystyle\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{T}\left\|\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(G_{k}^{t}-\mathbb{E}_{t}[G_{k}^{t}]\right)\right\|}_{\text{stochastic error}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{T}\left\|\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbb{E}_{t}[G_{k}^{t}]-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\right)\right\|}_{\text{optimization error}},$  |
L263: where $\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}[\cdot\mid\sigma\!(v_{k},\,\theta_{k}^{0},\,\xi_{k}^{0},\,\ldots,\,\xi_{k}^{t-1})]$ and $\sigma\{\cdot\}$ denotes the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the random variables.
L264: 
L265: The stochastic error term captures the deviation introduced by stochastic sampling, while the optimization error quantifies the deviation of $\theta_{k}^{t}$ from the inner optimum $\theta^{*}(v_{k})$.
L266: ##### Step 3: Bounding stochastic and optimization errors.
L267: 
L268: The stochastic error is controlled via the matrix Azuma inequality. The optimization error depends on the convergence of the inner-level SGD. Under the smoothness and gradient noise assumptions for $l$, controlling the optimization error reduces to bounding $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\theta_{k}^{t}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|$. Therefore, we establish a high-probability bound on the inner-level SGD trajectory in the following lemma.
L269: ###### Lemma 4.8.
L270: 
L271: Suppose Assumptions cite100†4.1 , cite102†4.3 and cite103†4.4 hold. For any outer iteration $k$, given the outer variable $v_{k}$ and set the inner stepsize as $\eta_{t}=4/(\mu(t+q))$ and $q\geq 8L^{2}/\mu^{2}$. Then, for any $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
L272: 
L273:  | $\displaystyle\|\theta_{k}^{t}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|^{2}$  | $\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q}{t+q}\right)^{2}\|\theta_{k}^{0}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{c^{\prime}\log(e/\delta)}{t+q},$  |
L274: where $c^{\prime}$ is the constant defined in (cite108†C.10 ).
L275: ###### Remark 4.9.
L276: 
L277: The proof of Lemma cite92†4.8 relies on bounding the moment-generating function of $\|\theta_{k}^{t}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|^{2}$, which yields a significantly tighter high-probability control than the mean-square bound $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\theta_{k}^{t}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|^{2}\right]$. The detailed proof is provided in Appendix cite40†C.1 .
L278: Combining the bounds on the stochastic and optimization errors yields a high-probability bound on $\|I_{k}^{T}-I(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|$, and consequently the desired Hessian inverse approximation error, which completes the proof of Theorem cite76†4.7 . Detailed derivations are deferred to Appendix cite42†C.3 .
L279: ### 4.2  Analysis for NHGD Convergence Rate
L280: 
L281: In this section, building on the Hessian inverse approximation result established in Section cite19†4.1.1 , we characterize the convergence of the NHGD algorithm. We present the main convergence theorem and then outline the proof sketch.
L282: 
L283: #### 4.2.1  Convergence rate
L284: ###### Theorem 4.10.
L285: 
L286: Suppose Assumptions cite100†4.1 , cite101†4.2 , cite102†4.3 , cite103†4.4 and cite106†4.5 hold. Set the parameters for Algorithm cite91†1 as $\alpha_{k}=1/(4L_{v})$ and $\eta_{t}=4/(\mu(t+\frac{8L^{2}}{\mu^{2}}))$, for any $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $T\geq T_{1}(\delta)$, the following holds with probability at least $1-\delta$:
L287: 
L288:  | $\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{T}\log\left(\frac{KT}{\delta}\right)\right),$  |
L289: where $L_{v}$ is defined in (cite109†D.1 ) and $T_{1}(\delta)$ is defined in (cite110†D.23 ).
L290: 
L291: Equivalently, by choosing $T=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1}\log(\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}))$ and $K=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})$, NHGD achieves an $\epsilon$-stationary point, with overall sample complexity $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$.
L292: ###### Remark 4.11.
L293: 
L294: NHGD achieves an $\epsilon$-stationary point with total sample complexity $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$, matching that of optimize-then-approximate methods (see Table cite78†1 ). Notably, the Hessian inverse approximation in NHGD can be performed in parallel with the inner-loop SGD, incurring no additional runtime overhead. This computational efficiency is further validated by the numerical results in Section cite31†5 .
L295: 
L296: #### 4.2.2  Proof Sketch of Theorem cite77†4.10 L297: ##### Step 1: Descent inequality for the outer level.
L298: 
L299: We begin by establishing a descent inequality for the outer-level objective. Under Assumptions cite100†4.1 and cite101†4.2 , the outer iterates satisfy
L300: 
L301:  | $$\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})\|^{2}\leq 16L_{v}(\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi^{*})+3\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})-\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k})\|^{2},$$  |
L302: where $\Phi^{*}$ denotes the optimal value of the overall problem. A formal statement of this result is provided in Lemma cite111†D.1 . This shows that establishing convergence guarantee reduces to bounding the hypergradient approximation error $\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})-\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k})\|$.
L303: ##### Step 2: Decomposition of the hypergradient approximation error.
L304: 
L305: Under the regularity conditions imposed by our assumptions, the hypergradient approximation error admits the following decomposition:
L306:  | $\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})-\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k})\|$  | $\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\|\theta_{k}^{T}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\|A_{k}^{T}-H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\|\right)$  |
L307:  |  | $\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\bigl\|(L_{k}^{T}-\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}\ell(v_{k},\theta^{*}(v_{k})))A_{k}^{T}\bigr\|\right),$  |
L308: where the formal statement of this result is provided in Lemma cite112†D.2 . Consequently, controlling the hypergradient error reduces to bound: (i) the inner-level SGD deviation $\|\theta_{k}^{T}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|$, (ii) the Hessian inverse approximation error $\|A_{k}^{T}-H(\theta^{*}(v_{k}))^{-1}\|$, and (iii) the cross-partial derivative approximation error $\|L_{k}^{T}-\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}\ell(v_{k},\theta^{*}(v_{k}))\|$.
L309: We next bound the inner-level SGD deviation $\|\theta_{k}^{T}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|$, whose error is coupled with $\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k})\|$, reflecting the intrinsic coupling between the inner-level SGD deviation and the outer objective. The Hessian inverse and cross-partial approximation errors, however, admit bounds independent of $v_{k}$, and are therefore addressed separately at the end.
L310: ##### Step 3: Bounding the inner-level SGD deviation.
L311: Lemma cite92†4.8 shows that inner-level SGD error depends on $\|\theta_{k}^{0}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|$. To further control this, we use the warm-start technique $\theta_{k}^{0}=\theta_{k-1}^{T}$, which yields the following recursion: $\|\theta_{k-1}^{T}-\theta^{*}(v_{k})\|\leq\|\theta_{k-1}^{T}-\theta^{*}(v_{k-1})\|+\frac{\alpha L}{\mu}\|\widehat{\nabla}\Phi(v_{k-1})-\nabla\Phi(v_{k-1})\|+\frac{\alpha L}{\mu}\|\nabla\Phi(v_{k-1})\|$.
L312: Applying this recursion yields a high-probability control of the hypergradient error, leaving only the Hessian inverse and cross-partial derivative approximation errors to be handled separately.
L313: ##### Step 4: Bounding the cross-partial derivative approximation error.
L314: 
L315: Theorem cite76†4.7 controls the Hessian inverse approximation error. An analogous argument yields the following sample-complexity bound for the cross-partial derivative approximation error, with detailed proof in Appendix cite43†C.4 .
