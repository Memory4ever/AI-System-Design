L326: ### A.1 Proof of Proposition cite68†1 L327: 
L328: In this section, we provide the complete proof of Proposition cite68†1 .
L329: ##### Clarification of the policy notation.
L330: In the main text, we write $\mathcal{S}=g_{\phi}({\mathbf{z}},t)$ to denote the output of the unmasking policy, which in practice can be stochastic. In this appendix, we adopt the equivalent distributional view under the simplifying assumption $|\mathcal{S}|=1$: namely, we write $g_{\phi}(i\mid{\mathbf{z}},t)$ for the conditional probability of selecting the next index $i$ given the current state $({\mathbf{z}},t)$.
L331: Sampling $I\sim g_{\phi}(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{z}},t)$ and setting $\mathcal{S}=\{I\}$ recovers the main-text notation.
L332: ##### Notation.
L333: 
L334: For ${\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}:=(\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathbf{m}\})^{L}$, define the unmasked and masked index sets $\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}}):=\{i\in[L]:{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\neq\mathbf{m}\}$ and $\mathrm{msk}({\mathbf{z}}):=[L]\setminus\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})$. For $i\in[L]$ and $v\in\mathcal{V}$, write $z^{(i\leftarrow v)}\in\mathcal{Z}$ for the sequence obtained by replacing the $i$-th entry of $z$ with $v$ (leaving all other coordinates unchanged).
L335: Let ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$ be a clean sequence in $\mathcal{V}^{L}$. For any partially-masked ${\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}$ and any $i\in\mathrm{msk}({\mathbf{z}})$, the (time-agnostic) ground-truth unmasking posterior is $p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{z}}):=\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}^{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})})$.
L336: 
L337: We now restate the formal version of Proposition cite68†1 .
L338: ###### Proposition 1 (Formal).
L339: Fix an integer $K\geq 1$ and a time grid $t_{j}:=1-j/K$ for $j=0,1,\dots,K$. Consider the following two Markov chains on $\mathcal{Z}$, both initialized at the fully masked state ${\mathbf{z}}_{t_{0}}=(m,\dots,m)$.
L340: Given ${\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}$, sample an index $I_{j}\sim g_{\phi}(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}},t_{j})$ (so $I_{j}\in\mathrm{msk}({\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}})$), then sample a token $V_{j}\sim p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{I_{j}}=\cdot\mid{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}})$, and set ${\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j+1}}:={\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}^{(I_{j}\leftarrow V_{j})}$. Let $q_{t_{j}}$ denote the law of ${\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}$ under this procedure. First sample ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and set $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{0}}=(m,\dots,m)$.
L341: Given $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}$, sample $I_{j}\sim g_{\phi}(\cdot\mid\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}},t_{j})$ and set $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j+1}}:=\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}^{(I_{j}\leftarrow{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{I_{j}})}$. Let $\tilde{q}_{t_{j}}$ denote the marginal law of $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}$ after sampling ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$. Then, for every $j\in\{0,1,\dots,K\}$, we have $q_{t_{j}}=\tilde{q}_{t_{j}}$.
L342: ###### Proof.
L343: 
L344: We show that the two chains have the same one-step transition kernel from every state ${\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}$.
L345: Fix a step $j$ and a state ${\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal{Z}$. Under the teacher-forced chain, the event $\{\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}}\}$ is possible only if the sampled clean sequence ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}$ matches ${\mathbf{z}}$ on the revealed coordinates, i.e., ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}$.
L346: Moreover, conditioned on ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}$, the probability of realizing $\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}}$ depends only on the policy randomness along the trajectory (since $g_{\phi}$ reacts only to the currently revealed tokens and $t$), and is therefore identical for all ${\mathbf{x}}_{0}$ that agree with ${\mathbf{z}}$ on $\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})$.
L347: Consequently, for some scalar $\alpha_{j}({\mathbf{z}})\geq 0$ and all ${\mathbf{x}}\in V^{L}$,
L348:  | $$\mathbb{P}(\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}_{0}={\mathbf{x}})=\alpha_{j}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{1}\{{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}\}.$$  |
L349: 
L350: By Bayes’ rule, this implies
L351: 
L352:  | $$\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}={\mathbf{x}}\mid\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}})\;\propto\;p_{\mathrm{data}}({\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{1}\{{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}_{\mathrm{um}({\mathbf{z}})}\}.$$  |
L353: In particular, for any masked coordinate $i\in\mathrm{msk}({\mathbf{z}})$ and any $v\in V$, $\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}})=p(x_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{z}})$ by the definition of the ground-truth unmasking posterior.
L354: 
L355: Now fix $i\in\mathrm{msk}({\mathbf{z}})$ and $v\in V$. Using the above conditional identity and the fact that $I_{j}$ is drawn from $g_{\phi}(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{z}},t_{j})$ given the current state, the teacher-forced transition probability is
L356:  | $$\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j+1}}={\mathbf{z}}^{(i\leftarrow v)}\mid\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}}\right)=g_{\phi}(i\mid{\mathbf{z}},t_{j})\,\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}})=g_{\phi}(i\mid{\mathbf{z}},t_{j})\,p(x_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{z}}).$$  |
L357: 
L358: On the other hand, the idealized inference chain satisfies
L359:  | $$\mathbb{P}\!\left({\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j+1}}={\mathbf{z}}^{(i\leftarrow v)}\mid{\mathbf{z}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}}\right)=g_{\phi}(i\mid{\mathbf{z}},t_{j})\,p(x_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{z}}),$$  |
L360: since it samples the same index distribution and then draws the revealed token from the same posterior. Therefore, the two chains have identical transition kernels and the same initial condition ${\mathbf{z}}_{t_{0}}=\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{0}}=(m,\dots,m)$. It follows by induction on $j$ that $q_{t_{j}}=\tilde{q}_{t_{j}}$ for all $j\in\{0,1,\dots,K\}$.
L361: $\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}_{t_{j}}={\mathbf{z}})=p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}=v\mid{\mathbf{z}})$ by the definition of the ground-truth unmasking posterior. ∎
L362: ##### Remark (continuous-time limit).
L363: The discrete-time proof above is stated on a grid and under the one-index-per-step idealization. In the continuous-time limit $K\to\infty$, the corresponding dynamics converge to a continuous-time Markov chain on $\mathcal{Z}$.
L364: The same argument applies at the level of rate matrices, which can be understood as the infinitesimal transition kernel: conditioned on the current partially revealed state, the next revealed token in the teacher-forced construction has the same conditional distribution as sampling from the ground-truth posterior, hence the two generators coincide. Therefore, the marginal agreement extends to all $t\in[0,1]$ in the continuous-time formulation.
L365: ### A.2 Proof of Proposition cite74†2 L366: 
L367: This section contains the formal version of Proposition cite74†2 and its proof.
L368: 
L369: We start with a general lemma about the minimizers of weighted cross-entropy loss functions.
L370: ###### Lemma 4 (Weighted cross-entropy is minimized by the true conditional).
L371: 
L372: Let $(X,Y)$ be any jointly distributed random variables where $Y$ takes values in a finite set $\mathcal{V}$. Let $w(X)\geq 0$ be any measurable function.
L373: 
L374: Consider the functional
L375: 
L376:  | $$\mathcal{L}(q):=\mathbb{E}\big[w(X)\,(-\log q(Y\mid X))\big],$$  |  | (3)
L377: 
L378: where $q(\cdot\mid x)$ ranges over all conditional distributions on $\mathcal{V}$.
L379: 
L380: Then any minimizer $q^{\star}$ satisfies
L381:  | $$q^{\star}(\cdot\mid x)=\mathbb{P}(Y=\cdot\mid X=x)$$  |  | (4)
L382: 
L383: for $\mathbb{P}$-almost every $x$ such that $w(x)>0$. If $w(x)>0$ holds $\mathbb{P}$-almost surely, the minimizer is unique up to $\mathbb{P}$-a.s. equality.
L384: ###### Proof.
L385: 
L386: Conditioning on $X=x$,
L387: 
L388:  | $$\mathbb{E}[-\log q(Y\mid X)\mid X=x]=H\big(\mathbb{P}(Y=\cdot\mid X=x)\big)+\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathbb{P}(Y=\cdot\mid X=x)\,\|\,q(\cdot\mid x)\big).$$  |  | (5)
L389: 
L390: Multiply by $w(x)\geq 0$ and take expectations over $X$. The entropy term is independent of $q$, while the KL term is nonnegative and equals $0$ iff $q(\cdot\mid x)=\mathbb{P}(Y=\cdot\mid X=x)$. This yields the claim and uniqueness on $\{w>0\}$. ∎
L391: ###### Proposition 2 (Formal).
L392: 
L393: Let the masking process take the form
L394: 
L395:  | $$p({\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{z}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}_{0},t)\propto\alpha_{t}({\mathbf{z}})\cdot\mathbf{1}\{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\mathbf{um}({\mathbf{z}})}={\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{um}({\mathbf{z}})}\},$$  |
L396: 
L397: where $\alpha_{t}:(\mathcal{V}\cup\{\mathbf{m}\})^{L}\to[0,1]$ is a weighing function. Denote the density over $(x_{0},t,x_{t})$ induced by this forward process by $p_{f}$, i.e.
L398:  | $$p_{f}(x_{0},t,x_{t})=p(x_{t}|x_{0},t)p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_{0})\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}(t),$$  |
L399: 
L400: where $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ is the density of the uniform distribution over $[0,1]$. Then any minimizer $f_{\theta}^{*}$ of the training loss
L401: 
L402:  | $$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{({\mathbf{x}}_{0},t,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\sim p_{f}}\biggl[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i\colon{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{i}=\mathbf{m}}-\log f_{\theta}^{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{i}\,|\,{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\biggr]$$  |
L403: is the ground-truth posterior of $p_{\mathrm{data}}$, and this posterior is equal to the distribution conditioned on the unmasked indices of $x_{0}$ and $x_{t}$ coinciding, i.e.
L404: 
L405:  | $$f_{\theta}^{*}(x_{0}\mid x_{t})=\mathbb{P}(x_{0}\mid x_{t})\propto\mathbf{1}\{x_{0}^{\mathbf{um}(x_{t})}=x_{t}^{\mathbf{um}(x_{t})}\}\,p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_{0}).$$  |
