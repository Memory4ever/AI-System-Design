L235: #### Counterexamples to Law of Large Numbers
L236: 
L237: The Law of Large Numbers and especially the Central Limit Theorem as stated above are subtle results. Indeed, we observe that the conclusion of our Law of Large Numbers (Theorem cite66†3.3 ) is not valid in the stated form for ReLU activation functions or for the Leaky ReLU case when all the matrices in the support of $\mu$ have positive entries. We state the following precise lemma.
L238: ###### Lemma 3.5.
L239: 
L240: (Counterexample to Law of Large Numbers for $|X_{\ell}|$) Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $M_{d}(\mathbb{R})$. Let $(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}_{\mu})$ be as in (cite87†5 ), $(X_{\ell})_{\geq 0}$ be as in (cite88†6 ). Then the conclusion of Theorem cite66†3.3 is false under either of the following assumptions:
L241: 
L242:   1. (1)
L243: 
L244: (ReLU activation function) When $\phi(x)=\max(x,0)$ and the entries of $\mu$ are independent and all distributed as $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})$ for $\sigma>0$.
L245:   2. (2)
L246: 
L247: (Measure supported on matrices with positive entries) When $\phi(x)=\max(x,\alpha x)$ for $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}$ and the entries of $\mu$ are independent and all distributed as $\mathrm{Unif}[0,a]$ for some $a>0$.
L248: ## 4 Lyapunov Initialization
L249: 
L250: #### Formulas for Lyapunov Exponent
L251: 
L252: In dynamical systems, Lyapunov exponents are usually difficult to calculate. It is remarkable that for the most standard initializations and for Leaky ReLU activation functions, we can actually give effective formulas for the Lyapunov exponents. We first state our formulas in the Gaussian case.
L253: ###### Theorem 4.1.
L254: 
L255: Let $\mu_{\sigma}$ be the probability measure on $M_{d}(\mathbb{R})$ with all coefficients being independent and distributed as $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})$ for some $\sigma>0$. Let $\phi=\max(x,\alpha x)$ be a Leaky ReLU activation function with $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}$. Then the Lyaponov exponent $\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma},\phi}$ from Theorem cite66†3.3 satisfies
L256: 
L257:  | $$\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma},\phi}=\log(\sigma)+I(d,\alpha),$$  |  | (10)
L258: 
L259: where $I(\alpha,d)$ is the integral given by
L260:  | $$I(d,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}-\frac{1}{2^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2t}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\alpha^{2}t}}\right)^{d}}{2t}dt.$$  |  | (11)
L261: 
L262: Next, we address the orthogonal group, where we denote as in (cite94†7 ) by $\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)$ the scaled orthogonal group for $\eta>0$ endowed with the Haar probability measure $m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)}$ (see Appendix cite32†B ).
L263: ###### Theorem 4.2.
L264: 
L265: Let $m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)}$ be the Haar probability measure on $\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)$, the group of orthogonal $d\times d$ matrices scaled by $\eta>0$. Let $\phi=\max(x,\alpha x)$ be a Leaky ReLU activation function with $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}$. Then the Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_{m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)},\phi}$ from Theorem cite66†3.3 satisfies for $I(d,\alpha)$ from (cite95†11 ) that
L266:  | $$\lambda_{m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)},\phi}=\log(\eta)+I(d,\alpha)-I(d,1).$$  |  | (12)

L298: Theorem cite98†4.1 and Theorem cite99†4.2 allow us to calculate Lyapunov exponents efficiently. Indeed, the integral (cite95†11 ) can be easily approximated by a quadrature method.
L299: Recall that for a Leaky ReLU function of slope $\alpha$, He initialization samples the matrix entries independently and distributed as $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{He}}^{2})$ with
L300: 
L301:  | $$\sigma_{\mathrm{He}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{d(1+\alpha^{2})}}.$$  |  | (13)
L302: 
L303: We then denote by $\lambda_{\mathrm{He}}$ the Lyapunov exponent resulting from $\mu_{\sigma}$ and $\phi$, that is
L304: 
L305:  | $$\lambda_{\mathrm{He}}=\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma_{\mathrm{He}}},\phi}=\log(\sigma_{\mathrm{He}})+I(d,\alpha).$$  |  | (14)
L306: Moreover, we denote by $\lambda_{\mathrm{orth}}$ the Lyapunov exponent of $m_{\mathrm{O}(d)}$, that is
L307: 
L308:  | $$\lambda_{\mathrm{orth}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{O}(d),\phi}=I(d,\alpha)-I(d,1).$$  |  | (15)
L309: 
L310: We show in Table cite100†1 and Section cite47†D.3 some numerical values for $\lambda_{\mathrm{He}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{orth}}$. We next state the following asymptotic expansion result for $d\to\infty$ for $I(d,\alpha)$.
L311: ###### Theorem 4.3.
L312: 
L313: (Asymptotic Expansion) Let $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}$ and let $I(d,\alpha)$ be from (cite95†11 ). Then for $d\to\infty$ it holds that
L314: 
L315:  | $$I(d,\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(d\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2d}+O_{\alpha}(d^{-2}),$$  |  | (16)
L316: 
L317: where $C_{\alpha}=\frac{5-2\alpha^{2}+5\alpha^{4}}{(1+\alpha^{2})^{2}}$ and the implied constant depends on $\alpha$.
L318: 
L319: In particular, for $\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma},\phi}$ from (cite101†10 ) it holds $d\to\infty$ that
L320:  | $$\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma},\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\sigma^{2}d\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2d}+O_{\alpha}(d^{-2})$$  |  | (17)
L321: 
L322: and for $\lambda_{m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)},\phi}$ from (cite102†12 ) it holds $d\to\infty$ that
L323: 
L324:  | $$\lambda_{m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)},\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\eta^{2}\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)-\frac{C_{\alpha}+2}{2d}+O_{\alpha}(d^{-2}).$$  |  | (18)
L325: We remark that it follows from (cite103†17 ) that $\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma_{\mathrm{He}}},\phi}=O_{\alpha}(d^{-1})$ and thus as $d\to\infty$, He initialization leads to a zero Lyapunov exponent. To prove the latter insight, we believe that all the theory developed in this paper is necessary and we have not found a heuristic argument to date. However, in low dimensions He initialization does not give a zero Lyapunov exponent, as Table cite100†1 shows.
L326: In addition, we can deduce from (cite104†18 ) that $\lambda_{m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)},\phi}$ converges to $\frac{1}{2}\log(\eta^{2}\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2})$ as $d\to\infty$.

L356: #### Lyapunov Initialization
L357: 
L358: Based on the Law of Large Numbers established in Theorem 2.2, to prevent the vanishing ($\lambda_{\mu,\phi}<0$) or exploding ($\lambda_{\mu,\phi}>0$) of activations, we suggest to choose our random weights such that the Lyapunov exponent is exactly zero:
L359: 
L360:  | $$\lambda_{\mu,\phi}=0.$$  |  | (19)
L361: For the Gaussian case, where $\mu_{\sigma}$ is the measure with independent entries distributed as $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})$, we denote by $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}>0$ the specific standard deviation that satisfies $\lambda_{\mu_{\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}},\phi}=0$. Using the formula derived in Theorem cite98†4.1 , this critical value can be calculated as
L362: 
L363:  | $$\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=\exp(-I(d,\alpha)).$$  |  | (20)
L364: In the context of orthogonal Initialization, we consider the scaled orthogonal group $\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)$ endowed with the Haar probability measure $m_{\eta\cdot\mathrm{O}(d)}$. We analogously define the critical scaling factor $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ as the value of $\eta>0$ that results in a Lyapunov exponent of zero, which by Theorem cite99†4.2 can be calculated as
L365: 
L366:  | $$\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}=\exp(I(d,1)-I(d,\alpha)).$$  |  | (21)
L367: Numerical values for $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ across various widths and slopes are provided in Table cite107†2 and in Section cite47†D.3 .

L384: #### Sampled Lyapunov Initialization
L385: Sampled Lyapunov Initialization is a refined strategy to counter depth-dependent stochastic fluctuations. Indeed, the Central Limit Theorem predicts that the logarithm of the activation norm, $\log|X_{\ell}|$, behaves as a Gaussian variable with a standard deviation of $O(\mkern-1.0mu\sqrt{\ell}\mkern-1.0mu)$. This causes the magnitude $|X_{\ell}|$ to fluctuate between $e^{-O(\mkern-1.0mu\sqrt{\ell}\mkern-1.0mu)}$ and $e^{O(\mkern-1.0mu\sqrt{\ell}\mkern-1.0mu)}$ as depth $\ell$ increases.
L386: To counter this, we generate $O(\mkern-1.0mu\sqrt{l}\mkern-1.0mu)$ candidate initializations. Denote by $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{input}}$ the input distribution on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, for example the uniform distribution on the input values. We then select the initialization where the expected output norm $\mathbb{E}_{x_{0}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{input}}}\!\left[|X_{\ell}|\right]$ is closest to $1$.
L387: We stress that the calculation of $\mathbb{E}_{x_{0}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{input}}}\!\left[|X_{\ell}|\right]$ should be performed before training and hyperparameter tuning, and is therefore a computationally efficient way to improve Lyapunov initialization.
