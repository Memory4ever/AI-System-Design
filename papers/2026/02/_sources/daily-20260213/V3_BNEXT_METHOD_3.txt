L148: ### 1.4 Contributions
L149: 
L150: This paper develops a statistical provability theory for agentic theorem proving. Concretely:
L151: 
L152:   * •
L153: 
L154: MDP formalization and the notion of statistical provability. We formalize modern agentic theorem provers as finite-horizon reachability MDPs and define provability as optimal time-bounded success probability, together with its distributional counterpart over instance distributions.
L155: 
L156:   * •
L157: Bellman-structure evaluation and certificates. We use Bellman recursions and inequalities to derive provability certificates and to bound the deviation between the optimal success probability and that achieved by score-guided planning algorithms.
L158: 
L159:   * •
L160: A component-sensitive bound on success probability. We show how proof complexity, representation geometry (via metric entropy / doubling-type conditions), tool feedback, retrieval, test-time search, and sequential statistical complexity (e.g., sequential entropy / sequential Rademacher-type quantities) enter the provability bounds.
L161: 
L162:   * •
L163: A bridge between logical hardness and statistical complexity. We position these results as a step toward connecting classical complexity barriers with the statistical structure of real mathematical corpora, clarifying when “typical” instances can be tractable for learned, agentic systems.
L164: ## 2 Problem Formulation
L165: 
L166: Here we formalize interactive formal theorem proving (with an LLM agent and an exact verifier/proof assistant) as a time-bounded reachability problem in a Markov decision process (MDP). We then develop a Bellman-structure–based evaluation of (i) provability (optimal success probability), (ii) deviation from optimality of score-based planning (greedy/top-$k$/beam/rollout), and (iii) certificate-style upper/lower bounds. Finally, we discuss easy vs hard instances.

L168: 
L169: $(K,d_{K})$ denotes a compact metric space used as an embedding space for individual goals, $M(K)$ denote the set of finite positive Borel measures on $K$. For $W>0$:
L170: 
L171:  | $$M_{\leq W}(K):=\{\mu\in M(K):\mu(K)\leq W\}.$$  |
L172: 
L173: For a bounded function $f:K\to\mathbb{R}$:
L174:  | $$\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|,\quad\mathrm{Lip}(f):=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d_{K}(x,y)},$$  |
L175:  | $$\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{BL}}:=\max\{\|f\|_{\infty},\mathrm{Lip}(f)\}.$$  |
L176: 
L177: The bounded Lipschitz metric on $M(K)$ is
L178: 
L179:  | $$d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq 1}\int_{K}fd(\mu-\nu).$$  |
L180: 
L181: “Goal solved” is represented by the zero measure $0\in M(K)$.
L182: All probabilities are defined on an underlying probability space on which the agent’s randomness (LLM sampling, exploration) and any environment randomness live.

L184: 
L185: ### 3.1 Informal picture
L186: 
L187: At each step the agent observes a proof assistant state (goals + local context + error messages) and chooses an action (e.g., a tactic/lemma invocation, or an LLM query template with retrieval parameters). The proof assistant deterministically (or stochastically, if external tools are randomized) returns the next state. L189: 
L190: Let $\mathcal{G}_{0}$ denote the (discrete) set of syntactic goals (or goal+context objects). Fix an embedding map
L191: 
L192:  | $$\phi:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to K.$$  |

L220:

L222: 
L223: Fix a horizon $B\in\mathbb{N}$. For a policy $\pi$ and initial state $\mu\in\mathcal{X}$, define the time-bounded success probability
L224: 
L225:  | $$V_{B}^{\pi}(\mu):=\Pr^{\pi}_{\mu}\left(\exists t\in\{0,1,\dots,B\}\ \text{such that}\ \mu_{t}\in G\right).$$  |
L226:

L228: 
L229:  | $$V_{B}^{*}(\mu):=\sup_{\pi}V_{B}^{\pi}(\mu).$$  |
L230: ###### Definition 2 (Finite-horizon value functions).
L231: 
L232: For $b=0,1,\dots,B$ define
L233: 
L234:  | $$V_{b}^{*}(\mu):=\sup_{\pi}\Pr^{\pi}_{\mu}\left(\exists t\in\{0,1,\dots,b\}\ \text{s.t.}\ \mu_{t}\in G\right).$$  |
L235:

L237: ###### Definition 3 (One-step optimal action-value).
L238: 
L239: For $b\geq 1$ define
L240: 
L241:  | $$Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}V_{b-1}^{*}(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a).$$  |
L242: 
L243: We have Bellman recursion (finite-horizon reachability). For $b\geq 0$:
L244: 
L245:  | $$V_{b+1}^{*}(\mu)=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{b+1}^{*}(\mu,a).$$  |
L246: 
L247: ###### Lemma 1 (Bounds and monotonicity).
L248: 
L249: For all $b$ and $\mu$, $0\leq V_{b}^{*}(\mu)\leq 1$ and $V_{b}^{*}(\mu)\leq V_{b+1}^{*}(\mu)$.
L250: ###### Proof.
L251: 
L252: Immediate from the definition of reachability probabilities. ∎

L254: ## 4 Existence of optimal value functions and optimal Markov policies
L255: 
L256: We state mild continuity assumptions ensuring that the supremum over actions is attained.
L257: ###### Assumption 1 (Weak continuity / Feller-type condition).
L258: 
L259: For every bounded continuous $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$, the map
L260: 
L261:  | $$(\mu,a)\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a)$$  |
L262: 
L263: is continuous on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}$.
L264: 
L265: This implies that $(\mu_{n},a_{n})\to(\mu,a)$ entails $P(\cdot\mid\mu_{n},a_{n})\Rightarrow P(\cdot\mid\mu,a)$ weakly.
L266: ###### Theorem 2 (Existence of maximizers and optimal Markov policies).
L267: 
L268: Assume: (1) $\mathcal{X}=M_{\leq W}(K)$ is compact under $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$ (cite66†Theorem 1 ), (2) $\mathcal{A}$ is compact, (3) the kernel $P$ satisfies cite67†Assumption 1 , (4) $G=\{0\}$ is closed (hence $\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]$ is upper semicontinuous).
L269: Then for each $b=1,\dots,B$: (a) The Bellman recursion defines a Borel-measurable $V_{b}^{*}$, (b) For each $\mu$, the supremum $\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{b}^{*}(\mu,a)$ is attained by some $a_{b}^{*}(\mu)\in\mathcal{A}$, (c) hence there exists an optimal deterministic Markov policy $\pi^{*}=(a_{b}^{*})_{b=1}^{B}$ achieving $V_{B}^{*}$.
L270: 
L271: The proof is based on backward induction. See cite50†Appendix C for the full proof.

L273: 
L274: The following monotonicity principle is central: it yields upper/lower bound certificates without solving the full MDP.
L275: 
L276: ###### Definition 4 (Bellman operator).
L277: 
L278: Define $T$ on bounded measurable $V:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]$ by
L279: 
L280:  | $$(TV)(\mu):=\mathbf{1}[\mu\in G]\ \vee\ \sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}V(\mu^{\prime})P(d\mu^{\prime}\mid\mu,a).$$  |
L281: 
L282: Then $V_{b+1}^{*}=TV_{b}^{*}$ with $V_{0}^{*}=\mathbf{1}[\cdot\in G]$.
L283: ###### Lemma 2 (Monotonicity).
L284: 
L285: If $V\leq W$ pointwise then $TV\leq TW$ pointwise.
L286: 
L287: ###### Proof.
L288: 
L289: Supremum and expectation preserve order. ∎
L290: ###### Theorem 3 (Sub-/super-solution certificates).
L291:

L293:

L295: 
L296:  | $$U_{b+1}\geq TU_{b}\quad\text{for all }b=0,\dots,B-1,$$  |

L672: ###### Lemma 6 (Arzelà–Ascoli compactness of the BL unit ball).
L673: 
L674: Let
L675: 
L676:  | $$\mathcal{F}:=\{f:K\to\mathbb{R}:\ \|f\|_{\mathrm{BL}}\leq 1\}.$$  |
L677:

L679: 
L680: ###### Proof.
L681: 
L682: Functions in $\mathcal{F}$ are uniformly bounded ($\|f\|\leq 1$) and equicontinuous (Lipschitz constant $\leq 1$). Since $K$ is compact, Arzelà–Ascoli applies. ∎
L683: ###### Lemma 7 ($d_{\mathrm{BL}}$ metrizes weak convergence on $M_{\leq W}(K)$).
L684: 
L685: Let $(\mu_{n})$ and $\mu$ be in $M_{\leq W}(K)$. Then
L686: 
L687:  | $$\mu_{n}\Rightarrow\mu\ \text{(weakly)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0.$$  |
L688: ###### Proof.
L689: 
L690: we show
L691: 
L692:   * •
L693: 
L694: ($\Leftarrow$) If $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0$, then $\int fd(\mu_{n}-\mu)\to 0$ for all bounded Lipschitz $f$. Bounded Lipschitz functions are dense in $C(K)$ on compact metric spaces, so convergence holds for all continuous $f$, i.e., $\mu_{n}\Rightarrow\mu$.
L695: 
L696:   * •
L697: ($\Rightarrow$) Assume $\mu_{n}\Rightarrow\mu$. Suppose $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{n},\mu)\not\to 0$. Then there exists $\varepsilon>0$ and a subsequence (still indexed by $n$) and $f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}$ such that
L698: 
L699:  | $$\left|\int f_{n}d(\mu_{n}-\mu)\right|\geq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }n.$$  |
L700: 
L701: By Lemma A.1, $(f_{n})$ has a further subsequence converging uniformly to some $f\in\mathcal{F}$. Then
L702:  | $\displaystyle\left|\int f_{n}d(\mu_{n}-\mu)\right|$  | $\displaystyle\leq\left|\int fd(\mu_{n}-\mu)\right|+\left|\int(f_{n}-f)d(\mu_{n}-\mu)\right|$  |
L703:  |  | $\displaystyle\leq\left|\int fd(\mu_{n}-\mu)\right|+\|f_{n}-f\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\mu_{n}-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.$  |
L704: Since $\mu_{n},\mu$ are positive with mass $\leq W$, $\|\mu_{n}-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\mu_{n}(K)+\mu(K)\leq 2W$, and $\|f_{n}-f\|_{\infty}\to 0$. Also, $f$ is continuous so $\int fd(\mu_{n}-\mu)\to 0$ by weak convergence. Hence the right-hand side tends to $0$, contradicting $\geq\varepsilon$.
L705: 
L706: Therefore $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0$. ∎
L707: ###### Lemma 8 (Compactness).
L708: 
L709: $M_{\leq W}(K)$ is compact under $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$.
L710: 
L711: ###### Proof.
L712: 
L713: $M_{\leq W}(K)$ is tight and weakly sequentially compact (by Prokhorov; on compact $K$, tightness is automatic). Any sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence; by Lemma A.2 that subsequence is Cauchy and convergent in $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$. Hence sequential compactness, hence compactness. ∎

L737: ## Appendix D Occupancy distribution, margin condition, fast rates, and what if margin fails
L738: ### D.1 Occupancy distribution $\nu_{b}$: definition and meaning
L739: 
L740: In finite-horizon control, the occupancy distribution captures where the algorithm actually visits, and is the correct object for distributional assumptions (like margins).
L741: 
L742: For a (possibly randomized) policy $\pi$ and an initial distribution $Q$ over states, define the depth-$b$ state occupancy as the law of $X_{b-1}$:
L743: 
L744:  | $$\nu_{b}^{\pi}(\cdot):=\Pr^{\pi}(X_{b-1}\in\cdot\mid X_{0}\sim Q).$$  |
L745: For reachability tasks, it is often more appropriate to condition on “not solved yet”:
L746: 
L747:  | $$\tilde{\nu}_{b}^{\pi}(\cdot):=\Pr^{\pi}(X_{b-1}\in\cdot\mid X_{0}\sim Q,\ T^{\pi}\geq b),$$  |
L748: 
L749: because decisions after termination are irrelevant.
L750: 
L751: For beam search, one can analogously define an occupancy over expanded nodes at depth $b$ induced by the beam’s node-selection rule; the key point is the same: $\nu_{b}$ is the distribution of states that matter at depth $b$ under the algorithm.
L752: ### D.2 Margin condition: what it says
L753: 
L754: Let $Q_{b}^{*}(x,\cdot)$ be the optimal one-step value at depth $b$. For top-$k$ analysis, define the $(k+1)$-gap
L755: 
L756:  | $$\Delta_{b}^{(k)}(x):=Q_{b}^{*}(x,a_{1}^{*}(x))-Q_{b}^{*}(x,a_{k+1}^{*}(x)),$$  |
L757: 
L758: where actions are ranked by $Q_{b}^{*}$.
L759: 
L760: The margin condition (cite128†Arlot and Bartlett, 2011 , Mammen-Tsybakov-style, see e.g.) states that near-ties are rare:
L761: 
L762:  | $$\nu_{b}(\Delta_{b}^{(k)}(X)\leq t)\ \leq\ C_{\Delta}t^{\beta}\quad\text{for small }t.$$  |
L763: Interpretation:
L764: 
L765:   * •
L766: 
L767: $\beta$ large $\Rightarrow$ “clear separation”: ambiguous states are rare.
L768: 
L769:   * •
L770: 
L771: $\beta$ small (or $0$) $\Rightarrow$ “many near-ties”: small score errors often change rankings.
L772:
