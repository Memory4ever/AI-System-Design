L492: In this section, we present auxiliary lemmas used in the proof of the main result.
L493: ###### Lemma B.1.
L494: 
L495: Assumptions cite100†4.1 , cite102†4.3 and cite103†4.4 imply the following:
L496: 
L497:   1. (1)
L498: 
L499: Gradient $\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ and $\nabla_{v}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ are $L$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to $\theta$ for any $v,\xi$. $\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)$ and $\nabla_{v}\ell(v,\theta)$ are $L$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to $\theta$ for any $v$.
L500: 
L501:   2. (2)
L502: 
L503: $\|\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)\|\leq L$, for any $v,\theta,\xi.$
L504: 
L505:   3. (3)
L506: $\|\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)\|\leq LR+C_{1}$, for any $v,\theta,\xi.$
L507: 
L508:   4. (4)
L509: 
L510: Function $\ell(v,\theta)$ is $\mu$-strongly convex with respect to $\theta$ for any $v$.
L511: ###### Proof.
L512: We first show part (1). Since function $l(v,\theta,\xi)$ is jointly $L$-smooth with respect to $(\theta,v)$, we can concluded that $\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ and $\nabla_{v}l(v,\theta,\xi)$ are both $L$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to $\theta$ and $v$ for any $\xi$. Then, by the unbiased gradient Assumption cite102†4.3 and Jensen’s Inequality, we can obtain that $\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)$ and $\nabla_{v}\ell(v,\theta)$ are $L$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to $\theta$ for any $v$.
L513: Then, we show part (2). For any $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}$ and unit vector $u$,
L514:  | $\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)x\|$  | $\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{v}\langle\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi),x\rangle\|$  |
L515:  |  | $\displaystyle=\left\|\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\langle\nabla_{\theta}l(v+\epsilon u,\theta,\xi)-\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi),x\rangle}{\epsilon}\right\|$  |
L516:  |  | $\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\left\|\langle\nabla_{\theta}l(v+\epsilon u,\theta,\xi)-\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi),x\rangle\right\|}{\epsilon}$  |
L517:  |  | $\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\left\|\nabla_{\theta}l(v+\epsilon u,\theta,\xi)-\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)\right\|\left\|x\right\|}{\epsilon}$  |  | (Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality)
L518:  |  | $\displaystyle{\leq}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{L\epsilon\|u\|\left\|x\right\|}{\epsilon}$  |  | (L-Lipschitz continuity of $\nabla_{\theta}l$ in $\theta$)
L519:  |  | $\displaystyle=L\|x\|.$  |
L520: By the definition of matrix operator norm, we have $\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)\|=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\frac{\|\nabla_{\theta,v}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)x\|}{\|x\|}\leq L$, which proves part (2).
L521: 
L522: Next, we show part (3). By gradient noise Assumption cite103†4.4 , L-smoothness of $\ell$ and the boundedness of domain $\Theta$, we obtain
L523:  | $\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)\|$  | $\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta,\xi)-\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)\|+\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta)-\nabla_{\theta}\ell(v,\theta^{*}(v))\|$  |
L524:  |  | $\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+L\|\theta-\theta^{*}(v)\|\leq LR+C_{1}.$  |
L525: 
L526: Finally, we show part (4). The strongly convexity of $l(v,\theta,\xi)$ with respect to $\theta$, implies that, for any $\theta,\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}$,
L527:  | $\displaystyle l(v,\theta,\xi)\geq l(v,\theta_{1},\xi)+\langle\nabla_{\theta}l(v,\theta_{1},\xi),\,\theta-\theta_{1}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\theta-\theta_{1}\|^{2}.$  |
L528: 
L529: Taking expectation with respect to $\xi$ on both sides, and applying the unbiased stochastic gradient Assumption cite102†4.3 yields the strong convexity of $\ell(v,\theta)$. ∎
L530: ###### Proposition B.2.
L531: 
L532: Under Assumptions cite100†4.1 , cite102†4.3 and cite106†4.5 , we have
L533: 
L534:  | $$I(\theta^{*}(v))=H(\theta^{*}(v)),\qquad\forall\,v.$$  |
L535: ###### Proof.
L536: 
L537: By the definition of the Hessian of the inner objective $\ell(v,\theta)$,
L538: 
L539:  | $\displaystyle H\big(\theta^{*}(v)\big)=\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\ell(v,\theta^{*}(v))=\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}[l(v,\theta^{*}(v),\xi)]=-\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}[\log p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))].$  |
L540: 
L541: According to Assumption cite102†4.3 , $\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\ell(v,\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}l(v,\theta,\xi)]$ , we have
L542:  | $\displaystyle H(\theta^{*}(v))=\,$  | $\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}[\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\log p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))]$  |
L543:  | $\displaystyle=\,$  | $\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\frac{\nabla_{\theta}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]$  |
L544:  | $\displaystyle=\,$  | $\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\frac{\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\frac{\nabla_{\theta}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))\nabla_{\theta}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))^{\top}}{p^{2}(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]$  |
L545:  | $\displaystyle=\,$  | $\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\frac{\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\left(-\log p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))\right)\nabla_{\theta}\left(-\log p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))\right)^{\top}\right]$  |
L546:  | $\displaystyle=\,$  | $\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\frac{\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]+I(\theta^{*}(v)).$  |
L547: To prove $H(\theta^{*}(v))=I(\theta^{*}(v))$, it remains to show that the first term on the right-hand side of the previous inequality vanishes. Since $q(\cdot)=p(\cdot,v,\theta^{*}(v))$ (Assumption cite106†4.5 ), we have
L548:  | $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[\frac{\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}\right]$  | $\displaystyle=\int\frac{\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}{p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))}q(\xi)d\xi$  |
L549:  |  | $\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))d\xi$  |
L550:  |  | $\displaystyle=\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}\int p(\xi;v,\theta^{*}(v))d\xi$  |
L551:  |  | $\displaystyle=\nabla_{\theta,\theta}^{2}1$  |
L552:  |  | $\displaystyle=0.$  |
L553: Therefore, $H(\theta^{*}(v))=I(\theta^{*}(v))$, completing the proof. ∎

L556: In this section, we focus on the inner loop of the algorithm. We first establish the convergence rate of the inner-level SGD (Lemma cite92†4.8 ). We then characterize the sample complexity of the Hessian inverse approximation (Theorem cite76†4.7 ) and the cross-partial derivative approximation (Proposition cite93†4.12 ).
L557: We consider a projected SGD algorithm for minimizing the inner objective function $\ell(v,\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot)}\left[l(v,\theta,\xi)\right]$:
L558: 
L559:  | $\displaystyle\theta_{k}^{t+1}=\Pi_{\Theta}\left(\theta_{k}^{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla_{\theta}l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})\right),\quad\,t=0,1,...,T-1.$  |  | (C.1)
L560: 
L561: where $\Pi_{\Theta}$ denotes the projection onto $\Theta=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\mid\|\theta\|\leq R\}$.
L562: We denote by $\mathcal{F}_{k}^{t-1}:=\sigma\!\left(v_{k},\,\theta_{k}^{0},\,\xi_{k}^{0},\cdots,\xi_{k}^{t-1}\right)$, the $\sigma$-algebra representing all information available up to iteration $(k,t-1)$. Consequently, under the conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}^{t-1}\right]$, the only remaining source of randomness is the fresh sample $\xi_{k}^{t}$ drawn at the $t$-th inner iteration.
L563: In the subsequent analysis of the inner problem, the upper-level variable $v_{k}$ is fixed. For notational simplicity, we therefore omit the subscript $k$ and $v_{k}$.
L564: We use the simplified notation: $l(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}):=l(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t},\xi_{k}^{t})$, $\;\ell(\theta_{t}):=\ell(v_{k},\theta_{k}^{t})$, $\;\theta^{*}:=\theta^{*}(v_{k})$, $\;\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\mathcal{F}_{k}^{t}$, $\;\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}_{\Xi\sim q(\cdot)}\!\left[\,\cdot\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{k}^{t-1}\right]$, $\;I_{t}:=I_{k}^{t}$, $\;A_{t}:=A_{k}^{t}$ and $\;L_{t}:=L_{k}^{t}$.
