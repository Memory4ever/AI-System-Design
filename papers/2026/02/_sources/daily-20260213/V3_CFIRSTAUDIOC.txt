L954@P20-21: 21A More Details Of MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer
L955@P21: A.1 Architecture
L956@P21: The encoder and decoder of MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer each consist of 68 causal Transformer blocks with a
L957@P21: 10 s sliding-window attention, enabling efficient streaming inference. To progressively reduce the sequence
L958@P21: length, the encoder inserts patchify operations [80] at the input stage and after layers 12, 24, and 36, with
L959@P21: patch sizes of 240/2/2/2, respectively. Since patchify operations modify the feature dimensionality, a linear
L960@P21: projection is applied after each patchify stage to map the hidden states to the corresponding Transformer
L961@P21: block dimension. This design maps raw 24 kHz waveforms to a low frame rate of 12.5 Hz.
L962@P21: The encoder is composed of four stages with hidden dimensions of 768, 768, 768, and 1280, respectively.
L963@P21: These stages contain 12, 12, 12, and 32 Transformer blocks. For each stage, the feed-forward network (FFN)
L964@P21: dimension is set to four times the corresponding hidden dimension. Multi-head self-attention uses 12, 12,
L965@P21: 12, and 20 attention heads for the four stages, respectively. All Transformer blocks employ rotary positional
L966@P21: embeddings (RoPE) [81].
L967@P21: The decoder mirrors the encoder architecture in a fully causal manner. Both the encoder and decoder contain
L968@P21: approximately 0.8B parameters and are trained from scratch.
L969@P21: Discrete tokenization is performed using a 32-layer residual vector quantizer (RVQ). Each layer uses a codebook of size 1024 with factorized vector quantization (latent dimension 8) [27] and L2-normalized codes.
L970@P21: Quantizer dropout with probability 1.0 is applied during training to enable variable-bitrate tokenization.
L971@P21: To encourage semantically structured discrete representations, we attach a 0.5B decoder-only causal language model [51] for audio-to-text supervision, which autoregressively predicts text conditioned on the
L972@P21: quantizer outputs. The audio-to-text tasks include ASR, multi-speaker ASR, and audio captioning.
L973@P21: For adversarial training, we employ a multi-period discriminator [26] and a complex STFT discriminator [27].
L974@P21: All components—encoder, quantizer, decoder, semantic head, and discriminators—are optimized jointly in
L975@P21: an end-to-end manner.
L976@P21: A.2 Dataset and Optimization.
L977@P21: We train MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer on approximately 3M hours of speech, sound, and music data, covering
L978@P21: both clean and in-the-wild recordings, and mixing audio-only and paired (audio, text) samples. For samples
L979@P21: with available transcriptions or captions, we apply an auxiliary audio-to-text training objective, while audioonly samples are used without text supervision. We optimize both the generator and discriminators using
L980@P21: AdamW [82] optimizer and conduct training in bfloat16 (bf16) precision. The generator is trained with a
L981@P21: learning rate of 1 × 10−4 and a weight decay of 0.01, while no weight decay is applied to the discriminators.
L982@P21: The loss weights are set to 𝜆sem=20, 𝜆rec=15, 𝜆cmt=0.25, 𝜆code=1.0, 𝜆adv=1.0, and 𝜆feat=2.0.
L983@P21: A.3 Training schedule.
L984@P21: Due to computational constraints, we adopt a two-stage training schedule to improve training efficiency: nonadversarial pretraining without discriminator-related losses for 520k steps (batch size 1536, approximately
L985@P21: 5 hours of audio per batch), followed by adversarial finetuning for 500k steps (batch size 768). All modules
L986@P21: are optimized end-to-end without pretrained encoders or semantic teachers [15, 17, 19, 20, 50].
L987@P21: B More Details on Evaluation of Audio Tokenizers
L988@P21: B.1 Reconstruction Evaluation Protocol
L989@P21: We evaluate the reconstruction quality of MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer and open-source audio tokenizers across
L990@P21: three domains: speech, sound, and music.
L991@P21-22: 22Objective evaluation. For speech reconstruction, we conduct evaluations on LibriSpeech test-clean (English) [83]
L992@P22: and AISHELL-2 (Chinese) [84]. We report speaker similarity (SIM), computed as the cosine similarity between speaker embeddings extracted from the original and reconstructed audio using a pretrained speaker
L993@P22: verification model2. In addition, we report short-time objective intelligibility (STOI) [85] and perceptual
L994@P22: evaluation of speech quality (PESQ) [86].
L995@P22: For sound and music reconstruction, following prior work [27], we evaluate on the AudioSet evaluation
L996@P22: subset [87] and MUSDB [88]. We report mel-spectrogram distance and short-time Fourier transform (STFT)
L997@P22: distance as objective metrics.
L998@P22: Subjective evaluation. In addition to objective metrics, we conduct a crowd-sourced listening test based on
L999@P22: the MUSHRA protocol [89]. In this test, each listener rates the perceptual quality of reconstructed audio
L1000@P22: samples on a 1–100 scale.
L1001@P22: For tokenizers that support variable bitrate decoding, we report results at multiple bitrates to characterize
L1002@P22: reconstruction quality across different bitrate regimes.

L1146@P24: C More Details Of Bitrate Controllable Speech Generation
L1147@P24: C.1 Architecture
L1148@P24: The Temporal Transformer is initialized from Qwen3-1.7B [7]. The Depth Transformer is randomly initialized
L1149@P24: and consists of 4 Transformer blocks with a hidden size of 1536 and an FFN dimension of 8960.
L1150@P24: C.2 Training Details
L1151@P24: We adopt a global batch size of 1.35M tokens, including both text tokens and speech tokens, where speech
L1152@P24: tokens are counted at a frame rate of 12.5 Hz. During training, text tokens and speech tokens are concatenated
L1153@P24: along the temporal dimension to form the input sequence for the TTS model. FlashAttention-2 [90] is used to
L1154@P24: accelerate training. All models are trained using the AdamW optimizer with a peak learning rate of 2×10−4.
L1155@P24: For ablation studies, models are trained for 50k steps, while the final models are trained for 200k steps.
L1156@P24: C.3 Inference Details
L1157@P24: At inference time, to maintain consistency with the training procedure, we synthesize speech in a continuationbased manner. Given a prompt audio with its transcription and a target text to be synthesized, we concatenate the prompt transcription tokens xprompt, the target text tokens xsyn, and the prompt audio tokens sprompt
L1158@P24: 11https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-Tokenizer-12Hz
L1159@P24-25: 25into a single input sequence. The TTS model then autoregressively predicts the speech tokens corresponding
L1160@P25: to the target text. Finally, the predicted speech tokens are decoded into waveforms using the CAT decoder.
L1161@P25: D More Details on Baseline Text-to-Speech Systems
L1162@P25: We compare our CAT-TTS system with a wide range of open-source text-to-speech (TTS) models. These
L1163@P25: models can be broadly categorized into three groups.
L1164@P25: The first group consists of cascaded TTS systems that employ multiple generative models, such as AR+NAR
L1165@P25: or NAR+NAR architectures. Representative examples include MaskGCT [41], FireRedTTS [34], CosyVoice2 [40],
L1166@P25: Qwen2.5-Omni [48], CosyVoice3 [91], IndexTTS2 [43], FlexiCodec-TTS [92], and GLM-TTS [44].
L1167@P25: The second group includes purely non-autoregressive TTS systems, such as F5-TTS [36].
L1168@P25: The third group comprises prior fully autoregressive TTS models based on either discrete or continuous representations, including Llasa [32], SparkTTS [33], OpenAudio-s1 [93], HiggsAudio-v2 [60], FireRedTTS2 [34],
L1169@P25: DiTAR [72], and VoxCPM [73].
L1170@P25: CAT-TTS adopts a purely autoregressive architecture based on discrete tokens to perform zero-shot TTS in an
L1171@P25: end-to-end manner, directly generating speech from text without relying on predefined intermediate representations, such as semantic tokens [17, 40]. Moreover, CAT-TTS supports variable-bitrate speech generation
L1172@P25: through Progressive Sequence Dropout.
