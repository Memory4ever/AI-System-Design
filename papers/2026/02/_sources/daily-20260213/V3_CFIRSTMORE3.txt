L4@P0: Abstract
L5@P0: Discrete audio tokenizers are fundamental to empowering large language models with native audio processing and generation capabilities. Despite recent progress, existing approaches often rely
L6@P0: on pretrained encoders, semantic distillation, or heterogeneous CNN-based architectures. These designs introduce fixed inductive biases that limit reconstruction fidelity and hinder effective scaling.
L7@P0: In this paper, we argue that discrete audio tokenization should be learned fully end-to-end using a
L8@P0: homogeneous and scalable architecture. To this end, we first propose CAT (Causal Audio Tokenizer
L9@P0: with Transformer), a purely Transformer-based architecture that jointly optimizes the encoder, quantizer, and decoder from scratch for high-fidelity reconstruction. Building on the CAT architecture,
L10@P0: we develop MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, a large-scale audio tokenizer featuring 1.6 billion parameters,
L11@P0: pre-trained on 3 million hours of diverse, general audio data. We show that this simple, fully endto-end approach built from homogeneous, causal Transformer blocks scales gracefully and supports
L12@P0: high-fidelity reconstruction across diverse audio domains. Across speech, sound, and music, MOSSAudio-Tokenizer consistently outperforms prior codecs over a wide range of bitrates, while exhibiting predictable improvements with increased scale. Notably, leveraging the discrete tokens from our
L13@P0: model, we develop the first purely autoregressive TTS model that surpasses prior non-autoregressive
L14@P0: and cascaded systems. Furthermore, MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer enables competitive ASR performance
L15@P0: without auxiliary encoders. Our findings position the CAT architecture as a unified, scalable interface
L16@P0: for the next generation of native audio foundation models.

L64@P1: Unlike text, audio contains both fine-grained acoustic details and long-range structure, making discrete tokenization more challenging [11]. A unified audio tokenizer should enable high-fidelity reconstruction of
L65@P1: diverse audio signals while remaining compatible with autoregressive sequence modeling [14–16]. Existing
L66@P1: approaches typically address these requirements through pretrained encoders [17–22], multi-stage training
L67@P1: pipelines [14, 23, 24], or architecture-specific inductive biases [25–27], achieving strong performance under
L68@P1: particular design choices. However, such designs introduce additional dependencies and architectural constraints that make it difficult to scale models, data, and quantization capacity in a unified manner. From
L69@P1: this perspective, we draw inspiration from the success of large language models, where simple and efficient
L70@P1: architectures trained on large-scale data have proven critical for achieving strong performance [28, 29]. We
L71@P1: posit that enabling an audio tokenizer to reach a higher performance ceiling similarly requires a simple and
L72@P1: scalable architecture that can be trained end-to-end on large amounts of data. Such a design emphasizes joint
L73@P1: optimization and scale, while minimizing reliance on external priors, pretrained components, or complex
L74@P1: architectural heuristics.
L75@P1: In this work, we propose MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, a fully end-to-end audio tokenizer that serves as a unified
L76@P1: discrete interface for autoregressive audio language models. Our tokenizer, build on CAT (Causal Audio
L77@P1: Tokenizer with Transformer) architecture, operates at a 24 kHz sampling rate with a low token frame rate
L78@P1: of 12.5 Hz, and jointly optimizes the encoder, quantizer, decoder, and discriminator within a single training
L79@P1: pipeline, without relying on pretrained encoders, distillation, or separate optimization of individual components. Both the encoder and decoder are built entirely from causal Transformer blocks, resulting in a simple
L80@P1: and scalable architecture that is naturally aligned with autoregressive modeling [30]. All components of
L81@P1: MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer are designed to operate in a streaming manner, enabling low-latency inference and
L82@P1: consistent training–inference behavior [15, 25, 26].
L83@P1: By scaling large amounts of paired audio–text data, MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer learns discrete representations
L84@P1: that are both structurally rich and acoustically expressive, while remaining robust across a wide range of
L85@P1: bitrates. As a result, the tokenizer achieves high-quality reconstruction of general audio, including speech,
L86@P1: sound, and music, from very low to high bitrate regimes, providing a strong lower bound and a high upper
L87@P1: bound for downstream audio language models.
L88@P1: Across speech, sound, and music, MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality
L89@P1-2: at all evaluated bitrates. Leveraging its discrete tokens, we further introduce a purely autoregressive text2to-speech model with a Progressive Sequence Dropout training strategy, which naturally exploits the tokenizer’s robustness across bitrates and, for the first time, enables a fully autoregressive discrete TTS system [31–34] to outperform prior non-autoregressive [35, 36] and cascaded approaches [37–44]. In addition,
L90@P2: MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer supports competitive automatic speech recognition performance without requiring
L91@P2: an auxiliary audio encoder, matching or exceeding models that rely on dedicated audio encoders combined
L92@P2: with large language models [45–49]. Together, these results demonstrate that CAT architecture provides a
L93@P2: scalable and effective foundation for audio compression, understanding, and generation within a unified
L94@P2: autoregressive framework.
L95@P2: Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
L96@P2: • Homogeneous and Scalable Architecture: We propose CAT(Causal AudioTokenizer with Transformer),
L97@P2: a purely Transformer-based architecture for discrete audio tokenization. By utilizing a homogeneous
L98@P2: stack of causal Transformer blocks, CAT provides a simple and highly scalable discrete interface, minimizing fixed inductive biases and facilitating effective model scaling.
L99@P2: • Large-Scale General Tokenization: Based on the CAT architecture, we developMOSS-Audio-Tokenizer,
L100@P2: a 1.6-billion-parameter audio tokenizer pre-trained from scratch on 3 million hours of diverse audio. It achieves high-fidelity reconstruction across speech, sound, and music at a low frame rate of
L101@P2: 12.5 Hz. The model natively supports variable bitrates ranging from 0.125 kbps to 4 kbps and enables
L102@P2: low-latency, frame-level streaming encoding and decoding for real-time applications.
L103@P2: • Breakthrough in End-to-End Autoregressive Audio Generation: Leveraging CAT’s discrete tokens,
L104@P2: we develop the first purely autoregressive (AR) TTS system that outperforms prior non-autoregressive
L105@P2: and cascaded models. Furthermore, we propose Progressive Sequence Dropout, a training strategy
L106@P2: that enables a single autoregressive model to perform variable-bitrate speech generation by effectively
L107@P2: utilizing the tokenizer’s hierarchical quantization structure.
L108@P2: • Consistent Scaling Performance: We investigate the scaling behavior of the CAT architecture with
L109@P2: respect to model parameters and training computation (via batch size). Our results demonstrate that
L110@P2: CAT exhibits consistent performance gains in reconstruction quality as the model capacity and total
L111@P2: computational budget increase, establishing it as a unified and robust foundation for future large-scale
L112@P2: audio foundation models.
L113@P2: 2 Rethinking Discrete Audio Tokenization for Future Audio Foundation Models
L114@P2: We rethink discrete audio tokenization from the perspective of autoregressive audio language modeling.
L115@P2: Analogous to text tokenizers in large language models, a discrete audio tokenizer should serve as a native
L116@P2: interface that bridges raw audio signals and autoregressive sequence modeling [14, 15, 50]. This viewpoint
L117@P2: places stringent requirements on the structure, representation capacity, and scalability of the tokenizer, beyond traditional objectives of audio compression or reconstruction.
L118@P2: From this perspective, we identify several key design principles that an audio tokenizer must satisfy in order
L119@P2: to effectively support autoregressive audio language models.
L120@P2: Unified Audio Representation. A tokenizer should provide a unified discrete representation capable of modeling and reconstructing diverse audio domains, including speech, sound, and music. Crucially, the resulting
L121@P2: tokens should preserve both fine-grained acoustic information and semantic structure, enabling them to
L122@P2: function as a meaningful sequence for autoregressive modeling rather than merely a compressed signal.
L123@P2: Simplicity and Scalability. To enable efficient scaling with model capacity, data, and computation, the tokenizer architecture should remain simple and homogeneous. Excessive architectural heterogeneity or reliance on specialized components can introduce fixed bottlenecks that hinder joint scaling and limit the
L124@P2: effectiveness of large-scale training.

L155@P3: 3 Causal Audio Tokenizer with Transformer (CAT)
L156@P3: 3.1 Homogeneous Architecture for Scalable Audio Tokenization
L157@P3: A central design goal of CAT is to enable scalable audio tokenization that can seamlessly integrate with
L158@P3: large autoregressive and multimodal foundation models. To this end, we adopt a CNN-free architecture
L159@P3: that is built entirely upon causal Transformer blocks, as illustrated in Figure 2. Compared to prior neural
L160@P3: audio codecs that rely heavily on convolutional inductive biases or hybrid CNN–Transformer designs, our
L161@P3: approach deliberately minimizes architectural specialization, favoring simplicity, uniformity, and scalability.
L162@P3: Fully Transformer-based encoder–decoder. Both the encoder and decoder in CAT are implemented as stacks of
L163@P3: causal Transformer blocks, forming a CNN-free architecture and enabling streaming encoding and decoding.
L164@P3: CAT operates directly on raw audio waveforms at both the input and output, avoiding intermediate signal
L165@P3: representations such as mel-spectrograms. The input waveform is first patchified into a sequence of fixeddimensional vectors and processed by the causal Transformer encoder. To progressively compress long audio
L166@P3: sequences into a compact representation, we insert patchify operations between Transformer blocks, which
L167@P3: gradually reduce the temporal resolution. As a result, the encoder maps 24 kHz waveforms into discrete
L168@P3: token sequences at an average rate of 12.5 frames per second. The decoder mirrors this process in reverse,
L169@P3: reconstructing the waveform from discrete tokens in a fully causal manner. Further implementation details
L170@P3: are provided in Appendix A.
L171@P3: Scalable residual vector quantization. For discretization, we employ residual vector quantization (RVQ). To
L172@P3: support robust modeling across a wide range of bitrates, we adopt 𝑁𝑞 = 32 residual quantization layers and
L173@P3: enable quantizer dropout during training. This variable-bitrate design directly facilitates the controllable
L174@P3: audio generation framework introduced later.

L254@P5: 3.3 Bitrate Controllable Audio Generation
L255@P5: End-to-end variable-bitrate autoregressive speech generation. Building on CAT, we construct CAT-TTS,
L256@P5: a fully end-to-end, purely autoregressive speech generation model that supports variable-bitrate synthesis.
L257@P5: The model directly generates speech from text tokens and a speaker prompt by predicting CAT’s RVQ tokens at a controllable depth, without requiring semantic disentanglement [50, 53, 54] or cascading multiple
L258@P5: generative models [39, 40, 44]. By leveraging CAT as a unified discrete interface, both linguistic content and
L259@P5: acoustic information are modeled within a single autoregressive framework.
L260@P5: Autoregressive modeling over RVQ tokens. Since CAT represents audio using residual vector quantization (RVQ), we adopt the Temporal Transformer + Depth Transformer architecture [15] for multi-stream
L261@P5: autoregressive modeling. The Temporal Transformer captures long-range dependencies along the temporal dimension, while the Depth Transformer models the coarse-to-fine residual structure across RVQ layers
L262@P5: within each time step. Under this design, each RVQ token conditions only on tokens from previous time
L263@P5: steps and on preceding RVQ layers at the current time step, ensuring strict causality without information
L264@P5: leakage.
L265@P5: Progressive Sequence Dropout. To enable robust generation across a wide range of bitrates within a single
L266@P5: model, we propose Progressive Sequence Dropout, a simple yet effective training strategy that requires no architectural modifications or additional parameters. During training, dropout is activated with probability
L267@P5: 𝑝. When activated, we uniformly sample a prefix length 𝐾 ∈ {1, . . . , 𝑁𝑞 − 1}, where 𝑁𝑞 denotes the total
L268@P5: number of RVQ layers, and discard RVQ tokens from layers 𝐾+1 to 𝑁𝑞 . Otherwise, all RVQ layers are retained. This strategy exposes the model to truncated RVQ prefixes during training, where a prefix length
L269@P5: is randomly sampled independently for each training sample, encouraging the model to learn conditional
L270@P5: generation under varying bitrates.

L315@P6: 4 Experiments
L316@P6: 4.1 Implementation Details
L317@P6: Building upon the CAT architecture, we develop MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, a large-scale audio tokenizer
L318@P6: featuring 1.6 billion parameters. The model utilizes a causal Transformer-based encoder–decoder paired
L319@P6: with hierarchical patching, which facilitates efficient streaming audio modeling. Discrete representations
L320@P6: are learned using a 32-layer residual vector quantizer with quantizer dropout to support variable-bitrate
L321@P6: tokenization. To encourage semantic alignment, we attach a decoder-only causal language model for audioto-text supervision.
