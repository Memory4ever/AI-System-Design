L205: ### 3.3 Second moment vs. variance normalization
L206: The preceding subsections showed robustness to outliers and heavy-tailed noise. We now consider a complementary low-variance regime common in later training, where gradients align closely with the signal. In this setting, we compare second-moment and variance-based normalizers via the update directions they induce. The second moment mixes signal and noise, scaling as $\mathbb{E}[g_{t}^{2}]=(\mathbb{E}[g_{t}])^{2}+\mathrm{Var}(g_{t})$, whereas the variance isolates the noise.
L207: When $\mathrm{Var}(g_{t})\ll(\mathbb{E}[g_{t}])^{2}$, second-moment normalization becomes overly conservative and collapses toward sign-based updates, while variance normalization preserves magnitude information up to an inverse-noise preconditioner.
L208: To formalize this intuition, we adopt a standard approximation from the analysis of exponential moving averages. Over the effective averaging window of length $(1-\beta_{2})^{-1}$, we assume the gradient distribution varies slowly, allowing the stochastic gradient to be decomposed into a deterministic signal and a noise term with slowly varying second-order statistics.
L209: Under this locally stationary approximation, the bias-corrected exponential moving averages act as estimators of the gradient’s second moment and variance. This assumption does not require global stationarity, only moment stability over the EMA window.
L210: ###### Assumption 3.1.
L211: 
L212: At iteration $t$, the stochastic gradient admits the decomposition $g_{t}=\nabla F(x_{t-1})+\xi_{t}$, where $\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\xi_{t,i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\approx\sigma_{i}^{2}$ remains approximately constant over the EMA window. The estimators satisfy $\mathbb{E}[v_{t,i}]\approx\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})^{2}+\sigma_{i}^{2}$ and $\mathbb{E}[s_{t,i}]\approx\sigma_{i}^{2}$.
L213: Under Assumption cite94†3.1 , both LaProp and MVN-Grad admit the coordinate-wise recursion $x_{t}=x_{t-1}-\eta\,u_{t}$, where $u_{t}=\beta_{1}u_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})z_{t}$, but they differ in the normalized direction $z_{t}$, i.e.,
L214: 
L215:  | $$z_{t}^{\mathrm{LP}}=\mathrm{sign}(g_{t})\,\sqrt{\frac{1}{(1+\frac{v_{t}-g_{t}^{2}}{g_{t}^{2}})}},\quad z_{t}^{\mathrm{MV}}=\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{s_{t}}}.$$  |
L216: Thus, the comparison reduces to characterizing these directions in the low-variance regime. To isolate the effect of the normalizer and suppress stochastic fluctuations, we formalize this regime as follows.
L217: ###### Assumption 3.2 (Small variance relative to signal).
L218: 
L219: Along the trajectory, $g_{t}\approx\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})$ and $\sigma_{i}^{2}\ll\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})^{2}$.
L220: 
L221: Under Assumptions cite94†3.1 –cite95†3.2 , the two normalization schemes admit distinct deterministic limits. We analyze them in turn. For LaProp, which uses second-moment normalization, the EMA satisfies $v_{t}\approx\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})^{2}$. As a result, the corrective factor obeys $\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+(v_{t}-g_{t}^{2})/g_{t}^{2}}}\approx 1,$
L222: and the normalized direction reduces to $z_{t}^{\mathrm{LP}}\approx\mathrm{sign}(g_{t})\approx\mathrm{sign}(\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1}))$. Thus, in the low-variance regime, LaProp behaves like a momentum-smoothed sign method and largely discards gradient magnitude information. In contrast, MVN-Grad uses variance normalization, for which $\hat{s}_{t}$ estimates the coordinate-wise variance $\sigma_{i}^{2}$.
L223: When this estimate is stable and strictly positive on the EMA time scale, the normalized direction satisfies $z_{t}^{\mathrm{MV}}=\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{s_{t}}}\approx\frac{\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})}{\sigma_{i}}$. Consequently, MVN-Grad preserves gradient magnitudes up to an inverse-noise preconditioner and avoids collapse to a sign direction. Although the above discussion applies broadly, focusing on smooth objectives allows a precise separation and sharp convergence rates in low-variance regimes.
L224: ###### Theorem 3.3.
L225: 
L226: Let $F$ be $L$-smooth and bounded below on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, and suppose Assumptions cite94†3.1 –cite95†3.2 hold. In addition, assume a high-SNR stochastic oracle: $g_{t}=\nabla F(x_{t-1})+\xi_{t}$ with $\E[\xi_{t}\!\mid\!\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0$, and for some $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $\sigma\geq 0$, $|\xi_{t,i}|\leq\delta\,|\nabla_{i}F(x_{t-1})|$ a.s. and $\E[\xi_{t,i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq\sigma^{2}$ $\forall i$.
L227: (i) (MVN-Grad, upper bound.) Assume isotropic noise $\sigma_{i}=\sigma>0$ for all $i$ and a stable variance estimate $s_{t}\approx\sigma^{2}\mathbf{1}$ along the trajectory, so that the limiting MVN-Grad direction satisfies $z_{t}^{\mathrm{MV}}\approx g_{t}/\sigma$. Define $\alpha:=\eta(1-\beta_{1})/\sigma$ and assume $\alpha\leq(1-\beta_{1}^{2})/L$. Then it suffices to take $T=\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^{2})$ iterations to ensure $\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\E\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon$.
L228: (ii) (LaProp, lower bound.) There exists an $L$-smooth function $F$ (satisfying the same assumptions and oracle model above) and an initialization such that, in the low-variance phase where $z_{t}^{\mathrm{LP}}\approx\sign(g_{t})$, the following holds. For LaProp with step size $\eta_{t}=\tfrac{c}{L\sqrt{t+1}}$, achieving $\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon$ requires $T=\Omega\!\left(\tfrac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)$ iterations.
L229: Theorem cite68†3.3 shows a sharp separation between variance-based and second-moment normalization in low-variance regimes. By comparing MVN-Grad and LaProp, two methods that share the same normalize-then-momentum structure, we isolate the effect of the normalizer itself. In this setting, variance normalization yields dimension-free convergence, whereas second-moment normalization induces sign-like dynamics and dimension-dependent slowdown.
L230: A similar separation holds for Adam and AdaBelief, though it is confounded by differences in update ordering.
L231: ## 4 Experiments
L232: We compare MVN-Grad with Adam, AdaBelief, and LaProp on two benchmarks: (i) image classification on CIFAR-100 using ResNet-18, and (ii) autoregressive language modeling with GPT-style Transformers on WikiText-103 and OpenWebText. Across all experiments, we fix the model architecture and all non-optimizer hyperparameters and tune only $(\eta,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})$ and, when applicable, the variance-normalization parameter $\varepsilon_{s}$.
L233: For each benchmark, we first perform a coarse sweep to identify stable learning-rate ranges, followed by a focused sweep over $(\eta,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})$ and $\varepsilon_{s}$ when applicable. We select the best configuration using one validation seed, then rerun it with multiple seeds and report mean $\pm$ std.

L294: ## 5 Conclusion
L295: 
L296: We proposed MVN-Grad, an Adam-style optimizer that applied momentum after variance-based normalization. Our analysis showed this ordering reduced conditional update variance and bounded spike sensitivity; in the small-variance (high-signal) regime it avoided sign-collapse and the resulting dimension-dependent slowdowns. On CIFAR-100 and GPT-style language modeling, MVN-Grad matched or improved over Adam/AdaBelief/LaProp with similar computational cost.

L303: ## Appendix A Extended related work: Adam-style optimizers
L304: #### A taxonomy of Adam variants.
L305: 
L306: Most Adam-style optimizers modify (i) the adaptivity statistic and its update rule, (ii) the coupling between numerator and denominator (including update ordering or temporal decorrelation), (iii) step-size control (e.g., bounds or variance rectification), (iv) practical regularization details (e.g., weight decay), and/or (v) systems constraints such as memory footprint or large-batch training.
L307: #### Convergence and stability fixes.
L308: 
L309: A key theoretical line identifies settings where Adam can fail and proposes convergent variants such as AMSGrad [cite57†RKK19 ]. Related stability-oriented modifications include controlling the growth of second-moment accumulators (Yogi [cite73†ZRSKK18 ]) and rectifying early-stage variance of the adaptive learning rate (RAdam [cite74†LJHCLGH19 ]).
L310: #### Decoupling momentum and adaptivity.
L311: 
L312: Several methods aim to reduce harmful interactions between historical momentum and stochastic normalization. LaProp [cite59†ZWU20 ] applies momentum after normalization to separate these effects, which is closely related in spirit to our focus on ordering. AdaShift [cite60†ZZLWZY18 ] uses temporally shifted statistics to decorrelate numerator and denominator.
L313: #### Alternative scaling rules.
L314: 
L315: Beyond second-moment normalization, AdaBelief [cite64†ZTDTDPD20 ] adapts step sizes using deviations from the predicted gradient. This line targets scaling behavior and can be viewed as variance-sensitive normalization.
L316: #### Practical refinements and generalization-motivated variants.
L317: 
L318: AdamW [cite75†LH+17 ] decouples weight decay from the gradient step. Memory and implementation constraints motivated AdaFactor [cite76†SS18 ] and SM3 [cite112†AGKS19 ]. Other variants aim to mitigate the adaptivity–generalization gap or extreme effective learning rates via bounded/partial adaptivity, including AdaBound [cite113†LXLS19 ] and Padam [cite114†CZTYCG18 ]. Further modifications address scale-invariant weights (AdamP [cite115†HCOHYKUH20 ]).
L319: #### Large-batch and large-scale training.
L320: 
L321: Layer-wise adaptations such as LAMB [cite116†YLRHKBSDKH19 ] and methods like NovoGrad [cite117†GCHKLLLNZC19 ] target stability and efficiency in large-batch regimes.
L322: #### Interpretations of Adam and sign-like behavior.
L323: 
L324: Several works interpret Adam as combining sign-like directions with variance-dependent magnitudes [cite62†BH18 ], and analyze generalization differences between adaptive methods and SGD [cite56†WRSSR17 ]. These perspectives connect directly to the sign-collapse phenomenon discussed in Section cite14†3.3 .
L325: #### Beyond diagonal second-moment preconditioning.
L326: 
L327: Recent optimizers incorporate curvature information using inexpensive diagonal Hessian estimates and clipping, including Sophia [cite77†LLHLM23 ] and related diagonal-curvature methods such as AdaHessian [cite78†YGSMKM21 ]. We include these for context, though they go beyond strictly Adam-style diagonal second-moment preconditioning. Related preconditioning methods include Shampoo [cite118†GKS18 ] and Muon [cite119†JJBJCNB ].

L348: ### B.1 Proof of Theorem cite66†3.1 L349: 
L350: ###### Proof.
L351: 
L352: Fix an iteration $t$ and define
L353: 
L354:  | $$Y_{t}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{t}}+\varepsilon},\qquad X_{t}:=\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{s_{t}}+\varepsilon}=g_{t}\,Y_{t}.$$  |
L355: 
L356: We can write the updates as
L357: 
L358:  | $$\Delta_{t}^{AB}\;=\;\beta_{1}m_{t-1}Y_{t}+(1-\beta_{1})X_{t},$$  |
L359: 
L360: and, since MVN-Grad applies momentum after normalization,
L361: 
L362:  | $$\Delta_{t}^{MV}\;=\;\beta_{1}u_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})X_{t},$$  |
L363: 
L364: where $u_{t-1}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable.
L365: #### Conditional variance gap identity.
L366: 
L367: Because $m_{t-1}$ and $u_{t-1}$ are $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable, we have
L368:  | $\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\Delta_{t}^{AB}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]$  | $\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\beta_{1}m_{t-1}Y_{t}+(1-\beta_{1})X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]$  |
L369:  |  | $\displaystyle=\beta_{1}^{2}m_{t-1}^{2}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+(1-\beta_{1})^{2}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]$  |
L370:  |  | $\displaystyle\quad+2\beta_{1}(1-\beta_{1})m_{t-1}\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right],$  |
L371: 
L372: and
L373:  | $$\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\Delta_{t}^{MV}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=\mathrm{Var}\!\left[\beta_{1}u_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=(1-\beta_{1})^{2}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].$$  |
L374: 
L375: Subtracting yields
L376: 
L377:  | $$\Delta\mathrm{Var}_{t}=\beta_{1}^{2}m_{t-1}^{2}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+2\beta_{1}(1-\beta_{1})m_{t-1}\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].$$  |  | (3)
L378: #### Evaluate the covariance term via symmetry.
L379: 
L380: Let $z:=g_{t}-m_{t-1}.$ By assumption, conditional on $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ the random variable $z$ is symmetric and centered $\E[z\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0$. Moreover, using the recursion $m_{t}=\beta_{1}m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})g_{t}$, we have
L381: 
L382:  | $$g_{t}-m_{t}=\beta_{1}(g_{t}-m_{t-1})=\beta_{1}z.$$  |
L383: 
L384: Since $s_{t}=\beta_{2}s_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})(g_{t}-m_{t})^{2}$, it follows that
L385:  | $$s_{t}=\beta_{2}s_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})\beta_{1}^{2}z^{2},\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad Y_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{2}s_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})\beta_{1}^{2}z^{2}}+\varepsilon}.$$  |
L386: 
L387: Therefore $Y_{t}$ is an even function of $z$, while $zY_{t}$ and $zY_{t}^{2}$ are odd functions of $z$. Also,
L388: 
L389:  | $$X_{t}=g_{t}Y_{t}=(z+m_{t-1})Y_{t}.$$  |
L390: 
L391: Thus,
L392:  | $\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]$  | $\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},(z+m_{t-1})Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]$  |
L393:  |  | $\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},zY_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+m_{t-1}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].$  |
L394: 
L395: To handle the first term, note that
L396: 
L397:  | $$\E[zY_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0\quad\text{and}\quad\E[zY_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0$$  |
L398: because $zY_{t}$ and $zY_{t}^{2}$ are odd in the (conditionally) symmetric variable $z$. Hence
L399: 
L400:  | $$\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},zY_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=\E[zY_{t}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]-\E[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\,\E[zY_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0,$$  |
L401: 
L402: and therefore
L403: 
L404:  | $$\mathrm{Cov}\!\left[Y_{t},X_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=m_{t-1}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].$$  |
L405: #### Conclude.
