L288: ##### Optimal policies exist but are not informative.
L289: In deterministic finite-horizon MDPs, an optimal policy ($\pi^{*}$ that attains the maximum value $V^{*}$) exists in very large classes (e.g., measurable policies). This is classical (demonstrated in cite59†Appendix C ), and mathematically useful (it justifies an “optimal value” benchmark), but it is not directly constructive for theorem proving. Our focus is therefore on realistic restricted classes (e.g., Lipschitz policies) and on distributional guarantees under data generation models.
L290: ## 3 Data generation models: a base distribution and a latent proof-DAG distribution
L291: 
L292: A central premise of this work is distributional analysis: worst-case theorem proving is intractable, but empirical benchmarks are highly non-uniform. We therefore introduce explicit data distributions.
L293: ### 3.1 Cut-free baseline distribution induced by a reference policy $q$
L294: 
L295: Fix a reference stochastic policy $q(a\mid x)$ (i.e., any proposal distribution such as a pretrained LLM, or random draws from theorem libraries such as Mathlib (cite153†The mathlib community, 2020 )). Running $q$ in the deterministic prover yields trajectories (proof attempts)
L296: 
L297:  | $$y=(x_{0},a_{0},x_{1},a_{1},\dots,x_{T}),\qquad x_{t+1}=F(x_{t},a_{t}).$$  |
L298: Conditioning on success within a truncation length $L$ (or horizon $T$) induces a success-filtered distribution over theorem instances and proof traces:
L299: 
L300:  | $$Q_{\mathrm{tree}}(dx,dy)\ \propto\ \Big(\text{rollout of }q\text{ producing a successful trace }y\text{ from }x\text{ within }L\Big).$$  |
L301: The parameter $L$ controls the average proof length under $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$. For theoretical constructions one may also use success-conditioned sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) / Doob-transform style sampling to generate successful traces without rejection (details deferred to cite63†Appendix D ).
L302: ### 3.2 Cut-structured distributions via latent proof DAGs
L303: 
L304: To model lemma/cut/sketch structure, we introduce a latent-variable model where the latent variable is a proof DAG $Z$. We write
L305: 
L306:  |  | $\displaystyle p_{\theta}^{+}(x,y,z)\propto p_{\theta}(x)\,p_{\theta}(z\mid x)\,p_{\theta}(y\mid x,z)\,\mathbf{1}\{\mathsf{Succ}(x,y,z)\},$  |
L307:  |  | $\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{DAG}}(dx,dy)=\int p_{\theta^{\star}}^{+}(x,y,z)\,dz.$  |
L308: Here $Z$ captures reusable subgoals and their decomposition structure (depth-labeled DAG), while $y$ is an observed cut-free trace (or decision-point log) generated by expanding $Z$ into a tree and filling in low-level solver steps. The truncation by $\mathbf{1}_{\mathsf{Succ}(x,y,z)}$ ensures the distribution is supported on successful objects. The latent model compactly expresses a wide family of “proof-friendly” inductive biases via a small set of interpretable parameters, including:
L309: 
L310:   * •
L311: $D$: maximum decomposition depth,
L312: 
L313:   * •
L314: 
L315: $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$: effective branching of unique subgoals (after sharing),
L316: 
L317:   * •
L318: 
L319: $\alpha\in(0,1)$: contraction rate of local proof complexity under decomposition (shorter low-level proofs at deeper levels).
L320: 
L321: The main theorems below do not depend on a particular parametric form of $p_{\theta}(z\mid x)$ beyond the induced decision counts and margin conditions.
L322: ##### From DAGs to observed traces.
L323: 
L324: The observation model $p_{\theta}(y\mid x,z)$ can be read as “unfolding” the DAG into a cut-free proof trace (or a collection of local traces), e.g. by expanding reused lemmas into repeated subproofs. This connects directly to cut elimination: cut-free proofs correspond to (possibly much larger) trees obtained by unfolding a shared DAG.
L325: ## 4 Policies, learning rules, and search rules
L326: 
L327: We study two prover pipelines that correspond to two ways of interacting with cut structure.
L328: 
L329: ### 4.1 Flat (cut-free) model: ERM + top-$k$ search
L330: 
L331: The flat baseline ignores latent cut structure and learns a single policy $\pi_{\mathrm{flat}}\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}$ from cut-free data (e.g., from $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$ or from unfolded traces under $Q_{\mathrm{DAG}}$).
L332: ##### Learning (ERM).
L333: 
L334: Given i.i.d. data from distribution $Q$, we learn a single policy $\pi_{\mathrm{flat}}\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}$ by empirical risk minimization under log-loss:
L335: 
L336:  | $$\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}}\in\arg\min_{\pi\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}}\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,a)\in\mathcal{D}}-\log\pi(a\mid x),$$  |
L337: where $\mathcal{D}$ is the collection of decision-point pairs extracted from the traces. Because states depend on past actions through the deterministic kernel, the effective sample sequence is sequential; generalization is controlled by sequential Rademacher/covering complexity of $H$ (details in cite105†Section G.2 ).
L338: ##### Search (top-$k$).
L339: At test time, we perform top-$k$ proof search: at each state $x$, propose the $k$ highest-probability actions under $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\cdot\mid x)$ (restricted to a finite candidate set), try them with verifier feedback, and backtrack as needed. The key analytical event is a coverage event: the existence of a successful proof whose required actions always lie within the proposed top-$k$ lists.
L340: In our analysis, we assume a Tsybakov-type margin condition (explained below) on the policy’s ranking of the correct action among candidates, which converts one-step imitation error (mixture KL) into a misranking probability and then into a finite-horizon success bound.
L341: #### 4.1.1 Top-$k$ margin condition (Tsybakov-type).
L342: Our top-$k$ analysis relies on a margin condition that controls how stable the correct action ranking is under small policy perturbations. Fix a finite candidate set $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{cand}}(x)\subset\mathcal{A}$ at each state $x$, and let $q(\cdot\mid x)$ denote the reference (data-generating) action distribution restricted to $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{cand}}(x)$. Write $q_{(1)}(x)\geq q_{(2)}(x)\geq\cdots$ for the descending order statistics of $\{q(a\mid x):a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{cand}}(x)\}$.
L343: We define the top-$k$ margin (gap) at $x$ by
L344:  | $$\Delta_{k}(x):=q_{(k)}(x)-q_{(k+1)}(x),$$  |
L345: with the convention $q_{(k+1)}(x)=0$ if $|\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{cand}}(x)|=k$. Intuitively, large $\Delta_{k}(x)$ means the $k$-th and $(k\!+\!1)$-th candidates are well separated under $q$, so the top-$k$ set is robust to estimation error; small $\Delta_{k}(x)$ indicates near-ties, where small deviations can flip the top-$k$ membership.
L346: The Tsybakov-type margin condition postulates that near-ties are rare under the decision-point distribution: there exist constants $C>0$ and $\beta>0$ such that for all $u>0$,
L347:  | $$\mathbb{P}\big(\Delta_{k}(X)\leq u\big)\leq C\,u^{\beta},$$  |
L348: 
L349: where $X$ is a random decision state drawn from the induced mixture distribution over decision points (decomposition, solver, or flat, depending on the context). This assumption converts one-step imitation error (e.g. mixture KL) into a bound on the probability that a learned policy misranks the correct action outside the top-$k$ list, yielding the characteristic exponent $p=\beta/(\beta+2)$ in our success probability bounds.
L350: ### 4.2 Hierarchical (cut-aware) model: EM + subgoal decomposition + top-$k$
L351: 
L352: The hierarchical model explicitly separates high-level and low-level decisions:
L353: 
L354:   * •
L355: 
L356: a decomposition policy $\pi_{\mathrm{dec}}\in H_{\mathrm{dec}}$ proposes cuts/lemmas/sketches or other subgoal decompositions;
L357: 
L358:   * •
L359: 
L360: a solver policy $\pi_{\mathrm{sol}}\in H_{\mathrm{sol}}$ closes terminal subgoals produced by decomposition.
L361: Operationally, the prover alternates between (i) choosing a decomposition that produces reusable subgoals and (ii) solving the resulting subgoals, with memoization on canonicalized goals (sharing).
L362: ##### Learning (EM/VAE-style alternating updates).
L363: 
L364: Because the decomposition structure is not observed directly, we treat $Z$ as latent and use an inference model (variational posterior) $r_{\phi}(z\mid x,y)$. Learning proceeds by alternating:
L365: 
L366:   * •
L367: 
L368: E-step: update an amortized $\phi$ to fit $r_{\phi}$ by maximizing an ELBO;
L369: 
L370:   * •
L371: 
L372: M-step / policy step: update $(\pi_{\mathrm{dec}},\pi_{\mathrm{sol}})$ by posterior-weighted ERM, i.e.,
L373:  | $$\min_{\pi_{\mathrm{dec}},\pi_{\mathrm{sol}}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim r_{\phi}(\cdot\mid x^{(i)},y^{(i)})}\big[\mathcal{J}(\pi_{\mathrm{dec}},\pi_{\mathrm{sol}};x^{(i)},y^{(i)},z)\big],$$  |
L374: 
L375: where $\mathcal{J}$ is the sum of log losses over decomposition and solver decision points induced by $z$.
L376: Sequential Rademacher/covering complexity yields generalization bounds for this posterior-weighted ERM (treating $r_{\phi}$ as fixed during the policy update), and the inference mismatch is controlled by the expected posterior KL $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{KL}(r_{\phi}\|p(z\mid x,y))]$, i.e., the ELBO gap.
L377: ##### Search (top-$k$ at both levels).
L378: The hierarchical prover uses top-$k$ search at both levels: it proposes $k_{\mathrm{dec}}$ decomposition candidates and $k_{\mathrm{sol}}$ solver actions for subgoals, with verifier feedback and backtracking. The resulting success bound splits into two additive contributions (decomposition vs. solver), reflecting a balance between high-level structural exploration and low-level proof search.
L379: This decomposition is particularly important when the latent model implies that solver complexity contracts with depth (parameter $\alpha$) while the number of unique subgoals grows with $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
L380: ##### Why hierarchy can be exponentially better.
L381: Under cut structure, the latent DAG may have size $O(\lambda^{D})$ in depth $D$ (unique subgoals with sharing), whereas full cut elimination can unfold it into a cut-free tree of size $\Omega(\Lambda^{D})$ with $\Lambda>\lambda$.
L382: Since our success guarantees amplify one-step errors linearly (explained later) in the number of required decision points, the flat (cut-free) learner must drive per-step errors much lower to compensate, resulting in exponentially larger sample requirements under standard learning curves. This yields a theoretical justification for subgoal decomposition as a distributionally rational inductive bias.
L383: ## 5 Main Results Overview
L384: 
L385: We state the core assumptions and three main theorems at a high level. Each theorem is formulated as a lower bound on finite-horizon success probability (goal-reaching probability) and is designed to cleanly separate (i) intrinsic search structure and (ii) learnable one-step imitation errors. Proofs and details are deferred to appendices: cite68†Appendices E , cite77†F and cite114†H .
L386: 
L387: ### 5.1 Assumptions
L388: ###### Assumption 1 (Deterministic finite-horizon MDP).
L389: 
L390: Transitions are deterministic ($X_{t+1}=F(X_{t},A_{t})$), success is reaching $G$ within horizon $T$.
L391: 
L392: ###### Assumption 2 (Compact metric embeddings and Lipschitz policies).
L393: 
L394: $(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})$ and $(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}})$ are compact metric spaces; policies are Lipschitz in $x$.
L395: ###### Assumption 3 (Top-$k$ search with finite candidate sets and completeness).
L396: 
L397: Top-$k$ is taken over a finite candidate set; the search is complete w.r.t. these lists.
L398: ###### Assumption 4 (Tsybakov-type top-$k$ margin condition).
L399: 
L400: Let $\Delta_{k}(S)$ be the generator top-$k$ gap at state $S$ (difference between the $k$-th and $(k+1)$-th probabilities under the reference generator on the candidate set). We assume
L401: 
L402:  | $$\mathbb{P}(\Delta_{k}(S)\leq u)\leq C\,u^{\beta}\qquad(u>0),$$  |
L403: under the appropriate decision-point mixture distribution. This converts one-step imitation errors (e.g. mixture KL) into top-$k$ misranking probabilities with exponent $p=\beta/(\beta+2)$.
L404: ###### Assumption 5 (Sequential generalization (sequential Rademacher/covering)).
L405: 
L406: For ERM under log-loss on sequentially generated decision points, we control the excess risk via sequential Rademacher complexity, which is further bounded by a Dudley-type integral of sequential covering numbers. This yields explicit rates in terms of metric entropy (e.g. doubling dimension) under Lipschitz hypotheses.
L407: ###### Assumption 6 (Latent inference quality (hierarchical only)).
L408: 
L409: The mismatch between the variational posterior $r_{\phi}(z\mid x,y)$ and the true posterior is controlled by the expected posterior KL, equivalently the expected ELBO gap.
L410: 
L411: ###### Assumption 7 (Cut-elimination blow-up (for separation)).
L412: 
L413: For cut-structured problems, fully eliminating cuts expands a depth-$D$ proof DAG into a cut-free proof tree with an exponentially larger effective decision count.
L414: 
L415: ### 5.2 Flat (cut-free) learning
L416: ##### Setting.
L417: 
L418: Data are generated from the cut-free distribution $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$ induced by a reference policy $q$ (success-filtered). A flat policy $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}}\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}$ is learned by ERM on cut-free traces, and test-time search is top-$k$ with completeness and finite candidate sets.
L419: ###### Theorem 1 (informal; Statistical provability lowerbound of flat (cut-free) learning).
L420: 
L421: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 and cite158†5 , there exists $\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(C,\beta)$ such that
L422:  | $$\mathbb{E}\big[V^{\mathrm{flat}\text{-}\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}(T,X)\big]\ \geq\ 1-K_{\mathrm{flat}}\Big(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k}(S)]}_{\text{intrinsic top-$k$ mass loss}}+\underbrace{\tilde{C}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}})^{p}}_{\text{learned misranking}}\Big),$$  |
L423: 
L424: where $K_{\mathrm{flat}}$ is the expected number of decision points along a successful proof under $Q_{\mathrm{tree}}$, $m_{k}(S)$ is the generator top-$k$ mass at state $S$, and $p=\beta/(\beta+2)$.
L425: Moreover, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
L426: 
L427:  | $$\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{flat}})\ \leq\ \inf_{\pi\in H_{\mathrm{flat}}}\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\pi)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}(n,T,\delta;H_{\mathrm{flat}}),$$  |
L428: 
L429: where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}$ is controlled by sequential Rademacher/covering complexity (cite158†Assumption 5 ).
L430: ##### Interpretation.
L431: 
L432: Success probability degrades linearly with effective proof length $K_{\mathrm{flat}}$ and improves with (i) larger intrinsic top-$k$ mass, (ii) stronger margins (larger $\beta$), and (iii) smaller one-step imitation error (more data / simpler class). Thus, even as a lower bound, the result highlights that reducing effective proof length is a principled route to higher success.
L433: 
L434: ### 5.3 Hierarchical (cut-aware) learning
L435: ##### Setting.
L436: 
L437: Data are generated from the cut-structured distribution $Q_{\mathrm{DAG}}$ with latent proof DAG $Z$. We learn a decomposition policy $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{dec}}\in H_{\mathrm{dec}}$ and a solver policy $\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}\in H_{\mathrm{sol}}$ via EM/VAE-style alternation: variational inference $r_{\phi}(z\mid x,y)$ and posterior-weighted ERM. Test-time search uses top-$k$ at both levels with completeness and finite candidate sets.
L438: ###### Theorem 2 (informal; Statistical provability lowerbound of Hierarchical (cut-aware) learning).
L439: 
L440: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 , cite158†5 and cite159†6 , and the margin conditions for both decision types, there exist $\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dec}},\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{sol}}$ such that
L441:  | $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[V^{\mathrm{hier}\text{-}\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}(T,X)\big]\ \geq\ 1$  | $\displaystyle-K_{\mathrm{dec}}\Big(\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\mathrm{dec}}(S)]+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dec}}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{dec}})^{p_{\mathrm{dec}}}\Big)$  |
L442:  |  | $\displaystyle-K_{\mathrm{sol}}\Big(\mathbb{E}[1-m_{k_{\mathrm{sol}}}^{\mathrm{sol}}(S)]+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{sol}}\,\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{sol}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{sol}})^{p_{\mathrm{sol}}}\Big),$  |
L443: where $K_{\mathrm{dec}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{sol}}$ are the expected numbers of high-level and low-level decisions along a successful hierarchical proof, and $\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}$ denotes the one-step mixture KL under the true success-filtered posterior decision distribution.
L444: 
L445: Furthermore, with probability at least $1-\delta$, each one-step KL admits the three-term decomposition
L446:  | $$\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}(\hat{\pi}_{\mathrm{typ}})\ \leq\ \underbrace{\inf_{\pi\in H_{\mathrm{typ}}}\bar{\mathrm{KL}}_{\mathrm{typ}}^{\star}(\pi)}_{\text{approximation}}+\underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seq}}(n,T,\delta;H_{\mathrm{typ}})}_{\text{sequential estimation}}+\underbrace{O\!\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{PostKL}(\phi)}\right)}_{\text{inference (ELBO gap)}},\qquad\mathrm{typ}\in\{\mathrm{dec},\mathrm{sol}\}.$$  |
L447: ##### Interpretation.
L448: The hierarchical guarantee decomposes additively into a high-level and a low-level term, reflecting the need to balance decomposition quality with local solving quality. The bound makes explicit how latent inference (ELBO tightness), sequential generalization, and margins jointly control success. Structural parameters such as $(D,\alpha,b_{\mathrm{eff}})$ influence success through the induced counts $K_{\mathrm{dec}},K_{\mathrm{sol}}$ (e.g. contraction $\alpha$ can reduce low-level workload).
L449: ### 5.4 Exponential separation (cut-aware vs cut-free on cut-structured problems)
L450: 
L451: ##### Setting.
L452: 
L453: Consider the same cut-structured problem distribution $Q_{\mathrm{DAG}}$. Compare:
L454: 
L455:   * •
L456: 
L457: Cut-aware hierarchical learning/search: cite160†Theorem 2 .
L458: 
L459:   * •
L460: 
L461: Cut-free flat learning/search: first eliminate cuts (expand the proof DAG into a cut-free tree), then learn a single flat policy and run top-$k$ search as in cite161†Theorem 1 .
L462: ##### Structural blow-up assumption.
L463: 
L464: cite162†Assumption 7 , there exist constants $\Lambda>1$ and $\lambda\geq 1$ with $\lambda\ll\Lambda$ such that the effective decision counts satisfy
L465: 
L466:  | $$K_{\mathrm{flat}}\ \geq\ c\,\Lambda^{D},\qquad K_{\mathrm{hier}}:=K_{\mathrm{dec}}+K_{\mathrm{sol}}\ \leq\ C\,\lambda^{D}.$$  |
L467: ###### Theorem 3 (informal; Exponential separation (cut-aware vs cut-free on cut-structured problems)).
L468: Under cite154†Assumptions 1 , cite155†2 , cite156†3 , cite157†4 , cite158†5 , cite159†6 and cite162†7 , (and matching margin/learning-curve assumptions), to achieve the same target success level $1-\delta$, a cut-free flat learner requires exponentially more data/resources than a cut-aware hierarchical learner. Concretely, if one-step learning follows $\bar{\mathrm{KL}}\lesssim N^{-\gamma}$ and the margin exponent is $p\in(0,1)$, then sufficient sample sizes obey
L469:  | $$N_{\mathrm{flat}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{flat}}}{\delta}\right)^{1/(p\gamma)},\qquad N_{\mathrm{hier}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{hier}}}{\delta}\right)^{1/(p\gamma)},$$  |
L470: 
L471: hence
L472: 
L473:  | $$\frac{N_{\mathrm{flat}}}{N_{\mathrm{hier}}}\ \gtrsim\ \left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{D/(p\gamma)}.$$  |
L474: ##### Interpretation.
L475: When proofs have reusable cut/lemma/sketch structure, explicitly modeling and searching over this structure (subgoal decomposition + memoization) reduces the effective number of required decisions from $\Omega(\Lambda^{D})$ to $O(\lambda^{D})$, yielding an exponential improvement in the learning burden required to reach a fixed success probability.
L476: This provides theoretical support for hierarchical subgoal decomposition mechanisms used in modern agentic theorem provers, without contradicting worst-case hardness: the gain is distributional and hinges on the presence of reusable structure in the problem distribution.
L477: ## 6 Conclusion
L478: We presented a theoretical framework for LLM-guided formal theorem proving as a finite-horizon deterministic MDP over compact metric state/action spaces, together with distributional assumptions that capture “proof-friendly” structure.
L479: Modeling cut/lemma/sketch reuse via a latent proof DAG yields success guarantees for hierarchical top-$k$ search in which (i) search and learning contributions separate cleanly and (ii) learning is controlled by sequential complexity measures even in the presence of latent structure.
L480: Most importantly, when cut elimination provably expands reusable DAG structure into exponentially larger cut-free trees, we obtain an exponential separation: cut-aware hierarchical learning and search can achieve a given success level with exponentially fewer samples than any flat cut-free approach under the same margin-driven analysis.
L481: This provides a principled explanation for why subgoal decomposition, despite being a difficult auxiliary task, can be a statistically and computationally rational design choice in modern agentic theorem provers.
