[h1] CAGE: Certified Authorization under Typed-Return Uncertainty
[p] Tool-using LLM agents act on typed tool returns, records pairing provenance and categorical fields with numerical values. Runtime permission gates generally authorize the observed return and action, leaving the decision unprotected against small errors in how the return was bound to its source. We ask whether a candidate action stays authorized over a declared neighborhood of plausible correctly bound returns: one admissible binding fault plus bounded numerical drift. We prove that certifying the categorical and numerical channels separately does not compose: perturbations that are safe on each channel alone can jointly turn the same action unsafe. CAGE certifies this joint neighborhood directly, enumerating the discrete branches exactly and certifying the continuous perturbation within each branch. Across synthetic, policy-as-code, regulatory, and real-transaction settings, CAGE removes the in-budget false allows that accurate pointwise gates admit, while keeping a useful fraction of decisions autonomous. When the policy is executable, CAGE-Exact certifies the policy itself; otherwise CAGE-Lip and CAGE-RS certify a learned gate under an explicit, measured fidelity assumption.

[h2] Introduction
[p] LLM agents operate in a loop (reason, emit a structured tool request, observe, continue ), so tool observations become inputs to future actions: external bytes influence control flow . Deployed agent harnesses increasingly place a runtime permission gate between the model and tool execution ; current gates are heuristic, pre-execution, and defined over the proposed call: they decide whether it may run, and leave open whether the agent may safely act on what it returns. Existing defenses concentrate on the untrusted text channel: sanitizing or spotlighting tool outputs , structured prompting and instruction hierarchies , and information-flow control by design ; these act on the text or data-flow channel, complementary to the typed authorization decision we certify (§).
[p] Existing runtime hooks can host the required decision, but permission gates, runtime enforcers, and LLM judges all evaluate the realized return–action pair pointwise. We change the object of authorization from the observed point to the joint neighborhood : a candidate action is authorized only when it is safe for every correctly bound return in .
[p] We therefore study the node , where is a typed tool return and is a candidate downstream action; the LLM may still propose , and the runtime gate decides whether executing it is authorized under a specified perturbation model, abstention routing the residual to human review.
[p] The central vulnerability is a joint-gap attack: a return-action pair may be clean-safe at the observed point and pass discrete-only and continuous-only checks, yet fail under a joint perturbation, because the discrete part of the return changes the semantics of the numerical fields, as in the screening example above. We call such points (category of the oracle taxonomy): safe at the observed point and under each marginal check, yet unsafe for some . The defense, our monitor (Certified Authorization Gate for Execution), certifies the actual joint perturbation set: enumerate the finite discrete neighborhood exactly and certify each continuous branch with a sound backend. When the policy is executable, CAGE-Exact certifies it directly; otherwise, CAGE-Lip or CAGE-RS certifies a learned gate under a measured fidelity assumption (Table ).
[h4] Contributions.
[p] We formalize authorization after a tool return as a decision under bounded semantic uncertainty: the runtime observes a validated record , but the correctly bound return may be any . Proposition establishes that return-dependent safety requires reading the realized return ( of matched pairs, Section ); CAGE replaces pointwise evaluation with certification over the full joint neighborhood (Figure ).
[p] We prove separate certificates for the categorical and numerical channels do not imply safety over their Cartesian product (Theorem ): joint-gap witnesses are safe at the observed point and under each marginal perturbation, yet unsafe for a combined move; the witness interval has length and the empirical witness frequency follows this geometry (Section ).
[p] CAGE enumerates exactly and certifies each branch by a backend from an assumption ladder, with a backend-independent abstention floor (Proposition ). For the learned rungs, the certificate is over the gate; it is policy-sound under an explicit gate–policy fidelity condition, measured throughout the evaluation.
[p] We calibrate the discrete and continuous budgets from injected return-assembly faults, derive a deployment-specific freshness limit, and evaluate adaptive attacks and policy shift. The formal guarantee is separated from its operational preconditions and residual risk; external-validity claims concern existence, mechanism, and frequency within the evaluated settings, and field prevalence is not measured.

[h4] Runtime enforcement, verification, and what stays uncertified.
[p] Two axes separate our node from recent agent-security work (Figure ): whether a defense reads the typed pair , and whether it is under bounded uncertainty between the validated record and its correctly bound return. A growing line of reads the action and checks it against a policy but offers no uniform guarantee over bounded semantic return uncertainty: VeriGuard formally verifies a synthesized policy and runs a per-action monitor ; AgentSpec enforces trigger/predicate/enforcement rules with no trajectorial analysis ; PFI and SAFEFLOW import OS-style isolation and information-flow control . These runtime defenses evaluate the observed action–state pair and do not provide a certificate uniform over ; CAGE provides this uniform neighborhood certificate, while the runtime placement follows this enforcement line. The certified line targets corruptions, adversarial tool (ToolCert ) and poisoning (TPA ); neither addresses certification over at inference, an open surface in a recent SoK . Beyond agent security, access-control policy verification , attribute-based access control , runtime shielding , and robust optimization verify, enforce, or optimize the policy itself; CAGE certifies the authorization over at inference. Our safety predicate follows the oracle-function formalization of agent security , realized with a typed gate, since LLM judges measure adversarial robustness no better than chance under shift .

[h2] Discussion, Scope, and Limitations
[p] CAGE formulates authorization over typed tool returns as a joint-neighborhood decision. The non-composition result identifies pairs that are safe under each marginal check yet unsafe under a combined binding and numerical deviation. Across policy-engine, regulatory, and real-transaction settings, the joint certificates remove measured in-budget policy false allows while retaining useful autonomy; CAGE-Exact is policy-certified on the verified fragment, CAGE-Lip and CAGE-RS extend it to learned gates under an explicit gate–policy fidelity condition.
[p] The guarantee applies to a single authorization decision and assumes a validated typed-return constructor, complete mediation, an enumerable discrete neighborhood, and continuous uncertainty within the calibrated freshness and numerical budget; budget-escape (, Figure ) and constructor-corruption () measurements quantify what happens when these conditions weaken. Cross-turn accumulation, tool selection, prompt-injection resistance, MCP metadata, and multi-step execution remain uncertified; boundary-seeking traffic can inflate abstention but never converts it into a false allow (Table S41); learned backends require continued gate–policy auditing (false-allow at ; Table S43).
[p] The results support joint-neighborhood authorization as a runtime control wherever typed-return uncertainty can be measured and enforced. Further work should measure provenance-conditioned policies and binding faults in the field, extend the certificate to sequential decisions, and tie calibration to live validation infrastructure. CAGE therefore provides a calibrated authorization mechanism for decisions whose safety depends on uncertain typed returns.

[h2] Extended background
[h4] Tool-using agents and harness boundaries.
[p] An LLM agent runs a thinkact-via-toolobserve loop. The tool has two faces: the executable function (shell command, HTTP request, SQL query, file edit, payment API, monitoring endpoint), which can cause side effects, and the representation shown to the model (name, description, JSON schema), which can be poisoned, especially when tools are discovered dynamically through protocols such as MCP . In Claude Code the model emits structured tool-use blocks while the harness validates, checks permissions, dispatches tools, optionally sandboxes execution, and stores transcripts ; the safety layer includes rule-based permission checks, hooks, an auto-mode classifier, built-in and MCP tools, sandboxing, and subagents. The right object to certify is thus the runtime decision boundary exposed by the harness rather than the raw LLM.
[h4] MCP, tool poisoning, and indirect prompt injection.
[p] Indirect prompt injection differs from direct injection: malicious instructions enter through third-party content (emails, web pages, retrieved documents, tool outputs, tool descriptions, or agent-to-agent messages) , are read by the model, and influence future privileged actions, as quantified by dedicated benchmarks . MCP-style discovery expands the surface: an external server may publish tool descriptions that become part of the agent context, so tool-metadata poisoning is close to RAG poisoning over tool descriptions, with the added consequence that selected tools have real side effects . We target a different stage: a tool has already returned a typed object, and the question is whether the agent may act on it.
[h4] Permission classifiers and their limits.
[p] Deployed permission systems are mostly pre-execution: Claude Code auto mode classifies proposed tool calls into safe, unsafe, or manual-approval, with a three-tier structure (read-only auto-allowed, in-project edits may bypass the classifier, shell/external actions passed to a transcript classifier), and a stress test reports high false-negative rates on ambiguous DevOps tasks plus a coverage gap where the same state-changing effect is reachable through an unevaluated tool path . Safety thus depends on where the boundary sits as much as on classifier accuracy: a classifier over tool calls does not certify whether a later decision on a returned object is robust.
[h2] Fault mechanisms and a worked example
[p] Throughout, the typed constructor, schema validation, and the integrity and freshness checks constitute the (TCB) assumed by the certificate: the guarantee is conditional on this base, and Section 6.5 measures what happens as it weakens (constructor corruption, freshness escape).
[p] The discrete budget models a single admissible fault rather than arbitrary string rewriting: may be a stale provenance tag, a schema-version mismatch, a confused policy pack, a multiplexed return attributed to the wrong tool, or a TOCTOU race between an environment label and the numerical fields. The continuous budget models bounded distortion of operational quantities that remain subject to range checks, unit normalization, plausibility filters, signed telemetry, or partial integrity checks, so captures the residual uncertainty left after ordinary validation rather than free choice of values. Section 6.5 injects seven such faults (provenance/policy-pack/TOCTOU swaps, stale cache reads, schema skew, key collisions, numeric jitter, normalization skew) and measures the induced ; re-read jitter () and normalizer skew () are covered fully, while the faults misses (schema transposition, key collisions) are exactly those a schema/identity validation layer is meant to catch. The catalogue measures faults; simultaneous faults in one return-assembly window are excluded by an explicit fault-independence assumption (Section 3.2), with the deterministic backend’s coverage (Table ) as the fallback if compounding is realistic in a given stack.
