5 Related Work
Prior work documents prompt-cache redundancy at narrow scopes,
including positional sinks at the first ∼\sim4
tokens (Xiao et al., 2024), mid-layer compression valleys
driven by massive activations (Queipo-de-Llano et al., 2025), the
lost-in-the-middle effect in which models underperform on relevant
content placed in the middle of long
contexts (Liu et al., 2024), and ICL summarisation into compact
internal task and function
vectors (Hendel et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2024). A
complementary mechanistic line argues that in-context copying is
implemented by induction heads, a distributed attention pattern rather
than a vector summary (Olsson et al., 2022). KV-cache
compression schemes (Cai et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023; Ge et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023)
prune by attention magnitude, and our results suggest a complementary
axis of substitution by structural placeholders at upper layers.
Ganesh et al. (2025) is the closest methodologically, an
attack feasibility study that hijacks generation by overwriting
trailing token positions across all KV layers with fixed donor. We
ask a different question and run a graded donor-noise ladder, expose
an algorithmic-overlap inversion, and add the form-vs-content
zero-vs-blank dissociation.

7 Perplexity on Pythia 1.4B: The Frobenius-Perplexity Gap
We now deploy the framework’s compressed weights into Pythia 1.4B
and measure WikiText-2 perplexity. This experiment tests whether
the Frobenius improvements of Section 6
translate to downstream language-modeling quality. They do not.
We report the main comparison in
Section 7.2, then conduct a
diagnostic sweep in Section 7.3
to rule out methodological causes, and finally develop the
mechanistic explanation in
Section 7.4.
7.1 Setup
Model and compression target.
Pythia 1.4B (Biderman et al., 2023)
(EleutherAI/pythia-1.4b, 2424 transformer layers,
D=2048D=2048). We compress the attention output projections
WO(L)W_{O}^{(L)} at every transformer layer at d/D=0.25d/D=0.25
(retained rank d=512d=512 per matrix; 24⋅2048⋅51224\cdot 2048\cdot 512
weights total, about 7%7\% of the model’s 1.41.4B parameters). MLP
and other attention weights (WQ,WK,WVW_{Q},W_{K},W_{V}) are left
uncompressed. This is the same setup used by
Wang et al. (2025b) for their main WO-only
ablation.
Calibration.
Following V2’s protocol, we use 256256 randomly-sampled WikiText-2
(Merity et al., 2017) training sequences (each of length
20482048, seed 4242) to capture per-layer input activations XLX_{L}
via forward pre-hooks. Each hook accumulates the second-moment
matrix XL​XL⊤∈ℝD×DX_{L}X_{L}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}, which is sufficient for
the V2-style whitening ΣL​ΣL⊤=XL​XL⊤\Sigma_{L}\Sigma_{L}^{\top}=X_{L}X_{L}^{\top} and
for the generalized projection surgery formula below. The
calibration step takes about 3030 minutes on CPU and is shared
across all compression methods for a fair comparison.
Compressed-weight surgery.
Given sections (SL,SL+1)(S_{L},S_{L+1}) from any method, the deployed
compressed weight at layer LL uses the generalized
projection formula:

This is the unique rank-dd factorization through (SL,SL+1)(S_{L},S_{L+1})
that minimizes activation reconstruction error ‖W(L)​XL−W~(L)​XL‖F2\|W^{(L)}X_{L}-\widetilde{W}^{(L)}X_{L}\|_{F}^{2} for given sections. The
orthogonal-projection alternative W~(L)=SL+1​SL+1⊤​W(L)​SL​SL⊤\widetilde{W}^{(L)}=S_{L+1}S_{L+1}^{\top}W^{(L)}S_{L}S_{L}^{\top} is empirically inferior
(Section 7.3); we use

Bias projection.
When the linear layer has a bias term bb, the compressed
bias is b′=SL+1​SL+1⊤​bb^{\prime}=S_{L+1}S_{L+1}^{\top}b, projecting the bias
through the output section. This preserves the affine structure

7.4 Mechanism
The diagnostic data points at a clear mechanism for the
negative result. We summarize it in two observations.
Observation 1: activation error tracks perplexity; Frobenius does not.
Across all 99 configurations we tested (the four methods in
Table 2, the four λ\lambda values in
Table 3, and the three corrected-anchor
variants in Table 4), the Spearman rank
correlation between per-layer activation error and perplexity is
+0.97+0.97, while the rank correlation between weight-Frobenius
reconstruction and perplexity is −0.43-0.43 (Frobenius is
anti-correlated with perplexity in the interior of the
bundle). Activation error is the correct proxy; Frobenius error
is not.
Observation 2: the residual stream decouples layers in the forward pass.
The bundle energy (3) couples adjacent layers
through the term ‖SL+1⊤​W(L)​SL‖F2\|S_{L+1}^{\top}W^{(L)}S_{L}\|_{F}^{2}. The
implicit assumption is that section SL+1S_{L+1} matters for layer
LL’s compressed weight and for layer L+1L+1’s compressed
weight, providing cross-layer information sharing in the
optimization. In the forward pass of a deployed transformer,
however, layer LL applies W~(L)\widetilde{W}^{(L)} to the residual
stream activations XL∈ℝDX_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{D} — not to anything that has
been projected through SLS_{L}. The residual stream is full
DD-dimensional; the next layer applies W~(L+1)\widetilde{W}^{(L+1)}
to that same full-dimensional residual stream, plus whatever
W~(L)\widetilde{W}^{(L)} contributed. The bundle’s joint optimization
optimizes a counterfactual coupling — one that would exist if
the model were trained to factor through low-dimensional
sections, but that does not exist in the trained model.
Formally: the bundle’s coupling term ‖SL+1⊤​W(L)​SL‖F2\|S_{L+1}^{\top}W^{(L)}S_{L}\|_{F}^{2} is maximized when the directions of W(L)W^{(L)} that
align with SL+1S_{L+1} also align with SLS_{L} on the input side.
But for the deployed model, what matters is ‖W(L)​XL−W~(L)​XL‖2\|W^{(L)}X_{L}-\widetilde{W}^{(L)}X_{L}\|^{2}, which is maximally reduced by
choosing SL+1S_{L+1} as the top-dd left singular subspace of
W(L)​ΣLW^{(L)}\Sigma_{L} — exactly V1’s per-layer choice. The bundle
makes SL+1S_{L+1} a compromise between V1’s per-layer
optimum and a coupling-induced direction; the compromise is
strictly worse per-layer.
Quantifying the trade-off.
We can see the trade-off directly in our data: the bundle’s
λ=0\lambda=0 activation error is 5.76×1085.76\times 10^{8}, which is
the sum of per-layer activation errors when sections are
joint-optimized. V1’s per-layer-optimized sections give total
activation error 1.93×1081.93\times 10^{8}, a 3×3\times improvement.
The bundle could match V1 per-layer by setting each SL+1S_{L+1} to
the per-layer optimum, but then ‖SL+1⊤​W(L)​SL‖F2\|S_{L+1}^{\top}W^{(L)}S_{L}\|_{F}^{2}
would be sub-maximal, and the bundle energy would be lower. The
bundle’s energy maximum and the per-layer activation-error
minimum are different points in section space, and the
deployment depends on the latter.
7.5 Discussion of the negative result
We emphasize what the negative result is and is not.
What it is.
A robust empirical finding: across 99 tested configurations of the
bundle (four λ\lambda values, two anchor conventions, two surgery
formulas), no configuration approaches SVD-LLM V1’s deployed
perplexity. The best bundle configuration is 2.8×2.8\times worse
than V1, and the bundle’s 46%46\% Frobenius advantage at d/D=1/4d/D=1/4
translates to a 3×3\times perplexity disadvantage.
What it is not.
It is not a claim that cross-layer methods can never help LLM
compression. We tested a specific form of cross-layer coupling —
joint Frobenius optimization through the bundle energy — on a
specific weight class (attention WOW_{O}) at a specific
compression ratio (d/D=1/4d/D=1/4) on a specific model
(Pythia 1.4B). Cross-layer methods that target a different
objective (e.g., direct minimization of per-layer activation
error with shared calibration costs, but not joint optimization
of cross-layer Frobenius coupling), or that target a different
weight class (e.g., MLP weights, which we did not test), or that
operate at different compression ratios or model scales, may give
different results.
What it implies for method design.
The Spearman rank correlation observation —
activation error tracks perplexity at +0.97+0.97, Frobenius error tracks
perplexity at −0.43-0.43 — is the most actionable takeaway. Methods
that report only Frobenius reconstruction improvements as evidence
of better LLM compression should be viewed with skepticism;
activation error or direct perplexity comparison is the meaningful
metric. We hope our framework and our diagnostic methodology serve
as a useful template for evaluating future cross-layer SVD methods.
