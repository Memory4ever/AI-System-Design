## 2609.10992 — Demystifying the Privacy-Utility Trade-off in LLM Interactions

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10992v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10992); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `2 / 2 / 3 = 7`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** prompt privacy cannot be optimized by a context-free delete rule. The utility of a sensitive statement varies with user intent and with interactions among sensitive attributes; a local extractor–planner–restorer can therefore choose Keep/Remove/Replace under explicit privacy- or utility-priority modes. The durable increment is the conditional control problem, not a formal privacy guarantee.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** local state is a set of extracted sensitive statements plus an action plan. Stage 1 extracts statements; Stage 2 selects Keep/Remove/Replace per item; only the sanitized prompt goes to the remote model; Stage 3 locally restores withheld context into the remote response. The user-facing control is one of two learned operating modes: privacy priority or utility priority. Distillation uses DeepSeek-V4-Flash as teacher and Qwen3-4B as the student, named Veilmind-4B in the paper ([§4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10992v1)).
- **Implementation status:** the paper specifies data construction, teacher synthesis, prompts, model identities and decoding settings. Its exact-v1 page does not expose a public implementation repository; implementation and results were not independently run here.
- **Evaluation contract:** the analysis corpus has 9,757 prompts (5,928 real, 3,829 synthetic); a 384-item controlled subset fixes the number of sensitive statements at five. Gemini-2.5-Flash at temperature 0 generates original/sanitized responses, and Skywork-Reward-V2 provides the marginal-utility proxy ([§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10992v1)). Framework evaluation uses a manually verified Uprise subset (`N=200`) plus Pupa-tnb, with Papillon and PUFT variants as baselines. DeepSeek-V4-Flash judges pairwise utility; privacy is sensitive-statement retention in the revised prompt ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10992v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** Table 1 and Figure 6 report the strategy mix and privacy–utility frontier; on Uprise, distillation moves utility-mode utility from 75.5% to 86.8% and privacy-mode leakage from 52.6% to 40.5%. Table 2 ablates Extraction, Sanitization Guidelines and Restoration: omitting extraction increases leakage from 52.6% to 71.6%, while omitting restoration leaves privacy unchanged but lowers utility to 62.0% ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10992v1)). Appendix C reports the Pupa-tnb comparison and attribute-pair interactions.
- **What is proved:** within the stated datasets, prompts, generators and LLM-judge metrics, context-conditioned editing and restoration beat the listed static/delegation baselines and each pipeline stage has a separable measured role. Removal versus replacement and pairwise attribute effects are empirically non-uniform.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** no differential-privacy, non-inference, information-theoretic, or attack-resistance guarantee is supplied. Sensitive data remains local before restoration, but the restored final response can contain it; the paper measures prompt retention rather than all leakage channels. The same small family of frontier models participates in annotation, generation, teaching and judging, so model-mediated construct validity is a major dependency. The interaction study has sparse cells and a controlled 384-item subset; the main framework test is only 200 manually verified prompts. Replacement can add false premises, while removal can destroy required constraints. Multi-turn evolution of goals and privacy boundaries is explicitly future work, and there is no dedicated limitations section.

## 2609.11020 — K/V-Cache Interventions Dissociate Representation Alignment from Persona Expression

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11020); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 1 / 3 = 7`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** moving a decoder's hidden representation toward a target persona is not sufficient for coherent target-persona behavior. The K/V cache is a persistent but structurally constrained intervention surface: mid-layer replacement can preserve output quality, whereas full replacement can achieve comparable representational alignment while collapsing into repetition.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** the manipulated state is the autoregressive per-layer K/V trajectory. Thirteen configurations vary full versus early/mid/late layer bands, finer mid sub-bands, position shuffle/lag and source–target interpolation. A same-token-sequence target-forward control forces source- and target-conditioned caches to consume the identical realized token sequence, then replaces selected cache bands, testing whether the internal shift survives without different imported tokens ([§3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1)).
- **Implementation status:** experiments use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in BF16 on one H100, with 32 layers, 32 query heads and eight GQA K/V heads. The code-release manifest says the artifact will be released at an anonymized location upon acceptance, so no public implementation was available to inspect or reproduce ([Appendix I](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1)).
- **Evaluation contract:** one deliberately high-separation persona pair (`7.2` in the learned persona space versus a typical distance near `3`) and one sourdough seed prompt are used; each of 13 cells has ten samples at temperature 0.7 and 50 new tokens. Text behavior is hand-curated persona-marker density plus type–token ratio; representation is a reused supervised 10-dimensional persona subspace at layer 28; K/V cosine dumps use only the first sample in each cell because of storage cost ([§3.1, §3.3–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** mid-layer replacement (layers 9–20) yields target-marker density `15.52/100 words`, TTR `0.766`, and mid-band V gap `+0.89`; full replacement yields higher marker density `24.81` but TTR `0.650` and conspicuous repetition. Late-layer replacement is target-aligned at layer 28 but has TTR `0.393`. In the same-token control, the layer-28 target-ward gap remains `+3.60` for layers 9–20 versus `+3.83` in the main battery, excluding different generated-token histories as the sole explanation ([§4.2–4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1), Tables 2–3 and 8).
- **What is proved:** in this single controlled setting, representation alignment and surface behavior dissociate both by layer location and intervention scope. The same-token control supports the narrow exclusion “not solely imported token history.” Position lag and shuffle fail to transfer either representation or persona behavior under the tested operations.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** the post-treatment shared token sequence is not a fully independent persona counterfactual. The work does not causally isolate K versus V, heads, or positions; cosine analyses are correlational and K/V dumps are `n=1`. All principal results use one model, one far-apart persona pair, one topic and 50-token generations; the 150-token persistence check has only `n=3`. Marker lists are partly topic-confounded and TTR is only a repetition proxy. It does not establish a general persona-control method or long-horizon identity fidelity ([Limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11020v1)).

## 2609.11058 — EMMI: Edge Multi-Modal Intelligence

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11058v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11058); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `2 / 3 / 2 = 7`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** an edge/server MLLM split can move the communication boundary from raw inputs or architecture-specific activations to a compact, unified multimodal representation. The evidence establishes a task-specific feasibility regime when bandwidth dominates, not general MLLM reasoning or formal privacy.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** modality-specific encoders create features, cross-modal fusion constructs one representation, an edge compressor maps it to a 64-dimensional latent, and the server decoder reconstructs the fused feature for a frozen LLaVA backbone. Task-agnostic training combines reconstruction and contrastive alignment; task-aware training substitutes supervised contrastive structure. Compression dimension and objective are the main controls ([§III-B–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11058v1)).
- **Implementation status:** CLIP ViT-B/32 or MobileCLIP2-S0 encodes image/text; a trainable two-layer MLP turns the received representation into eight soft-prompt tokens for frozen LLaVA-1.5-7B, followed by a binary head. The exact-v1 page gives no public code artifact, and future work explicitly defers evaluation on edge hardware.
- **Evaluation contract:** MS-COCO image–caption matching only, using a 72K/5K/5K split. Both encoders emit 512-dimensional vectors; the fused 2,048-dimensional representation is compressed to 64 dimensions. Baselines are uncompressed transfer, PCA, BlockPCA, LDA, AE and VAE. Server inference is a binary image–text matching classifier, not open-ended generation. Latency is assembled as edge processing plus payload/bandwidth transmission plus server time; transmission is calculated for 0.1/10/50/100 Mbps rather than measured over a deployed wireless system ([§IV-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11058v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** TaskAwareAE reaches 98.08% with CLIP and 98.32% with MobileCLIP, within 0.16 percentage points of uncompressed while shrinking the payload from 8,192 B to 256 B (`32×`). Reported compression is below 1 ms and modality encoding dominates edge compute. At the assumed 0.1 Mbps, calculated transmission falls from 655.4 ms to 20.5 ms and estimated total latency from about 903 ms to 269 ms (`3.4×`); the saving falls to 0.68 ms at 100 Mbps ([§IV-B–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11058v1), Tables II–III).
- **What is proved:** under the paper's task, models, cached two-stage training and arithmetic bandwidth model, task-aware latent compression preserves binary matching accuracy while reducing transmitted bytes; its value is strongly bandwidth-regime dependent.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** no on-device energy, memory, thermal, radio variability, queuing, concurrency or end-to-end deployment measurement is reported. The task does not demonstrate open-ended multimodal reasoning, heterogeneous sensors, non-aligned encoders or dynamic workloads. The claim that compact latents preserve privacy is unsupported by leakage attacks or a formal threat model; reducing exposure of raw inputs is not equivalent to confidentiality. Task-aware compression requires labels and may discard information needed by other tasks. LDA nearly matches the task-aware autoencoder on the narrow benchmark, limiting attribution to the proposed learned pipeline. Future work names edge hardware, complex modalities and heterogeneous devices as untested.

## 2609.11061 — Fork Where the Model Changes Its Mind

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11061v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11061); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 2 / 3 = 8`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** under a fixed tree-RLVR rollout budget, forks are more valuable near boundaries where the model's answer belief changes than at midpoint or high-token-entropy locations. Fork placement is therefore a credit/data-selection control, not just a tree-shape parameter.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** candidate boundaries are newline positions (at most 16, with midpoint fallback). Probe-JS elicits a short answer distribution at each boundary and ranks consecutive Jensen–Shannon shifts; Lens-Shift teacher-forces the committed answer and detects changes in logit-lens depth/shape. A separately fitted Belief-Shift Vector (BSV) is used only for pre-RL validation because checkpoint drift makes it unsafe for RL selection. The selected forks enter the common TreeRL tree; leaf verifier rewards supply authority, while global and parent-local advantages assign credit ([§3.1–3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11061v1)).
- **Implementation status:** training uses `verl` with vLLM rollouts on one `8×H200` node, temperature 1.0, learning rate `1e-6`, and 192 kept rollouts per step. Recipes and logs are described in appendices, but the exact-v1 page provides no public code repository; no run was reproduced here.
- **Evaluation contract:** pre-RL selector validation compares chosen boundaries with Monte-Carlo rollout-value curves on AIME 2025–2026 and GPQA-Diamond over four probe models. RL uses OLMo-3-7B, Nemotron-9B and Qwen3-4B/8B across mathematics and competitive programming. All arms retain the same tree scheme and rollout budget, isolating fork criterion, but each row is reported at its own best aggregate checkpoint. Qwen arms are exactly 680-step matched; OLMo/Nemotron use common caps and may stop earlier. Probe FLOP upper bounds range from 0.45–1.3% for math and 0.67–4.3% for code depending on model ([§3.6, §4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11061v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** a belief-shift selector leads all eight pre-RL model/benchmark panels. In RL, OLMo math rises from the strongest baseline aggregate 20.7 to Probe-JS 23.3; Nemotron math from 40.9 to 45.1; Qwen3-4B from 28.7 to Lens-Shift 29.6. OLMo code improves from 31.0 to 34.9, but Nemotron code is a counterexample: midpoint leads 64.5 versus Probe-JS 64.0. Qwen's gain shrinks below one point as trained siblings converge to the same answer 63% of the time ([§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11061v1), Table 3). Ablations show tree size and composite local/global credit matter; pure GRPO drops OLMo math to 15.2 versus 23.3 composite ([§7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11061v1), Table 4).
- **What is proved:** on the tested math/code setups, belief-shift reads rank value-changing boundaries better than the listed selectors before RL and usually improve training under a common tree/rollout envelope. The negative Nemotron-code result and model-dependent sibling contrast delimit the effect. BSV transfer is evidence about a diagnostic direction, not evidence that BSV is stable enough for online RL.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** there is no dedicated limitations section. Best-per-arm checkpoint selection is not a single terminal-budget comparison and can favor noisy peaks; training-seed replication and uncertainty for the RL deltas are not reported as a full multi-seed causal study. The paper does not re-establish TreeRL versus chain RL, so it proves fork placement conditional on that tree scheme. Probe cost is an analytic upper bound, not end-to-end wall-clock/communication measurement. A belief read can be miscalibrated, waste forks and change the sampled training distribution even though the leaf verifier still owns correctness; “cannot corrupt training” should therefore not be generalized beyond verifier-gated labels. Benefits diminish when siblings fail to diverge, and transfer beyond the tested math/code verifiers remains open.

## 2609.11063 — The Information Geometry of Large Language Models Is Shared, Learned, and Controllable

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11063); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 3 / 3 = 9`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** next-token probability laws carry a canonical Fisher–Rao geometry tied to cross-entropy, shared more reliably across architectures than raw activation geometry. That geometry is learned with the output law and supplies a coordinate-aware cost for minimum-disturbance interventions. Output behavior constrains output geometry; it does not identify a unique hidden representation.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** system state is the categorical next-token distribution and its pullback Fisher metric at a model layer. The theory relates affine-softmax cross-entropy to output Fisher curvature, spectral structure and class-conditioned hidden moments. For control, a desired output covector is preconditioned by the Fisher metric/natural gradient; matrix-free Hessian-vector products and conjugate-gradient/Woodbury solves avoid materializing the full matrix. Finite moves are treated as local and require damping or relinearization rather than one unbounded step ([Part I, §10–11](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)).
- **Implementation status:** the paper specifies float precision, model revisions, hardware classes, seeds and a reproducibility map. Derived data and matrix-free/analysis code are available only from the corresponding author on reasonable request, not at a public immutable artifact inspected here ([Data and Code availability](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)).
- **Evaluation contract:** evidence spans controlled synthetic language assignments across transformer/state-space/recurrent models; public model-pair geometry batteries; human completion distributions; randomized evidence-depth training; knowledge editing; instruct-model steering; SAE ablations and dictionary learning. These are distinct protocols, not one pooled benchmark. The strongest causal evidence is in matched synthetic assignments and randomized depth interventions; cross-model/human alignment and many intervention studies remain empirical correlations or matched engineering comparisons ([§12–20](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** eight independent 64-context/64-outcome language pairs across three architectures and two capacities yield 96 test runs, supporting that learned output geometry follows the assigned language law. A fully crossed evidence-depth experiment holds weights, minibatches, exposure and length fixed across three four-layer decoder families and two controlled languages; all six 95% intervals and all 48 seed means show delayed acquisition for deeper evidence ([§3.1, §7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)). Proposition 3 explicitly constructs identical-output models with negative hidden-geometry agreement, ruling out a behavior-derived positive hidden-geometry floor ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)). In a matched fixed routed-edit comparison, Fisher and Euclidean edits both achieve the `+5` log-odds target on 30/30 edits, but Fisher has `10.57×` lower other-token KL (95% bootstrap 8.52–13.37). The larger query-time routed results use different access/routing conditions and are descriptive rather than a controlled rank against ROME/MEMIT ([§13](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11063v1)).
- **What is proved:** the paper's mathematical identities/bounds follow under stated affine-softmax, regularity, boundedness and margin assumptions. Controlled assignments support output-law rather than architecture ownership of the geometry; the explicit symmetry construction proves output equivalence need not preserve hidden geometry. Matched interventions support Fisher cost as a lower-disturbance local operator in the tested settings.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** valid theoretical floors are vacuous at the measured model-to-reference distances (observed variance near 0.005 versus bounds near 1.3), and early held-out agreement predictions are undefined or inaccurate in many cells. The paper does not prove every LLM converges to one geometry, that raw activation coordinates are meaningless, or that local quadratic cost guarantees safe large nonlinear edits. Human alignment is defined through specific completion/coarse-graining/embedding protocols; after conditioning on predictive risk, the residual log-parameter coefficient includes zero, so scale is not an independent universal cause. Some headline editing comparisons use different routing and method-access conditions. Full GPU pullback solves stop at 2.8B; 6.9B evidence uses graph-free spectra and CPU calibration/offload. Code is request-only, so implementation and benchmark claims remain unreproduced.

## 2609.11065 — MOSAIC: Query-Aware Exploration Policy Adaptation for GraphRAG

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11065); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 2 / 3 = 8`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** GraphRAG retrieval should treat each query as a bounded control problem over seed selection, traversal, stopping and evidence retention, while keeping the graph, indexes and answer generator shared. This is stronger than query-conditioned relevance alone but narrower than choosing arbitrary per-query pipelines.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** an LLM analyzer maps the query's evidence requirements to a finite policy vector: seed limit/mode, relation or hybrid retrieval, depth, beam width, anchor strength, connector use, stop regime, evidence cap/floor and adaptive reservation. Categorical outputs are allowlisted, numbers clamped and missing/invalid fields fall back to conservative defaults. The policy governs a common relation/target/specificity/anchor/transition/coherence path score and shared pruning pool; it cannot execute code or invent retrieval operators ([§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)).
- **Implementation status:** graphs are built once with a LightRAG-style pipeline, OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large`, and GPT-4o-mini at temperature 0 for analyzer and answer generation. No training is added. The evaluated implementation contains proprietary components; the authors only intend a controlled-access repository subject to approval, so no public reproducible artifact was available ([Artifact and Reproducibility Statement](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)).
- **Evaluation contract:** every question from GraphRAG-Bench Medical (2,062 questions/2,406 documents) and Novel (2,010/461) is used. Primary ACC is query-count weighted; Evidence Recall and Context Relevancy evaluate retrieval. Fixed Narrow/Medium/Wide and budget-matched fixed controls share the exact graph/generator; other GraphRAG and G-Reasoner figures are externally reported and not component matched ([§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** on Medical, Mosaic ACC is 76.97 versus 72.03 for budget-matched fixed and 67.01 for fixed medium; it evaluates 81.9% fewer paths and retains 47.2% fewer evidence items than fixed wide. However it is slower end to end: 9.68 s versus 5.29 s for fixed medium because the analyzer adds 2.82 s ([§7.1–7.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1), Tables 4, 8–9). Transfer is mixed: HotpotQA `-0.3 EM`, MuSiQue `+1.8 EM`, 2Wiki `-12.6 EM` versus externally reported, benchmark-trained G-Reasoner ([§8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)). A qualitative failure has the analyzer over-predict mediation, expand to depth 5, and fall to 24% versus 99% for narrow retrieval ([§9](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)).
- **What is proved:** the component-matched fixed-policy experiments establish that query-specific bounded policies can allocate less graph search more effectively than several global operating points on the evaluated graphs. Logs and counterfactual control-disable tests show the policy does not merely collapse to one configuration or retrieve uniformly more.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** interacting controls prevent attribution to every prompt phrase or low-level score; diagnostic counts are non-additive. The analyzer can misclassify evidence structure, guardrails cannot repair that semantic error, and graph construction errors remain an upstream ceiling. Open-ended creative generation does not improve uniformly. Current latency and monetary cost exceed fixed retrieval. External baselines and transfer use asymmetric training/pipelines, so they do not establish state of the art. Proprietary components and controlled access block independent reproduction ([§10–11](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11065v1)).

## 2609.11067 — When Noise Fabricates Bias

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11067v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11067); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 2 / 3 = 8`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** meaning-preserving surface noise can systematically change LLM-as-a-judge bias measurements, often turning a judge's own neutral decision into a directional decision more frequently than the reverse. The durable implication is to stress-test evaluator robustness and separate stability from correctness, not to treat the paper's clean labels as human ground truth.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** state is each judge's clean A/B/None decision and its decision on a perturbed version of the same response. Five seeded noise families—typo, informal spelling, punctuation collapse, colloquial, and combined—vary nominal intensity. A “fabrication” is self-baseline None→A/B; “erasure” is A/B→None. Using each judge's own clean label controls family-specific baseline tendencies ([§3.2–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11067v1)).
- **Implementation status:** the authors link a public Fable code/toolkit repository from the paper ([GitHub](https://github.com/dong4918-skku/Fable)). The repository was not audited or executed in this review; only exact-v1 paper evidence is accepted here.
- **Evaluation contract:** 42 CLEAR-Bias templates × 91 name pairs produce 3,822 free-form Llama-3.1-8B responses across seven categories. Four judges—Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-4-12B-it and GPT-5.4—emit A/B/None. The 3,822 rows are not independent because they reuse 42 templates; the paper adds template-level sign tests. Nominal `p=0.3` is called realistic, but realized token changes differ by noise family, and the rule-generated perturbations are not manually semantics-checked per item ([§3, §4.5, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11067v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** at typo `p=0.3`, Llama has 240 fabrications versus two erasures (`120×`); Qwen 112/20 (`5.6×`), Gemma 31/9 (`3.4×`), and GPT 30/24 (`1.3×`). Across 100 judge–condition pairs, erasure exceeds fabrication only eight times and none is statistically significant; fragile judges show the largest asymmetry. Combined noise can amplify fabrication up to `2.5×` over a single noise type. Religion and disability lead the raw fabricated counts under combined `p=0.3` ([§4.2–4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11067v1), Tables 5–7).
- **What is proved:** relative to each judge's own clean decision, seeded surface perturbations cause asymmetric decision instability in the tested benchmark, and the direction/strength differs by judge. Template-level analysis shows the headline fragile-judge asymmetry is not solely an item-count artifact.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** self-baseline flips do not establish absolute bias correctness or whether a directional flip follows the culturally appropriate stereotype direction. “Meaning-preserving” is a rule-design assumption not verified instance by instance, and heavy corruption can harm intelligibility. Names alone imperfectly encode some demographic axes. Authentic user-noise frequencies are not measured, `p=0.3` is an operational choice, and all base responses come from one generator. Repeated templates/name pairs still require hierarchical random-effects treatment for full inference. Results support evaluator QA, not a universal estimate of real-world social bias ([Limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11067v1)).

## 2609.11085 — Beyond Solver Verdicts: Generative Reward Models for Autoformalization

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11085); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 2 / 3 = 8`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** a binary solver verdict cannot distinguish reference-equivalent formalizations from verdict-preserving but semantically different ones (VPU). Offline access to a gold formal reference and bidirectional Z3 equivalence can supervise a generative Yes/No verifier that later scores `(natural-language problem, candidate encoding)` without the reference, enabling candidate selection and adaptive compute allocation.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** offline state includes problem `x`, candidate `s`, reference `s*`, solver verdict and bidirectional equivalence label. Small edits that preserve the solver verdict but break equivalence supply hard negatives. A frozen 27B instruction model with LoRA learns the probability of the answer token Yes versus No; no random classification head is added. At deployment, the reference is absent and the scalar score gates escalation or weights selection. Proposition 1 proves only that a function of the binary verdict alone has AUROC 0.5 on verdict-matched pairs; it is not an impossibility theorem for richer verifiers ([§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)).
- **Implementation status:** LoRA training is reported as roughly 12 H100-80GB GPU-hours; evaluation/agentic inference uses BF16 on the same hardware. Code, adapters and benchmarks are promised “upon publication,” and the reproducibility statement says “on acceptance”; they were not publicly available or reproduced at review time ([§10, Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)).
- **Evaluation contract:** the primary real-translator benchmark has 950 encodings from 197 source problems, including 260 VPU cases and excluding mutation-generated test negatives. IDs are disjoint, but a normalized audit finds 298/950 rows share source statements seen in training under new IDs; the text-disjoint subset has 652 rows. Detection uses AUROC and problem-clustered bootstrap. Downstream evidence covers static Best-of-N and an adaptive Proof-of-Thought loop across nine benchmarks/backends. Most secondary ablations are single runs; three training seeds are reported for the principal GenV variants ([§5, Appendix D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** GenV+HN scores AUROC 0.961 on all 950 rows and 0.955 on the normalized-text-disjoint subset, versus self-consistency 0.863 and solver-only 0.5. The `+11.3` point adaptive-system headline decomposes on identical recorded pools into `+9.3` from vote@5 over single-shot, `+1.2` from gated escalation, and only `+0.9` from verifier selection; advisory feedback with equal sampling budget gives approximately zero. Across eight static backends, the pooled gain over vote is `+0.6`, with several individual negative or zero deltas ([§6, Tables 1, 5–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)). Gradient attribution localizes single edits at 0.821 exact/0.925 within one, but the paper explicitly treats this as diagnostic rather than causal ([§7, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)).
- **What is proved:** the narrow binary-verdict information limit is formal. Under the paper's Z3-defined construct, generative whole-encoding supervision distinguishes VPU from reference-equivalent cases better than listed solver/structural baselines, and most of the agentic headline gain is correctly attributable to added candidate sampling rather than the verifier itself. Input-dependence ablations show the shipped verifier uses the relationship between problem and encoding, although an encoding-side prior remains.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** Z3 reference equivalence is not human intent; it requires decidability, a fully specified satisfiable gold reference and bounded solver conditions. Two unsatisfiable formulas are vacuously equivalent, so omissions from an inconsistent reference are invisible. Formal-style shifts can cause threshold drift, and synthetic single-edit negatives do not model correlated multi-error repair. The split has substantial statement overlap despite identifier disjointness, though the text-disjoint result narrows this concern. Localization/SAE results are retrospective diagnostics, not causal repair mechanisms; the best SAE feature is selected retrospectively. The system detects and allocates compute but does not show that score feedback repairs errors. Public artifacts remain pending ([§9–10, Appendix B–D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11085v1)).

## 2609.11146 — The Oligarch Barely Steers Model Collapse in Multi-Model Ecosystems

- **Identity / access / score:** [exact v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11146v1), [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11146); exact-v1 access succeeded. Score `3 / 2 / 3 = 8`. First public `2026-09-11T08:00:00+08:00`; current history has only v1 and no withdrawal notice.
- **Central claim:** in the tested recursive multi-model data ecosystems, supplier-share concentration has little effect on the collapse destination or speed relative to the shared degradation itself. Pool composition—how readily constituent models are moved, or the fraction of human text—better predicts speed. The result challenges a simple “largest supplier steers the corpus” model, but is explicitly bounded to small open models and five generations.
- **Mechanism, state, and control:** in each generation, every model produces text; fixed supplier shares sample a common pool; every model is then fully fine-tuned **from its clean base weights** on that pool and replaces the previous generation. Thus weights do not accumulate across generations: the experiment isolates recursive corpus contamination. Controls vary number of players (`K=3/5/8/13`), equal versus 28% head share, head identity, injected 90% directional-bias probes, and 0/25/50% human-data replacement ([§3, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11146v1)).
- **Implementation status:** natural ecosystems use 13 public 1–4B base models. Each model generates 800 texts per generation; each shared pool contains 2,100 texts; five generations and three paired seeds are used. Full-parameter BF16 fine-tuning is one epoch at `2e-5`, effective batch 16 and max length 768. Two documented vLLM failures required backend deviations. Models/corpora/scorers are public, but code, configuration and per-arm measurements are only promised for future release and were not reproduced ([Appendix A, Reproducibility Statement](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11146v1)).
- **Evaluation contract:** collapse direction/distance is primarily cosine geometry under a frozen encoder, with three audit encoders and encoder-free perplexity/distinct-4/self-BLEU checks. Natural concentration stops at 28%; 90% shares belong only to injected three-model probes and cannot form one continuous share curve. The “travel propensity” predictor is fitted post hoc across 19 arms and evaluated with leave-one-arm-out `Q²`; three seeds quantify within-arm variation ([§4, Appendices D–F](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11146v1)).
- **Direct evidence:** across `K=5/8/13`, mean endpoint separation among share/head-identity arms is only 2.6–3.2% of their own five-generation drift; same-seed separation is below the cross-seed noise floor on all four encoders. In the 90% injected probe, only one of three injected biases holds; the strongest science bias falls rather than dominates. Replacing 25% human text slows drift at all four K values; at K=13, drift falls from 99 to 72, and 50% human text lowers it to 54, while direction cosines remain 0.996/0.993. Share-weighted travel propensity explains `R²=0.68`, leave-one-arm-out `Q²=0.55`, but all five K=8 arms lie above the fit and more than 30% remains unexplained ([§4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11146v1), Table 1/Figures 2–5).
- **What is proved:** within these paired small-model simulations, changing concentration/head identity moves endpoints far less than the recursive drift; human text consistently slows the five-generation path without measurably redirecting it. Multiple encoder audits support the qualitative invariance. The clean-base reset makes the claim specifically about data-chain recursion rather than compounded optimizer/weight-state inheritance.
- **What is not proved / trade-offs / failures:** there is no dedicated limitations section, but the conclusion itself bounds the claim. The main experiments are 1–4B, at most 13 players and five generations. A single 7–8B probe is under-converged: at generation 5 between-arm separation remains as large as drift, reaching 0.40 only by generation 10, so scale and convergence are confounded. Player count and membership change together in the nested design. The travel-propensity index is post hoc, partly built from the same ecosystem family, misses K=8 systematically and is not a law. The primary ruler is encoder-dependent, and encoder-free measures do not independently verify endpoint geometry. Resetting from clean weights omits continual-training state, optimizer carryover, architectural scaling, data dedup/filtering feedback and strategic supplier behavior found in real markets. The work supports no policy recommendation or universal claim that concentration is harmless.
