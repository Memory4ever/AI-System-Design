Pay Only for Disagreement: Certified No-Regression Verdicts for Model Updates with Matching Label-Complexity Bounds

\nameVishnu Bindu Balachandran \emailvishnubindubalachandran@outlook.com
\addrIndependent Researcher
\addrwww.vishnubindubalachandran.com
Abstract

Every production model is updated, by retraining, fine-tuning, quantization, or a silent vendor swap, and each update risks being worse than what it replaced. We formalize update promotion as certified paired risk-difference auditing. Our starting point is a support identity. The risk difference between two models lives on the inputs where they disagree, observable without labels. We build Discern, a sequential two-tier protocol. A zero-label tier certifies benign updates whose disagreement rate is below tolerance from unlabeled traffic alone. An audited tier labels only sampled disagreements through an anytime-valid confidence sequence, valid at every stopping time and under any label-routing rule, even an adversarial judge. We prove finite-sample validity and matching label-complexity bounds Θ~​(ρ2/ε2)\widetilde{\Theta}(\rho^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}) at the rate level, so exploiting free disagreement provably saves a factor Θ​(1/ρ)\Theta(1/\rho) over any pairing-blind auditor, and the guarantee composes across an unbounded sequence of promotions from one error budget. Across 14,00014{,}000+ replayed audit streams over 785785 update pairs, including LoRA fine-tunes of language models up to 1.4B parameters, miscoverage is 0.00020.0002 (nominal 5%5\%), power 0.9860.986 with zero false alarms, and 56%56\% of benign updates certify with zero labels. Each audit emits a machine-checkable evidence record for EU AI Act post-market monitoring.

Keywords: model updates, anytime-valid inference, label complexity, confidence sequences, deployment monitoring

1 Introduction

Machine learning systems in production are not static artifacts. Teams retrain on fresh data, adjust hyperparameters, fine-tune on new domains, quantize weights for cheaper inference, distill large models into small ones, and absorb silent upstream changes when a managed API provider swaps its backbone. Each such event replaces a deployed model ff with a candidate gg, and each replacement carries the same operational risk, since the update may quietly be worse than what it replaced, either overall or on a slice of traffic that matters. Post-deployment incident reports and the model-update literature on churn and backward compatibility document this failure mode repeatedly (Milani Fard et al., 2016; Yan et al., 2021; Srivastava et al., 2020).

Despite the ubiquity of the event, the promotion decision is statistically informal in most organizations. The dominant practices are (i) fixed offline test suites, which cannot reflect current traffic and are exhausted by repeated use, (ii) A/B tests on business metrics, which are slow, costly in exposure, and do not certify model correctness, and (iii) uniform labeling campaigns on sampled traffic, which are statistically sound but pay for an enormous number of labels that carry no information about the update. The third point is the crux. If the old and new model agree on a traffic point, that point contributes exactly zero to the risk difference between them, no matter what the true label is. Labels spent on agreements are wasted, and under realistic updates the models agree on 80%80\% to 99.9%99.9\% of traffic.

This paper treats the promotion decision as a first-class statistical object with certified verdicts, matching label-complexity bounds, and lifetime soundness across a sequence of updates. We study the paired risk difference Δ=𝔼​[ℓ​(g​(X),Y)]−𝔼​[ℓ​(f​(X),Y)]\Delta=\mathbb{E}[\ell(g(X),Y)]-\mathbb{E}[\ell(f(X),Y)] between candidate gg and incumbent ff, signed so that Δ>0\Delta>0 means the update is a regression. The operator wants one of two certified verdicts at tolerance ε\varepsilon and confidence 1−δ1-\delta, either a no-regression certificate asserting Δ<ε\Delta<\varepsilon, or a regression alarm asserting Δ>0\Delta>0. The audit must run on live traffic, must remain valid when the operator peeks continuously and stops as soon as evidence suffices, and must spend as few human labels as possible.

The support identity and its consequences.

Everything follows from one elementary observation. Write A=𝟏​{f​(X)≠g​(X)}A=\mathbf{1}\{f(X)\neq g(X)\} for the disagreement indicator, observable for free because both models already score the traffic. For any loss that depends on the input only through the prediction, agreement forces identical losses, so the paired difference DD vanishes whenever A=0A=0 and

Δ=𝔼​[D​A],|Δ|≤B​ρ,ρ:=ℙ​(A=1),\Delta\;=\;\mathbb{E}[D\,A],\qquad|\Delta|\;\leq\;B\,\rho,\qquad\rho:=\mathbb{P}(A=1), (1)

where BB bounds the loss range. Identity (1) has two consequences that we develop into a complete theory. First, if unlabeled traffic certifies that ρ≤ε/B\rho\leq\varepsilon/B, then |Δ|≤ε|\Delta|\leq\varepsilon is certified with zero labels. Quantized builds, routine retrains, and hyperparameter touches typically live in this regime, and in our experiments 98%98\% of such benign updates certify label-free. Second, when ρ\rho is not negligible, all information about Δ\Delta is concentrated on disagreements, so an auditor should label only there. Figure 1 overviews the resulting protocol. We make this precise with an importance-weighted estimator wrapped in an empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence, and we prove it is not merely a good idea but a rate-optimal one. Its label complexity matches an information-theoretic lower bound up to logarithmic factors, and the gap to any pairing-blind procedure is exactly the disagreement rate itself.

A scope note belongs up front. The support identity requires the loss to depend on the input only through the prediction (Assumption 1), which holds for the standard supervised losses and for the classification fine-tunes in our experiments, but excludes losses that read model scores or a judge’s verdict, including calibration and preference or reward losses. Our certified guarantees are for that prediction-determined class. A soft generalization to score-reading losses that are Lipschitz in the model output, which reaches calibration and smooth reward objectives, is developed in Section 5.6; open-ended judged generation remains outside the theory and we do not claim it.

Why this is the right target for a certified method.

A certificate is only as valuable as the decision it unlocks. The decision here is concrete and recurring, namely whether to promote or hold an update. Regulation is converging on the same object. Article 72 of the EU AI Act requires providers of high-risk systems to run post-market monitoring proportionate to the risk, and logging obligations under Article 12 require traceable evidence for system changes. A Discern audit emits, per promotion, a machine-checkable tuple (tolerance, confidence, verdict, label spend, stopping time, per-slice outcomes) that serves directly as such evidence. No comparable finite-sample instrument exists in the update-monitoring literature, which either assumes labeled evaluation sets, provides asymptotic or heuristic monitoring, or certifies single-model properties rather than the paired difference that the promotion decision actually consumes (Section 2).

2 Related Work

Paired comparison of classifiers.

The observation that paired accuracy comparison hinges on discordant pairs goes back to McNemar (1947), and sequential treatments of two-sample testing are classical (Wald, 1945). Restricting adjudication to discordant pairs to save labels recurs in fixed-sample model evaluation, where Musgrove et al. (2023) report over a 90%90\% reduction in adjudications, but under an assumed class prevalence and with neither a sequential guarantee nor a lower bound. Our contribution is not the identity itself but its consequences under modern constraints, namely anytime validity under continuous monitoring, adaptive label routing with importance-weighting, matching label-complexity bounds, and a zero-label tier. Recent resolution diagnostics for paired evaluation (Kotawala, 2026) quantify whether a fixed-nn comparison has enough labeled points to resolve, but assume the labels already exist. We optimize and certify the labeling process itself.

Anytime-valid inference.

Our confidence sequences build on time-uniform boundaries from nonnegative-supermartingale arguments (Ville, 1939; Robbins, 1970), modern empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences (Howard et al., 2021; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, 2024), and the game-theoretic testing programme (Ramdas et al., 2023). Betting-style audits have been used for fairness properties of a fixed model (Chugg et al., 2023) and adaptive audits of a single AI system with anytime-valid guarantees (Zhou et al., 2026). Sequential drift attribution for LLM evaluation pipelines (Li, 2026) monitors a judged pipeline over time. Closest in mechanism, Karampatziakis et al. (2021) build off-policy confidence sequences with importance weights inside a betting martingale and apply them to gated deployment of a contextual-bandit system, and Xu et al. (2024) construct anytime-valid risk-controlling prediction sets under active labeling with a query budget. These share our core machinery, Horvitz–Thompson weights within an anytime-valid betting confidence sequence under adaptive querying. None of these anytime-valid audits target the paired update delta, none exploit the disagreement support to drive a zero-label tier, and none characterize label complexity or prove a matching lower bound. We use the confidence-sequence machinery as a component and prove rate-matching complexity results for the promotion problem.

Model comparison and update approval.

Two prior lines come closest to our object. Sawade et al. (2012) compare the risks of two given models on a fixed labeling budget by sampling test instances from a variance-optimal instrumental distribution, the paired object with active, importance-weighted labeling. Their guarantee is an asymptotic normal-approximation test at a fixed horizon. We add what deployment needs and what their analysis does not provide, namely finite-sample anytime-valid verdicts, an explicit label-complexity characterization with a matching lower bound, and a zero-label tier. Notably, their optimal instrumental distribution already concentrates on informative points, and our Theorem 8 can be read as the information-theoretic reason why. Separately, Feng et al. (2021) and Feng (2021) study approval policies for modifications to deployed ML systems as an online hypothesis testing problem with error control across a sequence of approvals, including the bio-creep phenomenon. That line certifies sequences of approvals on labeled test batches. We certify each promotion from unlabeled traffic at optimal label cost, our audits compose across a sequence of promotions with family-wise control of bio-creep (Proposition 13), and their alpha-investing refinement ports directly. Podkopaev and Ramdas (2022) track the risk of a single deployed model with anytime-valid guarantees, and Shanmugam et al. (2025) estimate metrics for multiple models from labeled and unlabeled data without certified verdicts. Neither targets the paired promotion decision. Concurrent with this work, Shawn (2026) propose a McNemar-style anytime-valid commit gate for self-evolving agents, using a testing-by-betting e-process on paired instances to control the false-commit rate. That design shares our anytime-valid, paired, evidence-on-disagreements structure, and reaches similar conclusions about uncontrolled greedy acceptance, but provides neither a zero-label certificate, nor a label-complexity characterization, nor a matching lower bound, which are the central objects here.

Label-efficient evaluation.

Active testing and model-assisted evaluation reduce labels for estimating a single model’s risk (Kossen et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2023a), with sequential and active variants of two-sample testing in Li et al. (2024, 2025). Prediction-powered and surrogate-based estimators use model outputs to sharpen single-quantity inference (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a; Zrnic and Candès, 2024), and Boyeau et al. (2025) apply prediction-powered inference to pairwise model comparison from synthetic labels. These are fixed-horizon estimators of a metric, not certified anytime-valid promotion verdicts, and none provides a label-complexity lower bound. The estimand differs in a way that changes the complexity theory. For the paired difference, an observable signal (disagreement) supports the entire estimand, which makes the optimal label complexity quadratic rather than linear in ρ\rho and creates the Θ​(ρ)\Theta(\rho) separation we prove. Cross-model disagreement has been proposed as a label-free correctness heuristic (Gorbett and Jana, 2026) and disagreement-aware aggregate evaluation appears in Bonagiri et al. (2026). These works use disagreement as a signal for prediction quality, not as the support of a certified estimand, and provide no finite-sample guarantees.

Disagreement-based active learning.

The principle that labels are informative only where competing hypotheses disagree is the foundation of disagreement-based active learning, from the query-by-disagreement of Cohn et al. (1994) to the agnostic A2A^{2} algorithm of Balcan et al. (2009) and its analysis through Hanneke’s disagreement coefficient (Hanneke, 2007, 2014). That theory bounds the labels needed to learn a low-error classifier by the rate at which the disagreement region of a shrinking version space contracts around the target hypothesis. Our problem differs in both object and mathematics. We learn no classifier and maintain no version space. We hold two fixed given models and certify a scalar functional, the paired risk difference, with an anytime-valid guarantee. The disagreement region is fixed at the observable rate ρ\rho rather than shrinking with a disagreement coefficient, and our tolerance ε\varepsilon bounds a risk difference rather than the excess error of a learned hypothesis. Consequently our Θ~​(ρ2/ε2)\widetilde{\Theta}(\rho^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}) characterization is a mean-estimation and sequential-testing complexity for a functional, proved by a change-of-measure construction (Theorem 8), not a version-space label-complexity bound, and neither result subsumes the other. What the two share is the operational core we take as our starting point, that information concentrates on disagreements. The bound |Δ|≤B​ρ|\Delta|\leq B\rho of Lemma 2 is elementary and is the evaluation-side counterpart of the classical disagreement-region bound. The idea of using model disagreement on unlabeled data to validate and compare classifiers is older still, going back to the co-validation of Madani et al. (2004), who showed that disagreement lower-bounds error and can compare models without labels, and to the unlabeled-data generalization bounds of Kääriäinen (2005), which bound a risk gap by an unlabeled disagreement probability. Concurrent work of Bazinet et al. (2026) likewise certifies the gap between two predictors’ risks by evaluating a disagreement bound on unlabeled data, for static generalization certificates rather than a sequential update verdict. Our contribution is not this bound but its sequential certification, the zero-label tier it enables, and the matching two-sided label-complexity theory built around it.

Update churn and backward compatibility.

The applied literature documents the operational pain of updates, from prediction churn between successive models (Milani Fard et al., 2016) to negative flips and backward-compatibility metrics (Yan et al., 2021) and regression testing practice for ML systems (Srivastava et al., 2020). These works measure or reduce disagreement. We complete the pipeline. Given that updates disagree, we certify, at optimal label cost, whether the disagreement hurts.

Model multiplicity.

Predictive multiplicity (Marx et al., 2020; Black et al., 2022) studies distinct models with similar average risk. The disagreement region between near-equivalent models is exactly where our audits concentrate, and multiplicity explains why the benign-update regime (Δ≈0\Delta\approx 0, ρ\rho moderate) is common and why certifying it cheaply matters.

3 Setting and the Support Identity

Let (Xt,Yt)t≥1(X_{t},Y_{t})_{t\geq 1} be an i.i.d. stream from an unknown distribution PP over 𝒳×𝒴\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y} (drift is treated in Section 5.7). An incumbent model ff and a candidate gg map 𝒳\mathcal{X} to a prediction space 𝒜\mathcal{A}, and a loss ℓ:𝒜×𝒴→[0,B]\ell:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,B] scores predictions. Define the paired difference and its mean

Dt=ℓ​(g​(Xt),Yt)−ℓ​(f​(Xt),Yt)∈[−B,B],Δ=𝔼​[Dt],D_{t}\;=\;\ell\big(g(X_{t}),Y_{t}\big)-\ell\big(f(X_{t}),Y_{t}\big)\in[-B,B],\qquad\Delta\;=\;\mathbb{E}[D_{t}], (2)

so Δ>0\Delta>0 means the candidate is worse (a regression) and Δ≤0\Delta\leq 0 means it is no worse. For 0/10/1 loss, Δ\Delta is the accuracy drop and B=1B=1. The auditor observes both predictions on every point for free, hence also the disagreement indicator At=𝟏​{f​(Xt)≠g​(Xt)}A_{t}=\mathbf{1}\{f(X_{t})\neq g(X_{t})\} with rate ρ=ℙ​(At=1)\rho=\mathbb{P}(A_{t}=1), and may request the label YtY_{t} at unit cost.

Assumption 1 (Prediction-determined loss)

The loss is a function of the prediction and label alone, ℓ​(a,y)\ell(a,y), with no other dependence on the input. Its operative content is that whenever the two models make the same prediction, f​(Xt)=g​(Xt)f(X_{t})=g(X_{t}), they incur the same loss, so Dt=0D_{t}=0.

Assumption 1 holds for every standard supervised loss (0/10/1, cost-sensitive misclassification, top-kk, bounded regression losses on point predictions, exact-match and slot-level scores for structured prediction). It fails only when the loss consults model internals, for example calibration losses on confidence scores. Every guarantee in this paper is for this deterministic-prediction class. Judged and preference-based losses, including chat-quality and RLHF objectives, are outside it, and our language-model experiments are classification fine-tunes that satisfy Assumption 1 (Section 6.1). We discuss the extension in Section 8.

Verdicts.

Fix a tolerance ε∈(0,B)\varepsilon\in(0,B) and confidence level δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1). At any data-dependent time the auditor may issue one of two verdicts.

  • •

    Safe(ε)(\varepsilon), which asserts Δ<ε\Delta<\varepsilon (promote the update).

  • •

    Regression, which asserts Δ>0\Delta>0 (block or roll back).

Safe(ε)(\varepsilon) is a tolerance statement in the spirit of noninferiority testing. It asserts Δ<ε\Delta<\varepsilon rather than Δ≤0\Delta\leq 0, and we use no-regression in this ε\varepsilon-tolerance sense throughout. A procedure is (ε,δ)(\varepsilon,\delta)-sound if, with probability at least 1−δ1-\delta, no false verdict is ever issued at any time. Soundness at every stopping time is not optional in deployment, because promotion pipelines poll the audit continuously and act the moment a verdict fires, which is precisely the regime in which fixed-nn tests lose their guarantees (Ramdas et al., 2023).

Setting δ0+δ1=δ\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}=\delta makes the combined procedure (ε,δ)(\varepsilon,\delta)-sound (Theorem 4). Three design points deserve emphasis.

Judges accelerate, never invalidate.

A learned judge (an auxiliary model scoring which disagreements look suspicious) can drive πt\pi_{t} to concentrate labels where regressions would show. Because validity requires only predictability and the floor πmin\pi_{\min}, a judge that is stale, biased, or adversarial can waste budget but cannot break coverage (Theorem 5). This resolves the central tension of LLM-as-judge evaluation. The judge is used for efficiency, the guarantee never depends on its quality.

Per-slice verdicts.

Promotion decisions are rarely global. Partition traffic into KK slices (regions, languages, document types) and run one CS per slice with budget δ1/K\delta_{1}/K. Union coverage gives simultaneous validity of all KK verdicts (Proposition 12), enabling verdicts of the form “safe overall, regressing on slice kk” with localization guarantees.

Compute cost.

5.3 Matching label-complexity lower bounds

The next results show the protocol is not merely efficient but optimal, and quantify exactly what the pairing signal is worth. Proofs use a change-of-measure argument for adaptively stopped, adaptively routed procedures in the style of sequential identification lower bounds (Kaufmann et al., 2016), with a two-world construction in which unlabeled observations are common to both worlds and only labeled disagreements carry information. The change-of-measure machinery is standard, and the two-atom construction is related to noise-rate lower bounds in active learning (Hanneke, 2014). What is new is not the technique but the object it is applied to, a paired risk difference whose signal lives on an observable measure-ρ\rho support, which is what produces the ρ\rho-dependence and, against a pairing-blind auditor, the Θ​(1/ρ)\Theta(1/\rho) separation. The construction does not reduce to substituting ρ\rho for a noise rate in an existing bound, because the two worlds must share their entire unlabeled stream, including the disagreement pattern, and differ only in the labels on disagreements.

In the regime ρ≥2​ε\rho\geq 2\varepsilon, the ratio of optimal pairing-blind to optimal pairing-aware label complexity is Θ​(1/ρ)\Theta(1/\rho). Observing both models’ predictions on unlabeled traffic, a pure compute cost, cuts the human labeling bill by the inverse disagreement rate.

Corollary 10 is the paper’s economic statement. At the disagreement rates of real update pairs, whose medians run from below 10−310^{-3} to 0.150.15 across update types (Table 1) and reach 0.450.45 on individual pairs, the factor ranges from a few-fold at the high-ρ\rho end to beyond 1000×1000\times when ρ\rho is small.

Remark 11 (What the pairing-blind class prices)

The separation prices exactly one capability, observing the pair’s agreement on unlabeled traffic. An auditor granted the disagreement indicators AtA_{t} on unlabeled points is by definition not pairing-blind. It can restrict labeling to disagreements and attain the pairing-aware rate of Theorem 7. The class of Theorem 9 is therefore not a tailored weakening but the natural dichotomy between auditors that shadow-score all traffic and those that label sampled traffic without doing so, the latter being standard when running both models on the full stream is not instrumented. The Θ​(1/ρ)\Theta(1/\rho) gap is the operational value of that instrumentation, a compute cost, measured in the labels, a human cost, that it saves. The gap does not survive granting the blind auditor the disagreement signal, and that is the point. The signal is precisely what pairing is.

5.5 Sequences of promotions

Deployed systems are not updated once. A promoted candidate becomes the next incumbent, a new candidate arrives, and the sequence of approvals carries its own risk, the bio-creep phenomenon of Feng et al. (2021) in which repeated tolerant approvals let quality drift downward. Our audits compose across such a sequence at no additional modeling cost.

Proposition 13 (Sequence-level soundness)

Let updates arrive as a possibly adaptive sequence, where the kk-th incumbent-candidate pair and audit configuration may depend on all earlier data and verdicts. Run audit kk on fresh traffic at levels (δ0(k),δ1(k))(\delta_{0}^{(k)},\delta_{1}^{(k)}) with ∑k≥1(δ0(k)+δ1(k))≤δseq\sum_{k\geq 1}\big(\delta_{0}^{(k)}+\delta_{1}^{(k)}\big)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{seq}}. Then with probability at least 1−δseq1-\delta_{\mathrm{seq}}, no audit in the entire sequence ever issues a false verdict. In particular the number of regressing updates that are ever certified Safe at their stated tolerances is zero with probability at least 1−δseq1-\delta_{\mathrm{seq}}.

With the schedule δ(k)∝k−2\delta^{(k)}\propto k^{-2} the guarantee covers an unbounded stream of future updates from a single constant budget, and with an equal split it covers a known horizon. Each successive audit is more expensive by only a log⁡(1/δ(k))\log(1/\delta^{(k)}) factor (Theorem 7), so the price of lifetime soundness is logarithmic in the update index. The alpha-investing refinement of Feng et al. (2021), which grows the error budget after good outcomes, ports directly and can improve power, but the union schedule already rules out bio-creep in the family-wise sense.

Two measurability points make the argument precise. First, the kk-th audit may be chosen fully adaptively, because its configuration is HkH_{k}-measurable and Theorem 4 is applied conditionally on HkH_{k}; adaptivity of the promotion policy costs nothing. Second, the proposition runs each audit on fresh traffic, which is what lets audit kk’s increments be conditionally valid given HkH_{k}. If instead audits share or overlap traffic, family-wise soundness still holds, since the union bound over ∑kδ(k)\sum_{k}\delta^{(k)} needs no independence between audits, and only the label-efficiency of concurrent audits degrades, not their validity.

5.7 Beyond stationarity

Under distribution shift there is no fixed Δ\Delta to certify. Two honest statements are available. First, the predictable-plug-in CS remains a valid CS for the running weighted average of the conditional means Δ~t=∑i≤tλi​𝔼​[Zi∣ℱi−1]/∑i≤tλi\widetilde{\Delta}_{t}=\sum_{i\leq t}\lambda_{i}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]/\sum_{i\leq t}\lambda_{i} (Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, 2024), so verdicts can be read as statements about traffic-averaged regression over the audit window. The unrestarted CS under two aggressive synthetic drift schedules miscovers a moving running-average target (6.4%6.4\% under smooth drift, 15.9%15.9\% under adversarial bursts, Table 2), and the windowed variant repairs it on paired same-seed streams (0.00010.0001 and 0.0650.065 against 0.0630.063 and 0.1560.156 unrestarted, Section 6.5). 7 Deployment and Governance Artifact

Each completed audit emits a compact evidence record, comprising the tolerance and confidence, both tiers’ final intervals, the verdict and its stopping time, the number of labels consumed and their routing trace, and the per-slice table. The record is sufficient for a third party to recompute the boundary crossing from logged increments, making the promotion decision auditable after the fact. Records compose across successive updates under the budget schedule of Proposition 13, so a chain of promotions carries one lifetime soundness guarantee. We present the regulatory connection as motivation rather than a compliance claim. The record is the kind of technical log contemplated by the logging and post-market monitoring duties for high-risk systems under the EU AI Act (Articles 12 and 72), and by internal model-risk-management review boards that must sign off on updates. A legal assessment is beyond our scope. We provide the emitter and a reference validation script in the accompanying code release.

8 Limitations and Scope

Losses through internals. Assumption 1 excludes losses that consult scores rather than predictions (calibration error). For losses Lipschitz in the model output, the soft support identity of Proposition 14 recovers the zero-label certificate and the audited tier with ρ\rho replaced by the soft-disagreement moments; matching the lower bound there, and handling open-ended judged generation where the output is discrete text, remain open. Drift. Our guarantee under drift is the weighted-average statement of Section 5.7, and the burst experiment shows the unrestarted CS can miscover a moving target by design. The windowed variant restores per-regime verdicts at a logarithmic price, and choosing windows adaptively is future work. Scale of the LLM zoo. The language-model pairs reach 1.41.4B parameters, which fits our audit-with-exact-ground-truth methodology. Nothing in the engine consumes anything beyond paired predictions and labels, so scale enters only through ρ\rho and BB, and the identical behaviour from 160160M to 1.41.4B supports the claim. Auditing a 7070B model is a matter of shadow-scoring cost, not new statistics. Auditing preference-tuned chat updates, where the loss itself is a judged quantity, requires the internals extension above, and our zoo contains no DPO or RLHF update pairs for the same reason. Baseline adaptations. No off-the-shelf sequential method targets the paired update delta, so our prediction-powered and active-testing comparisons (Section 6.4) are our own sequential adaptations of those estimators, constructed generously (both receive the pairing-aware surrogate for free) but still adaptations. Allocation. Per-slice budgets use the uniform δ1/K\delta_{1}/K split. Optimal Neyman-style allocation across slices is analyzed nowhere in this paper and left open. Sequences of promotions. Proposition 13 gives family-wise soundness across a promotion chain from a union schedule. What it does not give is adaptive budget allocation. The alpha-investing policies of Feng et al. (2021) spend error budget in response to outcomes and can be strictly more powerful over long sequences, and combining them with our per-audit label optimality is open. Estimand. Δ\Delta certifies average regression, and per-slice auditing covers known strata. Certifying against unknown subpopulations connects to multicalibration and is a natural extension.

9 Conclusion

The promotion decision for a model update reduces to a paired estimand with an observable support set, and taking that structure seriously yields a complete theory, giving anytime-valid certificates robust to arbitrary label routing, a zero-label tier that covers the bulk of real updates, matching upper and lower bounds characterizing the label complexity at Θ~​(ρ2/ε2)\widetilde{\Theta}(\rho^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}) up to logarithmic factors, and a proven Θ​(ρ)\Theta(\rho) separation that prices the value of running both models on unlabeled traffic. The protocol certified real update pairs across four modalities and real LoRA fine-tunes with essentially zero excess risk and a small fraction of the labeling cost of current practice. We believe certified, rate-optimal update auditing belongs in every promotion pipeline, and we will release the complete machinery for that purpose upon publication.

The complete pipeline is orchestrated by a single resumable script with per-audit atomic artifacts, frozen-gate evaluation, figure generation, and a final status report. Feature caches (RoBERTa, LayoutLMv3, ResNet, ViT, DINOv2, CLIP) are produced by standard pretrained extractors run once and reused here as fixed representations, so no result depends on how they were computed. The release comprises the audit engine (∼300{\sim}300 lines for both tiers), the update-pair builders, all 14,23314{,}233 per-stream records itemized in Table 6, and one-command reproduction of every table and figure. The pre-registration document with its frozen gates and timestamped amendments, and the reserved-seed protocol, are part of the same artifact. The full package will be released publicly upon publication and is available to reviewers on request.
