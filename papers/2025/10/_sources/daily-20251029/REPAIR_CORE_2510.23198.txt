Abstract
Continual pre-training (CPT) for domain adaptation must balance target-domain gains with stability on the base domain. Existing CPT scaling laws typically assume a fixed pre-training budget, which limits their ability to forecast adaptation outcomes for models trained at different tokens-per-parameter (PTPP). We present PTPP-aware adaptation scaling laws that make the pre-training budget an explicit variable, enabling accurate prediction of adaptation loss at unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}. On a multilingual setup (English/Arabic →\rightarrow French), PTPP-aware formulations trained on early stages (P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}={15,31}) predict target loss at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279 and outperform a PTPP-agnostic D-cpt transfer baseline on metrics (Huber-on-log, MAErel, calibration slope); full diagnostics (RMSE, MAPE) are in the appendix. Beyond forecasting, we show a practical use case: planning replay ratios and adaptation token budgets that satisfy target and forgetting constraints under compute limits.
1 Introduction
Capabilities of LLMs (large language models) continue to scale with model size, data size, and thus the total compute used for pre-training. Language models trained on a mixture of domains, dominated by web-scale corpora, yield general LLMs (Biderman et al., 2023; Dey et al., 2023; Team et al., 2025; OLMo et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025). These generalist models may not perform well in tasks requiring specialized knowledge (e.g., in fields such as medicine, law, finance) or those requiring language capabilities beyond the dominant pre-training language. We must therefore adapt these models to new, domain-specific, or target-language-specific data.
This adaptation process presents a fundamental challenge: achieving strong performance in the target domain while preserving general capabilities (avoiding catastrophic forgetting (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017)).
Various strategies have been proposed to minimize forgetting (Chen et al., 2025; Ostapenko et al., 2022; Biderman et al., 2024; Ibrahim et al., 2024; Gupta et al., 2023).
Pre-training scaling laws are well established—e.g., relations between model/data size and performance in Kaplan et al. (2020); Hoffmann et al. (2022)—whereas CPT-specific laws are comparatively underexplored. D-CPT extends Chinchilla with replay to study compute-optimal CPT at a fixed pre-training stage (Que et al., 2024), and forgetting laws quantify degradation on the pre-training domain at that stage (Bethune et al., 2025).
However, most CPT scaling laws—D-CPT and forgetting laws included—assume a fixed pre-training budget (a single P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} stage), which limits forecasting across budgets. Prior work indicates that P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} modulates learning dynamics and downstream adaptation (Springer et al., 2025; Ash and Adams, 2020; Lyle et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2024). We therefore condition explicitly on P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}, yielding PTPP-aware adaptation laws that predict target-domain loss at unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} and clarify replay–stage interactions. Our central question: can a law fit at early stages (P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}={15,31}) forecast target validation loss at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279?
Although our experiments focus on language adaptation, treating P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} as an explicit driver of adaptation dynamics is broadly applicable. Prior multilingual adaptation work underscores the need to mitigate and estimate forgetting while acquiring target competence (de Vries and Nissim, 2021; Fujii et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Gosal et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). Our contributions include:
1.
PTPP-aware adaptation laws. We extend CPT scaling laws by integrating the pre-training budget (P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}) as an explicit variable in the functional form.
2.
Forecasting at unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}. Fits at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=15,31 predict French loss at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279 and outperform a P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}-agnostic D-cpt baseline on all metrics; a handful of 241M-scale “anchor” points at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279 (20 calibration measurements at the evaluation stage) further improve accuracy at low-cost.
3.
Planning under constraints. Using the fitted law, we find an optimal replay ratio and adaptation token budget that satisfy target and forgetting constraints under compute limits.

Anchors.
We also report a few-shot variant that augments the fit with 20 small-scale (241M) anchors collected at the evaluation stage (P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279) across the (r,D)(r,D) grid; all other P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279 points remain held out (unlike the oracle, which fits on the full P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279 set). These anchors tighten calibration (slope →1\to 1) and error metrics at low-cost.

GPT-2–style decoders are pre-trained on English/Arabic (source) and adapted to French (target). Takeaways.
Across French at unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279, the gated+floor variant (Form 3) is consistently best, with low errors and near-ideal calibration (slope ≈0.99{\approx}0.99) both without anchors and with 20 small-scale anchors; the gated-only variant (Form 2) is reliably second and ahead of D-cpt transfer. Anchors uniformly tighten Huber/RMSE and calibration without changing the methods’ rankings.
On the English/Arabic source domain (Appendix Appendix A: Full metric tables), the picture depends on supervision at the evaluation stage: without anchors, floor-only (Form 1) suffices—suggesting P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} mainly shifts the baseline—whereas with anchors the gated-only form (Form 2) becomes best, revealing a data-efficiency (shape) effect once lightly calibrated at P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279. Overall, results support that a) the preferred functional form can be domain and/or supervision-dependent and b) a direct link exists between pre-training compute and adaptation efficiency that manifests as both a floor shift and a learning-curve shape change; few-shot anchors prove to be a low-cost way to calibrate the latter.
4 Use Case: Joint Compute and Replay Optimization
In domain adaptation, one must balance forgetting of the source domain with improvements in the target domain, under strict compute budgets. A key feature of our method is that ptpp-aware scaling-law fits allow prediction of both losses at an unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} (279), making it possible to solve this trade-off analytically rather than through brute-force sweeps.
We consider a target model scale of N=8.1N=8.1B, pretrained at P​tpp=279\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279, and seek the smallest adaptation tokens-per-parameter (A​tpp\mathit{A}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}) that meets the forgetting and target-performance constraints, under the Form 1 hypothesis for English/Arabic loss and Form 3 for French. Let the adaptation budget A​tpp=D/N\mathit{A}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=D/N. We solve:
minA​tpp≥0,r∈[0,1]⁡A​tpps.t.Δ​Lsrc​(N,D,r, 279)≤δ,Ltgt​(N,D,r, 279)≤τ.\min_{\mathit{A}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}\geq 0,\;r\in[0,1]}\;\;\mathit{A}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\Delta L_{\mathrm{src}}\bigl(N,\;D,\;r,\;279\bigr)\leq\delta,\;\;L_{\mathrm{tgt}}\bigl(N,\;D,\;r,\;279\bigr)\leq\tau.
where Δ​Lsrc=Lsrc​(N,D,r,P​tpp)−Lsrc​(N,0,1,P​tpp)\Delta L_{\mathrm{src}}=L_{\mathrm{src}}(N,D,r,\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}})-L_{\mathrm{src}}(N,0,1,\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}), NN is model size, and r∈[0,1]r\in[0,1] the replay ratio. Constraints are given by tolerated forgetting δ\delta (e.g. +2%) and target French loss threshold τ\tau=1.8. 5 Conclusion
We proposed PTPP-aware adaptation scaling laws that condition on the pre-training budget and predict target performance at unseen P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}. On French at P​tpp=279\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}{=}279, laws fit at early stages (P​tpp∈{15,31}\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}\in\{15,31\}) generalize well and outperform a P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}-agnostic D-cpt transfer baseline; a small set of 241M-scale anchors further improves accuracy. Empirically, pre-training progress modulates both the loss floor and the adaptation efficiency, and a few low-cost anchors further enhance the prediction performances; on the source domain, floor shifts explain most gains without anchors, while the light anchoring reveals a data-dependent effect at P​tpp=279\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}{=}279. These fits enable the optimization of replay and adaptation tokens under compute constraints. Promising directions include investigating how language transfer shapes the P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} effect, extending to additional P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}} stages and domains, assessing task-level metrics, and adding uncertainty quantification with cost-aware anchor selection.

Let (yi,y^i)(y_{i},\hat{y}_{i}) be observed and predicted validation losses at the held-out stage (P​tpp\mathit{P}\mathrm{\textsc{tpp}}=279). We evaluate errors primarily in log space to capture multiplicative miss and stabilize heteroscedasticity. Define the log–residuals ri=log⁡y^i−log⁡yir_{i}=\log\hat{y}_{i}-\log y_{i}.
Huberlog\mathrm{Huber}_{\log} ( ↓\downarrow ) is the mean Huber loss applied to rir_{i} with threshold δ=0.02\delta{=}0.02:
Huberδ​(r)={12​r2,|r|≤δ,δ⁡(|r|−12​δ),|r|>δ,Huberlog=1n​∑iHuberδ​(ri).\mathrm{Huber}_{\delta}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}r^{2},&|r|\leq\delta,\\[2.0pt]
\delta\bigl(|r|-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\bigr),&|r|>\delta,\end{cases}\qquad\mathrm{Huber}_{\log}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\mathrm{Huber}_{\delta}(r_{i}).
It is quadratic near zero (like MSE) but linear for outliers, making it robust.
