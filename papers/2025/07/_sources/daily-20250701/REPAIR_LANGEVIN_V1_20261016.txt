Abstract
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models represent an entirely new class of generative AI methods that have yet to be fully explored. Critical damping has been successfully introduced in Critically-Damped Langevin Dynamics (CLD) and Critically-Damped Third-Order Langevin Dynamics (TOLD++), but has not yet been applied to dynamics of arbitrary order. The proposed line of work generalizes Higher-Order Langevin Dynamics (HOLD), a recent state-of-the-art diffusion method, by introducing the concept of critical damping from systems analysis.

1 Introduction
Fundamentally, generative AI converts a high-dimensional, and otherwise intractable, data distribution into a simple distribution. Today, the dominant methodology for image, video, and non-linguistic sample generation is the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (diffusion model) Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015); Ho et al. (2020). Our proposed enhancement to this framework accelerates convergence because modeling the nnth order diffusion-time derivative smooths the denoising process.
NVIDIA’s seminal Critically-Damped Langevin Dynamics (CLD) improves upon the standard diffusion process by adding an auxiliary variable, referred to as velocity Dockhorn et al. (2022). The addition of velocity smooths the data variable’s convergence from the data distribution to latent distribution, and vice versa, because the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process is not directly applied to the data variable. The concept of “critical damping” is borrowed from classical systems theory to describe the family of optimal parameter choices. Third-Order Langevin Dynamics (TOLD) Shi and Liu (2024a) improves upon CLD by simplifying the forward and backward stochastic processes and adding a second auxiliary variable, acceleration. Critically-Damped Third-Order Langevin Dynamics (TOLD++) Sterling and Bugallo (2025) advances TOLD by deriving a set of critically-damped third-order dynamics and proving that these dynamics result in optimal convergence. Beyond optimality, critically damping the third-order system simplifies the TOLD algorithm, reducing computational cost. The invention of TOLD++ raises the question: is it possible to critically damp dynamics of arbitrary order? From our preliminary theoretical results, the answer is yes.
To push the potential of this technique even further, we propose Critically-Damped Higher-Order Langevin Dynamics (HOLD++). We further argue that critical damping is optimal, with a small caveat, and discuss the tradeoff between higher model orders and computational burden.
2 Related Works
Underdamped Diffusion Bridges Blessing et al. (2025) have recently been proposed to accelerate the dynamics of both the forward and backwards processes with control neural networks. Their approach fundamentally differs from this work’s as we only consider optimal constantly parameterized stochastic processes. It is important to note that in reference to the works of Cheng et al. (2017); Blessing et al. (2025), that damping in this context is defined a bit differently: in these other works, Langevin dynamics without auxiliary variables are overdamped as they do not model system acceleration, and Langevin dynamics with one auxiliary variable are under damped.
Since its invention, Higher Order Langevin Dynamics have found many applications, including the tasks of voice generation Shi and Liu (2024b) and noisy image restoration Shi and Liu (2024c). They have even been adapted to operate on manifolds Bortoli et al. (2022), and Lie Groups Zhu et al. (2025). The latter has recently found application in generative modeling of chemical structures Cornet et al. (2025).

A consequential result is proven in Theorem 3, that the critically damped parameter choice is optimal for a fixed trace of the forward matrix 𝐅\mathbf{F}. This result generalizes the optimality known for second-order dynamics and proven for third-order dynamics in Sterling and Bugallo (2025). There is a small caveat discussed after the theorem, but this result holds on unconstrained γ1\gamma_{1}.
4 Optimality of Critical Damping
Theorem 3.
If ξ\xi is fixed, then the critically damped parameter choices are optimal according to the following objective:

Tr(𝐅)=∑i=1nλi=−ξ→ξ=−∑i=1nλiTr(\mathbf{F})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=-\xi\quad\quad\to\quad\quad\xi=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}
