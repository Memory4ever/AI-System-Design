Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across diverse domains, but their training remains resource- and time-intensive, requiring massive compute power and careful orchestration of training procedures. Model souping-the practice of averaging weights from multiple models of the same architecture-has emerged as a promising pre- and post-training technique that can enhance performance without expensive retraining. In this paper, we introduce Soup Of Category Experts (SoCE), a principled approach for model souping that utilizes benchmark composition to identify optimal model candidates and applies non-uniform weighted averaging to maximize performance. The core idea is to move beyond uniform averaging by identifying "expert" models for each weakly-correlated category and combining them with optimized weights to maximize aggregate performance. The method consists of four key steps formalized in Algorithm 1: (1) correlation analysis to detect low-correlation category pairs, (2) expert model selection based on per-category performance rankings, (3) weight optimization over a discrete grid to maximize overall benchmark score, and (4) weighted model souping to produce the final combined model. Unlike prior methods that average all available models uniformly, SoCE first filters candidates based on anti-correlation criteria, ensuring diversity in expertise, and then applies non-uniform weights to emphasize stronger contributors. The algorithm operates on a set of candidate models and a benchmark with multiple categories. The evaluation compares three variants: Uniform Souping (all models averaged equally), Uniform Souping with SoCE model selection (strategic selection but uniform weights), and full SoCE (selection plus optimized weighting). On BFCL, SoCE achieves state-of-the-art performance: 80.68% accuracy for 70B models (2.7% improvement over the best individual model) and 76.50% for 8B models (5.7% relative gain). The optimal configuration for 70B models combines xLAM-2-70b-fc-r, CoALM-70B, and watt-tool-70B with weights 0.5, 0.2, and 0.3, respectively. For 8B models, weights of 0.7, 0.2, and 0.1 are optimal. Ablation studies show that both model selection and weight optimization contribute significantly: selection alone improves performance, and adding weight tuning yields additional gains of 2.28% (70B) and 3.44% (8B). On MGSM, SoCE outperforms individual models and uniform souping, achieving a 1.57% relative improvement over the best baseline. Uniform souping of all candidates leads to performance regression, but SoCE mitigates this through expert selection and optimized weighting. Similarly, on ∞-Bench, SoCE improves the best candidate by 0.66%, with both selection and weighting contributing positively.
