L327@P5: 5 Incrementally detokenizing ill-formed UTF-8 sequences
L328@P5: It is common for language model applications to stream or otherwise incrementally generate tokens, decoding and displaying the decoded tokens opportunistically. Interactive systems, for example, can display the intermediate steps of the generation to the user; hosted
L329@P5: language models on a remote server might send tokens to the client as soon as they become
L330@P5: available, rather than wait for generation to complete before sending anything downstream.
L331@P5: As we have shown in Section 4, certain combinations of tokens can be ill-formed UTF-8,
L332@P5: causing a process that attempts to decode them to apply one of the coping strategies of
L333@P5: Subsection 3. The decoding processes used in deployed tokenizers use the third strategy
L334@P5: described at the end of Section 3: “replacing the [ill-formed] section with other symbols”,
L335@P5: in particular they use the symbol U+FFFD (“replacement character”); our formalism
L336@P5: calls it η.

L338@P5: for all the tokens before interpreting them all together. This problem was detected in serving engines for language models, which host language models and serve them through
L339@P5: an API locally or over the network: these engines failed to generate correct text, because
L340@P5: they incorrectly assumed that the conversion from bytes to well-formed UTF-8 was a homomorphism. This problem and a common mitigation strategy were introduced to Hugging Face TGI by a user’s issue (Hugging Face, 2025; 0x1997, 2023) and percolated thence
