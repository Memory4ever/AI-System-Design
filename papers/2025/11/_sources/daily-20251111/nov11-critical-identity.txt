L5@P0: Abstract
L6@P0: Subword tokenization segments input text according to a pre-defined vocabulary to feed it into a language model; the language model, in turn,
L7@P0: generates a sequence made from this same vocabulary. The members of the
L8@P0: vocabulary can be built of code points or bytes. Using code points means
L9@P0: that all members of the vocabulary are valid UTF-8 characters. However, it
L10@P0: also requires thousands of initial members to achieve acceptable coverage
L11@P0: of inputs. Beginning with bytes, on the contrary, avoids out-of-vocabulary
L12@P0: errors with only 256 initial members of the vocabulary, but the members of
L13@P0: the vocabulary and sequences of them are not guaranteed to be valid UTF8. Sequences that are not valid UTF-8 break code that assumes its input
L14@P0: to be valid UTF-8. Applications of language models must account for the
L15@P0: breakage thereby introduced. In this paper, we formalize tokenization using monoid theory and prove that tokenizers whose vocabularies contain
L16@P0: tokens that are ill-formed UTF-8 can always produce sequences that are illformed UTF-8. We show formally that attempting to incrementally convert
L17@P0: tokens back to a string and interpret the results as UTF-8 gives different results than converting the whole sequence of tokens at once. This formal
L18@P0: result predicts real-world bugs: we evaluate mitigations for the problem
L19@P0: identified and provide case studies of major foundation models and constrained generation systems.
L20@P0: 1 Introduction
L21@P0: Large language models (LLMs) compute the probability of a given sequence of tokens
L22@P0: drawn with replacement from a finite vocabulary. Tokenization is the process of cutting an
L23@P0: input string into tokens such that each token is a member of a predefined vocabulary. Traditionally, tokenization used prior knowledge of the language, such as words, morphemes,
L24@P0: or phonemes, to separate the text into useful elements (Grefenstette & Tapanainen, 1994;
L25@P0: Palmer, 2000). Contemporary natural language processing has moved to using “subword”
L26@P0: tokenization, where the vocabulary is derived automatically from a corpus of texts rather
L27@P0: than programmed manually (Mielke et al., 2021).
L28@P0: The text the tokenizer works with is stored in computers as bytes. In order to interpret these
L29@P0: bytes as characters meaningful to humans, we must define an encoding scheme mapping
L30@P0: between a sequence of bytes and a sequence of characters. The predominant scheme is
L31@P0: called UTF-8, regulated by Unicode Technical Committee (2025, §3.4, 3.9.3, 3.10). As of
L32@P0: 2025, UTF-8 is used by 98.5% of all websites (W3Techs, 2025) and is required by the W3C
L33@P0: for user agents (van Kesteren, 2024, §4.2).

L35@P0: languages and models. Contemporary language models’ tokenizers typically work with the
L36@P0: bytes on the bottom row of Figure 1 rather than the characters on the top row. Abstracting
L37@P0: away the bytes to focus on the characters is an instance of a “leaky” abstraction, a common
L38@P0: issue in software engineering: the information that we tried to hide by an abstraction ends
L39@P0: up being exposed (often as we need to handle errors) (see Spolsky, 2002; Kiczales et al.,
L40@P0: 1992; Kiczales, 1991).

L8@P0: Language models exhibit remarkable natural language generation capabilities but remain prone to
L9@P0: hallucinations, generating factually incorrect information despite producing syntactically coherent
L10@P0: responses. This study introduces the Licensing Oracle, an architectural solution designed to stem
L11@P0: hallucinations in LMs by enforcing truth constraints through formal validation against structured
L12@P0: knowledge graphs. Unlike statistical approaches that rely on data scaling or fine-tuning, the Licensing
L13@P0: Oracle embeds a deterministic validation step into the model’s generative process, ensuring that only
L14@P0: factually accurate claims are made. We evaluated the effectiveness of the Licensing Oracle through
L15@P0: experiments comparing it with several state-of-the-art methods, including baseline language model
L16@P0: generation, fine-tuning for factual recall, fine-tuning for abstention behavior, and retrieval-augmented
L17@P0: generation (RAG). Our results demonstrate that although RAG and fine-tuning improve performance, they
L18@P0: fail to eliminate hallucinations. In contrast, the Licensing Oracle achieved perfect abstention precision
L19@P0: (AP = 1.0) and zero false answers (FAR-NE = 0.0), ensuring that only valid claims were generated with
L20@P0: 89.1% accuracy in factual responses. This work shows that architectural innovations, such as the Licensing
L21@P0: Oracle, offer a necessary and sufficient solution for hallucinations in domains with structured knowledge
L22@P0: representations, offering guarantees that statistical methods cannot match. Although the Licensing Oracle
L23@P0: is specifically designed to address hallucinations in fact-based domains, its framework lays the
L24@P0: groundwork for truth-constrained generation in future AI systems, providing a new path toward reliable,
L25@P0: epistemically grounded models.
L26@P0: Keywords: language models, hallucination, knowledge graphs, retrieval-augmented generation, SHACL
L27@P0: validation, epistemic calibration, truth-constrained architectures
L28@P0: 1. Introduction
L29@P0: Language Models (LMs)1 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in natural language generation;
L30@P0: however, they remain fundamentally unreliable in terms of factual accuracy, often producing statements
L31@P0: that are syntactically correct but empirically false. This phenomenon, commonly referred to as
L32@P0: "hallucination," arises from the limitations of the transformer architecture, which is optimized for
L33@P0: generating fluent sequences of tokens based on statistical patterns rather than grounding output in verifiable
L34@P0: knowledge (Kalai et al., 2025).
L35@P0: In our prior study, "How Large Language Models Are Designed to Hallucinate" (Ackermann & Emanuilov,
L36@P0: 2025) we argued that hallucination is not merely an artifact of insufficient training data or poor fine-tuning
L37@P0: but rather a structural feature of the transformer model itself. Specifically, transformers function as
L38@P0: coherence engines that lack the mechanisms to regulate epistemic commitments or enforce truth
L39@P0: constraints. Consequently, LLMs generate plausible-sounding claims without being able to distinguish
L40@P0: between true and false information.

L44@P1: To address this fundamental issue, we propose the concept of a Licensing Oracle, an architectural
L45@P1: enhancement that integrates the formal verification of factual claims into the generative process. Unlike
L46@P1: statistical learning methods, which rely on data patterns and probabilistic reasoning, the Licensing Oracle
L47@P1: enforces epistemic boundaries through deterministic validation against a structured knowledge graph and
L48@P1: the The Shapes Constraint Language (SHACL) rules. This ensures that LMs can generate claims that are
L49@P1: both logically consistent and supported by verifiable evidence, thereby eliminating hallucinations
L50@P1: regarding structured factual knowledge.
L51@P1: In this study, we build upon the theoretical foundation established in our earlier work and present the results
L52@P1: of a series of experiments designed to evaluate the effectiveness of the Licensing Oracle. Our experiments
L53@P1: involved five distinct approaches to factual question-answering: baseline LM generation, fine-tuning for
L54@P1: factual recall, fine-tuning for abstention, retrieval-augmented generation, and graph-based RAG that
L55@P1: incorporates the Licensing Oracle for formal validation. The experimental results demonstrate that while
L56@P1: statistical methods (e.g., fine-tuning and RAG) can influence factual accuracy, they cannot eliminate
L57@P1: hallucinations in a reliable or deterministic manner. In contrast, the Licensing Oracle achieves near-perfect
L58@P1: abstention precision (AP = 1.0) and effectively eliminates false answers, providing a structural solution to
